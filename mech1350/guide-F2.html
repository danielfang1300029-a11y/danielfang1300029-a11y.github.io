Decision procedure · which area, which equation (F2.1)
  1. Number the sections along the flow and list what is given at each (\(D\), \(V\), \(Q\)). Cue: "velocity at section 1" plus two diameters means one ratio.
  2. Choose the area of each section: round pipe or jet \(\pi D^2/4\); thin radial sheet leaving a plate \(\pi Dt\); a stated area ("half that at station 1") stays a symbol. Convert mm and cm to m first.
  3. Write the budget: steady, so in = out. One inlet, one outlet: \(V_1A_1=V_2A_2\). Several openings: \(\sum(VA)_\text{in}=\sum(VA)_\text{out}\); identical openings at the same level share equally. Gas with changing density: keep \(\rho\).
  4. "Relate symbolically": stop at the ratio (\(V_2=2V_1\)); no numbers are wanted.
  5. Flow rates: \(Q=VA\) at any one section; \(\dot m=\rho Q\) (kg/s); weight flow \(\gamma Q\) (N/s).
  6. Sense check: smaller area, higher speed; \(\gamma Q\div\dot m=g\).
Answer skeleton · the two F2.2 sketches (2 marks each)

Diagram to draw (control volume): the object redrawn, a dashed boundary around it that cuts each inlet and outlet jet straight across, an arrow and label (\(U_1\), \(A_1\)…) on every flow crossing it, and any support force the boundary cuts (scale reaction \(R\)).

Diagram to draw (diverging channel): walls widening from region 1 (\(A_1\)) to region 2 (\(A_2\)); 4–6 streamlines, evenly spaced and parallel in region 1, fanning out to follow the walls, wider spacing in region 2, arrows in the flow direction.

Marker points: (1) boundary closed and dashed; (2) every inlet/outlet cut perpendicular to the flow and labelled in/out; (3) external forces at cuts shown if a force is asked later. Streamlines: (1) do not cross or touch; (2) outer ones follow the walls; (3) spread where the area grows (lower speed), with arrows.

2F2.1 · Continuity: Q = AV, mass & weight flow rates

\(A=\pi D^2/4\) in m² → \(V_1A_1=V_2A_2\) (sum over openings) → \(Q=VA\), \(\dot m=\rho Q\), weight flow \(\gamma Q\). Learn it: note-F2 §6 flow rates · §7 continuity equation · §8 examples 1–2 · §11 continuity in exams

Ready answer · one inlet, one outlet (liquid) \[\begin{aligned}Q&=V_1A_1=V_2A_2\\V_2&=V_1\frac{A_1}{A_2}=V_1\Big(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\Big)^2\\\dot m&=\rho Q,\qquad \dot W=\gamma Q\end{aligned}\]

In words: what goes in each second comes out each second; halve the diameter and the speed rises four times.

Dec 2024 Section B Q4(b)2 marksparts (a)(d): F3.6; (c): F3.4; (e): F4.2no Section C this sitting

(Section B data: density of water = 1,000 kg/m³; acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s²; pressure means gauge pressure.) As is shown in Figure B4, an air duct flow-metering device consists of a stagnation probe at station 2 and a static pressure tap at station 1. The cross-sectional area of the duct at station 2 is one-half that at station 1, where the diameter of the duct is 10 cm. Air of density 1.2 kg/m³ flows through the duct to discharge into atmospheric air at the open end (station 2). A water manometer is connected between the stagnation probe and the pressure tap, and a reading of 1 cm is measured. You may assume that blockage of flow due to the probe at station 2 is negligible.

(b) By continuity, relate symbolically the velocities at the two stations. (2 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. Air at these speeds is treated as incompressible; you are given \(A_2\) as a fraction of \(A_1\).

Solution
  1. Steady flow, density constant (low-speed air, \(\rho=1.2\u{kg/m^3}\) throughout): \(\rho V_1A_1=\rho V_2A_2\), so \(V_1A_1=V_2A_2\).
  2. \(A_2=\tfrac12A_1\), so \(V_1A_1=V_2\cdot\tfrac12A_1\).
\[\boxed{V_2=\frac{A_1}{A_2}V_1=2V_1}\]

Marker note: "symbolically" means the relation, not numbers; state the assumption (steady, incompressible) for the second mark.

Part (d) later gives \(V_1\approx12.8\u{m/s}\), \(V_2\approx25.6\u{m/s}\): the ratio checks.

Dec 2023 Q1(a)–(d)3 + 3 + 3 + 3 marksAlso: Dec 2022 Q1(a)–(d) (word for word)

The inner diameters of a circular pipe in sections 1 and 2 are 50 mm and 100 mm respectively. Water at 70 °C is flowing with an average velocity of 8 m/s at section 1. Calculate: (The specific weight of water at 70 °C is 9.59 kN/m³, the density of water is 978 kg/m³)

(a) Velocity at section 2. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. The diameter doubles: by what factor does the area grow?

Solution
  1. Steady, incompressible: \(V_1A_1=V_2A_2\), and \(A\propto D^2\).
  2. \(V_2=V_1(D_1/D_2)^2\) \(=8(0.05/0.1)^2=8(0.25)\).
\[\boxed{V_2=2\u{m/s}}\]

Marker note: write the continuity equation first; \(8\times\tfrac{50}{100}=4\) m/s (diameter, not area) scores at most the method mark.

(b) Volume flow rate. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. \(Q=VA\) at either section gives the same answer.

Solution
  1. \(A_1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.05)^2\) \(=1.963\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\).
  2. \(Q=V_1A_1=8(1.963\times10^{-3})\).
  3. Check at section 2: \(A_2=7.854\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\), \(2A_2\) gives the same.
\[\boxed{Q=0.0157\u{m^3/s}}\]

(= 15.7 L/s; keep \(0.015708\) for (c) and (d).)

(c) Weight flow rate. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. Weight per cubic metre × cubic metres per second. Which given property is a weight per volume?

Solution
  1. \(\gamma=9.59\u{kN/m^3}=9590\u{N/m^3}\).
  2. \(\dot W=\gamma Q=9590(0.015708)\).
\[\boxed{\dot W=150.6\u{N/s}}\]

(d) Mass flow rate. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. Use the density, not the specific weight.

Solution
  1. \(\dot m=\rho Q=978(0.015708)\).
  2. Check: \(150.6/15.36=9.81\u{m/s^2}=g\) ✓.
\[\boxed{\dot m=15.4\u{kg/s}}\]

Marker note: units carry the marks in (c) and (d): N/s for weight flow, kg/s for mass flow.

May 2023 Q3(b)2 markspart (a): F2.2; (c): F4.2

(Data line: \(g\) = 9.81 m s⁻², density of water = 1,000 kg m⁻³.) A water tank with an internal cross-sectional area of 0.1 m² and weight 4.5 N when empty is placed on a scale. Water flows in through an opening on the top (point 1) and out through the two openings on the sides (points 2 and 3). All the openings have equal area (\(A_1=A_2=A_3=0.01\u{m^2}\)). The water velocity on the top is \(U_1=3\u{m\,s^{-1}}\) vertically downward. Under steady-flow conditions, the water depth in the tank is \(h=0.6\u{m}\).

(b) Calculate the water discharge velocity through the opening on the sides \(U_2\) or \(U_3\). (2 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. "Steady" means the level does not change: in = out. The two side openings are identical and at the same height.

Solution
  1. Steady level: \(U_1A_1=U_2A_2+U_3A_3\).
  2. Same area, same depth: by symmetry \(U_2=U_3\), so \(3(0.01)=2U_2(0.01)\).
\[\boxed{U_2=U_3=1.5\u{m/s}}\]

Marker note: one mark for continuity with both outlets, one for the symmetry argument; \(U_2=3\) m/s ignores the second outlet.

May 2022 Q9(a)2 markspart (b): F2.2

(a) As fluids flow with a flow rate Q in a diverging channel with an initial cross-sectional area A1 in region 1 and a final cross-sectional area A2 in region 2, where A2 > A1, please write down the corresponding flow rates (Q1, Q2) and velocities (V1, V2) in regions 1 and 2 respectively. Compare Q1 vs Q2, and V1 vs V2. (2 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. What is conserved along the channel, and what does a bigger area do to the speed?

Solution
  1. Steady, incompressible: the same volume passes every section each second, \(Q_1=Q_2=Q\).
  2. \(V_1=Q/A_1\), \(V_2=Q/A_2\); since \(A_2\gt A_1\), \(V_2\lt V_1\).
\[\boxed{Q_1=Q_2=Q,\quad V_2=\frac{A_1}{A_2}V_1\lt V_1}\]
May 2021 Q1(a)4 marksparts (b)(c): F3.3

Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, density of air = 1.23 kg/m³, standard gravity = 9.8 m/s². A large open tank contains a layer of oil (relative density = 0.7) floating on water as shown in Figure C1. The flow is steady and energy loss is assumed to be negligible as water flows through the pipe, exits the nozzle and rises up in the air. It is also assumed that fluids in the tank are static. Determine

(a) the water velocity in the pipe. (4 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. The speed leaving the nozzle is on the figure; the pipe is twice the nozzle's diameter.

Solution
  1. Nozzle exit: \(V_n=5\u{m/s}\), \(A_n=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.1)^2\) \(=7.854\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\); so \(Q=0.0393\u{m^3/s}\).
  2. Pipe: \(A_p=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.2)^2=0.03142\u{m^2}\).
  3. Continuity: \(V_p=Q/A_p\) \(=5(0.1/0.2)^2\).
\[\boxed{V_\text{pipe}=1.25\u{m/s}}\]

Marker note: show \(Q\) at the nozzle and the area ratio; (b) and (c) build on this speed.

"5 m/s" beside the jet is read as the nozzle exit velocity; 2 m = nozzle exit above the pipe centreline, 1 m = pipe centreline above the floor (RESOLVED reading).

May 2019 Q3(a)4 marks(b): F3.3; (c): F3.6; (d): F4.1older format

(Data line: density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, density of air = 1.23 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².) Water flows from the pipe in Figure C3 as a free vertical jet and strikes a horizontal circular flat plate. The flow geometry is axisymmetrical, and friction may be ignored.

(a) Show that the velocity at section (1) is related to that at section (2) by \(V_1=1.6V_2\). (4 marks)

Hint

Type F2.1. Section (2) is the edge of a thin sheet 0.4 mm thick leaving a 0.1 m plate: the water leaves through the side of a short cylinder.

Solution
  1. Section (1), round jet: \(A_1=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.01)^2\) \(=7.854\times10^{-5}\u{m^2}\).
  2. Section (2), rim of the sheet: \(A_2=\pi Dt=\pi(0.1)(0.0004)\) \(=1.257\times10^{-4}\u{m^2}\).
  3. Continuity \(A_1V_1=A_2V_2\):
\[\begin{aligned}V_1&=\frac{A_2}{A_1}V_2\\&=\frac{\pi(0.1)(0.0004)}{\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.01)^2}V_2\end{aligned}\] \[\boxed{V_1=1.6\,V_2}\]

Marker note: the key mark is the rim area \(\pi Dt\); using the plate face \(\pi D^2/4\) gives 100, not 1.6.

3F2.2 · Control volume, governing laws, flux & streamline sketches

Dashed boundary cutting every jet straight across · streamlines fan out where the area grows · flux = amount crossing a section per unit time · laws: mass, momentum, energy. Learn it: note-F2 §3 control volume · §5 three basic laws · §6 flow rates and flux · §11 streamlines

Ready answer · definitions

Control volume: a fixed region of space chosen for analysis; fluid flows in and out through its boundary, the control surface.

Flux: the amount of a quantity (volume, mass, momentum, energy) carried across a section per unit time, e.g. mass flux \(\dot m=\rho VA\), momentum flux \(\dot mV\).

Basic laws: conservation of mass (continuity), Newton's second law (momentum), conservation of energy (first law).

May 2023 Q3(a)2 markspart (b): F2.1; (c): F4.2

A water tank with an internal cross-sectional area of 0.1 m² and weight 4.5 N when empty is placed on a scale. Water flows in through an opening on the top (point 1) and out through the two openings on the sides (points 2 and 3). All the openings have equal area (\(A_1=A_2=A_3=0.01\u{m^2}\)). The water velocity on the top is \(U_1=3\u{m\,s^{-1}}\) vertically downward. Under steady-flow conditions, the water depth in the tank is \(h=0.6\u{m}\).

(a) Sketch the control volume. (2 marks)

Hint

Type F2.2. Part (c) asks for the scale reading, so the boundary should also cut where the tank rests on the scale.

Solution
  1. Enclose the tank and all the water in it with a dashed boundary.
  2. Cut jet 1 just above the lid and jets 2, 3 just outside the side openings, each cut perpendicular to its flow; label \(U_1A_1\) in, \(U_2A_2\) and \(U_3A_3\) out.
  3. Cut through the tank's feet so the scale force \(R\) acts on the control volume (needed in (c)).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{dashed CV: tank + water}\\&\text{3 jets cut across}\\&R\text{ cut at the base}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: a boundary running along a jet (not across it) or missing an outlet loses the mark.

May 2022 Q9(b)2 markspart (a): F2.1

(Part (a): fluid flows with flow rate \(Q\) in a diverging channel, initial cross-sectional area \(A_1\) in region 1, final area \(A_2\) in region 2, \(A_2\gt A_1\).)

(b) Draw the streamlines in the diverging channel described in (a). (2 marks)

Hint

Type F2.2. Equal flow between neighbouring streamlines: where the fluid is slower, they must be further apart.

Solution
  1. Draw the channel walls widening from region 1 to region 2.
  2. Streamlines start parallel and evenly spaced in region 1; the outer ones follow the walls; all fan out smoothly, never crossing.
  3. Wider spacing in region 2 shows the lower speed (from (a), \(V_2\lt V_1\)); add flow arrows.
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{streamlines spread apart}\\&\text{as the channel widens}\end{aligned}}\]
May 2019 Q1(c)1 markother terms: F1.1, F4.4, F4.5older format

Briefly explain the following terms in the context of fluid flow. (9 marks for nine terms: streamline, pathline, flux, unsteady flow, non-uniform flow, ideal flow, head loss, drag, lift)

(c) Flux (1 mark)

Hint

Type F2.2. "Flux" always means "how much crosses per unit time". Give one example.

Solution
  1. Definition: the amount of a quantity (volume, mass, momentum or energy) carried by the flow across a surface per unit time.
  2. Example: mass flux \(\dot m=\rho VA\) (kg/s); momentum flux \(\dot mV\) (N).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{quantity crossing a}\\&\text{section per unit time}\end{aligned}}\]

marks per term are not printed; 1 mark each assumed (9 marks, nine terms). Some textbooks define flux per unit area (a flux density, e.g. kg/(m²·s)); the answer above follows the handout's usage (per unit time through a section).

ENGG1205 2013–14 Sem 1 Q1(f)2 marksolder course

(Data: density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².) What are the two fundamental physical laws that govern fluid flow?

Hint

Type F2.2. The F2 handout lists three basic laws; which two does every flow problem in this course need?

Solution
  1. Conservation of mass: the continuity equation, \(\sum(\rho VA)_\text{in}=\sum(\rho VA)_\text{out}\).
  2. The momentum principle (Newton's second law): net force = rate of change of momentum (momentum flux out − in).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{(i) conservation of mass}\\&\text{(ii) momentum principle}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: this matches the paper's printed answer, 1 mark each.

the 2026 handout (slides 9–11) lists three basic laws, adding conservation of energy (Bernoulli, F3). If a MECH1350 question asks for "the basic laws" without a number, name all three.

4Traps across F2

Traps
  • Diameter instead of area. Doubling \(D\) quarters the speed: Dec 2023 Q1(a) is 2 m/s, not 4 m/s.
  • Millimetres left in. 50 mm is 0.05 m; \(\tfrac{\pi}{4}(50)^2\) gives a "flow rate" a million times too large.
  • Mass flow vs weight flow. \(\dot m=\rho Q\) in kg/s; weight flow \(\gamma Q\) in N/s. Their ratio is \(g\).
  • Adding speeds at a junction. Flow rates add (\(U_1A_1=U_2A_2+U_3A_3\)); May 2023 Q3 gives 1.5 m/s per side, not 3.
  • Wrong outlet area for a radial sheet. The rim \(\pi Dt\), not the plate face \(\pi D^2/4\) (May 2019 Q3).
  • Numbers when "symbolically" is asked. Give the relation \(V_2=2V_1\) (Dec 2024 B4(b)).
  • Control-volume boundary along a jet. Cut every inlet and outlet straight across, and cut the support if a force follows.
  • Streamlines converging in a widening channel. They spread apart where the flow slows, and never cross.
  • Two laws or three? "Two fundamental laws": mass and momentum; the handout's list adds energy.
