Decision procedure · any body with a cable (H4.3)
  1. Which bodies? One body: one FBD. A floater carrying a sinker on a wire: one FBD each, plus (optionally) the whole system, where the wire tension is internal and cancels. Cue: "find the density of the cylinder" with no tension given → whole system first.
  2. Volumes under water. Fully submerged: the whole volume. Floating: cross-section × depth of submergence (\(d\) or \(h\)), not the full height. Thickness in mm → m.
  3. Fluid density. Use the fluid named in the question (sea 1027 kg/m³); a specific weight \(\gamma\) replaces \(\rho g\).
  4. Direction of \(T\). Away from the body along the cable: down to the bed (tethered), up to the crane (hung), down-and-out for inclined ropes (take \(T\cos\alpha\)).
  5. Balance. \(\sum F_\text{up}=\sum F_\text{down}\); moving bodies: \(\sum F=ma\), up positive.
  6. Unknowns. Two unknowns (area and weight) need two states: subtract to remove \(W\), then back-substitute. Check with the second state.
  7. Sense check. \(T\gt0\); a "sinker" on a crane needs \(T\) less than \(W\); a floater's density below 1000.
Answer skeleton · "Draw a free body diagram" (H4.3, 3–4 marks)

Diagram to draw: the body alone (no water, no quay), with each force as an arrow from its point of action, named and directed.

Marker points: (1) tension \(T\) up at the cable attachment; (2) weight \(W=\rho_sgV\) down through the centre of gravity; (3) buoyant force \(F_B=\rho_\text{sea}gV\) up through the centroid of the displaced volume (here the plate's centre); (4) a sentence: the horizontal pressure forces on the two faces are equal and opposite and cancel. Equation under the sketch: \(T+F_B=W\).

Model answer in the Dec 2021 Q3 card.

Answer skeleton · "State the Archimedes principle" / "explain" (H4.1, 2–4 marks)

Marker points: (1) a body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid feels an upward force (upthrust, buoyant force); (2) equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces, \(F_B=\rho_fgV_\text{sub}\); (3) acting vertically upward through the centroid of the displaced volume (centre of buoyancy). For "explain" parts: name what changes (weight or upthrust), compare them, and say what the body then does (accelerates, slows, stops, reverses).

2H4.1 · Archimedes & flotation concepts

\(F_B=\rho_fgV_\text{sub}\) through the centroid of the displaced volume; \(W_\text{app}=(\rho_b-\rho_f)gV\); sink if \(\rho_b\gt\rho_f\). Learn it: note-H4 §2 Archimedes' principle · §4 apparent weight · §5 float or sink · §7 concept questions

Ready answer · Archimedes' principle \[F_B=\rho_\text{fluid}\,g\,V_\text{displaced}\]

In words: a body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid is pushed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces, acting vertically upward through the centroid of the displaced volume (the centre of buoyancy).

Dec 2024 Q1(o)(p)1 + 1 markstrue/false

State true (T) or false (F) for each of the following statements.

(o) An object will sink in a liquid if its density is greater than that of the liquid. (1 mark)

Hint

Type H4.1. Compare \(W=\rho_bgV\) with the largest possible upthrust \(\rho_fgV\).

Solution
  1. Fully submerged, \(F_B=\rho_fgV\) and \(W=\rho_bgV\).
  2. \(\rho_b\gt\rho_f\) gives \(W\gt F_B\) even at full submersion: a net downward force, so it sinks.

\(\boxed{\text{True}}\)

the paper gives 16 marks for 16 statements; 1 mark each assumed.

(p) When completely submerged, an object weighs more in liquid A than in liquid B, which implies that liquid A is denser than liquid B. (1 mark)

Hint

Type H4.1. Write the apparent weight in each liquid; what makes it larger?

Solution
  1. \(W_\text{app}=W-\rho_\text{liq}gV\): the same \(W\) and \(V\) in both liquids.
  2. A larger apparent weight in A means a smaller upthrust in A, so \(\rho_A\lt\rho_B\): A is less dense.

\(\boxed{\text{False}}\)

Marker note: the review sheet line is "an object weighs less when immersed in a denser fluid"; the statement reverses it.

ENGG1205 2017–18 Sem 2 Q1(a)(b)(iii)4 + 4 marksparts (b)(i)(ii): H4.3Also (a): ENGG1205 2016–17 Sem 1 Q1(c) (3 marks), 2015–16 Sem 1 Q1(f), 2014–15 Sem 1 Q1(a), 2013–14 Sem 2 Q1(a), 2012–13 Q1(g) (2 marks each)older course

(Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s².)

(a) State the Archimedes principle. (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.1 (written). Three things: what the force is, how big, where it acts.

Solution
  1. A body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid experiences an upward force, the buoyant force (upthrust).
  2. Its size equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the body: \(F_B=\rho_fgV_\text{displaced}\).
  3. It acts vertically upward through the centroid of the displaced volume (the centre of buoyancy).
  4. (Reason, for full marks on the 4-mark version: it is the net result of the fluid pressure on the body's surface, which is larger on the lower parts because pressure grows with depth.)
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&F_B=\text{weight of fluid displaced,}\\&\text{up, through the centroid}\\&\text{of the displaced volume}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: the printed ENGG1205 solution gives exactly the size and the line of action; the 2-mark versions only need the size ("upthrust = weight of fluid displaced").

(b) A hot air balloon carrying a basket, of total mass 500 kg, is moored with two ropes, each of which makes an angle of 25° to the vertical, as shown in Figure C1.

(iii) When the hot air balloon travels vertically upwards, hot air is continuously released. This allows cooler air to enter through the bottom of the balloon. Describe and explain the effect of this on the motion of the balloon. You may assume that the volume of the balloon does not change. (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.1 (explain). At fixed volume, which of \(U\) and \(W\) changes when cool air replaces hot air?

Solution
  1. Upthrust \(U=\rho_\text{outside air}gV\): same volume, same outside air, so \(U\) stays (about) the same.
  2. Cooler air is denser than hot air, so the mass of air inside rises and the total weight \(W\) increases.
  3. Net upward force \(U-W\) falls; once \(W\gt U\) it points down: the balloon decelerates.
  4. It stops at a maximum height, then descends (accelerating downward while \(W\gt U\)).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{decelerates, stops at a}\\&\text{maximum height, then falls}\end{aligned}}\]

the printed solution words the cause as "buoyancy decreases"; strictly the upthrust is constant at fixed volume and the weight of the gas inside grows. Same conclusion; both wordings compare net force with weight.

3H4.2 · Free floating body: law of flotation

Floating freely: weight of body (+ cargo) = weight of fluid displaced; vertical sides make \(V_\text{sub}=A\times\) depth. Learn it: note-H4 §5 float or sink · §6 law of flotation

Ready answer · floating body with vertical sides \[\begin{aligned}L&=\frac{\gamma_b}{\gamma_w}H=\frac{\rho_b}{\rho_w}H\\\Delta W&=\gamma_wA_\text{wp}\,\Delta h\end{aligned}\]

In words: the submerged fraction of the height equals the density ratio; an extra load equals the weight of the extra slice of water pushed aside.

May 2025 Q4(a)4 marksAlso: Dec 2018 Q3(a) (4 marks, older format)part (b): previous syllabus

A cylinder, of diameter 0.3 m and height 1.0 m, with a specific weight of 7.8 kN/m³ is floating upright in water. [\(MB=I/V_d\), second moment of area of a circle, \(I=\pi D^4/64\).] (Acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s², density of water = 1000 kg/m³.)

(a) Determine the length of cylinder that is submerged in water, \(L\). (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.2. Weight of the whole cylinder = weight of water displaced by the length \(L\); the area cancels.

Solution
  1. Weight: \(W=\gamma_cAH\) with \(\gamma_c=7.8\u{kN/m^3}\), \(H=1.0\) m.
  2. Upthrust: \(F_B=\gamma_wAL\) with \(\gamma_w=1000\times9.81=9.81\u{kN/m^3}\).
  3. Floating: \(F_B=W\), so \(L=\dfrac{\gamma_c}{\gamma_w}H=\dfrac{7.8}{9.81}(1.0)\).
\[\boxed{L\approx0.795\u{m}}\]

Marker note: state the law of flotation in words before the equation; \(A=\pi(0.15)^2=0.0707\u{m^2}\) may be computed but cancels.

A rectangular flat-bottomed boat has a width of \(b=4\) m and a length of \(l=12\) m. The draught (distance between the waterline and the deepest point of the boat) before and after loading the cargo is \(h_1=0.5\) m and \(h_2=1\) m, respectively. The center of gravity after loading the cargo is \(g=0.85\) m from the bottom of the boat. (No figure. The specific weight of water is 9.81 kN/m³.)

(a) Find the weight of the loaded cargo G. (6 marks)

Hint

Type H4.2. Law of flotation before and after loading; the cargo is the difference.

Solution
  1. Before: boat weight \(=\gamma_wblh_1\) \(=9.81\times4\times12\times0.5\) \(=235.4\u{kN}\).
  2. After: boat + cargo \(=\gamma_wblh_2=470.9\u{kN}\).
  3. Cargo \(G=\gamma_wbl(h_2-h_1)\) \(=9.81\times48\times0.5\).
  4. The height \(g=0.85\) m is for part (b) only.
\[\boxed{G\approx235.4\u{kN}}\]

the paper prints \(\gamma_w=9.81\u{kN/m^3}\) only in part (b) and gives no \(g\) or \(\rho\) for Section B; the same value is used here.

ENGG1205 2016–17 Sem 1 Q1(d)8 markssymbolicolder course

As is shown in Figure C1, a spherical object of diameter \(d_o\) is made up of Material A and has a hollow core of diameter \(d_c\). If the relative density of Material A is \(r\gt1\), find the ratio of the diameters \(d_c/d_o\), in terms of \(r\), that will cause the object to be neutrally buoyant in water. Hint: the volume of a sphere \(=\tfrac\pi6\times\text{diameter}^3\). (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.2. Neutrally buoyant: weight of the shell = upthrust on the whole outer sphere.

Solution
  1. Shell weight: \(W=\tfrac\pi6(d_o^3-d_c^3)\,r\rho_wg\) (the core is empty).
  2. Upthrust: \(F_B=\tfrac\pi6d_o^3\rho_wg\) (the outer surface displaces the water).
  3. \(W=F_B\); divide by \(\tfrac\pi6d_o^3\rho_wg\): \(r(1-d_c^3/d_o^3)=1\).
  4. \(d_c^3/d_o^3=1-1/r\).
\[\boxed{\frac{d_c}{d_o}=\Big(1-\frac1r\Big)^{1/3}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution. Check: \(r=2\) gives 0.794; \(r\to1\) gives 0 (solid sphere).

ENGG1205 2013–14 Sem 2 Q1(b)8 markspart (d): H4.3older course

As shown in Figure C1, a layer of a liquid (immiscible with water) of relative density 0.75 floats on top of a layer of water in a beaker. A completely submerged cylinder of relative density 0.9 adjusts itself so that its axis is vertical and one part of its height is above the liquid-water interface and one part is below. Find the fraction of the cylinder height which is below the interface. (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.2. Call the fraction in water \(x\); the upthrust is the weight of water displaced plus the weight of liquid displaced.

Solution
  1. Cross-section \(A\), height \(H\); weight \(0.9\rho_wgAH\).
  2. Upthrust: water \(\rho_wgA(xH)\) + liquid \(0.75\rho_wgA(1-x)H\).
  3. Balance, cancel \(\rho_wgAH\): \(0.9=x+0.75(1-x)\).
  4. \(0.15=0.25x\).
\[\boxed{x=0.6\text{ of the height}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution (0.6).

4H4.3 · Tethered / suspended body force balance

FBD per body: \(W\) down, \(F_B=\rho_fgV_\text{sub}\) up, \(T\) away from the body; \(\sum F=0\) per body or for the system; two states → subtract. Learn it: note-H4 §8 tethered and hanging bodies · §4 apparent weight

Ready answer · the three balances \[\begin{aligned}\text{tied down: }&T=F_B-W\\\text{hung: }&T=W-F_B\\\text{two states: }&\rho gA(h_2-h_1)=T_2-T_1\end{aligned}\]

In words: a cable holding a floater down carries the excess upthrust; a cable holding a sinker up carries its apparent weight; the extra tension between two depths is the weight of the extra water displaced.

Dec 2024 Q3(i)(ii)5 + 5 markssame template: ENGG1205 2014–15 Sem 1 Q1(b)

As is shown in Figure B3, a cylinder floats upright in water with its axis being vertical. The cylinder is fastened by a tethering cable attached to the bottom of the cylinder. If the tension in the cable is 5 kN and 9 kN when the depth of submergence of the cylinder bottom, \(h\), is 1 m and 1.5 m, respectively. Determine (i) the cross-sectional area of the cylinder, and (ii) the weight of the cylinder. (Density of water = 1,000 kg/m³; acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s².)

(i) the cross-sectional area of the cylinder (5 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. FBD: \(F_B=W+T\) with \(F_B=\rho gAh\). Write it for both depths; \(W\) is the same in both.

Solution
  1. Cable to the bed pulls down: \(\rho gAh=W+T\).
  2. State 2 minus state 1: \(\rho gA(1.5-1)=9-5=4\u{kN}\).
  3. \(A=\dfrac{4000}{1000\times9.81\times0.5}\).
\[\boxed{A\approx0.815\u{m^2}}\]

the paper gives 10 marks for (i) and (ii) together; a 5 + 5 split is assumed.

(ii) the weight of the cylinder (5 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. Back-substitute \(A\) into either state.

Solution
  1. \(\rho gA=4\u{kN}/0.5\u{m}=8\u{kN}\) per metre of depth.
  2. State 1: \(W=\rho gAh_1-T_1=8(1)-5\).
  3. Check state 2: \(8(1.5)-3=9\u{kN}\) ✓.
\[\boxed{W=3.0\u{kN}}\]

Marker note: marks go for the FBD and the two equations; keep \(\rho gA=8000\) N/m unrounded so \(W\) comes out exactly 3 kN.

Dec 2023 Q4(i)(ii)6 + 3 marksAlso: ENGG1205 2016–17 Sem 2 Q1(c) (12 marks, older course)

As is shown in Figure B4, a spherical object is held in suspension in water by attachment through a wire to the bottom of a floating circular cylinder. The cylinder and the sphere have the same radius \(R\). If the height of the cylinder is \(h=7R\), the depth of submergence of the cylinder is \(d=5R\), and the relative density of the sphere is 1.5, find (i) the relative density of the cylinder; (ii) the tension in the wire if \(R=0.2\) m. Hint: the volume of a sphere \(=\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\times\text{radius}^3\). (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

(i) the relative density of the cylinder (6 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. One balance for cylinder + sphere together: the wire tension is internal and cancels.

Solution
  1. Let \(s\) = RD of the cylinder. Weights: cylinder \(s\rho_wg\pi R^2(7R)\); sphere \(1.5\rho_wg\tfrac43\pi R^3=2\rho_wg\pi R^3\).
  2. Upthrusts: cylinder \(\rho_wg\pi R^2(5R)\) (submerged 5R only); sphere \(\tfrac43\rho_wg\pi R^3\).
  3. System: total weight = total upthrust; divide by \(\rho_wg\pi R^3\): \(7s+2=5+\tfrac43\).
  4. \(7s=\tfrac{13}{3}\).
\[\boxed{s=\tfrac{13}{21}\approx0.619}\]

Marker note: matches the ENGG1205 printed answer 13/21. Using the full height 7R for the cylinder's upthrust is the usual slip.

(ii) the tension in the wire if \(R=0.2\) m (3 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. FBD of the sphere alone: it hangs, so \(T=W-F_B\).

Solution
  1. Sphere: \(T+F_{B,s}=W_s\), so \(T=(1.5-1)\rho_wg\tfrac43\pi R^3\).
  2. \(\tfrac43\pi(0.2)^3=0.03351\u{m^3}\).
  3. \(T=0.5\times1000\times9.81\times0.03351\).
  4. Check on the cylinder: \(F_{B,c}-W_c=\rho_wg\pi R^3(5-7s)\) also gives 164.4 N ✓.
\[\boxed{T\approx164.4\u{N}}\]
Dec 2022 Q3(a)(b)3 + 6 markssame template: Dec 2021 Q3, May 2019 Q3part (c): previous syllabus

A 5-mm thick steel plate, measuring 3 m by 2 m, is hanged from a port to the ocean with a cable. It is of uniform density at 7800 kg/m³ and is fully submerged in water. The density of ocean water is 1027 kg/m³. (Acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s², density of water = 1000 kg/m³.) The figure shows a quay-side davit with a pulley; the cable hangs vertically into the sea and holds the top of the plate, 3 m high and 2 m wide.

(a) Draw a free body diagram of the metal plate and indicate all the forces acting on it. (3 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3 (FBD). The plate alone; three vertical forces. Skeleton in At a glance.

Solution
  1. \(T\) up along the cable, at the top of the plate.
  2. \(W=\rho_sgV\) down through the plate's centre G.
  3. \(F_B=\rho_\text{sea}gV\) up through the centroid of the displaced volume, which is also the centre (fully submerged, uniform plate).
  4. Horizontal pressure forces on the two faces are equal and opposite: they cancel.

\(\boxed{T+F_B=W}\)

(b) What is the tension in the cable to hold the metal plate in place? (6 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. Volume first (5 mm = 0.005 m), then \(T=W-F_B\) with the sea-water density.

Solution
  1. \(V=3\times2\times0.005=0.03\u{m^3}\).
  2. \(W=7800\times9.81\times0.03\) \(=2295.5\u{N}\).
  3. \(F_B=1027\times9.81\times0.03\) \(=302.2\u{N}\).
  4. \(T=W-F_B\) \(=(7800-1027)(9.81)(0.03)\).
\[\boxed{T\approx1993\u{N}\approx1.99\u{kN}}\]

the question's ocean density 1027 kg/m³ overrides the head-of-section 1000 kg/m³ (existing reading); with 1000 the answer would be 2001 N.

Dec 2021 Q3(a)(b)4 + 6 marksAlso: May 2019 Q3(a)(b) (4 + 6 marks, older format)part (c): previous syllabus

A metal plate 3 mm thick with a uniform density of 7000 kg/m³ is hung from a port to the ocean. The density of ocean water is 1027 kg/m³ and gravitational acceleration is 9.81 m/s². The figure shows a quay-side davit with a pulley; the cable hangs vertically into the sea and holds the top of a fully submerged plate 4 m high and 2 m wide.

(a) Draw a free body diagram of the metal plate and indicate all the forces acting on it. (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3 (FBD). As Dec 2022 (a): the plate alone, three forces.

Solution
  1. \(T\) (cable) up at the top of the plate.
  2. \(W=\rho_sgV\), \(\rho_s=7000\), down through the centre G.
  3. \(F_B=\rho_\text{sea}gV\), \(\rho_\text{sea}=1027\), up through the centre of buoyancy (the plate's centre).
  4. Side pressures cancel; all three forces lie on one vertical line, so there is no moment.

\(\boxed{T+F_B=W}\)

Marker note: each force needs a name, a direction and a point of action; an FBD showing the water or the quay loses marks.

(b) What is the tension in the cable to hold the metal plate in place? (6 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. 3 mm = 0.003 m; \(T=(\rho_s-\rho_\text{sea})gV\).

Solution
  1. \(V=4\times2\times0.003=0.024\u{m^3}\).
  2. \(W=7000\times9.81\times0.024\) \(=1648.1\u{N}\).
  3. \(F_B=1027\times9.81\times0.024\) \(=241.8\u{N}\).
  4. \(T=W-F_B\).
\[\boxed{T\approx1406\u{N}\approx1.41\u{kN}}\]

sea-water density 1027 kg/m³ used (stated in the question; existing reading).

A hot air balloon carrying a basket, of total mass 500 kg, is moored with two ropes, each of which makes an angle of 25° to the vertical, as shown in Figure C1. (Take \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

(i) When the ropes are released, the balloon initially accelerates vertically upwards at 1.5 m/s². Find the magnitude of the buoyancy force. (5 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. After release only \(U\) and \(W\) act: Newton's second law, up positive.

Solution
  1. \(U-W=ma\).
  2. \(U=500\times1.5+500\times9.81\) \(=750+4905\).
\[\boxed{U=5655\u{N}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution; the upthrust is the same just before and just after release.

(ii) Calculate the tension in each rope before they are released. (7 marks)

Hint

Type H4.3. At rest: \(U=W+\) the vertical parts of the two rope tensions.

Solution
  1. Each rope pulls down-and-out at 25°: vertical part \(T\cos25^\circ\); horizontal parts cancel.
  2. \(U=W+2T\cos25^\circ\).
  3. \(T=\dfrac{5655-4905}{2\cos25^\circ}=\dfrac{750}{1.8126}\).
\[\boxed{T\approx413.8\u{N}\text{ per rope}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed 413.8 N.

ENGG1205 2014–15 Sem 1 Q1(b)18 markssame template: Dec 2024 Q3older course

As is shown in Figure C1, a cylinder floats in water with its axis being vertical. The cylinder is fastened by a tethering cable attached to the bottom of the cylinder. If the tension in the cable is 5 kN and 15 kN when the depth of submergence of the cylinder bottom, \(h\), is 1 m and 2 m, respectively. Determine (i) the radius of the cylinder, and (ii) the mass of the cylinder. (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.3. As Dec 2024 Q3: subtract the two states for \(A\), then \(r=\sqrt{A/\pi}\); \(M=W/g\).

Solution
  1. FBD: \(\rho_wghA=W+T\).
  2. Subtract: \(\rho_wg(2-1)A=15-5=10\u{kN}\), so \(A=\dfrac{10\,000}{9810}=1.019\u{m^2}\).
  3. \(r=\sqrt{A/\pi}=0.570\u{m}\).
  4. \(\rho_wgA=10\) kN/m, so \(W=10(1)-5=5\u{kN}\).
  5. \(M=5000/9.81\).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}r&\approx0.570\u{m}\\M&\approx510\u{kg}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: printed solution: \(r=0.57\) m, \(M=510\) kg (exact 509.7 kg).

ENGG1205 2013–14 Sem 2 Q1(d)6 markspart (b): H4.2older course

As shown in Figure C1, a layer of a liquid (immiscible with water) of relative density 0.75 floats on top of a layer of water in a beaker. A completely submerged cylinder of relative density 0.9 adjusts itself so that its axis is vertical and one part of its height is above the liquid-water interface and one part is below. (d) If the radius of the cylinder is 2 cm, how much force is needed to push the cylinder down by 1 cm from its equilibrium level (part of the cylinder remains above the interface)? (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.3. Pushing down moves a slice \(A\,d\) of the cylinder from the liquid into the water. The push equals the change in upthrust.

Solution
  1. Water upthrust rises by \(\rho_wgAd\); liquid upthrust falls by \(0.75\rho_wgAd\).
  2. Extra force \(F=0.25\rho_wgAd\), with \(A=\pi(0.02)^2=1.257\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\), \(d=0.01\) m.
  3. \(F=0.25\times1000\times9.81\) \(\times1.257\times10^{-5}\).
\[\boxed{F\approx0.0308\u{N}}\]

Marker note: the printed solution rounds to 0.03 N.

5H4.6 · Apparent weight: density from weight ratios

Each state: \(W_\text{app}=\rho_bgV-\rho_fgV_\text{sub}\); write the stated ratio, cancel \(gV\), solve. Learn it: note-H4 §4 apparent weight and submerged density

Ready answer · fraction of true weight \[\frac{W_\text{app}}{W}=1-\frac{\rho_f}{\rho_b}\cdot\frac{V_\text{sub}}{V}\]

In words: the weight lost is the density ratio times the submerged fraction; "apparent weight is \(k\) of the true weight" fully submerged gives \(\rho_b/\rho_f=1/(1-k)\).

ENGG1205 2015–16 Sem 2 Q2(a)6 marksolder course

The apparent weight of an object when completely submerged in water is half that when half submerged in water. What is the density of this object? (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.6. Write the two apparent weights; "half that when half submerged" is the ratio between them, not with the true weight.

Solution
  1. Fully: \((\rho_s-\rho_w)gV\). Half: \(\rho_sgV-\tfrac12\rho_wgV\).
  2. \((\rho_s-\rho_w)=\tfrac12(\rho_s-\tfrac12\rho_w)\).
  3. \(\tfrac12\rho_s=\tfrac34\rho_w\).
\[\boxed{\rho_s=1500\u{kg/m^3}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution.

ENGG1205 2015–16 Sem 1 Q1(g)6 marksolder course

An object, when completely submerged in water, has an apparent weight equal to two-thirds of its true weight. When completely submerged in another fluid, the apparent weight becomes half the true weight. What is the relative density of this fluid? (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.6. Two equations, each linking \(\rho_s\) to one fluid density; then divide.

Solution
  1. Water: \((\rho_s-\rho_w)gV=\tfrac23\rho_sgV\), so \(\rho_w=\rho_s/3\).
  2. Fluid: \((\rho_s-\rho_f)gV=\tfrac12\rho_sgV\), so \(\rho_f=\rho_s/2\).
  3. \(\rho_f/\rho_w=(1/2)/(1/3)\).
\[\boxed{\text{RD of the fluid}=1.5}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution (the object itself has RD 3).

ENGG1205 2013–14 Sem 1 Q1(g)4 marksolder course

When half submerged in water, a spherical object of homogeneous material will have an apparent weight equal to two-thirds of its weight in air. What is the relative density of the material of the object? (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.6. Half submerged: the upthrust uses \(V/2\); the weight uses all of \(V\).

Solution
  1. \(W-F_B=\tfrac23W\), with \(W=\rho_sgV\), \(F_B=\rho_wg\tfrac V2\).
  2. \(\rho_s-\tfrac12\rho_w=\tfrac23\rho_s\), so \(\tfrac13\rho_s=\tfrac12\rho_w\).
\[\boxed{\text{RD}=1.5}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution. (It cannot float half submerged on its own: it is held, e.g. on a balance.)

6H4.7 · Floating body with moments (anchored timber)

\(W\) at mid-length, \(F_B\) at the middle of the submerged part, \(T\) at the anchor: moments about the anchor give \(\rho_s\); \(\sum F_y=0\) gives \(T\). Learn it: note-H4 §9 floating body with moments

Ready answer · anchored floating stick \[\frac{\rho_s}{\rho_w}=\Big(\frac{\ell_\text{wet}}{\ell}\Big)^2,\qquad T=F_B-W\]

In words: weight × half the length = upthrust × half the wetted length; for 8 m wet of 10 m, \(\rho_s=0.64\rho_w\).

The homogeneous timber \(AB\), which is 0.1 m by 0.75 m in cross section, floats in water as shown in Figure C2. Determine the density of the timber and the tension in the rope. (Figure: the lower end A is tied by a short vertical rope to a rock on the bed; the timber rises to the right, 8 m of its length under water and the last 2 m to B in air. Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.7. Take moments about A so the rope force drops out; where does the upthrust act?

Solution
  1. \(W=\rho_sg(10\times0.1\times0.75)\) at 5 m from A (mid-length).
  2. \(F_B=\rho_wg(8\times0.1\times0.75)\) at 4 m from A (middle of the submerged 8 m).
  3. Moments about A, angle \(\theta\) to the horizontal: \(W(5\cos\theta)=F_B(4\cos\theta)\); \(\cos\theta\), \(g\) and the section cancel: \(50\rho_s=32\rho_w\).
  4. \(\rho_s=640\u{kg/m^3}\); \(W=4708.8\u{N}\), \(F_B=5886.0\u{N}\).
  5. Vertical: \(T+W=F_B\) (rope pulls A down to the rock).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\rho_s&=640\u{kg/m^3}\\T&\approx1177\u{N}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed 640 kg/m³ and 1177.2 N. The thin timber's slanted cut at the waterline is ignored, as in the printed solution.

ENGG1205 2014–15 Sem 2 Q1(b)14 markssame figure: ENGG1205 2017–18 Sem 1 Q2older course

The homogeneous timber \(AB\) of Figure C1 is 0.15 m by 0.35 m in cross section. Determine the density of the timber, and the tension in the rope. (Figure as above: anchored at A by a short vertical rope, 8 m under water, 2 m in air. Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Hint

Type H4.7. Same moments as 2017–18; only the tension changes with the cross-section.

Solution
  1. Moments about A: \(\rho_s(10)(5)=\rho_w(8)(4)\), so \(\rho_s=640\u{kg/m^3}\).
  2. Section area \(0.15\times0.35=0.0525\u{m^2}\).
  3. \(T=F_B-W\) \(=(1000\times8-640\times10)\) \(\times9.81\times0.0525\).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\rho_s&=640\u{kg/m^3}\\T&\approx824\u{N}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: matches the printed solution (824 N).

7Traps across H4

Traps
  • Full height instead of submerged depth. A floating cylinder displaces only \(A\times d\): Dec 2023 with \(7R\) instead of \(5R\) gives a nonsense RD.
  • 1000 instead of 1027. Sea water in the question beats the section header: Dec 2022 gives 2001 N instead of 1993 N.
  • Millimetres left in millimetres. A 5-mm plate is 0.005 m thick: \(V=0.03\u{m^3}\).
  • Tension in the wrong direction. Cable to the bed pulls down (\(T=F_B-W\)); cable to a crane pulls up (\(T=W-F_B\)).
  • Denser liquid, heavier reading. Wrong way round: an object weighs less in the denser liquid (Dec 2024 Q1(p)).
  • Upthrust at mid-length. It acts at the centroid of the displaced volume: 4 m from A for the timber, not 5 m (gives 800 instead of 640 kg/m³).
  • Half submerged halves the weight. Only the upthrust uses \(V/2\) (H4.6).
  • Inclined ropes. Only \(T\cos25^\circ\) holds the balloon down, and there are two ropes.
  • Wire tension in the system balance. For cylinder + sphere together it is internal: leave it out.
