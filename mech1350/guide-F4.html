F4.1Jet impact on plates & vanesappeared 5 times · last May 2025
Looks like
An air or water jet (\(D\), \(V\)) hits a flat plate (normal, inclined, with a hole) or a cup; "find the force to hold the plate", "the fraction of flow each way", "the weight of the hemisphere".
Do
CV round the plate; \(\dot m=\rho AV\); "out − in" in each direction; on a smooth plate the speed is unchanged; a vertical jet slows: \(V^2=V_0^2-2gh\).
Watch
Air density (1.23), not water; flow leaving along a normal plate has no \(x\)-momentum; use the speed where the jet arrives.
F4.2Momentum on pipe bends & tanksappeared 3 times · last Dec 2024
Looks like
A reducing elbow with \(P_1\), \(A_1\), \(U_2\), \(A_2\): "force to hold it in place"; a tank on a scale with a jet in and side outlets: "the reading on the scale"; a convergent duct: "force of the air on it".
Do
Continuity for the missing speed, then \(p_1A_1+R_x=\dot m(V_{2x}-V_{1x})\) and the same in \(y\); scale: \(N=W_\text{tank}+W_\text{water}+\dot m_1V_1\).
Watch
The \(pA\) term is often ten times the momentum terms; outlet to atmosphere has \(p=0\); state the direction of the answer.

F4.6Open channel: specific energy & critical depthappeared 2 times · last Dec 2021
Looks like
Rectangular channel, width \(b\), flow \(Q\), depth \(y\): "velocity; subcritical or supercritical?"; "alternate depth".
Do

Decision procedure · any force question (F4.1 + F4.2)
  1. Draw the CV and axes. Cut every jet or pipe square to the flow; put the plate, vane, bend or tank inside. Cue "hold stationary / in place / reading on the scale" = the support force crosses the CV boundary.
  2. Fill in the flows. Missing speeds from continuity (\(A_1V_1=A_2V_2\), splits add up); a vertical jet slows, \(V^2=V_0^2-2gh\); a smooth plate keeps the speed. Then \(\dot m=\rho AV\) for each opening.
  3. List the forces on the fluid. Support \(R_x,R_y\) (unknown, guess it positive); \(pA\) at each pipe section, pointing in; nothing at free jets; weights only if the CV contains water in a vertical direction (tank, vertical bend).
  4. Write "forces = out − in" for each direction, with signed velocity components.
  5. Solve and interpret. A negative \(R\) means it acts the other way. "Force to hold" is \(\vec R\) itself; "force of the fluid on the plate/duct" is \(-\vec R\). Give size and direction (angle).
Answer skeleton · stall and flaps (F4.4, Dec 2018 Q3(b)(c), 4 + 2 marks)

Diagram to draw: two aerofoils, side by side: small angle of attack with streamlines following both surfaces and a thin wake; large angle with the flow leaving the upper surface near the leading edge and a wide, eddying wake.

Marker points (about one mark each): (1) angle of attack increased beyond the stall (critical) angle; (2) the boundary layer on the upper surface cannot follow the curve against the rising pressure and separates; (3) a wide turbulent wake forms over the upper surface; (4) consequence: lift falls sharply, drag rises sharply, the aircraft loses height or control. Flaps: (5) lowering them increases camber (and area); (6) more lift at low speed (higher \(C_{L,\max}\)), so slower take-off and landing; they also add drag.

Model answer (exam length) is in the Dec 2018 Q3(b)(c) card.

2F4.1 · Jet impact on plates & vanes

CV round the plate; \(\dot m=\rho AV\); forces = momentum out − momentum in, direction by direction; smooth plate: same speed out, no force along it. Learn it: note-F4 §2 CV momentum equation · §3 flat plates · §4 inclined plates, vanes · §5 vertical jets

Ready answer · jets on fixed smooth plates \[\begin{aligned}\text{normal plate: }&F=\dot mV=\rho AV^2\\\text{plate with hole: }&F=\rho V^2(A_1-A_2)\\\text{inclined at }\theta\text{: }&F_n=\dot mV\sin\theta\\\text{180° cup: }&F=2\dot mV\end{aligned}\]

In words: the force is the momentum per second the plate destroys in its own direction. On an inclined plate the flow splits \(\tfrac{1+\cos\theta}{2}\) forwards and \(\tfrac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\) backwards; for a vertical jet use the speed where it arrives, \(V^2=V_0^2-2gh\).

May 2025 Q6(a)(b)4 + 5 marksBeyond the handoutssame template: Dec 2018 Q2

If the air velocity magnitude remains constant as the air flows over the plate surface

Figure redrawn from the paper: a horizontal air jet, \(D_j=30\u{mm}\), \(V_j=40\u{m/s}\), strikes a flat plate inclined at 30° to the horizontal; the flow leaves along the plate as \(V_2\) (up the slope) and \(V_3\) (down the slope); \(F_A\) acts on the plate at 90° to its surface.

(a) determine \(F_A\) the anchoring force required to hold the plate stationary; (4 marks)

Hint

Type F4.1. Take axes along and normal to the plate. Which outflows have a normal component?

Solution
  1. Jet: \(A=\tfrac\pi4(0.03)^2=7.069\times10^{-4}\u{m^2}\); \(\dot m=\rho AV\) \(=1.23(7.069\times10^{-4})(40)\) \(=0.03478\u{kg/s}\); \(\dot mV=1.391\u{N}\).
  2. Axes: \(s\) up the plate, \(n\) normal to the plate, pointing into it from the jet side. The jet arrives with \(V_n=V\sin30°=20\u{m/s}\) and \(V_s=V\cos30°=34.64\u{m/s}\).
  3. Both outflows run along the plate: no \(n\)-component. All jets are free (gauge \(p=0\)).
  4. \(n\)-momentum on the air: \(R_n=0-\dot mV\sin30°\) \(=-0.696\u{N}\): the plate pushes the air back, away from its surface.
  5. So the air pushes the plate with 0.696 N into its surface, and the anchor must push back with the same force, normal to the plate, towards the jet side (as drawn). Components: 0.348 N against the jet, 0.602 N upward.
\[\boxed{F_A=\dot mV\sin30°=0.696\u{N}}\]

Marker note: "smooth plate" means no force along it, so \(F_A\) is purely normal; quoting \(\dot mV=1.39\u{N}\) misses the \(\sin30°\).

the air density (1.23 kg/m³) is printed only in part (b); it is used for (a) as well.

(b) the fraction of mass flow along the plate surface in each of the two directions shown. (the density of the air is 1.23 kg/m³) (5 marks)

Hint

Type F4.1. Two unknowns, two equations: continuity, and momentum along the plate (where the smooth plate exerts no force).

Solution
  1. Speeds: the air stays at atmospheric pressure and the height change is negligible, so \(V_2=V_3=V=40\u{m/s}\) (as the question states).
  2. Continuity: \(\dot m_2+\dot m_3=\dot m\).
  3. \(s\)-momentum, no force along a smooth plate: \(0=\dot m_2V-\dot m_3V-\dot mV\cos30°\), so \(\dot m_2-\dot m_3=\dot m\cos30°\).
  4. Add and subtract the two equations: \(\dfrac{\dot m_2}{\dot m}=\dfrac{1+\cos30°}{2}\), \(\dfrac{\dot m_3}{\dot m}=\dfrac{1-\cos30°}{2}\).
  5. Numbers: \(\dot m_2=0.03245\u{kg/s}\) up the slope, \(\dot m_3=0.00233\u{kg/s}\) down it.
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\frac{\dot m_2}{\dot m}&=0.933\ (V_2)\\\frac{\dot m_3}{\dot m}&=0.067\ (V_3)\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: the density cancels in the fractions; it only fixes the mass flows (0.0325 and 0.0023 kg/s), which are worth stating.

May 2021 Q38 marksBeyond the handoutssame idea: May 2019 Q3(d)

A vertical jet of water having a nozzle exit velocity of 10 m/s with a diameter of 4 cm suspends a hollow hemisphere as indicated in Figure C3. If the hemisphere is stationary at an elevation of 5 m, determine its weight. (Assuming hemisphere is much smaller than its elevation) (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, standard gravity = 9.8 m/s².)

Hint

Type F4.1. First find how fast the jet is still moving 5 m up; then the hemisphere turns it through 180°.

Solution
  1. Mass flow (the same at every height): \(\dot m=1000\cdot\tfrac\pi4(0.04)^2(10)\) \(=12.57\u{kg/s}\).
  2. Speed at 5 m (free jet, Bernoulli, or \(v^2=u^2-2as\)): \(V^2=10^2-2(9.8)(5)=2\), \(V=1.414\u{m/s}\).
  3. CV round the hemisphere, \(y\) up. In: \(\dot m(+V)\) going up. Out: \(\dot m(-V)\) going down round the rim (smooth surface, same height, same speed).
  4. \(\sum F_y=R_y\) \(=\dot m(-V)-\dot m(+V)\) \(=-2\dot mV\): the hemisphere pushes the water down with \(2\dot mV\), so the water pushes it up with \(2(12.57)(1.414)\) \(=35.5\u{N}\).
  5. Stationary: this upward force balances the weight.
\[\boxed{W=2\dot mV=35.5\u{N}}\]

Marker note: the jet has slowed from 10 to 1.41 m/s by the time it arrives; using the nozzle speed gives 251 N, seven times too big.

\(g=9.8\u{m/s^2}\) as printed in this paper's data line (with 9.81: \(V=1.378\u{m/s}\), \(W=34.6\u{N}\)); the 5 m is taken as the height at which the jet is turned, since the hemisphere is "much smaller than its elevation".

May 2019 Q3(d)3 marksBeyond the handoutsparts (a)–(c): F2.1, F3.3, F3.6older format

Water flows from the pipe in Figure C3 as a free vertical jet and strikes a horizontal circular flat plate. The flow geometry is axisymmetrical, and friction may be ignored. (a) Show that the velocity at section (1) is related to that at section (2) by \(V_1=1.6V_2\). (b) Determine the discharge \(Q\). (c) Calculate the manometer reading \(H\). (Take density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s².)

Figure redrawn from the paper: pipe exit (1), 0.01 m diameter; plate 0.1 m diameter, 0.2 m above (1); the water leaves the plate edge (2) as a 0.4 mm sheet at speed \(V\); a tube through the plate centre shows a water column \(H\).

(d) Find the impact force on the plate. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F4.1 (after F2/F3 parts). With what speed does the jet reach the plate? Is that \(V_1\)?

Solution
  1. From (a)–(b): \(A_1=\tfrac\pi4(0.01)^2=7.854\times10^{-5}\), \(A_2=\pi(0.1)(0.0004)\) \(=1.257\times10^{-4}\u{m^2}\), so \(V_1=1.6V_2\).
  2. Bernoulli (1)→(2), both at atmospheric pressure, (2) 0.2 m higher: \(\dfrac{V_1^2}{2g}=\dfrac{V_2^2}{2g}+0.2\), so \(V_2=1.586\), \(V_1=2.538\u{m/s}\); \(Q=A_1V_1=1.99\times10^{-4}\u{m^3/s}\).
  3. The jet reaches the plate at the height of (2) and is still free, so it arrives with speed \(V_2=1.586\u{m/s}\).
  4. CV round the plate, \(y\) up: in \(\dot m(+V_2)\); out radially, no \(y\)-momentum. \(R_y=0-\rho QV_2\).
  5. Force of the water on the plate (upward): \(\rho QV_2=1000(1.993\times10^{-4})(1.586)\) \(=0.316\u{N}\).
\[\boxed{F=\rho QV_2=0.316\u{N}\text{ (up)}}\]

Marker note: using the pipe-exit speed \(V_1\) gives 0.506 N; a CV from the pipe exit gives the right 0.316 N only if the 0.190 N weight of the rising jet is included.

Dec 2018 Q28 marksBeyond the handoutssame template: May 2025 Q6older format

A circular plate is held perpendicular to an axisymmetric horizontal jet of air having a velocity of 40 m/s and a diameter of 80 mm as shown in Figure C2. A hole at the centre of the plate results in a discharge jet of air having a velocity of 40 m/s and a diameter of 20 mm. Determine the horizontal component of force required to hold the plate stationary. Take density of air = 1.23 kg/m³.

Hint

Type F4.1. Only two streams carry \(x\)-momentum: the jet coming in and the small jet through the hole.

Solution
  1. Areas: \(A_1=\tfrac\pi4(0.08)^2=5.027\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\), \(A_2=\tfrac\pi4(0.02)^2=3.142\times10^{-4}\u{m^2}\).
  2. Mass flows: in \(\dot m_1=1.23(5.027\times10^{-3})(40)\) \(=0.2473\u{kg/s}\); through the hole \(\dot m_2=0.01546\u{kg/s}\); the rest, 0.2318 kg/s, leaves along the plate face.
  3. CV round the plate, \(x\) along the jet; all jets free (\(p=0\)); the radial sheets have no \(x\)-velocity.
  4. \(R_x=\dot m_2V-\dot m_1V\) \(=0.618-9.892=-9.274\u{N}\).
  5. Negative: the support pushes towards the incoming jet (upstream).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}F&=\rho V^2(A_1-A_2)\\&=9.27\u{N}\text{, against the jet}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: the data line gives water (1000 kg/m³) but the jet is air: water density gives 7540 N.

3F4.2 · Momentum on pipe bends & tanks

Same CV recipe as F4.1, plus \(pA\) at every pipe section (pointing in) and weights in the vertical direction; continuity first for the missing speed. Learn it: note-F4 §2 CV momentum equation · §6 bends, nozzles · §7 tanks on scales

Ready answer · bend, nozzle, tank \[\begin{aligned}x:\ &p_1A_1+R_x=\dot m(V_{2x}-V_{1x})\\y:\ &R_y=\dot m(V_{2y}-V_{1y})\\\text{scale: }&N=W_t+W_w+\dot m_1V_1\end{aligned}\]

In words: support force + pressure forces (+ weights) on the fluid = momentum out − momentum in, for an inlet along \(x\) at gauge pressure \(p_1\) and an outlet to the atmosphere. "Force to hold the bend" = \(\vec R\); "force of the fluid on the duct" = \(-\vec R\). A tank scale reads the weights plus the momentum flux of the jet it stops.

Dec 2024 Section B Q4(e)5 marksBeyond the handoutsparts (a)–(d): F3.6, F2.1, F3.4same idea: May 2024 Q3

As is shown in Figure B4, an air duct flow-metering device consists of a stagnation probe at station 2 and a static pressure tap at station 1. The cross-sectional area of the duct at station 2 is one-half that at station 1, where the diameter of the duct is 10 cm. Air of density 1.2 kg/m³ flows through the duct to discharge into atmospheric air at the open end (station 2). A water manometer is connected between the stagnation probe and the pressure tap, and a reading of 1 cm is measured. You may assume that blockage of flow due to the probe at station 2 is negligible. (Section B data: density of water = 1,000 kg/m³, \(g\) = 9.81 m/s², pressure means gauge pressure.)

Earlier parts: (a) describe the flow near the stagnation probe; (b) relate the velocities at the two stations by continuity; (c) relate the pressure at station 1 to the velocity at station 2 by Bernoulli; (d) evaluate numerically the pressure at station 1 and the velocities at stations 1 and 2.

Figure redrawn from the paper: the static-tap limb (station 1) stands 1 cm higher than the probe limb.

(e) Calculate the force acting by the air flow on the convergent part of the duct. (5 marks)

Hint

Type F4.2. You need \(p_1\), \(V_1\), \(V_2\) from (d); then a CV of the air from station 1 to the open end, where \(p_2=0\).

Solution
  1. From (b)–(d): \(A_2=A_1/2\) gives \(V_2=2V_1\). The exit is open, \(p_2=0\), so the probe reads \(p_0=\tfrac12\rho V_2^2\); Bernoulli 1→2: \(p_1+\tfrac12\rho V_1^2=\tfrac12\rho V_2^2\), so \(p_0-p_1=\tfrac12\rho V_1^2\).
  2. Manometer: \(p_0-p_1=\rho_wg(0.01)=98.1\u{Pa}\), so \(V_1=\sqrt{2(98.1)/1.2}=12.79\u{m/s}\), \(V_2=25.57\u{m/s}\), \(p_1=\tfrac12\rho(V_2^2-V_1^2)=294.3\u{Pa}\).
  3. CV: the air from station 1 to the exit. \(A_1=\tfrac\pi4(0.1)^2=7.854\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\); \(\dot m=\rho A_1V_1=0.1205\u{kg/s}\).
  4. \(x\) along the flow: \(p_1A_1+R_x=\dot m(V_2-V_1)\), i.e. \(2.311+R_x=1.541\), so \(R_x=-0.770\u{N}\): the duct wall pushes the air upstream.
  5. Newton's third law: the air pushes the convergent part with 0.770 N downstream (along the flow, axisymmetric so no side force).
\[\boxed{F=0.770\u{N}\text{ downstream}}\]

Marker note: a neat check: here \(F=(p_0-p_1)A_1\) \(=98.1\times7.854\times10^{-3}\); forgetting \(p_1A_1\) gives the wrong sign (1.54 N upstream).

"force on the convergent part" is read as the net axial force of the air inside the duct between station 1 and the exit, in gauge pressure (the atmosphere outside cancels); the probe's own drag is ignored as the question allows. Manometer limb orientation as in RESOLVED (static limb higher); the numbers do not depend on it.

May 2024 Q310 marksBeyond the handoutssame idea: Dec 2024 B Q4(e)

Water flows steadily through a 90° reducing elbow (Figure C3). At the inlet to the elbow, the gauge pressure is \(P_1=220\u{kPa}\) and the cross-section area \(A_1=0.01\u{m^2}\). At the outlet, the elbow discharges to the atmosphere at which the flow speed is \(U_2=16\u{m\,s^{-1}}\) and the cross-section area \(A_2=0.0025\u{m^2}\). Calculate the force required to hold the 90° reducing elbow in place. Neglect the change in elevation. (Density of water 1,000 kg m⁻³; \(g\) = 9.81 m s⁻².)

Figure redrawn from the paper. Its label box reads "\(P_1\) = 120 kPa (gauge)"; the text says 220 kPa.

Hint

Type F4.2. Continuity gives \(U_1\). The outlet is a free jet (no pressure force); the inlet pressure pushes into the CV along \(+x\).

Solution
  1. Continuity: \(U_1=A_2U_2/A_1=4\u{m/s}\); \(\dot m=\rho A_1U_1=40\u{kg/s}\).
  2. Momentum fluxes: in \(\dot mU_1=160\u{N}\) along \(+x\); out \(\dot mU_2=640\u{N}\) along \(-y\). Pressure force \(P_1A_1=2200\u{N}\) along \(+x\); none at the free outlet.
  3. \(x\): \(P_1A_1+R_x=\dot m(0-U_1)\), so \(R_x=-160-2200\) \(=-2360\u{N}\).
  4. \(y\): \(R_y=\dot m(-U_2)-0=-640\u{N}\) (horizontal elbow: weight is not in this plane).
  5. Size \(\sqrt{2360^2+640^2}\); direction \(\tan^{-1}(640/2360)=15.2°\) below the \(-x\) axis.
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}R&=(-2360,\,-640)\u{N}\\|R|&=2445\u{N}\text{, 15.2° below }-x\end{aligned}}\]

The support pushes the elbow back against the inflow and down. With the figure's 120 kPa: \(R_x=-1360\u{N}\), \(R_y=-640\u{N}\), \(|R|=1503\u{N}\) at 25.2° below \(-x\).

Marker note: the pressure term (2200 N) is fourteen times the inflow momentum term; leaving it out is the usual way to lose most of the 10 marks.

text (220 kPa) and figure (120 kPa) disagree; frictionless Bernoulli from the other data gives 120 kPa (220 kPa would imply a 10.2 m head loss in the elbow). Answer follows the text, as RESOLVED; the 120 kPa result is given too.

May 2023 Q3(c)6 marksBeyond the handoutsparts (a)(b): F2.2, F2.1

A water tank with an internal cross-sectional area of 0.1 m² and weight 4.5 N when empty is placed on a scale. Water flows in through an opening on the top (point 1) and out through the two openings on the sides (points 2 and 3). All the openings have equal area (\(A_1=A_2=A_3=0.01\u{m^2}\)). The water velocity on the top is \(U_1=3\u{m\,s^{-1}}\) vertically downward. Under steady-flow conditions, the water depth in the tank is \(h=0.6\u{m}\). (a) Sketch the control volume. (b) Calculate the water discharge velocity through the opening on the sides \(U_2\) or \(U_3\). (Density of water 1,000 kg m⁻³; \(g\) = 9.81 m s⁻².)

(c) Determine the reading on the scale. (6 marks)

Hint

Type F4.2. CV round the tank and all the water in it; \(y\)-momentum. Which flows carry vertical momentum?

Solution
  1. (b) first: \(Q_1=A_1U_1=0.03\u{m^3/s}\), split equally, so \(U_2=U_3=0.015/0.01=1.5\u{m/s}\).
  2. CV: tank plus the water inside, cutting the three jets at the openings; \(y\) up. Forces in \(y\): scale \(N\) (up), tank 4.5 N and water \(\rho g(0.1)(0.6)=588.6\u{N}\) (down). Free jets: no pressure forces.
  3. Momentum in \(y\): in \(\dot m_1(-U_1)\); the side jets are horizontal (no \(y\)-part). \(\dot m_1U_1=1000(0.03)(3)=90\u{N}\).
  4. \(N-4.5-588.6\) \(=0-\dot m_1(-U_1)=90\).
  5. In \(x\): the side jets carry 22.5 N each in opposite directions and cancel; no friction force is needed.
\[\boxed{N=4.5+588.6+90=683.1\u{N}}\]

Marker note: the scale reads 90 N more than the weights: it also has to stop the falling jet. Show the CV (part (a)) with \(N\), both weights and the three momentum fluxes.

4F4.3 · Drag force & propulsion power

\(F_D=C_D\tfrac12\rho V^2A\) with the frontal area, \(P=F_DV\); convert km/h to m/s. Learn it: note-F4 §8 drag and lift · §9 drag force and power

Ready answer · power to push a body through a fluid \[P=F_DV=C_D\,\tfrac12\rho AV^3\]

In words: power = drag coefficient × dynamic pressure × frontal area × speed. Twice the speed needs eight times the power.

5F4.4 · Drag, lift & aerofoil concepts

Drag ∥ oncoming flow, lift ⊥ to it; stall = upper-surface separation at large angle of attack; flaps = more camber, more lift at low speed. Learn it: note-F4 §8 drag and lift · §10 aerofoils, stall, flaps

Ready answer · the definitions

Drag: the component of the total fluid force on a body parallel to the oncoming flow; it resists the relative motion. Lift: the component perpendicular to the oncoming flow. Both are \(C\,\tfrac12\rho V^2A\).

In words: split the fluid's push on the body into "along the wind" (drag) and "across the wind" (lift).

May 2022 Q63 marksBeyond the handoutsMC, condition(s)

Which of the following condition(s) are preferred for flying of planes?

  • (b) Smooth surface
  • (c) High drag
  • (d) High lift
Hint

Solution
  1. Flight needs lift to balance the weight: high lift (d) is preferred.
  2. Drag costs thrust and fuel: high drag (c) is not wanted. A smooth surface (b) keeps skin-friction drag low.
  3. Vortex shedding (a) means separated, unsteady flow: more drag and vibration, not wanted.

Dec 2018 Q3(b)(c)4 + 2 marksBeyond the handoutspart (a): F3.1older format

(b) With the aid of a sketch, briefly explain what happens to the flow around an airfoil when the airfoil stalls. What is the consequence of stalling? (4 marks)

Hint

Type F4.4 (written). Skeleton in At a glance: angle of attack, separation, wake; then lift and drag.

Solution

Draw the two sketches (attached flow at small angle of attack; separated flow with a wide wake at large angle), then write:

  1. As the angle of attack \(\alpha\) between the chord and the oncoming flow increases, lift increases, until the stall angle (typically 12–16°).
  2. Beyond it, the flow on the upper surface can no longer follow the curved surface against the rising pressure towards the trailing edge: the boundary layer separates, near the leading edge.
  3. A wide, turbulent, eddying wake fills the region above the wing.
  4. Consequence: the low pressure over the upper surface is lost, so lift drops sharply and drag rises sharply; the aircraft loses height and control unless the angle of attack is reduced.

Marker note: the sketch must show separation from the upper surface and the wake; "the plane stops" or "the engine stalls" earns nothing.

(c) Briefly explain the function of movable flaps in an airfoil. (2 marks)

Hint

Type F4.4. When are flaps lowered, and what do they change about the wing's shape?

Solution
  1. Lowering the trailing-edge flaps increases the camber (and often the area) of the wing.
  2. This gives more lift at low speed (a higher maximum lift coefficient), so the aircraft can take off and land slowly without stalling; the extra drag also helps slow it on approach. They are retracted in cruise to keep drag low.

6F4.5 · Head loss

Head loss = mechanical energy per unit weight turned into heat by friction, in metres; it is the \(h_L\) in the energy equation. Learn it: note-F4 §11 head loss

Ready answer · energy equation with a loss \[z_1+\frac{p_1}{\rho g}+\frac{V_1^2}{2g}=z_2+\frac{p_2}{\rho g}+\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+h_L\]

In words: total head upstream = total head downstream + the head lost to friction on the way.

Briefly explain the following terms in the context of fluid flow. (9 marks for the nine terms (a)–(i))

(g) Head loss

Hint

Type F4.5. Energy per what, lost to what, measured in what?

Solution

Head loss \(h_L\) is the mechanical energy per unit weight of fluid that is converted into heat (internal energy) by viscous friction and turbulence between two sections of a real flow; it is the drop in total head \(H=z+p/(\rho g)+V^2/(2g)\) from one section to the next, measured in metres of fluid.

\(\boxed{h_L=H_1-H_2\ \text{(m)}}\)

7F4.6 · Open channel: specific energy & critical depth

\(V=Q/(by)\); compare \(y\) with \(y_c\) (or Fr with 1); alternate depth = the other positive root with the same \(E_s\). Learn it: note-F4 §12 open channels

Ready answer · regime and alternate depth \[\begin{aligned}y_c&=\left(\frac{Q^2}{gb^2}\right)^{1/3},\ \mathrm{Fr}=\frac{V}{\sqrt{gy}}\\E_s&=y+\frac{Q^2}{2gb^2y^2}\end{aligned}\]

In words: deeper than critical (Fr below 1) is subcritical, shallower (Fr above 1) is supercritical. The alternate depth carries the same flow with the same specific energy, on the other side of \(y_c\).

  3. \(y=0.15\u{m}\lt y_c\): shallow and fast, supercritical.
  4. Check with the Froude number: \(\mathrm{Fr}=3.33/\sqrt{9.81(0.15)}=2.75\gt1\) ✓.

8Traps across F4

Traps
  • Water density for an air jet. The data line often prints water only; Dec 2018 Q2 with 1000 kg/m³ gives 7540 N instead of 9.27 N.
  • Nozzle speed at the plate. A vertical jet slows: May 2021 Q3 arrives at 1.41 m/s, not 10 m/s (35.5 N, not 251 N).
  • Forgetting \(pA\). In May 2024 Q3 the inlet pressure force (2200 N) dwarfs the momentum terms (160 N, 640 N).
  • Pressure force pointing the wrong way. \(pA\) always pushes into the CV; a free jet adds nothing.
  • Force on the fluid vs on the plate. The momentum equation gives the force on the fluid; the force on the plate or duct is equal and opposite. Say which you give, with a direction.
  • Force along a smooth plate. There is none: the anchoring force on an inclined plate is normal, \(\dot mV\sin\theta\).
  • km/h and length in drag. Divide by 3.6; use the frontal area \(\tfrac\pi4D^2\), not length × diameter.
  • Tank weight only. The scale also stops the falling jet: add \(\dot m_1V_1\) (90 N in May 2023).
  • "Alternate depth" = the depth you started with. The cubic has two positive roots; give the one on the other side of \(y_c\).
  • Horizontal/vertical for drag/lift. Define them relative to the oncoming flow.
