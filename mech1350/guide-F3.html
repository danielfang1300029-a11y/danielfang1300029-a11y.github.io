F3.6Pitot tube & stagnation-pressure measurementappeared 8 times · last Dec 2024
Looks like
"Describe a Pitot(-static) tube and how it measures velocity / flow rate"; wind-tunnel or duct manometer readings; a jet on a plate with a tube at the centre.
Do
Stagnation streamline: \(p_0-p=\tfrac12\rho V^2\); get \(p_0-p\) from the manometer (each fluid's density); \(V=\sqrt{2\Delta p/\rho}\); \(Q=VA\).
Watch
Air flow with a water manometer: use \(\rho_\text{air}\) in \(\tfrac12\rho V^2\), \(\rho_\text{water}\) in the manometer; an oil layer adds its own \(\rho gh\).
Decision procedure · any Bernoulli calculation (F3.3, F3.4, F3.6)
  1. Choose two points on one streamline: one where you know almost everything (free surface: \(V\approx0\), \(p=0\) or the gas/oil pressure on it; free jet: \(p=0\); stagnation point: \(V=0\)), one where the unknown is.
  2. Datum: put \(z=0\) at the lower point or a pipe centreline; write each \(z\) with sign.
  3. Write Bernoulli in head form and cross out the zero terms. Cue: "large tank" ⇒ surface \(V=0\); "level remains constant" ⇒ surface \(V=0\) exactly and \(Q_\text{in}=Q_\text{out}\).
  4. Count unknowns. Two speeds? Add continuity \(A_1V_1=A_2V_2\). A manometer instead of gauges? Walk the manometer (down \(+\rho gh\), up \(-\rho gh\)) to get \(\Delta p\).
  5. Solve, then \(Q=AV\). For a pressure inside a pipe, go back with a second Bernoulli (now with the pipe speed).
  6. Check: jet speeds of a few m/s per metre of head; gauge pressure in a pipe below the surface is positive; a stagnation pressure is the largest on its streamline.
Answer skeleton · "explain the everyday effect" (F3.2, 3–5 marks)

Diagram to draw: the object, streamlines crowded where the flow is fast, "low \(p\)" and "high \(p\)" labels, and the resulting force or motion arrow.

Marker points (about one mark each): (1) where the fluid is squeezed or moving fast, with continuity \(A_1V_1=A_2V_2\) if a narrowing is involved; (2) Bernoulli \(p+\tfrac12\rho V^2=\) const ⇒ low pressure there; (3) where the pressure is higher; (4) the net force or flow from high to low pressure and the effect asked for.

Model answers: chimney · garden hose · two boats · bladeless fan.

Answer skeleton · "describe a Pitot (-static) tube and how to use it" (F3.6, 6–7 marks)

Diagram to draw: the probe in a pipe facing upstream, the stagnation point at its mouth, the static holes (or a wall tap), and a U-tube with the reading \(H\).

Marker points: (1) what it is: an L-shaped tube with its open end facing into the flow; (2) at the mouth the fluid is stopped (stagnation point), so the tube senses \(p_0\); (3) static pressure \(p\) from side holes or a wall tap (piezometer); (4) Bernoulli along the stagnation streamline: \(p_0-p=\tfrac12\rho V^2\); (5) the manometer gives \(p_0-p=(\rho_m-\rho)gH\); (6) \(V=\sqrt{2(p_0-p)/\rho}\), times \(C_v\approx0.98\)–1 for a real probe; (7) for a flow rate: \(Q=\bar VA\), with \(\bar V\) from a traverse across the pipe (the probe gives the local speed at its tip only); align the probe with the flow.

Model answers: May 2023 Q1(b) · Dec 2021 Q1(a)(b).

2F3.1 · Bernoulli concepts: heads, stagnation pressure, validity

Heads are energy per unit weight (m); \(p_0=p+\tfrac12\rho V^2\); test the five assumptions. Learn it: note-F3 §2 equation & validity · §4 heads · §9 stagnation point

Ready answer · the three heads \[\underbrace{\frac{p}{\rho g}}_{\text{pressure head}}+\underbrace{\frac{V^2}{2g}}_{\text{velocity head}}+\underbrace{z}_{\text{elevation head}}=H\]

In words: each term is energy per newton of fluid, so each is a length [L] (metres); along a streamline of an ideal flow the total head \(H\) is constant. Pressure head + elevation head = piezometric head (the height a piezometer tube shows).

May 2022 Q43 marksMC

Which of the following flow scenarios will not be described by Bernoulli's flows?

  • (a) Air flow of red wine in a decanter
  • (b) Lift of airfoil of an aeroplane
  • (c) Blood flow in our eyes
  • (d) Pulling experienced in subway tunnel
Hint

Type F3.1. Run the five assumptions on each option. Which flow is slow, in tiny vessels, and dominated by friction?

Solution
  1. (b) Aerofoil lift: faster air over the curved upper surface, lower pressure there (handout slide 15). Bernoulli.
  2. (d) A train rushing through a tunnel or past a platform: fast air beside the train, low pressure, you feel pulled towards it. Bernoulli.
  3. (a) An aerating decanter or pourer: the wine speeds up through a narrow throat, its pressure drops below atmospheric and air is sucked into the wine (the Venturi effect). Bernoulli.
  4. (c) Blood in the eye flows slowly through very fine vessels (capillaries a few micrometres wide). Viscous friction dominates (Reynolds number far below 1), so the "inviscid" assumption fails; such flow is described by viscous (Poiseuille-type) laws, not Bernoulli.

\(\boxed{\text{(c) blood flow in our eyes}}\)

Marker note: in a written version, name the assumption that fails (no friction); the handout's own blood example (slide 17) is a narrowed artery, a much larger and faster flow.

the intended answer is not certain: the handout gives no rule for these four cases, and it lists blood flow (constricted arteries, slide 17) as a Bernoulli example. We chose (c) because capillary flow in the eye is friction-dominated. If the examiner read "air flow of red wine in a decanter" as an unsteady free-surface pour, (a) would be the intended answer.

May 2021 Q43 marksexplainsimilar: Dec 2024 B Q4(a)

Briefly explain the stagnation pressure.

Hint

Type F3.1. Where is a stagnation point? Write Bernoulli from the free stream to that point.

Solution
  1. A stagnation point is where the flow is brought to rest, \(V=0\): the nose of a body, the front of a car, the mouth of a Pitot tube.
  2. Bernoulli along the streamline that ends there (same height): \(p+\tfrac12\rho V^2=p_0+0\).
  3. So the stagnation pressure is the static pressure plus the dynamic pressure: the kinetic energy of the stopped fluid has been converted into pressure. It is the highest pressure on that streamline, and \(p_0-p\) measures the speed: \(V=\sqrt{2(p_0-p)/\rho}\) (the Pitot tube).
\[\boxed{p_0=p+\tfrac12\rho V^2}\]

Marker note: three marks = definition (fluid at rest), the equation with "static + dynamic" named, and one use (Pitot tube).

Dec 2018 Q3(a)3 marksexplainolder format

Define the term "energy head." What dimensions does it have?

Parts (b) and (c) of this question (flow round an aerofoil, flaps) are type F4.4, solved in the F4 guide.

Hint

Type F3.1. Divide every term of \(p+\tfrac12\rho V^2+\rho gz\) by \(\rho g\). What is left?

Solution
  1. A head is mechanical energy per unit weight of fluid (J/N): the height a column of the fluid would need to have that energy.
  2. Dividing Bernoulli by \(\rho g\): pressure head \(p/\rho g\), velocity head \(V^2/2g\), elevation head \(z\); their sum is the total (energy) head \(H\), constant along a streamline for ideal flow.
  3. Dimensions: J/N = N·m/N = m, so a head has the dimension of length, [L]; check \(V^2/2g\): (m²/s²)/(m/s²) = m.
\[\boxed{\text{head}=\frac{\text{energy}}{\text{weight}},\ \ [\mathrm{L}]\ (\mathrm{m})}\]

3F3.2 · Explaining everyday phenomena with Bernoulli and continuity

Narrow or fast ⇒ low pressure ⇒ force from high to low pressure; one sketch. Learn it: note-F3 §1 fast air, low pressure · §5 everyday applications · skeleton in At a glance

May 2025 Q13 marksexplain

Why does smoke rise faster in a chimney on a windy day?

Hint

Type F3.2. Compare the air speed (and so the pressure) just above the chimney top with that in the still room at the bottom.

Solution
  1. Wind blows fast across the top of the chimney; the air in the room (fireplace) at the bottom is almost still.
  2. Bernoulli, \(p+\tfrac12\rho V^2=\) const: where the air moves fast, its pressure is lower, so the pressure at the chimney top is below that at the bottom.
  3. This pressure difference pushes air and smoke up the chimney faster (on top of the usual buoyancy of hot gas), so the smoke rises faster and the chimney "draws" better on a windy day (handout slide 18).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{fast wind on top}\Rightarrow\text{low }p\text{ on top}\\&\Rightarrow\text{smoke pushed up}\end{aligned}}\]
May 2025 Q24 marksexplain

You can squirt water over a greater distance by placing your thumb over the end of a garden hose, than by leaving it completely uncovered. Explain how this works?

Hint

Type F3.2. What does the thumb do to the exit area, and so (continuity) to the exit speed? How does a faster jet travel further?

Solution
  1. The thumb reduces the exit area \(A\). Continuity, \(Q=AV\): for (roughly) the same flow, a smaller area means a higher exit speed \(V\).
  2. Energy view (Bernoulli): the water in the hose is at a pressure above atmospheric. At the exit the jet is at \(p=0\), so that pressure head is converted into velocity head, \(V\approx\sqrt{2p/\rho}\). With the thumb on, the flow rate falls a little, so less of the mains pressure is lost to friction along the hose and more of it becomes jet speed.
  3. A projectile's range grows with the square of its launch speed (range \(=V^2\sin2\theta/g\) without air drag), so the faster, thinner jet lands much further away.
  4. The price: less water per second (smaller \(Q\)); the thumb trades flow rate for speed.

\(\boxed{A\downarrow\ \Rightarrow\ V\uparrow\ \Rightarrow\ \text{range}\propto V^2\uparrow}\)

Marker note: continuity (smaller area, higher speed) is the expected core; pressure converted to speed and the range argument earn the remaining marks.

the hose is not in the handout; the friction-loss explanation (why \(Q\) stays almost the same) goes beyond it and follows standard textbook reasoning.

May 2025 Q34 marksexplain

Two boats moving in parallel paths close to one another risk colliding. Why?

Hint

Type F3.2. Watch the water from the boats' point of view. Where is the channel narrow?

Solution
  1. Relative to the boats, water streams backwards past both hulls. Between them it is squeezed through a narrow channel, so by continuity it flows faster there than past the outer sides.
  2. Bernoulli (same level): faster flow ⇒ lower pressure between the hulls.
  3. The higher pressure on the outer sides gives each boat a net sideways force towards the other, so they are drawn together and may collide (the same effect pulls you towards a passing train).
  4. Sketch: the skeleton figure in At a glance (crowded streamlines between the hulls, "low \(p\)" between, "high \(p\)" outside, force arrows).
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{narrow gap}\Rightarrow V\uparrow\Rightarrow p\downarrow\\&\Rightarrow\text{pushed together}\end{aligned}}\]
May 2022 Q85 marksexplain + sketchBeyond the handouts

Using any principles from the course, please describe how a bladeless fan work. Include schematics and equations to support your answers.

Hint

Type F3.2. Where are the hidden blades, and what happens to air forced out of a very narrow slit? What does a fast, low-pressure jet do to the still air around it?

Solution
  1. Hidden pump. An impeller (a small fan) in the base draws air in through the base grille and pushes it up into the hollow ring.
  2. Narrow slit, high speed. The air leaves the ring through a slit about 1 mm wide running round its inside edge. Continuity, \(Q_0=A_\text{slit}V_\text{slit}\): a tiny area means a high jet speed.
  3. Low pressure. The jet follows the ring's curved, aerofoil-shaped inner surface. Bernoulli, \(p+\tfrac12\rho V^2=\) const: the fast jet has a low pressure, lower than the still room air behind the ring and around it.
  4. Entrainment. The pressure difference pulls (induces) air from behind the fan through the ring, and the jet drags surrounding air along with it. The delivered flow is therefore much larger than the pumped flow: \(Q_\text{out}=Q_0+Q_\text{induced}+Q_\text{entrained}\) (makers quote up to about 15 times).
  5. Result: a smooth, large stream of air with no visible blades.
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{slit}\Rightarrow V\uparrow\Rightarrow p\downarrow\\&\Rightarrow\text{air drawn through the ring}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker note: five marks = sketch (base impeller, ring, slit, arrows) + continuity + Bernoulli + entrainment/induction + "output flow larger than pumped flow".

the bladeless fan is not in the handouts; the description follows the published working principle (impeller in the base, slit jet over a Coandă surface, entrainment). The amplification factor is the maker's claim, not examinable.

4F3.3 · Tanks & free jets (Torricelli family)

Free surface (\(V\approx0\)) → jet (\(p=0\)); continuity for the pipe; second Bernoulli for the pipe pressure; constant level ⇒ \(Q_\text{in}=Q_\text{out}\). Learn it: note-F3 §6 Torricelli · §12 Examples 7, 8, 10 · §14 Example 12

Ready answer · surface to jet \[\begin{aligned}&\frac{p_s}{\rho g}+z_s=\frac{V_j^2}{2g}+z_j\\&V_j=\sqrt{2g\Big(z_s-z_j+\frac{p_s}{\rho g}\Big)}\end{aligned}\]

\(p_s\) = gauge pressure on the water surface: 0 if open, the air pressure if pressurised, \(\rho_\text{oil}gH_\text{oil}\) under an oil layer. A vertical jet then rises \(V_j^2/2g\); pipe speed \(V_p=V_j(D_j/D_p)^2\).

In words: the head above the jet (height plus any surface pressure head) all turns into velocity head.

May 2025 Q59 marksnumerical

Water flows steadily from a nozzle into a large tank as shown in Fig. The water then flows from the tank as a jet of diameter \(d\). Determine the value of \(d\) if the water level in the tank remains constant. Viscous effects are negligible.

Redrawn: horizontal 4.5 cm pipe flowing to the left, ending in a nozzle of 3 cm exit diameter; a piezometer on the pipe stands 1.2 m above it; tank 1 m deep; outlet centreline 0.3 m above the floor.

Hint

Type F3.3. Two Bernoulli problems joined by "level constant": pipe (pressure head 1.2 m, moving) → nozzle exit (free jet, same height); then tank surface → outlet jet.

Solution
  1. Pipe (p) → nozzle exit (n), same height, \(p_p/\rho g=1.2\u{m}\), \(p_n=0\): \(1.2+\dfrac{V_p^2}{2g}=\dfrac{V_n^2}{2g}\).
  2. Continuity: \(V_p=V_n(3/4.5)^2=0.444V_n\).
\[\begin{aligned}V_n&=\sqrt{\frac{2(9.81)(1.2)}{1-0.444^2}}\\&=5.42\u{m/s}\end{aligned}\]
  1. \(Q=\tfrac\pi4(0.03)^2(5.42)\) \(=3.83\times10^{-3}\u{m^3/s}\).
  2. Tank surface → outlet, head \(1-0.3=0.7\u{m}\): \(V_o=\sqrt{2(9.81)(0.7)}=3.71\u{m/s}\).
  3. Level constant: \(Q_\text{out}=Q_\text{in}\), so \(\tfrac\pi4d^2V_o=Q\).
\[\begin{aligned}d&=\sqrt{\frac{4(3.83\times10^{-3})}{\pi(3.71)}}\\&\approx0.0363\u{m}\end{aligned}\] \[\boxed{d\approx36\u{mm}}\]

Marker note: the pipe velocity head must not be dropped: with \(V_p\) ignored, \(V_n=4.85\) m/s and \(d=34\) mm.

the scan is faint; values read as 4.5 cm, 3 cm, 1.2 m (piezometer above the pipe centreline), 1 m and 0.3 m.

Dec 2023 Q210 marksnumericalAlso: Dec 2022 Q2 (same wording and numbers)

A stream of refreshing beverage of diameter \(d=0.01\u{m}\) flows steadily from the cooler of diameter \(D=0.2\u{m}\). Determine the flowrate, \(Q\), from the bottle into the cooler if the depth of beverage in the cooler is to remain constant at \(h=0.2\u{m}\).

Hint

Type F3.3. If the depth stays constant, how fast does the free surface (1) move? Bernoulli 1 → 2, then \(Q_\text{in}=Q_\text{out}\).

Solution
  1. Depth constant ⇒ the surface does not move: \(V_1=0\) exactly; \(p_1=p_2=0\) (open surface, free jet); \(z_1-z_2=h=0.2\u{m}\).
  2. Bernoulli 1 → 2: \(h=V_2^2/2g\), so \(V_2=\sqrt{2(9.81)(0.2)}=1.98\u{m/s}\).
  3. Outflow: \(Q_\text{out}=\tfrac\pi4(0.01)^2(1.98)\).
  4. Constant depth ⇒ inflow from the bottle = outflow.
\[\boxed{Q\approx1.56\times10^{-4}\u{m^3/s}}\]

(0.156 L/s). The cooler diameter \(D\) and point (3) are not needed.

Marker note: say why \(V_1=0\) (constant level) and state \(Q_\text{in}=Q_\text{out}\); marks are lost for using \(D\) in a continuity step that does not exist.

May 2023 Q2(a)(b)5 + 5 marksnumerical

The paper gives: Bernoulli equation \(z+\dfrac{P}{\rho g}+\dfrac{U^2}{2g}=\text{constant}\) (C1), \(g\) = 9.81 m s⁻², density of water \(\rho\) = 1,000 kg m⁻³.

A pressurized tank delivering a vertical water jet is shown in Figure C2. Calculate:

Redrawn: closed tank, compressed air (gauge \(P_1\)) on the water surface (point 1), water depth \(h\) = 2 m above the outlet level; the pipe runs along the bottom and turns up into a nozzle at point 2, level with the tank bottom; the vertical jet rises to point 3, \(H\) = 12 m above point 2.

(a) The water jet discharge velocity \(U_2\) at the nozzle (point 2). (5 marks)

Hint

Type F3.3. Bernoulli from the nozzle (2) to the top of the jet (3): what are \(P_3\) and \(U_3\)?

Solution

6F3.5 · Venturi meter in practice: advantages & limitations

Advantage: small permanent loss, no moving parts, accurate. Drawback: turbulence/separation break the assumptions behind \(C_d\). Learn it: note-F3 §7 flow meters

May 2024 Q1(b)(c)3 + 3 marksexplainpart (a): F3.4Beyond the handouts

(b) List out the key practical advantage of a Venturi flowmeter for flow measurements. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F3.5. Compare with an orifice plate: what does the long, gentle diffuser give back?

Solution
  1. Small permanent pressure (energy) loss: the gradual diffuser after the throat recovers most of the pressure, so little pumping energy is wasted (an orifice plate loses much more).
  2. No moving parts, smooth walls: robust, little wear, needs little maintenance, can pass dirty or slurry flows.
  3. Accurate and repeatable: \(C_d\approx0.95\)–0.99 and well known, so \(Q=C_dQ_\text{ideal}\) from a simple manometer reading.

(c) Briefly comment on the drawback of using a Venturi flowmeter for flow measurements in case turbulent flows occur and even flows separate in the diffuser. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F3.5. Which Bernoulli assumptions does (C2) rely on? What does separation do to pressure recovery?

Solution

\[p_2+\rho_wgh-\rho_\text{oil}g(0.025)=0\] \[\begin{aligned}h&=\frac{474.5+900(9.81)(0.025)}{1000(9.81)}\\&=\frac{474.5+220.7}{9810}\end{aligned}\] \[\boxed{h\approx0.0709\u{m}=70.9\u{mm}}\]

Marker note: the water stands higher on the tunnel side (suction); the oil adds to the reading. Without the oil, \(h=48.4\) mm.

(b) Determine the difference between the stagnation pressure on the front of the car and the pressure in the test section. (3 marks)

Hint

Type F3.6. The car's nose is a stagnation point on a streamline from the test section, same height.

Solution
\[\begin{aligned}p_3-p_2&=\tfrac12\rho V_2^2\\&=\tfrac12(1.23)(27.78)^2\end{aligned}\] \[\boxed{p_3-p_2\approx475\u{Pa}}\]

(So \(p_3\approx0\): the nose recovers atmospheric pressure, as it should for ideal flow from still air.)

8Traps across F3

Traps
  • Dropping the pipe velocity head. Nozzle and Venturi problems need \(1-(A_2/A_1)^2\): May 2025 Q5 gives 34 mm instead of 36 mm without it.
  • Dropping \(\Delta z\). A vertical contraction 1.2 m tall changes \(Q\) by about 10% (May 2024 Q2: 0.384 vs 0.350 m³/s).
  • Bernoulli across two fluids. Oil on water: turn the oil into a surface pressure \(\rho_\text{oil}gH\), then apply Bernoulli in the water only.
  • Absolute vs gauge. Free jets, open surfaces and open duct ends are \(p=0\) gauge; answers are gauge unless asked.
  • Wrong density. Air flow: \(\rho_\text{air}\) in \(\tfrac12\rho V^2\), \(\rho_\text{water}\) in the manometer; liquid in a U-tube: \(\rho_m-\rho\), not \(\rho_m\).
  • Constant level. It means \(V_\text{surface}=0\) and \(Q_\text{in}=Q_\text{out}\); the tank diameter is usually not needed.
  • km/h and mm. 100 km/h = 27.78 m/s; 25 mm = 0.025 m; diameters, not radii, in \(A=\tfrac\pi4D^2\).
  • Written answers without the physics. "Fast ⇒ low pressure" earns little unless you say where it is fast (continuity), where the pressure is higher, and what force results.
  • Pitot = local speed. For a flow rate you need the mean speed over the section, not the tip speed.
