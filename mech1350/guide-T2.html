Definitions (note-T2 §1–§4): extensive = depends on size (mass, volume, total energy); intensive = does not (\(T\), \(P\), \(\rho\), \(v\), \(h\)). State principle: two independent intensive properties fix the state of a simple system. Thermodynamic equilibrium = thermal + mechanical + phase + chemical. A process is defined by initial state, final state and path. A pure substance has a uniform, unchanging chemical composition (water with its vapour: yes; liquid air with gaseous air: no).

Decision procedure · the steam question (T2.3 + T2.4)
  1. List the states. Write state 1, state 2 (3 …) with everything given: \(P\), \(T\), volumes, mass, "x = …". Cues: "half of the mass vaporizes" means \(x=0.5\); "one-third condenses" means \(x=2/3\); "saturated liquid" \(x=0\); "liquid and vapour in equilibrium" = mixture.
  2. Region test for each state: compare \(T\) with \(T_\text{sat}\) at its \(P\) (or \(v\) with \(v_f\), \(v_g\)). Constant pressure means \(P\) is the same in every state.
  3. Pick the table. Mixture or saturated: A-5 (by pressure) or A-4 (by temperature). Superheated: A-6 block at that \(P\). Compressed liquid: A-7, or \(y_{f@T}\) from A-4.
  4. Mass and quality. Given volumes of each phase: \(m_f=V_f/v_f\), \(m_g=V_g/v_g\), \(x=m_g/m\). Given total \(V\) and \(m\): \(v=V/m\), \(x=(v-v_f)/v_{fg}\).
  5. Specific values at each state: \(v\) and \(h\) (mixture: \(y_f+xy_{fg}\)).
  6. Totals. \(\Delta V=m(v_2-v_1)\), \(Q=m(h_2-h_1)\); or the shortcuts \(m(1-x)h_{fg}\), \(mxh_{fg}\) for finishing a phase change.
  7. Words and units. m³ and kJ (or MJ); "increase / decrease", "added / removed". Quote the table row you used.
Answer skeleton · "show the process on a T-v / P-v diagram" (T2.2)

Diagram to draw: the dome and one process line, with the five states of water named on it.

Marker points (about one mark each): (1) axes labelled (\(T\) or \(P\) up, \(v\) across); (2) saturation dome: saturated liquid line, saturated vapour line, critical point; (3) the process line with the right shape (T-v isobar: steep, flat at \(T_\text{sat}\), rising; P-v isotherm: steep, flat at \(P_\text{sat}\), falling; P-v isobar: horizontal); (4) initial and final states in the correct regions, with an arrow; (5) the numbers: pressure, \(T_\text{sat}\), final temperature.

Model answer: May 2025 Q5(a) card; the "explain the difference" version: Dec 2023 Q3 card.

Answer skeleton · "explain the state principle" (T2.1, 4–5 marks, set three times)

Marker points: (1) the statement: the state of a simple compressible system is fixed by two independent intensive properties; (2) "simple" = no electrical, magnetic, gravitational or surface-tension effects; (3) once two are fixed, every other property has a definite value (tables, equations of state); (4) "independent": in a saturated mixture \(T\) and \(P\) are tied, so a second property such as \(x\) or \(v\) is needed; (5) a one-line example (water at 1 MPa and 300 °C: one row of Table A-6).

2T2.1 · Property & state vocabulary

Halve the system: halved = extensive, unchanged = intensive; two independent intensive properties fix a state; equilibrium = thermal + mechanical + phase + chemical; a process = initial state + final state + path. Learn it: note-T2 §1 properties · §2 state & equilibrium · §3 processes · §4 pure substances

Ready answer · the four lists examiners use
  • Extensive: mass, volume, total internal energy \(U\), enthalpy \(H\). Intensive: temperature, pressure, density, specific volume, specific energy and enthalpy (\(u\), \(h\)).
  • Thermodynamic equilibrium: thermal, mechanical, phase, chemical (not "dynamic").
  • A process needs: initial state, final state, process path (not time, not energy input).
  • Pure substance: one uniform, unchanging composition in every phase present: N₂, water + steam, gaseous air. Not: salt water, liquid air + gaseous air, oil + water.
Dec 2024 Q13 marks

Name three intensive properties of a thermodynamic system.

Hint

Type T2.1. Which properties stay the same if you cut the system in half?

Solution
  1. An intensive property does not depend on the size (mass) of the system: cutting the system in half leaves it unchanged.
  2. Examples: temperature \(T\), pressure \(P\), density \(\rho\) (also specific volume \(v\), specific enthalpy \(h\)).

\(\boxed{T,\ P,\ \rho}\) (any three intensive properties)

Marker note: one sentence of definition makes the list safe; never list mass, volume or total energy.

Dec 2024 Q34 marksAlso: Dec 2022 Q3 (5 marks), May 2022 Q3 (5 marks), same question

Explain what the state principle is and explain its relationship with the thermodynamic state of a simple system.

Hint

Type T2.1. Three things to say: how many properties of what kind, what "simple" means, what "the state is fixed" implies for all the other properties.

Solution
  1. State principle: the thermodynamic state of a simple compressible system is completely specified by two independent, intensive properties.
  2. Simple system: one with no effects of electrical, magnetic or gravitational fields, or of surface tension.
  3. Relation to the state: once two independent intensive properties are known, the state is fixed: every other property (\(v\), \(u\), \(h\), \(s\), …) has one definite value, read from tables or calculated. Changing any property changes the state.
  4. Independent matters: in a saturated mixture \(T\) and \(P\) are tied (\(T=T_\text{sat}(P)\)), so they count as one; a second property such as the quality \(x\) or \(v\) is needed. Example: water at 1 MPa and 300 °C is one row of Table A-6.

Marker note: for the 5-mark versions add the "independent" point with the boiling example; that is the step most answers miss.

Dec 2023 Q23 marks

Which of the following equilibrium state is required for a system to be in thermodynamic equilibrium (can be more than one answer)?

  • (a) Thermal equilibrium
  • (b) Dynamic equilibrium
  • (c) Mechanical Equilibrium
  • (d) Phase Equilibrium
  • (e) Chemical Equilibrium
Hint

Type T2.1. Four kinds are required; one option is not a thermodynamic term.

Solution
  1. Thermodynamic equilibrium = uniform temperature (thermal), no unbalanced force or pressure (mechanical), constant mass of each phase (phase), unchanging composition (chemical).
  2. "Dynamic equilibrium" is not one of them.

\(\boxed{\text{(a), (c), (d), (e)}}\)

Dec 2022 Q23 marks

Which of the following is required to define a thermodynamic process (can be more than one answer)?

  • (a) Initial state
  • (b) Final state
  • (c) Time
  • (d) Energy input
  • (e) Process path
Hint

Type T2.1. Three processes can join the same two states by different routes (handout slide 11).

Solution
  1. A process is a change from one equilibrium state to another along a path: it is defined by the initial state, the final state and the path between them.
  2. Time does not appear (a quasi-equilibrium process may be slow or fast); energy input is a result of the process, not part of its definition.

\(\boxed{\text{(a), (b), (e)}}\)

May 2022 Q13 marksmirror: Dec 2022 Q1 (extensive)

Which of the following characteristics of a system is an intensive property (can be more than one answer)?

  • (a) temperature
  • (b) mass
  • (c) volume
  • (d) specific volume
  • (e) specific enthalpy
Hint

Type T2.1. "Specific" means "per kilogram".

Solution
  1. Temperature does not depend on size.
  2. An extensive property divided by mass is intensive: \(v=V/m\), \(h=H/m\).
  3. Mass and volume are extensive.

\(\boxed{\text{(a), (d), (e)}}\)

May 2022 Q23 marks

Which of the following is a pure substance (can be more than one answer)?

  • (a) liquid nitrogen
  • (b) salt water
  • (c) mixture of liquified air and gaseous air
  • (d) mixture of liquid water and water vapor
  • (e) mixture of liquid water and cooking oil
Hint

Type T2.1. Same chemical make-up everywhere, and it does not change: check each phase.

Solution
  1. (a) N₂ only: pure. (d) liquid and vapour are both H₂O: pure.
  2. (c) liquid air is richer in oxygen than the gas above it (O₂ condenses at −183 °C, N₂ at −196 °C), so the composition differs between phases: not pure (handout slide 16).
  3. (e) oil and water are two substances that stay separate: not pure. (b) salt water: the salt fraction can be anything and boiling changes it: not pure.

\(\boxed{\text{(a), (d)}}\)

Salt water is not on the handout's slide 16; we follow note-T2 §4 (composition not invariable, so not pure). A textbook that calls every homogeneous mixture pure might also accept (b); state your reason in one line.

3T2.2 · Process paths on property diagrams

Dome (saturated liquid line, saturated vapour line, critical point) + one constant-property line, flat under the dome; states in the right regions; arrow. Learn it: note-T2 §3 property diagrams · §5 heating water · §6 T-v dome · §7 P-v; vaporization vs condensation

Ready answer · shapes of the constant-property lines
  • T-v, constant \(P\): steep rise in the compressed-liquid region, horizontal across the dome at \(T_\text{sat}(P)\), then rising to the right in the superheated region. A higher pressure gives a higher, shorter flat part.
  • P-v, constant \(T\): steep fall in the liquid region, horizontal across the dome at \(P_\text{sat}(T)\), then falling gently to the right.
  • P-v, constant \(P\): a horizontal line everywhere. P-T, constant \(T\): a vertical line.
  • Vaporization vs condensation: the same line, opposite direction; the flat part is where both phases coexist.
May 2025 Q5(a)3 markspart (b): T2.3; (c)(d): T2.4same template: May 2024 Q4(a)

A boiler contains a saturated liquid-vapor mixture of water with an initial volume of 2 m³, where the vapor takes up 95% of the total volume. The saturated mixture is heated at a constant pressure of 5 MPa until it fully vaporizes. The water vapor is continually heat at the same pressure until it reaches a final temperature of 400 °C.

(a) Sketch the entire heating process on a sketch of T-v diagram with respect to saturation lines. Indicate on the T-v diagram the saturation curve, process path, initial state and final state of the system. (3 marks)

Hint

Type T2.2. Start is a mixture (on the flat part of the 5 MPa isobar). Compare 400 °C with \(T_\text{sat}\) at 5 MPa.

Solution
  1. Table A-5, 5000 kPa: \(T_\text{sat}=263.94\) °C. State 1 is a mixture, so it lies on the flat part at 263.94 °C; its quality is 0.38 (part (b)), so it sits nearer the liquid line.
  2. Heating at 5 MPa: right along the flat line to the saturated vapour line ("fully vaporizes"), then up the isobar into the superheated region.
  3. State 2: 400 °C > 263.94 °C, superheated vapour (to the right of the dome; 400 °C is above the critical temperature but 5 MPa is below the critical pressure).

Marker note: the three marks are the dome (with critical point), the path shape and the two labelled states; write 5 MPa and 263.94 °C on the sketch.

Dec 2024 Q44 marks

Sketch the process path of an isobaric process on a Pressure-Volume (P-V) diagram and the process path of an isothermal process on a Pressure-Temperature (P-T) diagram.

Hint

Type T2.2. Which axis carries the property that stays constant?

Solution
  1. Isobaric = constant \(P\); \(P\) is the vertical axis of the P-V diagram, so the path is a horizontal line at \(P_0\) (arrow right for an expansion, left for a compression).
  2. Isothermal = constant \(T\); \(T\) is the horizontal axis of the P-T diagram, so the path is a vertical line at \(T_0\) (arrow up if the pressure rises, down if it falls).

Marker note: label the axes, the constant value (\(P_0\), \(T_0\)), states 1 and 2 and an arrow on each sketch.

Dec 2024 Q7(a)3 marksparts (b)(c)(d): T2.3

A piston-cylinder assembly is fitted with a frictionless weighted piston of area \(A=1\u{m^2}\) and a spring of spring constant \(k\). In the initial state of the apparatus shown in the figure, the piston is not in contact with the spring. Saturated liquid water at \(T=220\) °C fills the entire initial volume (\(V_1=1\u{m^3}\)) in the cylinder below the piston (State 1). The contents of the cylinder experience an isobaric, quasi-static heat transfer process that causes the piston to rise until the water is 99% vapor by volume (State 2). At this point, the piston just contacts the spring. The heat transfer process is then continued, causing the spring to compress until the pressure in the cylinder is \(P_3=7\u{MPa}\) (State 3).

(a) Sketch the path between states 1, 2 and 3 on a pressure-specific volume diagram. (3 marks)

Hint

Type T2.2. Two stages: piston free (what stays constant?), then the spring pushes back harder the more it is compressed.

Solution
  1. State 1: saturated liquid at 220 °C, \(P_\text{sat}=2319.6\u{kPa}\) (Table A-4): on the saturated liquid line.
  2. 1 → 2: the weighted piston alone sets the pressure, so \(P\) stays 2.32 MPa: a horizontal line into the dome to state 2 (a mixture, \(x_2\approx0.58\), part (c)).
  3. 2 → 3: the spring force grows linearly as the piston rises, so \(P\) rises linearly with \(V\): a straight line up and to the right, leaving the dome through the saturated vapour line and ending at 7 MPa in the superheated region (\(v_3\gt v_g\) at 7 MPa, part (d)).

Marker note: one mark each: state 1 on the liquid line, horizontal 1 → 2 under the dome, rising 2 → 3 ending outside the dome.

The sketch follows the physics in the stem (spring compressed, so \(V_3\gt V_2\)). With the printed numbers of parts (c) and (d), \(V_2=42.2\u{m^3}\) but \(V_3=30\u{m^3}\), which would put state 3 to the left of state 2; the paper's data are inconsistent here (see the Q7(b)–(d) card).

May 2024 Q35 markssame idea at constant \(P\): Dec 2023 Q3

Consider vaporization and condensation of water by changing the pressure of the system at a constant temperature. Sketch the pressure-specific volume (P-v) diagram for the two processes on two separate property diagrams. Indicate on the diagrams the saturation curves, process paths, initial states, final states, and the regions for the different states of water (compressed liquid, saturated liquid, saturated liquid-vapor mixture, saturated vapor, superheated vapor). Explain the difference(s) between the process path of the two diagrams for vaporization and condensation.

Hint

Type T2.2. Constant temperature on a P-v diagram: the isotherm falls, is flat across the dome at \(P_\text{sat}\), then falls gently. Which way does lowering the pressure move you?

Solution
  1. Two P-v diagrams, each with the dome (saturated liquid line on the left, saturated vapour line on the right, critical point on top) and one isotherm below the critical point.
  2. Vaporization (pressure lowered): start top-left in the compressed liquid, fall to the saturated liquid line, move right along the flat part at \(P_\text{sat}(T)\) through the mixture to the saturated vapour line, then fall into the superheated region.
  3. Condensation (pressure raised): start bottom-right in the superheated vapour, rise to the saturated vapour line, move left along the flat part, reach the saturated liquid line, then rise steeply into the compressed liquid.
  4. Difference: both follow the same isotherm; only the direction is opposite (vaporization: \(P\) decreasing, \(v\) increasing; condensation: \(P\) increasing, \(v\) decreasing). In the dome \(P\) stays at \(P_\text{sat}\) while the phase changes.

Marker note: name all five regions/states on each diagram; the explanation mark needs "same path, opposite direction".

Dec 2023 Q36 marksAlso: May 2022 Q4 (5 marks), same questionsame idea at constant \(T\): May 2024 Q3

Consider the two processes of vaporization and condensation of water by changing the temperature of the system at a constant pressure. Sketch the temperature-specific volume (T-v) diagram for the two processes in two separate T-v diagrams. Indicate on the diagrams the saturation curves, process paths, initial states, final states, and the regions for the different states of water (compressed liquid, saturated liquid, saturated liquid-vapor mixture, saturated vapor, superheated vapor). Explain the difference(s) between the process path of the two diagrams for vaporization and condensation.

Hint

Type T2.2. Constant pressure on T-v: steep, flat at \(T_\text{sat}\), rising. Heating moves you one way along it, cooling the other.

Solution

  2. Vaporization (heat added): start bottom-left in the compressed liquid, rise to the saturated liquid line, move right along the flat part at \(T_\text{sat}(P)\) through the mixture to the saturated vapour line, then rise into the superheated vapour.

5T2.4 · Volume change & energy at constant pressure

\(v\) and \(h\) at both ends, then \(\Delta V=m(v_2-v_1)\) and \(Q=m(h_2-h_1)\); finishing a phase change: \(m(1-x)h_{fg}\) or \(mxh_{fg}\). Learn it: note-T2 §8 enthalpy · §11 quality · §13 handout examples 3–4

In words: at constant pressure (any process) the heat equals the enthalpy change. To finish boiling, every kilogram of liquid still left needs \(h_{fg}\); to finish condensing, every kilogram of vapour still left must give up \(h_{fg}\).

(e) Determine the amount of energy transferred to the water. (4 marks)

Hint

Type T2.4. Constant pressure: \(Q=m(h_2-h_1)\), mixture at the start, Table A-6 at the end.

Solution
  1. Table A-5, 800 kPa: \(h_f=720.87\), \(h_{fg}=2047.5\u{kJ/kg}\).
  2. \(h_1=720.87+0.01822(2047.5)\) \(=758.17\u{kJ/kg}\).
  3. Table A-6, 0.8 MPa, 400 °C: \(h_2=3267.7\u{kJ/kg}\).
  4. \(Q=18.270(3267.7-758.17)\) \(=45\,850\u{kJ}\).
\[\boxed{Q\approx4.58\times10^{4}\u{kJ}=45.8\u{MJ}\ \text{added}}\]

Marker note: using \(\Delta U\) instead of \(\Delta H\) misses the work done pushing the piston; at constant pressure the heat is \(\Delta H\).

A piston–cylinder device contains 0.8 kg of superheated water vapor at 300 °C and 1 MPa. The water vapor is cooled at constant pressure until one-third of the mass condenses. The water mass then becomes a saturated liquid-vapor mixture.

(c) Determine the volume change. (5 marks)

Hint

Type T2.4. One-third condensed, so \(x_2=2/3\). \(v_1\) from Table A-6, 1 MPa, 300 °C.

Solution
  1. Table A-6, 1.0 MPa, 300 °C: \(v_1=0.25799\u{m^3/kg}\).
  2. Table A-5, 1000 kPa: \(v_2=0.001127+\tfrac23(0.193233)\) \(=0.12995\u{m^3/kg}\).
  3. \(\Delta V=0.8(0.12995-0.25799)\).
\[\boxed{\Delta V\approx-0.102\u{m^3}\ \text{(a decrease)}}\]

(d) Determine the additional amount of energy needed to be withdrawn from the saturated liquid-vapor mixture to complete the condensation. (5 marks)

Hint

Type T2.4. Two-thirds of 0.8 kg is still vapour.

Solution
  1. Vapour left: \(mx=0.8(2/3)=0.5333\u{kg}\).
  2. \(h_{fg}=2014.6\u{kJ/kg}\) at 1 MPa.
  3. \(Q=mxh_{fg}=0.5333(2014.6)\).
\[\boxed{Q\approx1074\u{kJ}\ \text{removed}}\]

The May 2021 paper printed no steam tables; Çengel A-5/A-6 values are used.

6Traps across T2

Traps
  • Volume fraction used as quality. "Vapour takes 95% of the volume" gives \(x=0.38\), not 0.95 (May 2025, May 2024); 80% of the volume is \(x=0.018\) (Dec 2021).
  • kPa and MPa mixed up. Table A-5 rows are in kPa (5000 kPa), Table A-6 blocks in MPa (5.0 MPa); May 2023's Table A-2 is in bar.
  • Wrong table for the region. Always do the region test first: a superheated state read from A-5, or a mixture read from A-6, gives nonsense.
  • Finishing a phase change. To complete vaporization use the liquid left, \(m(1-x)\); to complete condensation use the vapour left, \(mx\).
  • \(\Delta U\) instead of \(\Delta H\). Heat added at constant pressure is \(m(h_2-h_1)\), not \(m(u_2-u_1)\).
  • Gauge vs absolute pressure. Table pressures are absolute; a rocker or valve feels \(P-P_\text{atm}\) (May 2023 Q5(b)).
  • Wrong shape on P-v axes. A constant-pressure process is a horizontal line on a P-v diagram; only on T-v does it rise after the dome.
  • Missing labels on sketches. Dome, critical point, both saturation lines, states 1 and 2, arrow, and the numbers (\(P\), \(T_\text{sat}\), final \(T\)) carry the marks.
  • Specific vs total. \(v\), \(h\) are per kilogram; multiply by \(m\) before quoting a volume (m³) or an energy (kJ).
