Decision procedure · floating stability (H4.4)
  1. Draught. Law of flotation: \(\gamma_\text{body}\times\) body volume \(=\gamma_w\times\) submerged volume. Cue: "specific weight 7.8 kN/m³" or "draught after loading".
  2. Heights above the keel K. \(KB\) = half the draught (box or upright cylinder). \(KG\) = half the height for a uniform solid, otherwise the given value.
  3. Waterplane \(I\). The cross-section at the waterline, about the axis it tips about: circle \(\pi D^4/64\); rectangle \(l\,b^3/12\) with \(b\) the short side (the body tips the easy way).
  4. \(BM=I/V_d\) with \(V_d\) the submerged volume (the paper writes \(MB\)).
  5. \(GM=KB+BM-KG\). Positive: stable; negative: unstable; zero: neutral.
  6. Explain in one sentence: M above G gives a righting couple when tilted; M below G an overturning one.
Answer skeleton · cycle sketches (T4.5, Dec 2018 Q2(a) 3 marks, Q3(b) 4 marks)

Diagram to draw: \(P\) up, \(V\) across. Carnot: two isotherms \(T_H\) (upper) and \(T_L\) (lower), two steeper adiabats, corners numbered 1–4, arrows round the loop; one loop clockwise (engine), one anticlockwise (heat pump). Otto: points A–E as in the paper's Figure A3, each leg numbered.

Marker points: (1) correct curve shapes (adiabats steeper than isotherms; Otto has two vertical constant-volume legs); (2) direction of travel; (3) "isothermal / adiabatic / constant volume / constant pressure" on every leg; (4) where \(Q\) goes in and out; (5) net work out (engine) or in (heat pump), the loop area.

Model answers in the Dec 2018 Q2(a) card and the Dec 2018 Q3 card.

Answer skeleton · "is it stable? why?" (H4.5, 4 marks)

Diagram to draw: the plate upright with \(T\) at the attachment P, \(W\) and \(F_B\) at its centre; then the plate tilted by a small \(\theta\) about P, with \(W-F_B\) at G and its lever arm \(PG\sin\theta\).

Upright: \(T\), \(W\), \(F_B\) on one vertical line.

Tilted: \(W-F_B\) at G has a lever arm about P and turns the plate back.

Marker points: (1) uniform and fully submerged, so G and B are both at the plate's centre; (2) the cable attachment P is above them; (3) \(W\gt F_B\), net force down at G; (4) after a small tilt the net force has a lever arm \(PG\sin\theta\) about P and turns the plate back; (5) so it is stable (like a pendulum).

2T4.5 · Engine & heat-pump cycles on P-V (Carnot, Otto)

Examined in Dec 2018 only: Section A Q2(a), 3 marks, and Q3(a)(b), 6 + 4 marks. Carnot: isotherms + steeper adiabats, engine clockwise, heat pump anticlockwise; Otto: adiabats + constant-volume legs. Learn it: note-T4 §13 heat engines, Carnot and Otto cycles

Dec 2018 Q2(a)3 markssketchpart (b): T4.6older format

(a) Sketch a P-V diagram to show the difference between Carnot engine cycle and Carnot heat pump cycle, indicate the type of thermal changes in the diagram. (3 marks)

Hint

Type T4.5. Which two kinds of process make a Carnot cycle, and which of them is steeper on \(P\)-\(V\)? What changes when the cycle runs backwards?

Solution
  1. Same four curves for both. Two isotherms, \(PV=\) const, at \(T_H\) (upper) and \(T_L\) (lower), joined by two adiabats, \(PV^k=\) const, which are steeper. Number the corners 1 (top left) to 4.
  2. Engine, clockwise 1→2→3→4→1. 1→2 isothermal expansion at \(T_H\): heat \(Q_H\) in. 2→3 adiabatic expansion: \(Q=0\), \(T\) falls to \(T_L\). 3→4 isothermal compression at \(T_L\): heat \(Q_L\) out. 4→1 adiabatic compression: \(Q=0\), \(T\) rises to \(T_H\).
  3. Heat pump, anticlockwise 1→4→3→2→1. 1→4 adiabatic expansion: \(T\) falls to \(T_L\). 4→3 isothermal expansion at \(T_L\): heat \(Q_L\) absorbed from the cold side. 3→2 adiabatic compression: \(T\) rises to \(T_H\). 2→1 isothermal compression at \(T_H\): heat \(Q_H\) rejected to the warm side.
  4. The difference. Engine: heat in at high \(T\), net work out (enclosed area). Heat pump: heat in at low \(T\), net work in (same area), \(Q_H=W_\text{in}+Q_L\).

Marker note: the marks are for the two loops with opposite arrows and a label on every leg (isothermal or adiabatic, heat in or out); the curves need not be to scale.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{engine: clockwise, }W\text{ out}\\&\text{heat pump: anticlockwise, }W\text{ in}\end{aligned}}\]

"Carnot heat pump cycle" is read as the reversed Carnot cycle (refrigerator and heat pump run the same cycle; only the useful effect differs).

Dec 2018 Q3(a)(b)6 + 4 marksexplain, sketcholder format

Figure A3 shows the idealized and practical forms of indicator diagram for a four stroke internal combustion engine (Otto cycle).

Figure A3 redrawn. Left, idealized: A→B along a low constant pressure (double arrow), B→C compression curve, C→D vertical, D→E expansion curve, E→B vertical. Right, real: a rounded loop plus a thin pumping loop along the atmospheric line.

(a) Identify and explain three differences between the two diagrams. (6 marks)

Hint

Type T4.5 (written). Look at the corners: what does the ideal cycle assume happens instantly? Then look at the bottom line.

Solution
  1. Rounded, lower combustion peak. Ideal: heat is added instantly at constant volume (vertical C→D). Real: the mixture takes time to burn while the piston moves (the spark fires a little before top dead centre), so the rise is rounded and the peak pressure lower.
  2. Rounded release. Ideal: heat is rejected instantly at bottom dead centre (vertical E→B). Real: the exhaust valve opens before bottom dead centre and the gas takes time to blow down, so the corner is rounded and some expansion work is lost.
  3. Pumping loop. Ideal: intake and exhaust lie on one line (no area). Real: intake is slightly below and exhaust slightly above atmospheric pressure (valves and pipes resist the flow), so a thin loop of negative work appears: the pumping loss.
  4. Also accepted: real compression and expansion are not adiabatic (heat leaks to the cooled cylinder walls) and there is friction, so the real loop encloses less area: less net work per cycle.

Marker note: two marks per difference: one for naming it on the diagram, one for the physical reason.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{rounded peak, rounded release,}\\&\text{pumping loop: less net work}\end{aligned}}\]

(b) Copy the P-V diagram of the ideal Otto cycle. Add labels to the diagram to indicate the corresponding operation stages as well as the type of thermal changes in a combustion engine operation cycle. (4 marks)

Hint

Type T4.5. Name the four strokes of the engine (intake, compression, power, exhaust) and match each to a leg A→B, B→C, …; then say whether each leg is constant pressure, adiabatic or constant volume.

Solution
LegOperation stageThermal change
1 · A→Bintake stroke: mixture drawn inconstant pressure (atmospheric)
2 · B→Ccompression strokeadiabatic compression, \(Q=0\), \(T\) rises
3 · C→Dspark ignition and combustion at top dead centreheat \(Q_\text{in}\) added at constant volume
4 · D→Epower strokeadiabatic expansion, \(Q=0\), work out
5 · E→Bexhaust valve opens at bottom dead centreheat \(Q_\text{out}\) rejected at constant volume
6 · B→Aexhaust stroke: burnt gas pushed outconstant pressure (atmospheric)
  1. Copy the ideal diagram with A–E, then write the stage and the thermal change next to each leg (numbered here to keep the drawing readable).
  2. Intake and exhaust lie on the same line and enclose no area: the net work is the area of B–C–D–E.

Marker note: four strokes named and the two adiabatic and two constant-volume legs identified; heat in on C→D, heat out on E→B.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{adiabatic + const }V\text{ (heat in)}\\&\text{+ adiabatic + const }V\text{ (out)}\end{aligned}}\]

3T4.6 · Refrigeration COP & power (reversed Carnot)

Examined in Dec 2018 only: Section A Q2(b), 4 marks. Kelvin, \(\text{COP}_\text{Carnot}=T_L/(T_H-T_L)\), actual COP = fraction × Carnot, power = load ÷ COP. Learn it: note-T4 §13 refrigerators and COP

Ready answer · refrigerator power \[\dot W_\text{in}=\frac{\dot Q_L}{f\,\text{COP}_\text{Carnot}}\] \[\text{COP}_\text{Carnot}=\frac{T_L}{T_H-T_L}\]

In words: power needed = cooling load ÷ (fraction of Carnot × Carnot COP), with both temperatures in kelvin. For a heat pump used for heating, the useful effect is \(\dot Q_H\) and the Carnot COP is \(T_H/(T_H-T_L)\).

Dec 2018 Q2(b)4 marksnumericalpart (a): T4.5older format

(b) A fish freezing plant requires 20 tons of refrigeration. The freezing temperature is −40 °C while the ambient temperature is 24 °C. If the performance of the plant is 25 % of the theoretical reversed Carnot cycle working within the same temperature limits, state whether it is a heat engine problem or heat pump problem. Calculate the power required in kW. Given: 1 ton of refrigeration = 210 kJ/min. (4 marks)

Hint

Type T4.6. Which way is heat moved, and is work produced or spent? Then convert both temperatures to kelvin before the Carnot COP.

Solution
  1. Type of problem. Heat is taken from the cold fish (−40 °C) and rejected to the warmer surroundings (24 °C), and work must be supplied: a reversed cycle, so a heat pump (refrigerator) problem, not a heat engine.
  2. Kelvin: \(T_L=233.15\u{K}\), \(T_H=297.15\u{K}\).
  3. Carnot COP, with \(T_H-T_L=64\u{K}\): \[\text{COP}_\text{Carnot}=\frac{233.15}{64}=3.643\]
  4. Actual COP: \(0.25\times3.643=0.9107\).
  5. Load: \(20\times210=4200\u{kJ/min}\) \(=70\u{kW}\).
  6. Power: \(\dot W=70/0.9107\) \(=76.9\u{kW}\). (Heat rejected: \(70+76.9=146.9\u{kW}\).)

Marker note: one mark for "heat pump (refrigerator)" with the reason, then kelvin, COP and power; a negative COP from °C loses all the calculation marks.

\[\boxed{\text{heat pump; }\dot W\approx76.9\u{kW}}\]

"Performance" is read as the COP (25% of the reversed-Carnot COP). The paper's 1 ton = 210 kJ/min is used (the standard value is about 211 kJ/min); 273.15 is used for kelvin, and 273 gives 76.9 kW as well.

4H4.4 · Stability of a floating body (metacentre)

Examined in May 2025 Q4(b), 7 marks; May 2024 Q2(b), 8 marks; Dec 2018 Q3(b), 6 marks (Section B). Draught → \(KB\), \(KG\); smallest \(I\); \(BM=I/V_d\); \(GM=KB+BM-KG\). Learn it: note-H4 §10 the metacentre · §6 law of flotation

May 2025 Q4(b)7 markspart (a): H4.2 on guide-H4Also: Dec 2018 Q3(b), 6 marks

A cylinder, of diameter 0.3 m and height 1.0 m, with a specific weight of 7.8 kN/m³ is floating upright in water. [\(MB=I/V_d\), second moment of area of a circle, \(I=\pi D^4/64\)] (Section B: \(g=9.81\) m/s², \(\rho_w=1000\) kg/m³.)

(a) Determine the length of cylinder that is submerged in water, \(L\). (4 marks; solved on guide-H4: \(L=0.795\) m)

(b) Is the cylinder stable? Support your answer with calculations and explanations. (7 marks)

Hint

Type H4.4. You need three heights above the bottom K: B (centre of the submerged part), G (centre of the cylinder) and M (\(BM=I/V_d\) above B). Where is M compared with G?

Solution
  1. Draught (part (a)): weight = upthrust, \(7.8\,A(1.0)=9.81\,A\,L\), so \(L=0.7951\u{m}\).
  2. Displaced volume: \(V_d=\tfrac{\pi}{4}(0.3)^2(0.7951)\) \(=0.05620\u{m^3}\).
  3. Waterplane (a circle of diameter 0.3 m): \(I=\pi(0.3)^4/64\) \(=3.976\times10^{-4}\u{m^4}\).
  4. \(BM=I/V_d\) \(=3.976\times10^{-4}/0.05620\) \(=0.00708\u{m}\).
  5. \(KB=L/2=0.3976\u{m}\) (centre of the submerged part); \(KG=1.0/2=0.5\u{m}\) (uniform solid).
  6. \(GM=KB+BM-KG\) \(=0.3976+0.0071-0.5\) \(=-0.0954\u{m}\).
  7. Explanation. M is 95 mm below G. When the cylinder tilts, B moves only 7 mm × θ sideways, while G is high; the weight at G and the upthrust at B′ form a couple that turns it further over. It falls over and floats on its side.

Marker note: marks for \(V_d\), \(I\), \(BM\), the positions of B and G, \(GM\), and the conclusion with its reason (M below G).

\[\boxed{GM\approx-0.095\u{m}:\ \text{unstable}}\]

For interest: a 1 m tall cylinder of this material would float upright only if \(D\gt1.14\) m, because \(BM=D^2/(16L)\) must exceed \(KG-KB=0.102\) m.

Dec 2018 Q3(b) (6 marks) is the same question with \(d\) for \(L\), \(\gamma_w=9.81\) kN/m³ given and \(I=\pi r^4/4\) "about its diameter": the same \(I=3.976\times10^{-4}\) m⁴ and the same answer. The paper's \(MB\) is the distance written \(BM\) here.

May 2024 Q2(b)8 markspart (a): H4.2 on guide-H4

A rectangular flat-bottomed boat has a width of \(b=4\) m and a length of \(l=12\) m. The draught (distance between the waterline and the deepest point of the boat) before and after loading the cargo is \(h_1=0.5\) m and \(h_2=1\) m, respectively. The center of gravity after loading the cargo is \(g=0.85\) m from the bottom of the boat. (No figure in the paper.)

(a) Find the weight of the loaded cargo G. (6 marks; solved on guide-H4: 235.4 kN)

(b) Determine the stability of the boat after loading the cargo. For a rectangle with two sides \(a\) and \(b\), minimal \(I=\dfrac{b\,a^3}{12}\), when \(a\lt b\). The specific weight of water is 9.81 kN/m³. (8 marks)

Hint

Type H4.4. Use the draught after loading. The boat rolls about its long axis: which side gets cubed in the minimal \(I\)?

Solution
  1. Displaced volume after loading: \(V_d=4\times12\times1\) \(=48\u{m^3}\) (weight of boat + cargo \(=9.81\times48=470.9\u{kN}\)).
  2. \(KB=h_2/2=0.5\u{m}\) (centre of the submerged box).
  3. \(KG=0.85\u{m}\) (given; here "\(g\)" is a length, not gravity).
  4. Minimal \(I\) of the 4 m × 12 m waterplane: the short side (4 m, the paper's \(a\)) is cubed: \(I=12\times4^3/12\) \(=64\u{m^4}\).
  5. \(BM=I/V_d=64/48\) \(=1.333\u{m}\).
  6. \(GM=0.5+1.333-0.85\) \(=0.983\u{m}\).
  7. Explanation. M is 0.98 m above G, so a small roll moves B far enough sideways that the weight and the upthrust form a righting couple. (Pitching, about the short axis, uses \(I=4\times12^3/12=576\u{m^4}\): \(GM=11.65\) m, even more stable.)

Marker note: most marks go to the minimal \(I\) (64, not 576), \(BM\), \(KB\) at half the draught and the sign of \(GM\) with the reason.

\[\boxed{GM\approx0.98\u{m}\gt0:\ \text{stable}}\]

The paper's formula letters (\(a\) short side, \(b\) long side) clash with the stem's \(b=4\) m (width); we use the width 4 m as the short side \(a\) and the length 12 m as \(b\), which gives the minimal \(I=64\) m⁴.

5H4.5 · Stability of a suspended, submerged body

Examined in Dec 2022 Q3(c), Dec 2021 Q3(c) and May 2019 Q3(c), 4 marks each (Section B). Cable attachment P above G = B: a tilt gives a restoring moment \((W-F_B)\,PG\sin\theta\), stable. Learn it: note-H4 §11 suspended bodies · §8 hanging bodies: the force balance

Dec 2022 Q3(c)4 marksexplainparts (a)(b): H4.3 on guide-H4same template: Dec 2021 Q3(c)

A 5-mm thick steel plate, measuring 3 m by 2 m, is hanged from a port to the ocean with a cable. It is of uniform density at 7800 kg/m³ and is fully submerged in water. The density of ocean water is 1027 kg/m³. (Section B: \(g=9.81\) m/s².)

(a) Draw a free body diagram of the metal plate and indicate all the forces acting on it. (b) What is the tension in the cable to hold the metal plate in place? (3 + 6 marks; solved on guide-H4: \(T=1993\) N)

(c) The plate is assumed to be in a vertical position as shown. Is it stable, unstable or of neutral stability? Why? (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.5. Where are G and B for a uniform, fully submerged plate, and where is the cable attached? Tilt the plate a little and take moments about the attachment.

Solution
  1. Forces (parts (a)(b)): \(V=3\times2\times0.005=0.03\u{m^3}\); \(W=7800(9.81)(0.03)\) \(=2295.5\u{N}\) at G; \(F_B=1027(9.81)(0.03)\) \(=302.2\u{N}\) at B; cable tension \(T=W-F_B=1993\u{N}\) at the attachment P (top edge).
  2. Positions. The plate is uniform and fully submerged, so G and B are both at its centre, \(PG=1.5\u{m}\) below P.
  3. Small tilt \(\theta\) about P: G moves sideways by \(PG\sin\theta\); the net downward force \(W-F_B=1993\u{N}\) acts there, \(T\) still acts at P.
  4. Moment about P: \((W-F_B)\,PG\sin\theta\), directed back towards the vertical. For \(\theta=5^\circ\): \(1993\times1.5\times\sin5^\circ\approx261\u{N\,m}\), restoring.
  5. Conclusion. Every small tilt is undone: the plate hangs like a pendulum, so its vertical position is stable.

Marker note: "stable" earns little alone; the marks are for G = B at the centre, below the suspension point, and the restoring moment after a tilt.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{stable: P above G,}\\&\text{restoring moment}\end{aligned}}\]

"Stability" is read as tilting in the vertical plane of the figure (or out of it: same answer). Turning about the cable's own vertical line gives no moment at all (neutral), which the question does not ask about.

Dec 2021 Q3(c)4 marksexplainparts (a)(b): H4.3 on guide-H4Also: May 2019 Q3(c), 4 marks

A metal plate 3 mm thick with a uniform density of 7000 kg/m³ is hung from a port to the ocean. The density of ocean water is 1027 kg/m³ and gravitational acceleration is 9.81 m/s². (The figure shows the plate 4 m high and 2 m wide.)

(a) Draw a free body diagram of the metal plate and indicate all the forces acting on it. (b) What is the tension in the cable to hold the metal plate in place? (4 + 6 marks; solved on guide-H4: \(T=1406\) N)

(c) The plate is assumed to be in a vertical position as shown. Is it stable, unstable or of neutral stability? Why? (4 marks)

Hint

Type H4.5. Same reasoning as Dec 2022: locate G, B and the cable attachment, then tilt.

Solution
  1. Forces: \(V=4\times2\times0.003=0.024\u{m^3}\); \(W=7000(9.81)(0.024)\) \(=1648.1\u{N}\); \(F_B=1027(9.81)(0.024)\) \(=241.8\u{N}\); \(T=W-F_B=1406\u{N}\).
  2. Positions: uniform and fully submerged, so G = B at the centre, \(PG=2\u{m}\) below the attachment P.
  3. Tilt by \(\theta\): the net weight 1406 N at G has lever arm \(2\sin\theta\) about P and turns the plate back (5°: \(1406\times2\times\sin5^\circ\approx245\u{N\,m}\)).
  4. So the vertical position is stable: suspended from a point above its centre of gravity, like a pendulum.

Marker note: as in Dec 2022: the reason (positions and restoring moment) carries the marks.

6Traps across the previous-syllabus questions

Traps
  • °C in a COP. \(-40/(24+40)\) gives \(-0.625\); only kelvin works in \(T_L/(T_H-T_L)\).
  • Multiplying by the COP. Power = load ÷ COP: 70 kW ÷ 0.911, not × 0.911.
  • kJ/min left as kJ/min. 4200 kJ/min ÷ 60 = 70 kW.
  • Engine vs heat pump. Same Carnot loop, opposite direction: clockwise gives work out, anticlockwise needs work in.
  • Otto heat at constant pressure. The Otto cycle adds and rejects heat at constant volume (vertical legs).
  • KB at half the height. B is the centre of the submerged part: half the draught, not half the body.
  • The largest \(I\). A body tips the easy way: cube the short side (64 m⁴, not 576 m⁴).
  • M above B is not enough. Stability needs M above G; the May 2025 cylinder has M above B but unstable.
  • "Stable" without a reason. Name G, B, the attachment point or M, and the righting or overturning moment.
