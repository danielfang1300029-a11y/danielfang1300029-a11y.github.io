Exam guide: Determinants

L4At a glance

This chapter is very predictable. Every question is one of four types, and three of them reduce to one skill: computing a determinant quickly and without slips. The fourth (the rules) needs no computing at all, only the five properties used in the right order. Learn the four cards below, then work through the questions on this page in order.

Exam format
  • No calculators in any sitting since Dec 2022. Expand along the row or column with the most zeros, and take common factors out of rows first (May 2024 Q5 has a row \(-8,8,-8,8\)).
  • No formula sheet. You must know by heart: \(\det(kA)=k^n\det A\), \(\det(AB)=\det A\det B\), \(\det A^T=\det A\), \(\det A^{-1}=1/\det A\), the effect of each row operation, and the cofactor sign pattern.
  • Multiple choice since Dec 2024. Dec 2024: 2.5 marks, options A–E. Dec 2025: 2 marks, four options. The rules questions (L4.2) and "which value is singular" (L4.3) are the favourite MC items: the wrong options are exactly the usual slips (forgetting \(k^n\), losing a sign, dropping a root).
  • Time. 3 hours for 100 marks is about 1.8 minutes per mark. A \(3\times3\) determinant should take 2–3 minutes; a 10-mark "find det A, det Aᵀ, …" question about 12 minutes, most of it on part (a).

The four cards

L4.1 Evaluate a determinant 14 parts · 10 papers · 2.5–5 marks each

Looks like"Compute / find the determinant of …", a \(3\times3\) or \(4\times4\) of numbers, a matrix with a pattern (powers of 2, a common factor in each row) or with letters; "prove that this determinant equals …".

DoTake common factors out of rows or columns; create zeros with row replacements (no change); expand along the row or column with the most zeros; for letters, factorise as you go.

WatchThe sign pattern \(+\,-\,+\) of the cofactors. A swap changes the sign; scaling a row by \(k\) multiplies the determinant by \(k\). Proportional rows give 0 straight away.

L4.2 Determinant rules 21 parts · 10 papers · 2–5 marks a part

Looks likeGiven \(\det A\) (and \(\det B\)), find \(\det(2AB)\), \(\det(A^TBA^{-1})\), \(\det(A^{10})\) …; or a determinant whose rows are swapped, scaled or combined.

DoSplit into factors with \(\det(AB)=\det A\det B\); each scalar \(k\) gives \(k^n\) (\(n\) = size); \(\det A^T=\det A\); \(\det A^{-1}=1/\det A\); powers give powers.

Watch\(\det(2A)=2^n\det A\), not \(2\det A\). Replacing a row by "row + multiple of another" changes nothing, but "2 × row + another" doubles it.

L4.3 Parameter that makes it singular 9 parts · 7 papers · 2–6 marks

Looks like"For what value of \(a\) are the vectors dependent / is the matrix singular / does the system have a non-trivial solution / a unique solution / is it full rank?"

DoPut the vectors (or coefficients) into a square matrix, compute \(\det\) as a polynomial in the letter, factorise, and read the answer: singular ⇔ \(\det=0\).

WatchUnique solution / invertible / independent / full rank / "no nullspace vector" means \(\det\ne0\): the answer is "all values except the roots". Don't divide by an expression in the letter; factorise.

L4.4 Inverse by Cramer's rule 2 parts · 2 papers · 5–6 marks

Looks likeA \(3\times3\) matrix with a parameter \(\beta\) and many zeros; "use Cramer's rule to derive \(A^{-1}\), check the result, state the condition on \(\beta\)".

Do\(\det A\); all nine cofactors \(C_{ij}\); \(A^{-1}=\frac1{\det A}[C_{ij}]^T\) (transpose!); check \(AA^{-1}=I\); condition \(\det A\ne0\).

WatchForgetting the transpose, and the sign \((-1)^{i+j}\) on each cofactor. The two papers use the same matrix shape: drill one and you have both.

Learn the methods first in the chapter note: cofactor expansion, expanding along the most zeros, row operations, scaling and det(kA), products and transposes, Cramer's rule for the inverse; the summary in §12 lists every rule on one screen.

How to read the working on this page: \(C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}\) is the cofactor of entry \((i,j)\), where the minor \(M_{ij}\) is the determinant left after deleting row \(i\) and column \(j\). Row operation labels name the new row first: \(R_2-2R_1\) means "replace row 2 by row 2 minus 2 times row 1" (no change in the determinant).

L4.1Evaluate a determinant

14 parts · May 2025, Dec 2024 (MC), May 2024, Dec 2023 (×2); older format Dec 2018 (×2), Dec 2016 (×3), Dec 2015, Dec 2014, Dec 2013, May 2013 · Learn it: note §3, §4, §5, §6, §7

Method
  1. Look before you compute:
    • two rows (or columns) equal or proportional, or a zero row: the determinant is 0, done;
    • a common factor in a row or column: take it out in front (one factor per row);
    • triangular: multiply the diagonal.
  2. \(2\times2\): \(ad-bc\). \(3\times3\) or larger: find the row or column with the most zeros.
  3. If no row has two zeros, make some: replace a row by "row − multiple of another row" (no change in the determinant), using a row that starts with \(\pm1\). Keep track of every swap (sign change) and every scaling.
  4. Expand along that row or column: \(\det A=\sum_j a_{ij}C_{ij}\), signs from the chessboard \(\begin{smallmatrix}+&-&+\\-&+&-\\+&-&+\end{smallmatrix}\). Repeat on the smaller determinants.
  5. With letters: factorise at every step (look for a common factor such as \(\alpha-1\) before multiplying out).
  6. For "prove that …": change the left-hand determinant with column (or row) operations, quoting the property used at each step, until it becomes the right-hand side.
Ready answer · patterns worth knowing

Triangular (all zeros below or above the diagonal): \(\det=a_{11}a_{22}\cdots a_{nn}\).

Rank one (\(\mathbf u\mathbf v^T\), or rows all multiples of one row), size \(n\ge2\): \(\det=0\).

Vandermonde:

\[ \begin{vmatrix}1&a&a^2\\1&b&b^2\\1&c&c^2\end{vmatrix}=(b-a)(c-a)(c-b) \]

and in general the product of all differences \(x_j-x_i\) with \(i\lt j\).

Quote the first two as consequences of the row properties (note §5, §6); derive the Vandermonde result if asked for it (Dec 2014 Q4(b) below).

Parts (b)–(e): L4.2 on this page. Same parts, different matrix: Dec 2016 Q4.

For matrix

\[ A=\begin{bmatrix}2&3&-4\\4&0&5\\5&1&6\end{bmatrix} \]

find the following determinants. (a) \(\det(A)\) (b) \(\det(A^T)\) (c) \(\det(A^{-1})\) (d) \(\det(AA^T)\) (e) \(\det(2A)\)

Hint

L4.1. Row 2 and column 2 each contain a 0; expand along row 2, so only two \(2\times2\) minors are needed.

Solution
  1. Row 2 is \((4,0,5)\), signs \(-\,+\,-\): \[ \begin{aligned} \det A&=-4\begin{vmatrix}3&-4\\1&6\end{vmatrix}+0\\ &\quad-5\begin{vmatrix}2&3\\5&1\end{vmatrix} \end{aligned} \]
  2. Minors: \(3\cdot6-(-4)\cdot1=22\) and \(2\cdot1-3\cdot5=-13\).
  3. So \[ \begin{aligned} \det A&=-4(22)-5(-13)\\ &=-88+65=-23 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \boxed{\det A=-23} \]

Check along column 2:

\[ \begin{aligned} &-3(24-25)+0-1(10+16)\\ &\quad=3-26=-23\ \checkmark \end{aligned} \]

May 2024 Q55 marks

Calculate the determinant of

\[ A=\begin{bmatrix}3&-4&1&-3\\-2&-2&4&1\\-3&3&2&3\\-8&8&-8&8\end{bmatrix}. \]
Hint

L4.1, \(4\times4\). Row 4 has the common factor 8. Take it out; row 4 then starts with \(-1\): move it to the top and clear column 1.

Solution
  1. Take 8 out of row 4, swap rows 1 and 4, then clear column 1 with replacements:
    \[ \begin{vmatrix}3&-4&1&-3\\-2&-2&4&1\\-3&3&2&3\\-8&8&-8&8\end{vmatrix} \]

    \(\xrightarrow{\;\tfrac18R_4\;}\)

    \[ \begin{vmatrix}3&-4&1&-3\\-2&-2&4&1\\-3&3&2&3\\-1&1&-1&1\end{vmatrix} \]

    \(\xrightarrow{\;R_1\leftrightarrow R_4\;}\)

    \[ \begin{vmatrix}-1&1&-1&1\\-2&-2&4&1\\-3&3&2&3\\3&-4&1&-3\end{vmatrix} \]

    \(\xrightarrow{\;R_2-2R_1,\ R_3-3R_1,\ R_4+3R_1\;}\)

    \[ \begin{vmatrix}-1&1&-1&1\\0&-4&6&-1\\0&0&5&0\\0&-1&-2&0\end{vmatrix} \]
  2. Bookkeeping: the scaling took a factor 8 out, the swap changed the sign, the replacements changed nothing. So \(\det A=8\cdot(-1)\cdot\det B\), where \(B\) is the last matrix.
  3. Expand \(\det B\) along column 1 (only \(-1\) at the top): \[ \det B=-1\cdot\begin{vmatrix}-4&6&-1\\0&5&0\\-1&-2&0\end{vmatrix} \]
  4. Expand the \(3\times3\) along its row 2 (only the 5): \[ 5\begin{vmatrix}-4&-1\\-1&0\end{vmatrix}=5(0-1)=-5 \] so \(\det B=(-1)(-5)=5\).
  5. \(\det A=8\cdot(-1)\cdot5=-40\).

Marker: write down the factor for each operation (8 for the scaling, \(-1\) for the swap) as you go; most lost marks on this question are a dropped sign from the swap.

\[ \boxed{\det A=-40} \]

Dec 2023 Q2(a), (c)3 + 4 points

Part (b) (\(|A-I|=|A-2I|=|A-3I|=0\)): L5.3 in the L5 guide.

(a) Compute the determinant of

\[ \begin{bmatrix}a&1&0&0\\-1&b&1&0\\0&-1&c&1\\0&0&-1&d\end{bmatrix}. \]

(c) Let

\[ A=\begin{bmatrix}1&\sin\theta&1\\-\sin\theta&1&\sin\theta\\-1&-\sin\theta&1\end{bmatrix}, \]

where \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\). What is the range of the determinant of \(A\)?

Hint

L4.1 with letters. (a) Expand along row 1 (two zeros); each \(3\times3\) left over has a zero to use. (c) Write \(s=\sin\theta\), expand along row 1; one minor turns out to be 0.

Solution
  1. (a) Expand along row 1: \(a\,M_{11}-1\cdot M_{12}\), where \[ \begin{aligned} M_{11}&=\begin{vmatrix}b&1&0\\-1&c&1\\0&-1&d\end{vmatrix}\\ M_{12}&=\begin{vmatrix}-1&1&0\\0&c&1\\0&-1&d\end{vmatrix} \end{aligned} \]
  2. \(M_{11}\) along row 1: \[ \begin{aligned} &b(cd+1)-1(-d-0)\\ &\quad=bcd+b+d \end{aligned} \]\(M_{12}\) along column 1: \(-1\cdot(cd+1)\).
  3. So \[ \begin{aligned} \det&=a(bcd+b+d)+(cd+1)\\ &=abcd+ab+ad+cd+1 \end{aligned} \]
  4. (c) With \(s=\sin\theta\), expand along row 1: \[ \begin{aligned} \det A&=1\begin{vmatrix}1&s\\-s&1\end{vmatrix}-s\begin{vmatrix}-s&s\\-1&1\end{vmatrix}\\ &\quad+1\begin{vmatrix}-s&1\\-1&-s\end{vmatrix} \end{aligned} \]
  5. The minors are \(1+s^2\), \(-s+s=0\) and \(s^2+1\), so \(\det A=2+2\sin^2\theta\).
  6. For \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\), \(\sin^2\theta\) takes every value in \([0,1]\) (0 at \(\theta=0\), 1 at \(\theta=\frac\pi2\)), so \(\det A\) takes every value in \([2,4]\).

Marker: in (c) say why both ends are reached (name a \(\theta\) for each); "range" means the set of values, not just max and min.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{(a) }abcd+ab+ad+cd+1\\ \text{(c) }2\le\det A\le4\end{gathered}} \]

Check (a) with \(a=b=c=d=0\): the formula gives 1, and expanding the zero-diagonal matrix along row 1 gives \(-1\cdot(-1)\cdot(0\cdot0+1)=1\ \checkmark\)

Dec 2018 Q2(b), (c)3 + 5 marksolder format

Part (a) (true or false on independence): L2.3 in the L2 guide.

(b) Using \(|\circ|\) to denote determinant, find

\[ \left|\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4&5&6\end{bmatrix}\right|. \]

(c) In below, the \(a_i\)'s, \(b_j\)'s and \(c_k\)'s are real scalars. Prove that

\[ \begin{aligned} &\begin{vmatrix}a_1+c_1&b_1+a_1&c_1+b_1\\a_2+c_2&b_2+a_2&c_2+b_2\\a_3+c_3&b_3+a_3&c_3+b_3\end{vmatrix}\\ &\quad=2\begin{vmatrix}a_1&b_1&c_1\\a_2&b_2&c_2\\a_3&b_3&c_3\end{vmatrix} \end{aligned} \]
Hint

L4.1. (b) Multiply out: what do the rows of the product look like? (c) Call the columns \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf c\); the left side has columns \(\mathbf a+\mathbf c,\ \mathbf b+\mathbf a,\ \mathbf c+\mathbf b\). Replace column 1 by \(C_1-C_2+C_3\).

Solution
  1. (b) The product is \[ \begin{bmatrix}4&5&6\\8&10&12\\12&15&18\end{bmatrix} \] Row 2 = 2 × row 1 and row 3 = 3 × row 1. \(R_2-2R_1\) (no change) gives a zero row, so the determinant is 0.
  2. (c) Write \(D=\det[\,\mathbf a+\mathbf c\ \ \mathbf b+\mathbf a\ \ \mathbf c+\mathbf b\,]\) (columns). Adding multiples of other columns to a column does not change a determinant (\(\det A^T=\det A\), so column operations behave like row operations).
  3. \(C_1\to C_1-C_2+C_3\): \((\mathbf a+\mathbf c)-(\mathbf b+\mathbf a)+(\mathbf c+\mathbf b)=2\mathbf c\). Take the factor 2 out of column 1: \[ D=2\det[\,\mathbf c\ \ \mathbf b+\mathbf a\ \ \mathbf c+\mathbf b\,] \]
  4. \(C_3\to C_3-C_1\) gives \(\mathbf b\); then \(C_2\to C_2-C_3\) gives \(\mathbf a\): \[ D=2\det[\,\mathbf c\ \ \mathbf a\ \ \mathbf b\,] \]
  5. Swap columns 1 and 2, then columns 2 and 3: \([\mathbf c\ \mathbf a\ \mathbf b]\to[\mathbf a\ \mathbf c\ \mathbf b]\to[\mathbf a\ \mathbf b\ \mathbf c]\). Two swaps: the sign changes twice, so \[ D=2\det[\,\mathbf a\ \ \mathbf b\ \ \mathbf c\,] \] which is the right-hand side.

Marker: in (c) name the property behind every step (replacement: no change; factor out of a column; each swap: sign change). A proof by expanding all 27 terms is accepted but slow and error-prone.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{(b) }0\\ \text{(c) proved: }D=2\det[\mathbf a\ \mathbf b\ \mathbf c]\end{gathered}} \]

(b) in one line: any \(\mathbf u\mathbf v^T\) has rank 1, so for a \(3\times3\) it is singular.

Dec 2016 Q3(a), (b)6 pointsolder format

Find the determinants of the following matrices

\[ \text{(a)}\ \begin{bmatrix}2^1&2^2&2^3&2^4\\2^2&2^3&2^4&2^5\\2^3&2^4&2^5&2^6\\2^4&2^5&2^6&2^7\end{bmatrix} \] \[ \text{(b)}\ \begin{bmatrix}2^1&2^2&2^3&2^4\\2^2&2^4&2^6&2^8\\2^3&2^6&2^9&2^{12}\\2^4&2^8&2^{12}&2^{16}\end{bmatrix} \]

You do not need to compute the determinants explicitly and may leave the answer(s) as a product of numbers if you find it more convenient.

Hint

L4.1, patterns. (a) Compare row 2 with row 1. (b) Entry \((i,j)\) is \(2^{ij}=(2^i)^j\): take \(2^i\) out of row \(i\); what is left is a Vandermonde matrix.

Solution
  1. (a) Each row is 2 × the row above (row \(i\) = \(2^{i-1}\) × row 1). Proportional rows: \(R_2-2R_1\) gives a zero row, so the determinant is 0.
  2. (b) Entry \((i,j)\) is \(2^{ij}\). Take \(2^i\) out of row \(i\) (\(i=1,\dots,4\)), a factor \(2^{1+2+3+4}=2^{10}\) in total. With \(x_i=2^i\), what remains has rows \((1,\ x_i,\ x_i^2,\ x_i^3)\): \[ \begin{vmatrix}1&2&4&8\\1&4&16&64\\1&8&64&512\\1&16&256&4096\end{vmatrix} \]
  3. This is a Vandermonde determinant in \(x_1,\dots,x_4=2,4,8,16\), equal to the product of all differences \(x_j-x_i\) (\(i\lt j\)): \[ \begin{aligned} &(4-2)(8-2)(16-2)\\ &\quad\times(8-4)(16-4)(16-8)\\ &=2\cdot6\cdot14\cdot4\cdot12\cdot8 \end{aligned} \]
  4. So \[ \begin{aligned} \det&=2^{10}\cdot2\cdot6\cdot14\cdot4\cdot12\cdot8\\ &=2^{20}\cdot63 \end{aligned} \]

Marker: in (b) show where the factor \(2^{10}\) comes from and name the Vandermonde pattern; the question allows the answer as a product.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{(a) }0\\ \text{(b) }2^{20}\cdot63=66\,060\,288\end{gathered}} \]

Other forms of (b): \(2^{10}\cdot2\cdot6\cdot14\cdot4\cdot12\cdot8=2^{20}\cdot3^2\cdot7\).

Method
  1. Write the size \(n\) of the matrices next to the question; you need it for every scalar.
  2. Break the expression into a product of single factors: \[ \begin{aligned} &\det(k\,A^TB^{-1}A\cdots)\\ &=k^n\det(A^T)\det(B^{-1})\det(A)\cdots \end{aligned} \]
  3. Replace each factor with the Ready answer below; collect and cancel.
  4. If only a relation is given (\(A^3=B^2\), \(B^2+I=0\), \(AA^{-1}=I\)), take determinants of both sides first.
  5. For a determinant with rearranged rows, name the operation that turns the given determinant into the new one, row by row, and multiply the factors.
Ready answer · the rules (A, B are n × n)
ExpressionDeterminant
\(kA\)\(k^n\det A\)
\(AB\)\(\det A\det B\)
\(A^T\)\(\det A\)
\(A^{-1}\)\(1/\det A\)
\(A^m\)\((\det A)^m\)
\(I_n\)\(1\)

Row operations: swap two rows: \(\times(-1)\); multiply one row by \(k\): \(\times k\); add a multiple of another row to a row: no change. The same for columns.

Not a rule: \(\det(A+B)\ne\det A+\det B\) in general.

(b) Hence or otherwise, prove that if the matrix \(B\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) satisfies \(B^2+I_n=0\), then \(n\) must be even.

Hint

L4.2. (a) \(I_n\) is triangular. (b) Write \(B^2=-I_n\) and take determinants; the left side is a square of a real number.

Solution
  1. (a) \(I_n\) is diagonal (triangular), so its determinant is the product of the diagonal entries: \(1\cdot1\cdots1=1\).
  2. (b) \(B^2=-I_n\). Take determinants: \[ \begin{aligned} (\det B)^2&=\det(-I_n)\\ &=(-1)^n\det I_n=(-1)^n \end{aligned} \] using \(\det(kA)=k^n\det A\) and (a).

  4. \((-1)^n=1\) exactly when \(n\) is even.

Marker: say where "real" is used: for a complex matrix \((\det B)^2=-1\) is possible (e.g. \(B=iI_1\)).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{(a) }\det I_n=1\\ \text{(b) }(\det B)^2=(-1)^n\ge0\\ \Rightarrow n\text{ even}\end{gathered}} \]

L4.3Parameter that makes a matrix singular

9 parts · May 2026, Dec 2025 (×3, two MC), Dec 2024 (MC), May 2024, Dec 2022, Dec 2021; older format May 2019 · Learn it: note §7 (Beyond box: det A ≠ 0 exactly when A is invertible), §12 Summary

Method
  1. Build the square matrix: the given vectors as its columns, or the coefficients of the system (the right-hand side plays no part).
  2. Translate the question with the Ready answer below: is it asking for \(\det=0\) or for \(\det\ne0\)?
  3. Compute \(\det\) as a polynomial in the letter: expand along the row or column with the most zeros or with the letter alone; for symmetric patterns (Dec 2025 Q12) keep common factors such as \((\alpha-1)\) unexpanded.
  4. Factorise and solve \(\det=0\).
  5. Answer the question asked: "\(\det=0\)" questions → the roots; "\(\det\ne0\)" questions → every real number except the roots. For MC, test each option in the factorised determinant.
Ready answer · what the question means (A is n × n)
The question saysCondition
columns / vectors linearly dependent; \(A\) singular, rank-deficient, not invertible; \(A\mathbf x=\mathbf 0\) has a non-trivial (non-zero) solution; a non-zero nullspace vector exists\(\det A=0\)
independent; invertible, non-singular, full rank; \(A\mathbf x=\mathbf b\) has a unique solution; only \(\mathbf x=\mathbf 0\) in the nullspace\(\det A\ne0\)

Only for square \(A\). For \(n\) vectors in \(\mathbb R^n\) put them as the columns (or rows: \(\det A^T=\det A\)).

L4.4Inverse by Cramer's rule

2 parts · May 2023; older format May 2019 · Learn it: note §9, §10, §11 Example: a 3 × 3 inverse by cofactors

Method
  1. Compute \(\det A\) (expand along the row or column with the most zeros) and factorise it. The condition for \(A^{-1}\) to exist is \(\det A\ne0\).
  2. Explain the link with Cramer's rule in one line: column \(j\) of \(A^{-1}\) solves \(A\mathbf x=\mathbf e_j\), so by Cramer's rule \[ (A^{-1})_{ij}=\frac{\det A_i(\mathbf e_j)}{\det A}=\frac{C_{ji}}{\det A} \]
  3. Compute all nine cofactors \(C_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}\). A zero-heavy row or column makes many of them 0.
  4. Write the cofactor matrix \([C_{ij}]\), transpose it (the adjugate), divide by \(\det A\).
  5. Check: multiply \(A\) by the adjugate; you must get \((\det A)I\).
Ready answer · the exam's matrix shape

Both papers use \(A=\begin{bmatrix}p&0&q\\0&1&0\\r&0&s\end{bmatrix}\). Then \(\det A=ps-qr\) and

\[ A^{-1}=\frac1{ps-qr}\begin{bmatrix}s&0&-q\\0&ps-qr&0\\-r&0&p\end{bmatrix} \]

The corner entries behave like the \(2\times2\) inverse formula; the middle 1 stays as \(\det A/\det A=1\). Use this as a check, not as the exam working: the question asks you to derive it with cofactors.

Matrix

\[ A=\begin{bmatrix}4\beta&0&-5\\0&1&0\\-3&0&\beta\end{bmatrix}. \]

Use Cramer's rule to derive the inverse matrix \(A^{-1}\), then check the result and define the condition of the unspecified real number \(\beta\) for \(A^{-1}\) to exist.

Hint

L4.4. \(\det A\) along row 2. Of the nine cofactors, four are 0 because of the zeros in row 2 and column 2.

Solution
  1. Along row 2: \[ \det A=1\cdot\begin{vmatrix}4\beta&-5\\-3&\beta\end{vmatrix}=4\beta^2-15 \]
  2. By Cramer's rule, column \(j\) of \(A^{-1}\) solves \(A\mathbf x=\mathbf e_j\), giving \((A^{-1})_{ij}=C_{ji}/\det A\).
  3. Cofactors, row by row: \[ \begin{aligned} C_{11}&=\begin{vmatrix}1&0\\0&\beta\end{vmatrix}=\beta\\ C_{12}&=-\begin{vmatrix}0&0\\-3&\beta\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{13}&=\begin{vmatrix}0&1\\-3&0\end{vmatrix}=3 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} C_{21}&=-\begin{vmatrix}0&-5\\0&\beta\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{22}&=\begin{vmatrix}4\beta&-5\\-3&\beta\end{vmatrix}=4\beta^2-15\\ C_{23}&=-\begin{vmatrix}4\beta&0\\-3&0\end{vmatrix}=0 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} C_{31}&=\begin{vmatrix}0&-5\\1&0\end{vmatrix}=5\\ C_{32}&=-\begin{vmatrix}4\beta&-5\\0&0\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{33}&=\begin{vmatrix}4\beta&0\\0&1\end{vmatrix}=4\beta \end{aligned} \]
  4. Transpose the cofactor matrix and divide by \(\det A=D\), where \(D=4\beta^2-15\): \[ A^{-1}=\frac1D\begin{bmatrix}\beta&0&5\\0&D&0\\3&0&4\beta\end{bmatrix} \]
  5. Check, \(A\) times the adjugate: row 1 · column 1 \(=4\beta^2-15\); row 1 · column 3 \(=20\beta-20\beta=0\); row 3 · column 1 \(=-3\beta+3\beta=0\); row 3 · column 3 \(=-15+4\beta^2\); row 2 gives \((0,\,4\beta^2-15,\,0)\). So \(A\cdot\operatorname{adj}A=(4\beta^2-15)I\) and \(AA^{-1}=I\ \checkmark\)
  6. \(A^{-1}\) exists ⇔ \(\det A=4\beta^2-15\ne0\) ⇔ \(\beta^2\ne\frac{15}4\).

Marker: show the nine cofactors (zeros included), the transpose step, the check, and the condition; each is asked for.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}A^{-1}=\frac1D\begin{bmatrix}\beta&0&5\\0&D&0\\3&0&4\beta\end{bmatrix},\\ D=4\beta^2-15;\\ \text{exists iff }\beta\ne\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{15}}2\end{gathered}} \]

The middle entry simplifies to 1. Ready-answer check: \(p=4\beta,\ q=-5,\ r=-3,\ s=\beta\) gives corners \(s=\beta\), \(-q=5\), \(-r=3\), \(p=4\beta\) \(\checkmark\)

  2. Cofactors: \[ \begin{aligned} C_{11}&=\begin{vmatrix}1&0\\0&\beta\end{vmatrix}=\beta\\ C_{12}&=-\begin{vmatrix}0&0\\-6&\beta\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{13}&=\begin{vmatrix}0&1\\-6&0\end{vmatrix}=6 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} C_{21}&=-\begin{vmatrix}0&-2\\0&\beta\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{22}&=\begin{vmatrix}3\beta&-2\\-6&\beta\end{vmatrix}=3\beta^2-12\\ C_{23}&=-\begin{vmatrix}3\beta&0\\-6&0\end{vmatrix}=0 \end{aligned} \] \[ \begin{aligned} C_{31}&=\begin{vmatrix}0&-2\\1&0\end{vmatrix}=2\\ C_{32}&=-\begin{vmatrix}3\beta&-2\\0&0\end{vmatrix}=0\\ C_{33}&=\begin{vmatrix}3\beta&0\\0&1\end{vmatrix}=3\beta \end{aligned} \]
