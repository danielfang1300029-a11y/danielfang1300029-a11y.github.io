Exam guide: Complex Variables

P1At a glance

This chapter is very predictable. Every one of the 23 papers has a complex-numbers question, nearly always about 10 marks in four short parts, and the parts come from six types that repeat almost word for word: solve a quadratic, find a modulus or a big power, find n-th roots, describe a locus, find the image of a set, and (rarely) a short proof. The skill is converting between \(a+bi\) and \(re^{i\theta}\) quickly and without slips. Learn the six cards below, then work through the questions.

Exam format
  • No calculators since Dec 2022. Every answer here uses exact values: \(\cos\frac\pi6=\frac{\sqrt3}2\), \(\cos\frac\pi4=\frac{\sqrt2}2\), \(\cos\frac\pi3=\frac12\) and their multiples. Leave roots such as \(e^{i3\pi/10}\) in polar form; nobody expects \(\cos\frac{3\pi}{10}\) as a decimal.
  • No formula sheet. Know by heart: \(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\), De Moivre, the n-th root formula, \(1+w+\dots+w^{n-1}=0\) for \(w^n=1,\ w\ne1\), and the images of circles and lines under \(1/z\).
  • Multiple choice and answer-only parts. Dec 2025: Q16 and Q17 are 2-mark MC with four options, and Q26 is a 10-mark free-response question. May 2026: Q7 is ten answer-only short parts (marks per part not printed, about 2 each), three of them complex numbers. No working is marked there, so test your answer at a simple value (\(\theta=0\), \(w=1\)).
  • Time. 3 hours for 100 marks is about 1.8 minutes per mark: a 2-mark modulus in 3–4 minutes, a 3-mark image of a set in about 5 minutes, a sketch in 2 more.

The six cards

P1.1 Arithmetic and equations 9 parts · 9 papers · 2 marks where printed

Looks like"Find all complex numbers \(z\) satisfying \(z^2+4z+8=0\)"; "find real \(x,y\) with \(\frac{x}{1+i}+\frac{y}{2-i}=2+4i\)".

DoQuadratic formula (or complete the square) with \(\sqrt{-k}=i\sqrt k\). For real unknowns, clear the denominators and compare real and imaginary parts.

WatchNon-real coefficients (Dec 2025, May 2026): the roots are not conjugates, and you need the square root of a complex number.

P1.2 Modulus, polar form, De Moivre 22 parts · 15 papers · 2–3 marks where printed

Looks like\(|(1+i)^{-10}|\); "polar form of \(-1-\sqrt3i\)"; \((1+\sqrt3i)^{100}\); \((\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)^4\); "show \(\cos3x=4\cos^3x-3\cos x\)".

DoModulus: \(|zw|=|z||w|\), never expand. Powers: polar form, De Moivre, reduce the angle mod \(2\pi\), back to \(a+bi\).

Watch\(\sin\theta+i\cos\theta=e^{i(\pi/2-\theta)}\), not \(e^{i\theta}\). Find the angle from the quadrant, not from \(\tan^{-1}\) alone.

P1.3 n-th roots and roots of unity 18 parts · 15 papers · 2–5 marks

Looks like"Find all \(z\) with \(z^5=1+i\)"; "primitive 4th roots of unity"; "\(w\) a root of \(z^3=1\); find \(\frac1w+\frac1{w^2}\)".

Do\(z^n=Re^{i\alpha}\Rightarrow z=R^{1/n}e^{i(\alpha+2k\pi)/n}\), \(k=0,\dots,n-1\). For expressions in \(w\): reduce powers mod \(n\), then use \(1+w+\dots+w^{n-1}=0\).

WatchIf the question does not say \(w\ne1\), do the case \(w=1\) separately. Primitive means \(e^{2\pi ik/n}\) with \(\gcd(k,n)=1\).

P1.4 Loci 10 parts · 10 papers · 2–5 marks

Looks like\(|z-i|=|z+2|\); \(\left|\frac{z-1}{z+2}\right|=2\); \(\operatorname{Im}(z^2)=0\); \(|z-i|=|z|+1\).

DoPut \(z=x+yi\), square the moduli, simplify. Equal distances: a line (perpendicular bisector). Ratio \(\ne1\): a circle; complete the square.

WatchName the set (line, circle with centre and radius, finite set of points) and remove points where a denominator is 0.

P1.5 Image of a set under a map 17 parts · 15 papers · 3–5 marks

Looks like"Let \(D=\{z:|z|\gt2\}\) and \(f(z)=1/z\). Find \(f(D)\) and sketch it." Maps: \(1/z\), \(1/z^2\), \(z^2\), \(z^4\), \(iz\), \(2z+3\), \(iz+1\).

DoDiscs, sectors, circles centred at 0: polar, \(z=re^{i\theta}\). Lines and other circles: Cartesian, solve \(w=f(z)\) for \(z\) and substitute.

Watch\(1/z\) never gives 0, and is undefined at \(z=0\). In sketches, dashed edges and open dots for what is not included.

P1.6 Inequalities and proofs 3 parts · 3 papers · up to 5 marks

Looks like"Prove \(|z+w|\le|z|+|w|\)"; "show \(\sqrt2|z|\ge|\operatorname{Re}z|+|\operatorname{Im}\bar z|\)"; \(\cosh^2x-\sinh^2x\).

DoBoth sides \(\ge0\), so square; reduce to a square \(\ge0\). Hyperbolic: write in \(e^{x}\), \(e^{-x}\).

WatchSay why squaring keeps the inequality (both sides non-negative).

P1.1Complex arithmetic and solving equations

9 parts · May 2026, Dec 2025, Dec 2023, Dec 2022; older format Dec 2018, May 2017, May 2016, Dec 2015, May 2015 · Learn it: note §2, §5, §15, §17

Method
  1. Quadratic \(az^2+bz+c=0\). Use \(z=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\), or complete the square. If \(b^2-4ac=-k\lt0\) is real, write \(\sqrt{-k}=i\sqrt k\).
  2. If a coefficient is not real, the discriminant \(\Delta\) is complex. Find its square roots \(\pm(p+qi)\) by solving \((p+qi)^2=\Delta\): compare real and imaginary parts (\(p^2-q^2=\operatorname{Re}\Delta\), \(2pq=\operatorname{Im}\Delta\)) and guess small whole numbers first.
  3. Real unknowns in one complex equation. Clear each denominator by multiplying top and bottom by its conjugate, collect real and imaginary parts, and set them equal: two real equations for two unknowns.
  4. Write every answer as \(a+bi\) and check: the two roots of \(z^2+bz+c\) add to \(-b\) and multiply to \(c\).
Ready answer · real coefficients

For \(z^2+bz+c=0\) with \(b^2\lt4c\):

\[ z=-\frac b2\pm\frac{\sqrt{4c-b^2}}{2}\,i, \]

a pair of conjugates, both with modulus \(\sqrt c\).

Question 7 contains short questions; provide answers only. (g) Solve \(x^2+2ix+1=0\).

Hint

P1.1 with a non-real coefficient. Use the quadratic formula with \(a=1\), \(b=2i\), \(c=1\); the discriminant turns out to be a negative real number.

Solution
  1. Discriminant: \[ b^2-4ac=(2i)^2-4=-8, \] and \(\sqrt{-8}=2\sqrt2\,i\).
  2. \(x=\dfrac{-2i\pm2\sqrt2\,i}{2}=-i\pm\sqrt2\,i\).
  3. Check the sum and product: \(x_1+x_2=-2i=-b\) and \[ \begin{aligned} x_1x_2&=(-i)^2-(\sqrt2i)^2\\ &=-1+2=1=c\ \checkmark \end{aligned} \]

Marker: answer only, so write both roots clearly in \(a+bi\) form. They are not conjugates; that is expected, because \(b=2i\) is not real.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}x=(\sqrt2-1)i\ \text{ or}\\ x=-(\sqrt2+1)i\end{gathered}} \]

Dec 2025 Q26(b)part of 10 marks

Parts (a) P1.3, (c) and (d) P1.5 on this page.

State all complex solutions in the form \(a+bi\). (b) Find all complex solutions \(z\) to the equation \(z^2+2z+(1+2i)=0\).

Hint

P1.1 with a complex constant term. Complete the square: \(z^2+2z+1=(z+1)^2\). Then you need the two square roots of a complex number.

Solution
  1. Complete the square: \(z^2+2z+1=-2i\), so \((z+1)^2=-2i\).
  2. Square roots of \(-2i\): solve \((p+qi)^2=-2i\) with \(p,q\) real. \[ \begin{aligned} p^2-q^2&=0,\\ 2pq&=-2. \end{aligned} \] So \(q=-p\) (since \(q=p\) would give \(pq\gt0\)) and \(p^2=1\): \(p+qi=\pm(1-i)\).
  3. Hence \(z+1=1-i\) or \(z+1=-1+i\).
  4. Check: sum \(-i+(-2+i)=-2\ \checkmark\); product \((-i)(-2+i)=2i+1\ \checkmark\)

Marker: show how you found the square root of \(-2i\) (comparing parts, or \(-2i=2e^{-i\pi/2}\), whose square roots are \(\pm\sqrt2e^{-i\pi/4}\)).

\[ \boxed{z=-i\ \text{ or }\ z=-2+i} \]

Find \(x,y\in\mathbb R\) such that

\[ \frac{x}{1+i}+\frac{y}{2-i}=2+4i. \]

P1.2Modulus, polar form, De Moivre, powers

22 parts · May 2026 (×2), Dec 2025, Dec 2023 (×2), Dec 2022 (×2), Dec 2021 (×2); older format May 2019, May 2018, Dec 2017, Dec 2016, May 2016, May 2015, Dec 2014, May 2014 (×2), Dec 2013, Dec 2012 (×3) · Learn it: note §3, §4, §8, §9, §10

Method
  1. Modulus of a product, quotient or power: never multiply out. Use \(|zw|=|z||w|\), \(\left|\frac zw\right|=\frac{|z|}{|w|}\), \(|z^n|=|z|^n\) and \(|a+bi|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\).
  2. Polar form of \(a+bi\): \(r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\). Plot the point to see its quadrant; take the reference angle \(\alpha\) from \(\tan\alpha=\left|\frac ba\right|\); then \(\theta=\alpha,\ \pi-\alpha,\ -(\pi-\alpha),\ -\alpha\) in quadrants I, II, III, IV. Write \(z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=re^{i\theta}\).
  3. A big power \((a+bi)^n\): polar form first, then De Moivre \((re^{i\theta})^n=r^ne^{in\theta}\). Reduce \(n\theta\) by multiples of \(2\pi\), then convert back to \(a+bi\). For a quotient such as \(\frac{2}{\sqrt3-i}\), simplify it before the power.
  4. \(\sin\) and \(\cos\) swapped: rewrite as \(\cos(\cdot)+i\sin(\cdot)\) first (Ready answer). Test the final answer at \(\theta=0\).
  5. Multiple-angle identity (\(\cos3x\) in powers of \(\cos x\)): expand \((\cos x+i\sin x)^3\) by the binomial theorem, compare with \(\cos3x+i\sin3x\) (De Moivre), and replace \(\sin^2x\) by \(1-\cos^2x\).
Ready answer · polar forms that keep coming back \[ \begin{aligned} 1+i&=\sqrt2\,e^{i\pi/4},\\ 1-i&=\sqrt2\,e^{-i\pi/4},\\ 1+\sqrt3i&=2e^{i\pi/3},\\ \sqrt3-i&=2e^{-i\pi/6},\\ -1-\sqrt3i&=2e^{-2i\pi/3},\\ -i&=e^{-i\pi/2}. \end{aligned} \]

Swapped sine and cosine (\(\theta\) real):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin\theta+i\cos\theta&=e^{i(\pi/2-\theta)},\\ \sin\theta-i\cos\theta&=e^{i(\theta-\pi/2)},\\ \cos\theta-i\sin\theta&=e^{-i\theta}. \end{aligned} \]

Useful reductions: \(e^{2k\pi i}=1\), \(e^{i\pi}=-1\), \(i^4=1\), \((1+i)^2=2i\).

(Multiple choice question) Among all the complex numbers below, which one is equal to \((\sin\theta-i\cos\theta)^2\), where \(\theta\) is a real number?

(A) \(\sin2\theta-i\cos2\theta\)   (B) \(-\cos2\theta-i\sin2\theta\)   (C) \(\cos2\theta-i\sin2\theta\)   (D) \(-\cos2\theta+i\sin2\theta\)

\[ \boxed{\text{B}} \]

Dec 2023 Q5(c)3 points

Write \(\sin\theta+i\cos\theta\) in Euler's form \(re^{i\theta}\) where \(r\) is the modulus and \(\theta\) is the argument. Then simplify \((\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)^{2023}\).

Hint

P1.2. \(\sin\theta=\cos(\frac\pi2-\theta)\) and \(\cos\theta=\sin(\frac\pi2-\theta)\). Then De Moivre, and reduce \(\frac{2023\pi}2\) by multiples of \(2\pi\).

Solution
  1. Modulus \(\sqrt{\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta}=1\), and \[ \begin{aligned} &\sin\theta+i\cos\theta\\ &=\cos(\tfrac\pi2-\theta)+i\sin(\tfrac\pi2-\theta)\\ &=e^{i(\pi/2-\theta)}. \end{aligned} \] So \(r=1\) and the argument is \(\frac\pi2-\theta\).
  2. De Moivre: \[ \begin{aligned} &(\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)^{2023}\\ &=e^{i(2023\pi/2-2023\theta)}. \end{aligned} \]
  3. \(\frac{2023\pi}2=1010\pi+\frac{3\pi}2\) and \(1010\pi\) is a multiple of \(2\pi\), so \(e^{i2023\pi/2}=e^{i3\pi/2}=-i\).
  4. Hence the power is \[ \begin{aligned} &-i\,e^{-2023i\theta}\\ &=-i(\cos2023\theta-i\sin2023\theta)\\ &=-\sin2023\theta-i\cos2023\theta. \end{aligned} \]
  5. Check at \(\theta=0\): \(i^{2023}=i^{3}=-i\ \checkmark\)

Marker: the printed \(re^{i\theta}\) reuses the letter \(\theta\); write the argument as \(\frac\pi2-\theta\) so that it is clear it is not \(\theta\).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered} \sin\theta+i\cos\theta=e^{i(\pi/2-\theta)},\\ (\sin\theta+i\cos\theta)^{2023}\\ =-\sin2023\theta-i\cos2023\theta \end{gathered}} \]

Equivalent: \(e^{i(3\pi/2-2023\theta)}\).

Method
  1. All solutions of \(z^n=w\): write \(w=Re^{i\alpha}\). Then \[ \begin{gathered} z=R^{1/n}e^{i(\alpha+2k\pi)/n},\\ k=0,1,\dots,n-1. \end{gathered} \] Add \(2k\pi\) before dividing by \(n\). P1.5, Cartesian. Compute \(i(x+yi)+1\) and read off \(u\), \(v\) in terms of \(x\), \(y\).

Solution
  1. Compute \[ \begin{aligned} f(x+yi)&=ix-y+1\\ &=(1-y)+xi, \end{aligned} \] so \(u=1-y\), \(v=x\).
  2. Then \(x=v\), \(y=1-u\), and \(x+y=1\) becomes \(v+1-u=1\): \(v=u\).
  3. Every point \(u+ui\) is reached (take \(x=u\), \(y=1-u\)).
  4. Check: \(z=1\) gives \(1+i\), \(z=i\) gives \(0\); both on \(v=u\ \checkmark\)
\[ \boxed{f(D)=\{u+ui: u\in\mathbb R\}} \]

The line \(\operatorname{Im}w=\operatorname{Re}w\) (rotation by \(90^\circ\) about 0, then a shift by 1).

Let \(D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-i|=1\}\) and \(f:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C\) be defined as \(f(z)=iz\). Find \(f(D)\).

Hint

P1.5. \(f\) rotates by \(\frac\pi2\) about 0, so the circle stays a circle with the same radius. Where does the centre go? Or solve \(z=-iw\) and substitute.

Solution
  1. \(w=iz\iff z=\frac wi=-iw\).
  2. Substitute: \[ \begin{aligned} &|-iw-i|=1\\ &\iff|-i|\,|w+1|=1\\ &\iff|w+1|=1. \end{aligned} \]
  3. Every such \(w\) is reached (\(f\) is invertible).
\[ \boxed{f(D)=\{w: |w+1|=1\}} \]

The circle with centre \(-1\) (\(=i\cdot i\)) and radius 1.

P1.6Inequalities and proofs

3 parts · May 2025; older format May 2017, Dec 2012 · Learn it: note §6 (triangle inequality), §11 (hyperbolic functions)

Decision procedure
  1. An inequality between moduli (\(|z+w|\le|z|+|w|\), \(\sqrt2|z|\ge\dots\)): both sides are \(\ge0\), so it is equivalent to the inequality between the squares. Say this in one sentence.
  2. Square, using \(|z|^2=z\bar z=a^2+b^2\). Cancel what appears on both sides.
  3. What is left should be a square \(\ge0\) (like \((a-b)^2\ge0\)) or a known fact such as \(\operatorname{Re}u\le|u|\). If a square root is still there, isolate it; if the other side can be negative, that case is immediate; otherwise square again.
  4. Write the proof forwards or as a chain of "\(\iff\)", ending at a true statement.
  5. Hyperbolic functions: substitute \(\cosh x=\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}2\), \(\sinh x=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}2\) and expand.

Recognition cue: "prove" or "show that" with \(|\cdot|\) on both sides. Trap: squaring \(A\le B\) is only safe when \(A,B\ge0\).

May 2025 Q7(a)5 points

Part (b): P1.5 on this page.

Suppose \(z,w\in\mathbb C\), prove that

\[ |z+w|\le|z|+|w| \]

where \(|\cdot|\) is the modulus.

Hint

P1.6. Both sides are \(\ge0\): compare squares. \(|z+w|^2=(z+w)(\bar z+\bar w)\). You need \(\operatorname{Re}(z\bar w)\le|z\bar w|\).

Solution
  1. Both sides are non-negative real numbers, so the inequality holds if and only if it holds after squaring.
  2. Left side squared, using \(|u|^2=u\bar u\): \[ \begin{aligned} &|z+w|^2\\ &=(z+w)(\bar z+\bar w)\\ &=|z|^2+z\bar w\\ &\quad+\bar zw+|w|^2\\ &=|z|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(z\bar w)+|w|^2, \end{aligned} \] because \(\bar zw=\overline{z\bar w}\) and \(u+\bar u=2\operatorname{Re}u\).
  3. Right side squared: \[ \begin{aligned} &(|z|+|w|)^2\\ &=|z|^2+2|z||w|+|w|^2. \end{aligned} \]
  4. For any \(u=p+qi\): \(\operatorname{Re}u=p\le\sqrt{p^2+q^2}=|u|\). With \(u=z\bar w\): \[ \operatorname{Re}(z\bar w)\le|z\bar w|=|z||\bar w|=|z||w|. \]
  5. Hence \(|z+w|^2\le(|z|+|w|)^2\), and taking non-negative square roots, \(|z+w|\le|z|+|w|\). \(\blacksquare\)

Marker: show the reason for each step: why squaring is allowed, why \(\operatorname{Re}u\le|u|\), and \(|\bar w|=|w|\).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}|z+w|^2\le(|z|+|w|)^2\\ \Rightarrow\ |z+w|\le|z|+|w|\end{gathered}} \]

Slide 21 route: with \(z=a+bi\), \(w=c+di\), the inequality reduces to \(ac+bd\le\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\sqrt{c^2+d^2}\); if \(ac+bd\lt0\) it is immediate, otherwise square once more to get \((ad-bc)^2\ge0\).

Let \(z\) be a complex number, show that

\[ \sqrt2|z|\ge|\operatorname{Re}(z)|+|\operatorname{Im}(\bar z)|. \]
Hint

Solution

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}2|z|^2-(|x|+|y|)^2\\ =(|x|-|y|)^2\ge0\end{gathered}} \]
