Exam guide: Some Elementary Statistical Inferences

What the exam asks about chapter P4 and how to answer it: the three question types, a method card for each, one ready-made answer for every confidence interval the papers ask for, the critical values you read off the Φ table, and every past-paper question from Dec 2012 to May 2026 with a hint and a full solution. The ideas are taught in the P4 note; this page is for practice.

P4At a glance

This chapter is the most predictable in the course. Almost every Part II paper ends with a confidence interval, and there are only three shapes: a mean with \(\sigma\) known, a mean with \(\sigma\) replaced by \(s\), and a proportion. The method never changes: find \(z_{\alpha/2}\) in the \(\Phi\) table, compute the standard error, write centre \(\pm\) margin. Marks are lost on the critical value, on \(s\) versus \(s^2\), and on not saying what the interval means. Learn the Ready answer in P4.1 by heart.

Exam format
  • No calculators in any sitting since Dec 2022. Recent questions say "you can simply give the final expression without computing its numerical value": then \(\bar x\pm z\,\sigma/\sqrt n\) with the numbers put in is the answer. Simplify only what is easy (\(\sqrt{64}=8\), \(\sqrt{10000}=100\)).
  • Only the standard normal table \(\Phi(z)\) is given. No formula sheet, no \(t\) table. You must find \(z_{\alpha/2}\) yourself for odd levels (88%, 92%, 93%, 97% have all appeared). The table of these values is in the Ready answer below.
  • No \(t\)-distribution in this course. When \(\sigma\) is unknown, replace it by the sample standard deviation \(s\) and still use \(z\) (the handout does this even for \(n=10\)).
  • Multiple choice in Part II since Dec 2025 (2 marks, four options): Dec 2025 Q25 asked what a CI means. May 2026 Part II Q7 is ten answer-only short questions.
  • Time. 3 hours for 100 marks is about 1.8 minutes per mark. A 10-mark CI question is short once you know the table: aim for 10 minutes and use the rest for checking.

The three cards

P4.1 CI for a mean 17 parts · 14 papers · 2–10 marks

Looks like"Construct a 95% confidence interval for the mean …"; a sample size \(n\), a sample mean \(\bar x\) and a standard deviation (population \(\sigma\), or sample \(s\) or \(s^2\)); or "\(X_1,\dots,X_n\) from \(N(\mu,25)\), in terms of \(n\)". Also: how large must \(n\) be, and what does the interval mean.

DoFind \(z_{\alpha/2}\) from \(\Phi(z)=1-\frac\alpha2\); write \(\bar x\pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\) (or \(s\) for \(\sigma\)). Sample size: solve margin \(\le\) target for \(n\), round up.

Watch\(N(\mu,25)\) means \(\sigma^2=25\), so \(\sigma=5\). "Sample variance 4" means \(s=2\). "Within \(\pm E\)" is the half-width; "length" is the full width.

P4.2 CI for a proportion 6 parts · 6 papers · 5–10 marks

Looks likeA survey of \(n\) people, \(x\) of them have the property (infected, smokers, own a car, defective); or a coin tossed \(n\) times with \(x\) heads.

Do\(\hat p=\frac xn\); standard error \(\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}\); interval \(\hat p\pm z_{\alpha/2}\times\)SE.

WatchDivide by \(n\) inside the square root. With round numbers the root is exact: \(\sqrt{0.2\cdot0.8/10000}=0.004\).

P4.3 Point estimation 4 parts · 3 papers · 10-mark questions

Looks like"What would your estimate of \(p\) be? Justify." "Explain why \(\bar X\) is accurate when \(n\) is large." "Propose an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma^2\)."

DoName the estimator, then prove its expectation is the parameter (unbiased) and its variance tends to 0 (law of large numbers).

Watch"Justify" is most of the marks: a bare number scores little. For \(\sigma^2\) the divisor is \(n-1\), not \(n\).

Learn the method first in the chapter note: point estimators, why \(n-1\), what a CI is, critical values \(z_{\alpha/2}\), \(\sigma\) known, \(\sigma\) unknown, proportions, sample size.

Notation on this page: a \((1-\alpha)100\%\) CI uses \(z_{\alpha/2}\), the point with area \(\frac\alpha2\) to its right, so \(\Phi(z_{\alpha/2})=1-\frac\alpha2\). Values of \(\Phi\) are quoted from the table printed with the papers (4 decimal places).

P4.1Confidence interval for a mean

17 parts · Dec 2025 (×2, one MC), May 2024, Dec 2023 (×3), May 2023; older format May 2019, Dec 2018, Dec 2017, Dec 2016, May 2016, Dec 2015, Dec 2014, May 2014, Dec 2013, Dec 2012 · Learn it: note §5, §6, §7, §8, §10

Method
  1. Read off \(n\), \(\bar x\) and the spread. Decide which case you are in:
    • \(\sigma\) known: "population standard deviation", "from past experience the SD is", "the SD remains the same", or a model that fixes it (\(N(\mu,25)\) gives \(\sigma=5\); exponential with rate \(\lambda\) gives \(\sigma=\frac1\lambda\)).
    • \(\sigma\) unknown: only the sample's own standard deviation \(s\) or variance \(s^2\) is given. Use \(s\) in place of \(\sigma\) and still use \(z\).
  2. Find the critical value: level \((1-\alpha)100\%\) means \(\Phi(z_{\alpha/2})=1-\frac\alpha2\). Use the table in the Ready answer; for a new level, find \(1-\frac\alpha2\) inside the body of the \(\Phi\) table.
  3. Standard error: \(\sigma/\sqrt n\) (or \(s/\sqrt n\)). Margin: \(z_{\alpha/2}\times\) standard error.
  4. Answer: (\(\bar x-\)margin, \(\bar x+\)margin). With no calculator, stop at the expression if the question says so.
  5. Say why the normal table applies: "\(X_i\) normal, so \(\bar X\) is exactly normal", or "\(n\) is large, so by the CLT \(\bar X\) is approximately normal".
  6. Sample size question: write margin \(\le\) target (or \(2\times\)margin \(\le\) length), solve for \(n\), round up. Meaning question: give the repeated-sampling sentence.

Asked in: 88% Dec 2025; 90% Dec 2023, May 2017, May 2016, May 2015; 92% May 2024; 93% Dec 2024, May 2023; 97% May 2026, Dec 2018; 99% May 2019; 95% most papers. When the level sits between two entries, take the midpoint (88%, 90%, 99%) or the nearer entry (92%, 93%), and say which.

2. Mean, \(\sigma\) known (\(X_i\) normal, any \(n\); otherwise \(n\) large, by the CLT):

\[ \Big(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n},\ \ \bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}\Big) \]

3. Mean, \(\sigma\) unknown (\(n\) large; the course uses \(z\), no \(t\)): the same with the sample standard deviation \(s\):

\[ \Big(\bar x-z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n},\ \ \bar x+z_{\alpha/2}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}\Big) \]

If the sample variance \(s^2\) is given, take its square root first.

4. Proportion (\(x\) successes in \(n\) trials, \(\hat p=\frac xn\), \(n\) large):

\[ \hat p\pm z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}} \]

5. Sample size. The margin (half-width) is \(E=z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma/\sqrt n\). Then round up:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{within }\pm E_0:&\ \ n\ge\Big(\frac{z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma}{E_0}\Big)^2\\ \text{length}\le L:&\ \ n\ge\Big(\frac{2z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma}{L}\Big)^2 \end{aligned} \]

For a proportion with no \(\hat p\) yet, use \(\hat p(1-\hat p)\le\frac14\), that is, put \(\frac12\) in place of \(\sigma\).

6. The sentences to write. Justification: "Since \(n=64\) is large, by the CLT

\[ \begin{aligned} &P\Big(-z_{\alpha/2}\lt\frac{\bar X-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\lt z_{\alpha/2}\Big)\\ &\quad\approx1-\alpha, \end{aligned} \]

and rearranging the inequalities for \(\mu\) gives the interval." Meaning: "If we took many samples and built an interval from each in the same way, about 95% of the intervals would contain \(\mu\). The mean \(\mu\) is fixed; the interval is what varies."

Dec 2025 Q252 pointsmultiple choice

A 95% confidence interval for the mean height of students at a university is found to be (165 cm, 175 cm). Which of the following is the correct interpretation of this interval?

(A) There is a 95% chance that the true mean height is between 165 cm and 175 cm.
(B) 95% of all students have heights between 165 cm and 175 cm.
(C) If we repeated the sampling process many times, 95% of the calculated confidence intervals would contain the true mean height.
(D) The sample mean height is 170 cm with 95% certainty.

Hint

P4.1, meaning of a CI. Ask which quantity is random: the true mean, or the interval computed from the sample?

Solution
  1. The true mean \(\mu\) is a fixed number. The interval \(\bar X\pm1.96\,\sigma/\sqrt n\) changes from sample to sample, so it is the random object.
  2. "95%" describes the method: the random interval contains \(\mu\) with probability 0.95. Over many repeated samples about 95% of the intervals contain \(\mu\). That is (C).
  3. (B) is about individual students, not the mean. (D) is wrong: the sample mean is exactly the centre, 170, with no uncertainty at all.

Marker: (A) is the trap. Once the numbers 165 and 175 are fixed, \(\mu\) either is or is not between them; the 95% belongs to the procedure, not to this one interval.

\[ \boxed{\text{(C)}} \]

Dec 2025 Q28(a)(ii)part of a 10-point question

Part (a)(i) is a CLT probability, (c) exponential moments: P3.5 and P3.3 in the P3 guide. Part (b) uses an MGF, which is not taught: earlier-syllabus page.

A wind farm measures daily energy output (in megawatt-hours) from its turbines. The output follows an exponential distribution with rate parameter \(\lambda=\frac12\). Let \(X_1,X_2,\dots,X_n\) represent daily outputs from different turbines.

(a) The energy company monitors a sample of \(n=64\) turbines and the observed average daily energy output was \(\bar x=2.1\). (i) Approximate \(P(1.75\lt\bar X\lt2.25)\). (ii) Construct an 88% confidence interval for the true mean daily energy output.

Reading used: \(\sigma\) is taken from the model (\(\sigma=\frac1\lambda=2\)); no sample standard deviation is given, so this is the only usable value.

Hint

P4.1 with \(\sigma\) known from the model: an exponential with rate \(\lambda\) has standard deviation \(\frac1\lambda\). For 88%, find \(\Phi(z)=0.94\) in the table.

Solution
  1. Exponential with rate \(\lambda\): variance \(\frac1{\lambda^2}=4\), so \(\sigma=2\). Since \(n=64\) is large, by the CLT \(\bar X\) is approximately \(N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\).
  2. 88%: \(\alpha=0.12\), so we need \(\Phi(z)=0.94\). The table gives \(\Phi(1.55)=0.9394\) and \(\Phi(1.56)=0.9406\); 0.94 is halfway, so \(z_{0.06}\approx1.555\).
  3. Standard error \(\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}=\frac{2}{8}=0.25\). Margin: \[ 1.555\times0.25=0.38875\approx0.389. \]
  4. Interval: \(2.1\pm0.389\).

Marker: show where \(\sigma=2\) comes from (the rate \(\frac12\)) and which table entries gave 1.555.

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}2.1\pm1.555\cdot\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{64}}\\ \approx(1.711,\ 2.489)\end{gathered}} \]

Using 1.55 or 1.56 instead gives \(2.1\pm0.3875\) or \(2.1\pm0.39\); any of these, stated, is fine.

May 2024 Q1210%

Same shape: May 2023 Q11 (93%), May 2019 Q10 (99%).

Let \(X_1,X_2,\cdots,X_n\) be \(n\) random samples of the normal distribution \(N(\mu,25)\), where \(\mu\) is unknown. Use the standard normal distribution table next page to construct a 92% confidence interval of \(\mu\) in terms of \(n\) and \(X_1,X_2,\cdots,X_n\). (You can simply give the final expression without computing its numerical value.)

Hint

P4.1, \(\sigma\) known: \(N(\mu,25)\) gives the variance, so \(\sigma=\sqrt{25}\). For 92%, find \(\Phi(z)=0.96\).

Solution
  1. The \(X_i\) are independent \(N(\mu,25)\), so \(\bar X=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\) is exactly \(N\big(\mu,\frac{25}n\big)\), and \[ Z=\frac{\bar X-\mu}{5/\sqrt n}\sim N(0,1). \]
  2. 92%: \(\alpha=0.08\), need \(\Phi(z)=0.96\). The table gives \(\Phi(1.75)=0.9599\) (and \(\Phi(1.76)=0.9608\)), so \(z_{0.04}\approx1.75\).
  3. So \(P(-1.75\lt Z\lt1.75)\approx0.92\). Rearrange for \(\mu\): \[ \begin{aligned} &P\Big(\bar X-\tfrac{1.75\cdot5}{\sqrt n}\lt\mu\\ &\qquad\lt\bar X+\tfrac{1.75\cdot5}{\sqrt n}\Big)\approx0.92 \end{aligned} \]

Marker: \(\sigma=5\), not 25; and the answer must be written in terms of the \(X_i\), so define \(\bar X\).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\Big(\bar X-\frac{8.75}{\sqrt n},\ \bar X+\frac{8.75}{\sqrt n}\Big),\\ \bar X=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\end{gathered}} \]

Dec 2023 Q94 + 3 + 3 points

(a) You want to rent an unfurnished one-bedroom apartment in Central (Hong Kong) next year. The mean monthly rent for a random sample of 64 apartments advertised on a specific website is HKD 10000. Assume a population standard deviation of HKD 2000. Construct a 95% confidence interval of the population mean. (4 points)

(b) Your friend said to you that "the mean will fall within the calculated confidence interval 95% of the time." Do you agree? Explain. (3 points)

(c) How large a sample of one-bedroom apartments above would be needed to estimate the population mean within plus or minus HKD 500 with 90% confidence? Answer in fractional form (i.e., as a rational number.) (3 points)

Reading used: in (c) "rational number" is read as the exact bound \(n\ge\frac{108241}{2500}=43.2964\), followed by the whole-number sample size 44.

Hint

P4.1 in all three parts. (a) \(\sigma\) known, \(\sqrt{64}=8\). (b) Which is random, the mean or the interval? (c) "Within \(\pm500\)" is the half-width at 90%: solve \(1.645\cdot\frac{2000}{\sqrt n}\le500\).

Solution

(a)

  1. \(n=64\) is large, so by the CLT \(\bar X\approx N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\) with \(\sigma=2000\) known.
  2. 95%: \(z_{0.025}=1.96\) (\(\Phi(1.96)=0.9750\)). Standard error \(\frac{2000}{8}=250\); margin \(1.96\times250=490\).
\[ \boxed{10000\pm490=(9510,\ 10490)} \]

(b)

  1. Disagree. The population mean is a fixed number; it does not "fall" anywhere, and the calculated interval (9510, 10490) either contains it or does not.
  2. The 95% describes the procedure: if we took many random samples of 64 and computed an interval from each in the same way, about 95% of those intervals would contain the population mean.
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{Disagree: }95\%\text{ of such intervals}\\ \text{contain the fixed mean}\end{gathered}} \]

(c)

  1. 90%: \(z_{0.05}=1.645\). "Within \(\pm500\)" means margin \(\le500\): \[ \begin{aligned} 1.645\cdot\frac{2000}{\sqrt n}&\le500\\ \sqrt n&\ge1.645\times4=6.58 \end{aligned} \]
  2. Square (\(6.58=\frac{329}{50}\)): \[ n\ge\frac{329^2}{50^2}=\frac{108241}{2500}=43.2964. \]
  3. \(n\) is a whole number, so round up.

Marker: rounding down to 43 gives a margin slightly above 500; say "round up" and why.

\[ \boxed{n\ge\tfrac{108241}{2500}=43.2964,\ \text{so } n=44} \]

May 2023 Q1110%

Let \(X_1,X_2,\cdots,X_n\) be \(n\) random samples of the normal distribution \(N(\mu,9)\), where \(\mu\) is unknown. Use the standard normal distribution table next page to construct a 93% confidence interval of \(\mu\) in terms of \(n\) and \(X_1,X_2,\cdots,X_n\). (You can simply give the final expression without computing its numerical value.)

Hint

P4.1, \(\sigma=\sqrt9\) known. For 93%, find \(\Phi(z)=0.965\) and take the nearer table entry.

Solution
  1. \(\bar X=\frac1n\sum X_i\sim N\big(\mu,\frac9n\big)\) exactly, so \(\frac{\bar X-\mu}{3/\sqrt n}\sim N(0,1)\).
  2. 93%: \(\alpha=0.07\), need \(\Phi(z)=0.965\). The table gives \(\Phi(1.81)=0.9649\) and \(\Phi(1.82)=0.9656\); 0.9649 is closer, so \(z_{0.035}\approx1.81\).
  3. Margin \(1.81\times\frac{3}{\sqrt n}=\frac{5.43}{\sqrt n}\).
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\Big(\bar X-\frac{5.43}{\sqrt n},\ \bar X+\frac{5.43}{\sqrt n}\Big),\\ \bar X=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\end{gathered}} \]

Interpolating gives \(z\approx1.811\); either value, stated, is fine.

May 2019 Q1010 marksolder format

Let \(X_1,X_2,\dots,X_n\) be \(n\) random samples of the normal distribution \(N(\mu,4)\), where \(\mu\) is unknown. Write down the 99% confidence interval of \(\mu\) in terms of \(n\). What sample size \(n\) is required such that the width of this confidence interval is less than 0.7? (You can simply give the final expression without computing its numerical value.)

Hint

P4.1 with a sample size. \(\sigma=2\); for 99%, \(\Phi(z)=0.995\) lies between two table entries. Width is twice the margin.

Solution
  1. \(\bar X\sim N(\mu,\frac4n)\) exactly. 99%: \(\Phi(2.57)=0.9949\), \(\Phi(2.58)=0.9951\), so \(z_{0.005}\approx2.575\).
  2. Margin \(2.575\times\frac{2}{\sqrt n}=\frac{5.15}{\sqrt n}\). Interval: \(\big(\bar X-\frac{5.15}{\sqrt n},\ \bar X+\frac{5.15}{\sqrt n}\big)\).
  3. Width \(\frac{10.3}{\sqrt n}\lt0.7\): \[ \begin{aligned} \sqrt n&\gt\frac{10.3}{0.7}=\frac{103}{7}\\ n&\gt\frac{103^2}{7^2}=\frac{10609}{49}\approx216.5 \end{aligned} \]
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\bar X\pm\frac{5.15}{\sqrt n};\\ n\gt\Big(\frac{2\cdot2.575\cdot2}{0.7}\Big)^2,\ n\ge217\end{gathered}} \]

With \(z=2.58\) the bound becomes \(n\ge218\). The question accepts the expression.

Dec 2018 Q810 marksolder format

In a public exam, a survey of 1000 samples yields an average of 68.3. From the past experience, the standard deviation is around 10. Construct a 97% confidence interval for the average score. (You can simply give the final expression without computing its numerical value.)

Hint

P4.1, \(\sigma=10\) treated as known ("from past experience"). For 97%, find \(\Phi(z)=0.985\).

Solution
  1. \(n=1000\) is large, so by the CLT \(\bar X\approx N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)\) with \(\sigma=10\).
  2. 97%: \(\alpha=0.03\), need \(\Phi(z)=0.985\); the table gives \(\Phi(2.17)=0.9850\), so \(z_{0.015}=2.17\).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}68.3\pm2.17\cdot\tfrac{10}{\sqrt{1000}}\\ \approx(67.614,\ 68.986)\end{gathered}} \]

Dec 2017 Q8(b)part of a 10-point questionolder format

Part (a), a normal probability: P3.4 in the P3 guide.

(a) A manufacturer produces dental floss of mean diameter 0.2mm. Assume that the diameter of the dental floss produced follows the normal distribution \(N(0.2,0.01^2)\) with a standard deviation of 0.01mm. A dental floss is said to be acceptable if its diameter is between 0.18mm and 0.22mm. Find the probability that a dental floss produced is acceptable.

(b) (i) The manufacturer adopted a new technology for the production of dental floss. He then conducted a survey by collecting \(n\) random samples of the dental floss produced. The average diameter of the \(n\) random samples is found to be 0.21mm. Assuming that the standard deviation of the diameter of the dental floss produced remains the same, construct a 95% confidence interval for the mean diameter \(\mu\) in terms of \(n\). (ii) To have a good estimation, the manufacturer would like to make sure that the length of the 95% confidence interval is less than or equal to \(10^{-3}\). Please suggest a sample size \(n\) for this purpose.

Hint

P4.1 with \(\sigma=0.01\) from (a). (ii) "Length" is the full width, \(2\times\)margin.

Solution
  1. (i) The diameters are normal with \(\sigma=0.01\), so \(\bar X\sim N(\mu,\frac{0.01^2}{n})\). With \(z_{0.025}=1.96\), the margin is \(\frac{1.96\times0.01}{\sqrt n}=\frac{0.0196}{\sqrt n}\).
  2. (ii) Length \(\le10^{-3}\): \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{2(0.0196)}{\sqrt n}&\le0.001\\ \sqrt n&\ge39.2\\ n&\ge39.2^2=1536.64 \end{aligned} \]
  3. Round up: \(n=1537\) (or any larger \(n\)).
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{(i) }\Big(0.21-\tfrac{0.0196}{\sqrt n},\ 0.21+\tfrac{0.0196}{\sqrt n}\Big)\\ \text{(ii) } n\ge1537\end{gathered}} \]

May 2016 Q710%older format

The lifetime of certain type of devices is known to follow certain probability distribution. 100 such devices are tested and the sample mean and sample variance for the lifetime obtained are 3 years and 4 years\(^2\). Using the table on Page 5, find the 90% confidence interval for the mean lifetime of the device.

Hint

P4.1, \(\sigma\) unknown: use \(s\), the square root of the sample variance. 90% uses 1.645.

Solution
  1. The distribution is unknown but \(n=100\) is large, so by the CLT (with \(\sigma\) replaced by \(s\)) \(\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\) is approximately \(N(0,1)\).
  2. \(s^2=4\), so \(s=2\); standard error \(\frac{2}{10}=0.2\).
  3. 90%: \(\Phi(1.64)=0.9495\), \(\Phi(1.65)=0.9505\), so \(z_{0.05}\approx1.645\). Margin \(1.645\times0.2=0.329\).

Marker: the classic slip is to use 4 (the variance) as the standard deviation.

\[ \boxed{3\pm0.329=(2.671,\ 3.329)} \]

Dec 2015 Q7(b)part of a 15-point questionolder format

Part (a)(i): P3.1 in the P3 guide; part (a)(ii): P4.3 on this page.

(b) A postmix beverage machine is adjusted to release a certain amount of syrup into a chamber where it is mixed with carbonated water. A random sample of 25 beverages was found to have a mean syrup content of \(\bar x=1.10\) fluid ounces and a standard deviation of \(s=0.015\) fluid ounces. Find a 95% Confidence Interval on the mean volume of syrup dispensed.

Remark: For (b), you can use the following table but you don't have to compute numerical values of the desired confidence interval.

Reading used: the course has no \(t\)-distribution (the handout uses \(z\) with \(s\) even for \(n=10\)), and only the normal table is given, so the answer uses \(z=1.96\).

Hint

P4.1, \(\sigma\) unknown: replace \(\sigma\) by \(s=0.015\); \(\sqrt{25}=5\).

Solution
  1. \(\sigma\) is unknown; use \(s=0.015\) in its place, so \(\frac{\bar X-\mu}{S/\sqrt n}\approx N(0,1)\).
  2. Standard error \(\frac{0.015}{5}=0.003\). 95%: margin \(1.96\times0.003=0.00588\).
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}1.10\pm1.96\cdot\tfrac{0.015}{\sqrt{25}}\\ =(1.09412,\ 1.10588)\end{gathered}} \]

P4.2Confidence interval for a proportion

6 parts · May 2026, Dec 2024, Dec 2022; older format May 2018, May 2017, May 2015 · Learn it: note §9 CI for a proportion, §6 critical values

Method
  1. Each person (toss, device) is a Bernoulli trial: 1 if it has the property, 0 if not. Then \(\hat p=\frac xn\) is the sample mean of these 0s and 1s.
  2. Critical value \(z_{\alpha/2}\) from the table in the P4.1 Ready answer.
  3. Standard error \(\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}\). Without a calculator, split it as \(\frac{\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)}}{\sqrt n}\): with \(n=10000\), \(\sqrt n=100\).
  4. Interval \(\hat p\pm z_{\alpha/2}\times\)SE. Justify with the CLT: \(n\) is large, so \(\hat p\) is approximately normal with mean \(p\) and variance \(\frac{p(1-p)}n\), and \(p\) in the variance is replaced by \(\hat p\).
Ready answer · CI for a proportion \[ \Big(\hat p-z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}},\ \ \hat p+z_{\alpha/2}\sqrt{\tfrac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}\Big) \]

Model sentence: "Let \(X_i=1\) if the \(i\)-th person is infected and 0 otherwise; \(\hat p=\bar X=\frac{2000}{10000}=0.2\). Since \(n\) is large, by the CLT \(\frac{\hat p-p}{\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/n}}\approx N(0,1)\), so …"

Roots that come out exactly: \(\sqrt{0.2\times0.8}=0.4\), \(\sqrt{0.1\times0.9}=0.3\), \(\sqrt{0.5\times0.5}=0.5\).

P4.3Point estimation

4 parts · Dec 2021; older format Dec 2015, May 2013 (×2) · Learn it: note §3 Point estimators and unbiasedness, §4 why \(n-1\)

Method · a decision procedure
  1. "What would your estimate of \(p\) be? Justify." Code each trial as \(X_i\in\{0,1\}\). The estimate is the sample proportion \(\hat p=\bar X\). Justify in two lines: \(E(\hat p)=p\) (unbiased) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat p)=\frac{p(1-p)}n\to0\), so by the law of large numbers \(\hat p\) is close to \(p\).
  2. "Why is \(\bar X\) accurate when \(n\) is large?" Same two facts for a general mean: \(E(\bar X)=\mu\), \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}n\to0\); finish with Chebyshev or the weak law of large numbers.
  3. "Propose an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma^2\)." Give \(S^2\) with divisor \(n-1\) and prove \(E(S^2)=\sigma^2\) with the Ready answer below.
  4. Data given as a waiting time ("the 1000th head at the 5000th toss"): the number of tosses for the \(r\)-th head has mean \(\frac rp\). Set the observed value equal to it, or simply use the proportion of heads.
  5. If none fits: say which statistic you use, compute it, then show it is unbiased and its variance shrinks.
Ready answer · the two proofs

Mean. For i.i.d. \(X_i\) with mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\):

\[ E(\bar X)=\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n}. \]

Chebyshev: for every \(\varepsilon\gt0\),

\[ P(|\bar X-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}\to0. \]

Variance. \(S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2\). Expand, then use \(E(X_i^2)=\sigma^2+\mu^2\) and \(E(\bar X^2)=\frac{\sigma^2}n+\mu^2\):

\[ \begin{aligned} &E\Big[\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2\Big]\\ &\quad=\sum E(X_i^2)-nE(\bar X^2)\\ &\quad=n(\sigma^2+\mu^2)-(\sigma^2+n\mu^2)\\ &\quad=(n-1)\sigma^2, \end{aligned} \]

so \(E(S^2)=\sigma^2\). (Dividing by \(n\) instead gives mean \(\frac{n-1}n\sigma^2\): biased.)

Dec 2021 Q810 marks

Consider a biased coin which will come up heads with probability \(p\) and tails with probability \(1-p\) when flipped. The coin was independently flipped 1000 times and it came up heads for 400 times. What would your estimate of \(p\) be? Justify your answer.

Hint

P4.3. Let \(X_i=1\) for a head, 0 for a tail. Which statistic is an unbiased estimator of \(p\), and what is its variance?

Solution
  1. Let \(X_i=1\) if flip \(i\) is a head and 0 otherwise. The \(X_i\) are i.i.d. Bernoulli(\(p\)): \(E(X_i)=p\), \(\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=p(1-p)\).
  2. Estimator: the sample proportion \(\hat p=\bar X=\frac1{1000}\sum X_i\). Observed value \(\frac{400}{1000}=0.4\).
  3. Unbiased: \(E(\hat p)=\frac1{1000}\cdot1000p=p\).
  4. Accurate: \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat p)=\frac{p(1-p)}{1000}\le\frac1{4000}\), since \(p(1-p)\le\frac14\). By Chebyshev, \[ P(|\hat p-p|\ge\varepsilon)\le\frac{1}{4000\,\varepsilon^2}, \] which is small (the weak law of large numbers), so \(\hat p\) is close to \(p\) with high probability.

Marker: most of the marks are for the justification; state the estimator as a random variable, then show it is unbiased and its variance is small.

\[ \boxed{\hat p=\tfrac{400}{1000}=0.4} \]

Another check: 0.4 is the value of \(p\) that makes the observed result most likely, since \(p^{400}(1-p)^{600}\) is largest when \(\frac{400}p=\frac{600}{1-p}\), that is, \(p=0.4\).

May 2013 Q8(a), (b)10 marksolder format

Consider a sequence of independent identically distributed random variables \(X_1,X_2,\dots\), with unknown mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\). Let

\[ \bar X=\frac{X_1+X_2+\cdots+X_n}{n}. \]

(a) It is known that when \(n\) is large, \(\bar X\) is an accurate estimator of \(\mu\). Explain the reason.

(b) Propose an unbiased estimator of \(\sigma^2\) and explain why it is unbiased.

Reading used: the split of the 10 marks between (a) and (b) is not printed.

Hint

P4.3, both proofs of the Ready answer. (a) Compute \(E(\bar X)\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)\). (b) Divide by \(n-1\); expand \(\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2\).

Solution

(a)

  1. By linearity, \(E(\bar X)=\frac1n\cdot n\mu=\mu\): \(\bar X\) is unbiased.
  2. By independence, \(\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\frac1{n^2}\cdot n\sigma^2=\frac{\sigma^2}n\).
  3. Chebyshev: for any \(\varepsilon\gt0\), \[ P(|\bar X-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}\to0 \] as \(n\to\infty\) (weak law of large numbers). So for large \(n\), \(\bar X\) is within \(\varepsilon\) of \(\mu\) with probability close to 1.
\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}E(\bar X)=\mu,\ \operatorname{Var}(\bar X)=\tfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\to0\\ \Rightarrow\ \bar X\to\mu\ \text{(WLLN)}\end{gathered}} \]

(b)

  1. For \(n\ge2\), propose \[ S^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2. \]
  2. Expand: \(\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2=\sum X_i^2-n\bar X^2\), because \(\sum X_i=n\bar X\).
  3. From \(\operatorname{Var}=E(\cdot^2)-(E\cdot)^2\): \(E(X_i^2)=\sigma^2+\mu^2\) and \(E(\bar X^2)=\frac{\sigma^2}n+\mu^2\).
  4. So \[ \begin{aligned} &E\Big[\sum(X_i-\bar X)^2\Big]\\ &\quad=n(\sigma^2+\mu^2)-n\Big(\tfrac{\sigma^2}n+\mu^2\Big)\\ &\quad=(n-1)\sigma^2, \end{aligned} \] and dividing by \(n-1\) gives \(E(S^2)=\sigma^2\).

Marker: show the step \(E(\bar X^2)=\frac{\sigma^2}n+\mu^2\); it is the reason the divisor is \(n-1\).

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}S^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\bar X)^2,\\ E(S^2)=\sigma^2\end{gathered}} \]
