Counts are unique past-paper parts, 2014–2026; every part is solved below, December first, newest first. The curl, potential and flux parts before them are on the C3a and C3c guides.

◆Exam format and formula sheet for this chapter

  • December (main format): 3 hours, five 25-mark questions, answer any four (Q5, PDEs, is compulsory). A line integral is the last sub-part of part (a) of Q3 or Q4, worth 5–6 marks, after a curl / scalar-potential part (C3c.1) or a flux part whose answer Stokes’ theorem predicts (C3c.3). Seen in Dec 2025, 2024, 2017, 2016. Dec 2021 Q3 and Q4 are missing from the scans, so 2021 may also have had one.
  • “Do NOT use …”: recent papers say the integral must be done by parametrising, without the Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals or Stokes’ theorem, “as no marks will be given”. Use the short cut only as a private check of your answer.
  • Time budget: 25 marks ≈ 45 min per question, so a 5–6 mark line integral ≈ 9–11 min.
  • What the December formula sheet gives (same sheet 2014–2025): both line integrals in parametric form, \(\int_Cf\,ds=\int_a^bf(\vb r(t))\,|\vb r'(t)|\,dt\) and \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\int_a^b\vb F(\vb r(t))\cdot\vb r'(t)\,dt\); also the reduction formulas \(\int\sin^nx\,dx\), \(\int\cos^nx\,dx\) (useful for arcs).
  • What it leaves out: how to parametrise a segment, a graph or a circular arc (ready answer), the Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals, and the orientation rule linking a boundary curve to a surface normal (needed when the curve comes from a Stokes question).
  • May format (other semester; useful practice, different rules): May 2021–2024 five questions, all compulsory, no formula sheet; May 2024 Q3(a) asked a 10-mark work integral along a 3-D segment. May 2025–2026: eight 10-mark questions, none of this type so far.
  • Scalar (arc-length) line integrals \(\int_Cf\,ds\) are on the sheet and in the note (§2) but have not been asked on their own.

C3b.1Work integral \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) by parametrising the path

5 parts · 5 sittings (Dec 2025, 2024, 2017, 2016; May 2024) · 31 marks · always the last part of a vector-field question · Learn it · note §3 Parametrising · note §1

Type card · predictable
Looks like
“Evaluate \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) where \(C\) is the segment / parabola / semicircle / boundary triangle …”, often “without using Stokes’ theorem / the Fundamental Theorem” (5–10 marks).
Do
1. Parametrise \(C\): \(\vb r(t)\), \(a\le t\le b\), start at \(t=a\). 2. Find \(\vb r'(t)\). 3. Put \(\vb r(t)\) into \(\vb F\) and dot with \(\vb r'(t)\): one function of \(t\). 4. Integrate from \(a\) to \(b\) (closed polygon: repeat per side and add).
Watch
Direction: the path must run from the stated start to the stated end (for a surface boundary, anticlockwise seen from the normal’s tip). Reversing it flips the sign.
Check
Field conservative (curl \(=\vb 0\))? Then the answer is \(\varphi(\text{end})-\varphi(\text{start})\). Closed boundary of a surface? Then it equals the Stokes flux. Use these only to check, when the question bans them.
Ready answer · standard parametrisations (not on the sheet)
  • Segment from \(P\) to \(Q\): \(\vb r(t)=P+t\,(Q-P)\), \(0\le t\le1\); \(\vb r'(t)=Q-P\).
  • Graph \(y=g(x)\) from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\): \(\vb r(t)=t\,\ii+g(t)\,\jj\), \(a\le t\le b\); \(\vb r'(t)=\ii+g'(t)\,\jj\).
  • Circle of radius \(R\), centre the origin, anticlockwise: \(\vb r(t)=R\cos t\,\ii+R\sin t\,\jj\); \(\vb r'(t)=-R\sin t\,\ii+R\cos t\,\jj\). Upper half: \(0\le t\le\pi\).
\[\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\int_a^b\vb F\big(\vb r(t)\big)\cdot\vb r'(t)\,dt\]

In words: work = add up (force along the path × small step) = integral over the parameter of (field at the point) · (velocity of the point).

Ready answer · integrals that come up on arcs \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^\pi\sin^3t\,dt&=\tfrac43,\qquad \int_0^\pi\sin t\cos^2t\,dt=\tfrac23,\\ \int_0^\pi\sin^2t\,dt&=\tfrac\pi2,\qquad \int_0^\pi\sin t\cos t\,dt=0\end{aligned}\]

In words: write \(\sin^3t=\sin t\,(1-\cos^2t)\) and substitute \(u=\cos t\); a product with one \(\sin t\) and powers of \(\cos t\) always goes this way.

Past-paper parts (December, newest first)

Dec 2025 Q4(a)(iii)6 marks

Let \(\vb F=z\,\ii+x\,\jj+y\,\kk\), and let \(\Sigma\) be the triangular region in the plane \(x+y+z=1\) with vertices \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), with unit normal \(\vb n\) pointing upward. (Earlier parts: C3a.1, C3c.3: \(\nabla\times\vb F=\ii+\jj+\kk\) and \(\iint_\Sigma(\nabla\times\vb F)\cdot\vb n\,dS=\frac32\).) Let \(C\) be the boundary curve oriented consistently with \(\vb n\). Evaluate \(\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) without using Stokes’ theorem. (The boundary curve \(C\) consists of three straight line segments.)

Hint

Upward normal → go round anticlockwise seen from above: \(A(1,0,0)\to B(0,1,0)\to C(0,0,1)\to A\). One segment parametrisation per side.

Solution
  1. Orientation: with \(\vb n\) upward, the right-hand rule gives \(A\to B\to C\to A\) (anticlockwise when seen from above).
  2. \(C_1\) (\(A\to B\)): \(\vb r=(1-t,\,t,\,0)\), \(\vb r'=(-1,1,0)\), \(\vb F=(0,\,1-t,\,t)\); \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=1-t\); \(\int_0^1(1-t)\,dt=\frac12\).
  3. \(C_2\) (\(B\to C\)): \(\vb r=(0,\,1-t,\,t)\), \(\vb r'=(0,-1,1)\), \(\vb F=(t,\,0,\,1-t)\); \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=1-t\); integral \(\frac12\).
  4. \(C_3\) (\(C\to A\)): \(\vb r=(t,\,0,\,1-t)\), \(\vb r'=(1,0,-1)\), \(\vb F=(1-t,\,t,\,0)\); \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=1-t\); integral \(\frac12\).
  5. Add: \(\frac12+\frac12+\frac12=\frac32\), which agrees with the flux in (ii), as Stokes’ theorem says it must.

State the orientation and why (upward normal ⇒ anticlockwise from above) before parametrising; a reversed loop gives \(-\frac32\).

\(\displaystyle\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\frac32\)
Dec 2024 Q4(a)(iii)5 marks

Consider \(\vb F=y^ae^{xy}\,\ii+x^ae^{xy}\,\jj\), where \(a\gt0\) is a constant. (Part (i), C3c.1: \(\vb F\) is irrotational exactly when \(a=1\).) With this \(a\), evaluate \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) where \(C\) is the straight line segment from \((0,0)\) to \((1,\pi)\), using a parametrisation of the line segment. (Do NOT use the Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals, as no marks will be given.)

Hint

\(\vb r(t)=t\,\ii+\pi t\,\jj\), \(0\le t\le1\). The integrand is a perfect derivative: look for \(u=\pi t^2\).

Solution
  1. With \(a=1\): \(\vb F=ye^{xy}\,\ii+xe^{xy}\,\jj\).
  2. \(\vb r(t)=(t,\,\pi t)\), \(0\le t\le1\), \(\vb r'(t)=(1,\,\pi)\); on \(C\), \(xy=\pi t^2\).
  3. \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=\pi t\,e^{\pi t^2}\cdot1+t\,e^{\pi t^2}\cdot\pi\) \(=2\pi t\,e^{\pi t^2}\).
  4. \(\int_0^12\pi t\,e^{\pi t^2}dt=\big[e^{\pi t^2}\big]_0^1=e^\pi-1\) (substitute \(u=\pi t^2\)).

Show the integrand in \(t\) explicitly; writing \(\varphi=e^{xy}\) and substituting end points scores nothing here (use it only as a check: \(e^{\pi}-e^0\)).

\(\displaystyle\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=e^\pi-1\approx22.14\)
Dec 2017 Q3(a)(iii)5 marks

Given the vector field in \(\R^2\): \(\vb F=(x^2-y^2+x)\,\ii-(kxy+y)\,\jj\), where \(k\) is a real constant. (Part (i), C3c.1: \(\vb F\) is irrotational when \(k=2\).) For a general value of \(k\), evaluate \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) where \(C\) is the parabolic path \(y=x^2\) from \((0,0)\) to \((2,4)\).

Hint

“General \(k\)” means the field need not be conservative: you must parametrise. \(\vb r(t)=t\,\ii+t^2\,\jj\), \(0\le t\le2\).

Solution
  1. \(\vb r(t)=(t,\,t^2)\), \(0\le t\le2\); \(\vb r'(t)=(1,\,2t)\).
  2. On \(C\): \(F_1=t^2-t^4+t\), \(F_2=-(kt^3+t^2)\).
  3. \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=t^2-t^4+t-2kt^4-2t^3\) \(=-(1+2k)t^4-2t^3+t^2+t\).
  4. \(\int_0^2\dots\,dt=-(1+2k)\frac{32}5-8+\frac83+2\) \(=-\frac{146}{15}-\frac{64k}5\).

Check: with \(k=2\) the field has potential \(\varphi=\frac{x^3}3-xy^2+\frac{x^2}2-\frac{y^2}2\), and \(\varphi(2,4)-\varphi(0,0)=-\frac{106}3\), the same as the formula gives.

\[\begin{aligned}\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r&=-\frac{146}{15}-\frac{64k}5\\&=-\frac{2(73+96k)}{15}\end{aligned}\]
Dec 2016 Q3(a)(iii)5 marks

Given the vector field in \(\R^3\): \(\vb F=(\alpha y^2+4zx)\,\ii\) \(+\,y(\beta x+2z)\,\jj\) \(+\,(\alpha y^2+2x^2)\,\kk\), where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are constants. Evaluate \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) in terms of \(\alpha,\beta\), where \(C\) is the semicircular path \(\vb r(t)=\cos t\,\ii+\sin t\,\jj\) for \(t\in[0,\pi]\).

Hint

The path is given; on it \(z=0\). Only the \(\ii\) and \(\jj\) components of \(\vb F\) matter, because \(\vb r'\) has no \(\kk\) part.

Solution
  1. \(\vb r=(\cos t,\,\sin t,\,0)\), \(\vb r'=(-\sin t,\,\cos t,\,0)\).
  2. On \(C\) (\(z=0\)): \(F_1=\alpha\sin^2t\), \(F_2=\beta\sin t\cos t\) (\(F_3\) is multiplied by \(0\)).
  3. \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=-\alpha\sin^3t+\beta\sin t\cos^2t\).
  4. \(\int_0^\pi\sin^3t\,dt=\frac43\), \(\int_0^\pi\sin t\cos^2t\,dt=\frac23\) (ready answer).

Check: part (i) gives irrotational for \(\alpha=1,\beta=2\); then the answer is \(-\frac43+\frac43=0\), matching \(\varphi=xy^2+2x^2z+y^2z\) with \(\varphi(-1,0,0)-\varphi(1,0,0)=0\).

\(\displaystyle\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=-\frac{4\alpha}3+\frac{2\beta}3=\frac{2(\beta-2\alpha)}3\)

May format

May 2024 Q3(a)10 marksMay format

Determine the work done by \(\vb F=x\,\ii+3xy\,\jj-(x+z)\,\kk\) over a straight path from \((1,4,2)\) to \((0,5,1)\).

Hint

Segment: \(\vb r(t)=P+t(Q-P)\) with \(Q-P=(-1,1,-1)\), \(0\le t\le1\).

Solution
  1. \(\vb r(t)=(1-t,\,4+t,\,2-t)\), \(0\le t\le1\); \(\vb r'=(-1,\,1,\,-1)\).
  2. On \(C\): \(F_1=1-t\), \(F_2=3(1-t)(4+t)=12-9t-3t^2\), \(F_3=-(3-2t)\).
  3. \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=-(1-t)\) \(+(12-9t-3t^2)\) \(+(3-2t)\) \(=14-10t-3t^2\).
  4. \(\int_0^1(14-10t-3t^2)\,dt\) \(=14-5-1=8\).

The field is not conservative (\(\nabla\times\vb F=(0,\,1,\,3y)\ne\vb 0\)), so a parametrisation is the only way; “work done” = \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\).

\(\displaystyle W=\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=8\)

◆Before the exam

  • Write the parametrisation and its \(t\)-range first; the start point must be at the lower limit.
  • Segment from \(P\) to \(Q\): \(\vb r(t)=P+t(Q-P)\), \(0\le t\le1\), \(\vb r'=Q-P\) (a constant vector).
  • Substitute every \(x,y,z\) in \(\vb F\), including the ones that are zero on the path (e.g. \(z=0\) on a planar arc).
  • Write the integrand \(\vb F(\vb r(t))\cdot\vb r'(t)\) as one polynomial / trig expression in \(t\) before integrating: that line carries a mark.
  • Closed polygon: one parametrisation per side, all in the same direction round the loop; add the pieces.
  • Boundary of a surface with “upward” normal: go round anticlockwise when seen from above (right-hand rule).
  • Arc integrals: \(\int_0^\pi\sin^3t\,dt=\frac43\), \(\int_0^\pi\sin t\cos^2t\,dt=\frac23\), \(\int_0^\pi\sin^2t\,dt=\frac\pi2\).
  • Check, privately: if the field is conservative, \(\varphi(\text{end})-\varphi(\text{start})\) must equal your answer; for a closed boundary, the Stokes flux must.
