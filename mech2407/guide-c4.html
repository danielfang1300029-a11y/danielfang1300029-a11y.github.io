◆Exam format and formula sheet for this chapter

  • December (main format): 3 hours, five 25-mark questions, answer any four (Q5, PDEs, is compulsory). Fourier series is always part (b) of Q3 or Q4, worth 10–15 marks; part (a) of the same question is vector calculus. It appeared in every December paper we have (2014–2018, 2022–2025). Dec 2021 Q3 and Q4 are missing from the scans, so we cannot say whether 2021 had a Fourier part.
  • The December pattern: (i) sketch \(f\) over two or three periods (4–7 marks) → (ii) find the series, often “show it has the form \(A+B\sum\dots\)” (4–9 marks) → (iii) sometimes “deduce” a number series (3–4 marks). Types C4.1 then C4.2.
  • Time budget: 25 marks ≈ 45 min per question, so a 12-mark Fourier part ≈ 20 min: about 3 min for the sketch, 12 for the coefficients, 5 for the series sum.
  • What the December formula sheet gives (same sheet 2014–2025): the Fourier series of a \(T\)-periodic \(f\) on \([a,a+T]\), \(f(t)=\frac12a_0\) \(+\sum_{n\ge1}(a_n\cos n\omega t+b_n\sin n\omega t)\) with \(\omega=\frac{2\pi}T\), \(a_n=\frac2T\int_a^{a+T}f\cos n\omega t\,dt\), \(b_n=\frac2T\int_a^{a+T}f\sin n\omega t\,dt\); and reduction formulas for \(\int x^n\sin\), \(\int x^n\cos\), \(\int e^{\beta x}\sin^n\), \(\int e^{\beta x}\cos^n\).
  • What it leaves out (so they are Ready-answer boxes on this page): the even/odd short cuts (C4.1), the convergence (Dirichlet) theorem and the jump average (C4.2), the half-range formulas (C4.3) and Parseval's identity (C4.4). Also the standard integrals \(\int x\cos nx\) etc. (C4.1) — the sheet's reduction formula gets you there, but knowing the results saves 5 minutes.
  • Convention clash: this page and the note write the constant term as \(\frac12a_0\) with \(a_0=\frac2T\int f\), as on the December sheet. The May 2015–2019 sheet wrote it as \(A_0=\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^Lf\), which is the same number: \(A_0=\frac12a_0\) = the average of \(f\) over one period. Where a May question uses \(A_0\), the answers below say so.
  • May format (other semester; useful practice, different rules): 2015–2019 five 20-mark questions, all compulsory, Fourier is Q4 (sketch + series + series sum, or half-range + Parseval); 2021–2024 five questions with Fourier as Q4 (10–20 marks), 2025–2026 eight 10-mark questions with Fourier as Q7. From 2021 there is no formula sheet. Only May papers have asked half-range series (C4.3) and Parseval (C4.4); both are in the 2026 syllabus (note §5 §6), so be ready for them in December too.

C4.1Fourier series of a given periodic function

24 parts · 14 sittings (Dec 2025, 2024, 2023, 2022, 2018, 2017, 2016, 2014; May 2025, 2023, 2022, 2019, 2018, 2016) · 165 marks · Learn it · note §2 even/odd · note §4

Type card · predictable · which method?
Looks like
\(f\) given on one period (often piecewise): “sketch \(f\) over \([-2\pi,2\pi]\)”, then “find the Fourier series” or “show it has the form \(A+B\sum\dots\)”.
Which method?
  • Mirror image in the \(y\)-axis (even) → \(b_n=0\); only \(a_0,a_n\), integrate over half a period and double. (Dec 2025, 2017, 2014; May 2018(i).)
  • Point-symmetric about the origin (odd), or odd after subtracting a constant (\(f-c\) odd, e.g. \(2x+1\), \(1-\frac t2\)) → \(a_n=0\) for \(n\ge1\), \(\frac12a_0=c\); only \(b_n\). (Dec 2024, 2023; May 2025, 2023, 2022.)
  • Neither → find \(a_0,a_n,b_n\), splitting each integral at the break points. (Dec 2022, 2018, 2016; May 2019, 2018(ii), 2016.)
  • Period not \(2\pi\) → \(T=2L\), \(\omega=\frac{2\pi}T=\frac\pi L\): use \(\cos\frac{n\pi t}L\), \(\sin\frac{n\pi t}L\) and the factor \(\frac2T=\frac1L\).
  • A target form is given (\(A+B\sum\frac{\cos(2n-1)t}{(2n-1)^2}+\sum C_n\frac{\sin nt}n\)) → find general \(a_n,b_n\) first, simplify with \(\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\), then read off: a factor \(1-(-1)^n\) means only odd \(n=2k-1\) survive.
  • Same formula on a shifted interval (\(g=2x+1\) on \((0,2\pi)\) after \(f=2x+1\) on \((-\pi,\pi)\)) → write \(g(x)=f(x-c)+d\) and substitute; no new integrals (ready answer).
Do
1. Sketch first (it shows symmetry, jumps, the period). 2. State the symmetry and what it kills. 3. Set up \(a_0,a_n,b_n\) with the interval split; integrate by parts (ready answer). 4. Simplify with \((-1)^n\), write the series out.
Watch
The constant term is \(\frac12a_0\) (= the average of \(f\)), not \(a_0\). And integrate over the period the question gives (\([0,2\pi)\) is not \([-\pi,\pi)\) unless you re-write \(f\) there).
Ready answer · even / odd short cuts (not on the sheet)

For \(f\) with period \(T=2L\) (\(\omega=\frac\pi L\)):

\[\begin{aligned}&\text{even } f(-t)=f(t):\quad b_n=0,\\ &a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(t)\cos\frac{n\pi t}L\,dt\ \ (n\ge0);\\ &\text{odd } f(-t)=-f(t):\quad a_n=0\ (n\ge0),\\ &b_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(t)\sin\frac{n\pi t}L\,dt.\end{aligned}\]

Why: even × even and odd × odd are even (integral over \([-L,L]\) = twice the integral over \([0,L]\)); even × odd is odd (integral 0). If \(f-c\) is odd, the same holds for \(f-c\) and \(\frac12a_0=c\).

In words: an even function needs only cosines, an odd function only sines, and either way you integrate over half a period and double.

Ready answer · integrals and patterns that keep coming back \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^\pi x\cos nx\,dx&=\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2}\\ \int_0^\pi x\sin nx\,dx&=\frac{\pi(-1)^{n+1}}n\\ \int_{-\pi}^\pi x\sin nx\,dx&=\frac{2\pi(-1)^{n+1}}n\\ \int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx\,dx&=\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}\\ \int_{-\pi}^\pi x^2\cos nx\,dx&=\frac{4\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}\\ \int_0^\pi x^2\sin nx\,dx&=\frac{\pi^2(-1)^{n+1}}n+\frac{2[(-1)^n-1]}{n^3}\end{aligned}\]

Period \(2L\): \(\int_0^Lx\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx=\frac{L^2[(-1)^n-1]}{n^2\pi^2}\), \(\int_0^Lx\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx=\frac{L^2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\). (\(\int_{-\pi}^\pi x\cos nx=0\), \(\int_{-\pi}^\pi x^2\sin nx=0\): odd integrands.)

\(n\)12345
\(\cos n\pi\)−11−11−1
\(\sin n\pi\)00000
\(\sin\frac{n\pi}2\)10−101
\(\cos\frac{n\pi}2\)0−1010
\(1-(-1)^n\)20202

Rules: \(\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\); \(\sin n\pi=0\); \(\sin\frac{n\pi}2=(-1)^{k+1}\) for odd \(n=2k-1\), 0 for even \(n\); \(\cos\frac{n\pi}2=(-1)^k\) for even \(n=2k\), 0 for odd \(n\); \(1-(-1)^n\) keeps only odd \(n\).

In words: one integration by parts for \(x\), two for \(x^2\); every boundary term at \(\pi\) turns into a \((-1)^n\), and \((-1)^n-1\) is \(-2\) for odd \(n\) and 0 for even \(n\).

Ready answer · shifted copy

If \(g(x)=f(x-c)+d\) and \(f=\frac12a_0+\sum(a_n\cos nx+b_n\sin nx)\), then \[\begin{aligned}g(x)&=\tfrac12a_0+d\\&\quad+\sum\big[a_n\cos n(x-c)\\&\qquad+b_n\sin n(x-c)\big]\end{aligned}\]. For \(c=\pi\): \(\cos n(x-\pi)=(-1)^n\cos nx\), \(\sin n(x-\pi)=(-1)^n\sin nx\).

In words: sliding the graph sideways by half a period flips the sign of every odd harmonic; lifting it adds to the constant term only.

Dec 2025 Q4(b)(i)–(ii)4 + 4 marks

Consider the \(2\pi\)-periodic function \(f(t)=f(t+2\pi)\) where \(f(t)=t^2\) on \([-\pi,\pi)\).

(i) Plot the function \(f(t)\) from \(t\in[-2\pi,2\pi)\). (ii) Find the Fourier series of \(f(t)\).

Hint

Even function. Only \(a_0\) and \(a_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi t^2\cos nt\,dt\) (by parts twice).

Solution
  1. (i) A parabola \(t^2\) on each period, repeated every \(2\pi\). At \(t=\pm\pi\) both sides give \(\pi^2\), so the graph is continuous, with corners.

    \(f(t)\) on \([-2\pi,2\pi)\): value 0 at \(t=0,\pm2\pi\), value \(\pi^2\approx9.87\) at \(t=\pm\pi\).

  2. \(t^2\) is even, so \(b_n=0\).
  3. \(a_0=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi t^2\,dt=\frac{2\pi^2}3\), so \(\frac12a_0=\frac{\pi^2}3\).
  4. \[\begin{aligned}a_n &= \frac2\pi\int_0^\pi t^2\cos nt\,dt\\ &= \frac2\pi\cdot\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}\\ &= \frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\end{aligned}\] (two integrations by parts; the \(\sin n\pi\) terms vanish).

Say “\(f\) is even, so \(b_n=0\)” before dropping the sine terms; the mark is for the reason.

\(\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{\pi^2}3+4\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos nt\)

Written out: \(\frac{\pi^2}3-4\cos t+\cos2t-\frac49\cos3t+\cdots\). Used in C4.2.

Dec 2024 Q4(b)(i)–(ii)5 + 7 marks

Consider the \(2\pi\)-periodic odd function \(f(t)=f(t+2\pi)\) where

\[f(t)=\begin{cases}-\pi&-\pi\le t\lt-\frac\pi2\\2t&-\frac\pi2\le t\lt\frac\pi2\\ \pi&\frac\pi2\le t\lt\pi\end{cases}\]

(i) Plot the function \(f(t)\) from \(t\in[-2\pi,2\pi)\). (ii) Find the Fourier series of \(f(t)\). (You may keep the term \(\sin\left(\frac{n\pi}{2}\right)\) without further manipulation.)

Hint

Odd: \(a_n=0\). \(b_n=\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi f\sin nt\,dt\), split at \(\frac\pi2\).

Solution
  1. (i) Ramp of slope 2 from \((-\frac\pi2,-\pi)\) to \((\frac\pi2,\pi)\), flat at \(\pi\) on \([\frac\pi2,\pi)\), flat at \(-\pi\) on \([-\pi,-\frac\pi2)\); repeated. Jumps from \(\pi\) to \(-\pi\) at \(t=\pm\pi\) (filled dot = value taken, since intervals are \([\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)\)).

    \(f(t)\) on \([-2\pi,2\pi)\). At the jumps \(t=\pm\pi\) the Fourier series gives 0, the average. Orange dots: the value the Fourier series takes at each jump.

  2. \(f\) is odd, so \(a_0=a_n=0\) and \[\begin{aligned}b_n &= \frac2\pi\Big[\int_0^{\pi/2}2t\sin nt\,dt\\ &\quad+\int_{\pi/2}^\pi\pi\sin nt\,dt\Big]\end{aligned}\].
  3. \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^{\pi/2}2t\sin nt\,dt &= -\frac{\pi\cos(n\pi/2)}n\\ &\quad+\frac{2\sin(n\pi/2)}{n^2}\end{aligned}\]; \(\int_{\pi/2}^\pi\pi\sin nt\,dt=\frac\pi n\big[\cos\frac{n\pi}2-(-1)^n\big]\).
  4. Add: the \(\cos\frac{n\pi}2\) terms cancel, leaving \(\frac{2\sin(n\pi/2)}{n^2}-\frac{\pi(-1)^n}n\); multiply by \(\frac2\pi\).

The cancellation of the \(\cos\frac{n\pi}2\) terms is the check that your by-parts signs are right; if they don't cancel, look again.

\[\begin{aligned}f(t) &= \sum_{n=1}^\infty\Big[\frac{4\sin(n\pi/2)}{\pi n^2}\\ &\quad-\frac{2(-1)^n}n\Big]\sin nt\end{aligned}\]

\(b_1=2+\frac4\pi\), \(b_2=-1\), \(b_3=\frac23-\frac4{9\pi}\), …

Dec 2023 Q3(b)(i)–(ii)7 + 3 marks

Also set: Dec 2015 Q4(b)(i)–(ii) (7 + 5 marks; the 2015 hint for (ii) reads “the graph of \(g(x)\) is a translational shift of that of \(f(x)\)”)

Let \(f(x)\) be a \(2\pi\)-periodic function.

(i) Consider \(f(x)=2x+1\) as periodic over \((-\pi,\pi)\). Sketch the periodic function over \((-2\pi,2\pi)\) and show that the Fourier series of \(f(x)\) is of the form \(A+\sum_{n=1}^\infty B_n\sin nx\). Find \(A\) and \(B_n\).

(ii) Consider \(g(x)=2x+1\) as periodic over \((0,2\pi)\). Sketch the periodic function over \((-2\pi,2\pi)\). Use the Fourier series obtained in (i), or otherwise, to find the Fourier series of \(g(x)\). (Hint: use the relationship between \(g(x)\) and \(f(x)\).)

Hint

(i) \(f-1=2x\) is odd. (ii) For \(0\lt x\lt2\pi\), \(x-\pi\) lies in \((-\pi,\pi)\): compare \(g(x)\) with \(f(x-\pi)\).

Solution
  1. (i) Sketch: straight pieces of slope 2 from \(1-2\pi\) up to \(1+2\pi\) on each \((-\pi+2k\pi,\pi+2k\pi)\); jumps of \(-4\pi\) at \(x=\pm\pi\).

    \(f\): saw-tooth through \((0,1)\); open dots at the ends \(1\pm2\pi\approx7.28,\,-5.28\); the series gives 1 at the jumps. Orange dots: the value the Fourier series takes at each jump.

  2. \(f(x)-1=2x\) is odd, so \(a_n=\frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi(2x+1)\cos nx\,dx=0\) for \(n\ge1\): the series has the form \(A+\sum B_n\sin nx\).
  3. \(A=\frac12a_0=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi(2x+1)\,dx=1\).
  4. \[\begin{aligned}B_n &= \frac1\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi(2x+1)\sin nx\,dx\\ &= \frac2\pi\int_{-\pi}^\pi x\sin nx\,dx\\ &= \frac2\pi\cdot\frac{2\pi(-1)^{n+1}}n\\ &= \frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}n\end{aligned}\].
  5. (ii) Sketch: pieces of slope 2 from 1 to \(1+4\pi\) on each \((2k\pi,2k\pi+2\pi)\); jumps at \(x=0,\pm2\pi\).

    \(g\): the same saw-tooth slid right by \(\pi\) and lifted by \(2\pi\); the series gives \(1+2\pi\) at the jumps. Orange dots: the value the Fourier series takes at each jump.

  6. For \(0\lt x\lt2\pi\): \(f(x-\pi)=2(x-\pi)+1=g(x)-2\pi\), so \(g(x)=f(x-\pi)+2\pi\) for all \(x\) (both sides \(2\pi\)-periodic).
  7. \[\begin{aligned}g(x) &= 1+2\pi\\ &\quad+\sum\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}n\sin n(x-\pi)\end{aligned}\] and \(\sin n(x-\pi)=(-1)^n\sin nx\), so the coefficient is \(\frac{4(-1)^{2n+1}}n=-\frac4n\).

In (i) “show the form” needs the reason \(a_n=0\) (odd integrand), not just its value.

\[\begin{aligned}&\text{(i) } A=1,\quad B_n=\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}n\\ &\text{(ii) } g(x)=1+2\pi-4\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin nx}n\end{aligned}\]

Check (ii) directly: the average of \(2x+1\) over \((0,2\pi)\) is \(2\pi+1\), and \(\frac1\pi\int_0^{2\pi}(2x+1)\sin nx\,dx=-\frac4n\). Dec 2015 (iii) continues this question: C4.2.

Dec 2022 Q4(b)(i)–(ii)4 + 6 marks

Consider the \(2\pi\)-periodic function \(f(t)=\begin{cases}0&-\pi\lt t\le0\\ t&0\lt t\le\pi\end{cases}\)

(i) Sketch \(f(t)\) over \([-2\pi,2\pi]\). (ii) Show that the Fourier series of \(f(t)\) is of the form \[\begin{aligned}&A+B\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\cos(2n-1)t}{(2n-1)^2}\\&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\frac{\sin nt}{n}\end{aligned}\]. Find \(A\), \(B\) and \(C_n\).

Hint

Neither even nor odd. All integrals live on \([0,\pi]\): \(\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi t\cos nt\,dt\) and \(\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi t\sin nt\,dt\).

Solution
  1. (i) Zero on \((-\pi,0]\), a ramp from 0 up to \(\pi\) on \((0,\pi]\); repeated. Jump from \(\pi\) down to 0 at \(t=\pm\pi\) (value \(\pi\) taken there).

    \(f(t)\) on \([-2\pi,2\pi]\); filled dots at \((\pm\pi,\pi)\), open at \((\pm\pi,0)\).

  2. \(a_0=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi t\,dt=\frac\pi2\Rightarrow A=\frac12a_0=\frac\pi4\).
  3. \(a_n=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi t\cos nt\,dt=\frac{(-1)^n-1}{\pi n^2}\): 0 for even \(n\), \(-\frac2{\pi n^2}\) for odd \(n=2k-1\). So \(\sum a_n\cos nt=-\frac2\pi\sum\frac{\cos(2k-1)t}{(2k-1)^2}\).
  4. \[\begin{aligned}b_n &= \frac1\pi\int_0^\pi t\sin nt\,dt\\ &= \frac1\pi\cdot\frac{\pi(-1)^{n+1}}n\\ &= \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}n\end{aligned}\], i.e. \(C_n=(-1)^{n+1}\).

Show the odd/even split of \(a_n\) explicitly; that step is what “show it has the form” is testing.

\(\displaystyle A=\frac\pi4,\quad B=-\frac2\pi,\quad C_n=(-1)^{n+1}\)

Used in C4.2.

Dec 2018 Q3(b)(i)–(ii)4 + 8 marks

Consider the \(2\pi\)-periodic function \(f(x)=\begin{cases}2-\dfrac x\pi&0\le x\lt\pi\\[3pt] \dfrac{2x}\pi-2&\pi\le x\lt2\pi\end{cases}\)

(i) Sketch \(f(x)\) over \([-2\pi,2\pi]\). (ii) Show that the Fourier series of \(f(x)\) is of the form \[\begin{aligned}&A+B\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\cos(2n-1)x}{(2n-1)^2}\\&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^\infty C_n\frac{\sin nx}{n}\end{aligned}\]. Find \(A\), \(B\) and \(C_n\).

Hint

Integrate over \([0,2\pi]\) as given, split at \(\pi\). You need \(\int x\cos nx\) and \(\int x\sin nx\) on \([0,\pi]\) and on \([\pi,2\pi]\).

Solution
  1. (i) On \([0,\pi)\): a line falling from 2 to 1. On \([\pi,2\pi)\): a line rising from 0 to 2. Jump from 1 down to 0 at \(x=\pi\) (and \(-\pi\)); continuous at \(x=0,\pm2\pi\) (value 2).

    \(f(x)\) on \([-2\pi,2\pi]\); jump at \(x=\pm\pi\) from 1 to 0 (series value \(\frac12\)). Orange dots: the value the Fourier series takes at each jump.

  2. \[\begin{aligned}a_0 &= \frac1\pi\Big[\int_0^\pi(2-\frac x\pi)dx\\ &\quad+\int_\pi^{2\pi}(\frac{2x}\pi-2)dx\Big]\\ &= \frac1\pi\Big[\frac{3\pi}2+\pi\Big]=\frac52\end{aligned}\] so \(A=\frac12a_0=\frac54\).
  3. With \(\int_0^\pi x\cos nx=\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2}\), \(\int_\pi^{2\pi}x\cos nx=\frac{1-(-1)^n}{n^2}\) and \(\int\cos nx=0\) over each half: \[\begin{aligned}a_n &= \frac1\pi\Big[\frac{1-(-1)^n}{\pi n^2}+\frac{2(1-(-1)^n)}{\pi n^2}\Big]\\ &= \frac{3(1-(-1)^n)}{\pi^2n^2}\end{aligned}\] → \(\frac6{\pi^2n^2}\) for odd \(n\), 0 for even \(n\).
  4. \(b_n\): \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^\pi(2-\frac x\pi)\sin nx\,dx &= \frac{2(1-(-1)^n)}n\\ &\quad+\frac{(-1)^n}n\end{aligned}\] and \(\int_\pi^{2\pi}(\frac{2x}\pi-2)\sin nx\,dx=-\frac2n\). Sum \(=-\frac{(-1)^n}n\), so \(b_n=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi n}\).

Common slip: integrating over \([-\pi,\pi]\) with the \([0,2\pi)\) formulas. Stay on \([0,2\pi]\) (the sheet allows any \([a,a+T]\)).

\(\displaystyle A=\frac54,\quad B=\frac6{\pi^2},\quad C_n=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}\pi\)
Dec 2017 Q4(b)(i)–(ii)4 + 6 marks

Let \(f(t)\) be a 2-periodic function (\(f(t+2)=f(t)\)) given by \(f(t)=\begin{cases}\pi t&0\lt t\lt1\\ \pi(2-t)&1\le t\lt2\end{cases}\)

(i) Sketch the function over \((-2,4)\). Is \(f(t)\) an even or odd function? (ii) Find the Fourier series representation of \(f(t)\).

Hint

Triangle wave. \(T=2\), \(L=1\), \(\omega=\pi\). Even, so \(a_n=2\int_0^1\pi t\cos n\pi t\,dt\).

Solution
  1. (i) Triangles: 0 at even \(t\), peak \(\pi\) at odd \(t\).

    \(f(t)\) on \((-2,4)\): continuous triangle wave, height \(\pi\).

  2. Even: for \(-1\lt t\lt0\), \[\begin{aligned}f(t) &= f(t+2)\\ &= \pi(2-(t+2))\\ &= -\pi t\\ &= f(-t)\end{aligned}\]. So \(b_n=0\).
  3. \(a_0=\frac22\int_0^2f\,dt=\pi\) (triangle area \(\frac12\cdot2\cdot\pi\)), so \(\frac12a_0=\frac\pi2\).
  4. \[\begin{aligned}a_n &= 2\int_0^1\pi t\cos n\pi t\,dt\\ &= 2\pi\cdot\frac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2\pi^2}\\ &= \frac{2[(-1)^n-1]}{\pi n^2}\end{aligned}\]: \(-\frac4{\pi n^2}\) for odd \(n\), 0 for even.

Give the reason for “even” (a symmetry check or the sketch), not just the word.

\(\displaystyle f(t)=\frac\pi2-\frac4\pi\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\cos(2n-1)\pi t}{(2n-1)^2}\)

Dec 2016 Q4(b)(i)–(ii)4 + 9 marks

Let \(f(x)\) be \(2\pi\)-periodic, \(f(x)=\begin{cases}\pi^2&-\pi\lt x\lt0\\ (x-\pi)^2&0\lt x\lt\pi\end{cases}\)

(i) Sketch the function over \((-3\pi,3\pi)\). (ii) Show that the Fourier series representation of \(f(x)\) has the form

\[\begin{aligned}&A+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{B}{n^2}\cos nx+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac Cn\sin nx\\&\quad+D\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\sin(2n-1)x}{(2n-1)^3}.\end{aligned}\]

Find \(A,B,C,D\).

Hint

Neither even nor odd. For the \((x-\pi)^2\) piece substitute \(u=\pi-x\): then \(\cos n(\pi-u)=(-1)^n\cos nu\), \(\sin n(\pi-u)=(-1)^{n+1}\sin nu\).

Solution
  1. (i) Flat at \(\pi^2\) on \((-\pi,0)\), then a parabola falling from \(\pi^2\) at 0 to 0 at \(\pi\); repeated. Continuous at \(x=0\); jump from 0 up to \(\pi^2\) at \(x=\pm\pi,\pm3\pi\).

C4.2Sum a number series by evaluating a Fourier series at a point

7 parts · 7 sittings (Dec 2025, 2022, 2017, 2015; May 2019, 2018, 2016) · 25 marks · always the last part after a C4.1 series · Learn it · note §3

Type card · predictable
Looks like
“Use the series in (ii) to show / deduce that \(\frac{\pi^2}{8}=\sum\frac1{(2n-1)^2}\)” (3–4 marks).
Which point?
  • Target has all plus signs: cosine series with \((-1)^n\) in the coefficient → \(t=\pi\) (\(\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\) cancels it); coefficients without \((-1)^n\) → \(t=0\).
  • Target alternates over all \(n\) (\(1-\frac1{2^2}+\frac1{3^2}\dots\)): cosine series with \((-1)^n\) → \(t=0\).
  • Target alternates over odd \(n\) only (\(1-\frac13+\frac15\dots\)): sine series → \(t=\frac\pi2\) (\(\sin\frac{n\pi}2=1,0,-1,0\)), or \(t=\frac L2\) for period \(2L\).
  • Target is \(\sum\frac1{(2n-1)^2}\) from a series with only odd cosines → \(t=0\) (or wherever all the cosines equal 1).
Do
1. Pick the point. 2. Check \(f\) is continuous there (value \(f(t_0)\)) or not (value = jump average). 3. Substitute, move the constant across, solve for the sum.
Watch
At a jump the series equals \(\frac12[f(t_0^-)+f(t_0^+)]\), not \(f(t_0)\). State it: that line carries a mark.
Ready answer · convergence (Dirichlet) theorem (not on the sheet)

If \(f\) is periodic and piecewise smooth (finitely many jumps and corners per period), its Fourier series converges at every \(t\) to

\[\frac{f(t^-)+f(t^+)}2\]

which is \(f(t)\) wherever \(f\) is continuous.

Sums worth knowing (check your answer against them): \(\sum\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}6\), \(\sum\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}{12}\), \(\sum\frac1{(2n-1)^2}=\frac{\pi^2}8\), \(\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{(-1)^k}{2k+1}=\frac\pi4\), \(\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}\), \(\sum\frac1{(2n-1)^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{96}\).

In words: the series lands on the graph where the graph is unbroken, and exactly halfway up the gap where it jumps.

C4.3Half-range sine and cosine series

6 parts · 5 sittings (May 2026, 2024, 2021, 2017, 2015) · 64 marks · not yet in a December paper, but in the syllabus and needed for C5 · Learn it · note §5

Type card · predictable
Looks like
\(f\) given only on \([0,L]\) (“half range”); “sketch the even / odd periodic extension, state its period, find the cosine / sine series”, or “organise \(f\) as an even function”.
Do
1. Sketch \(f\) on \([0,L]\); mirror it in the \(y\)-axis (even → cosine series) or rotate it half a turn about the origin (odd → sine series) onto \([-L,0]\); repeat every \(2L\). 2. State the period \(2L\). 3. Use the half-range formulas. 4. Write out the first non-zero terms.
Watch
\(L\) is the length of the given interval, so the harmonics are \(\frac{n\pi x}L\) (not \(\frac{2n\pi x}L\)). And “fundamental period” is the smallest period: if \(f\) is symmetric about \(\frac L2\), the even extension repeats every \(L\) (May 2015).
Ready answer · half-range formulas (not on the sheet)

For \(f\) given on \(0\lt x\lt L\) (extensions have period \(2L\)):

\[\begin{aligned}\text{cosine: }f&=\tfrac12a_0+\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}L,\\ a_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\ \ (n\ge0);\\ \text{sine: }f&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L,\\ b_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

At the ends: the sine series gives 0 at \(x=0\) and \(x=L\) (jump average of an odd function); the cosine series gives \(f(0^+)\) and \(f(L^-)\). Which approximates better: note §5 Beyond box.

In words: mirror for cosines, rotate for sines, then use the even/odd short cut on the doubled interval.

Consider \(f(x)=x^2\) where \(0\lt x\lt L\). We are going to extend the above half range to the negative axis and represent \(f(x)\) as a Fourier sine series or a Fourier cosine series.

(b) Sketch 3 periods and determine the corresponding Fourier representation based on your answer.

Hint

(a) Which extension has jumps? (b) Even extension of \(x^2\) = \(x^2\) on \((-L,L)\), period \(2L\).

Solution
Answer skeleton · part (a)
  1. Name the choice: the cosine series (even extension).
  2. Reason 1: the even extension is continuous (at \(x=\pm L\) both sides give \(L^2\)); the odd extension jumps from \(L^2\) to \(-L^2\) at \(x=\pm L\).
  3. Reason 2: so the cosine coefficients fall like \(\frac1{n^2}\), the sine coefficients only like \(\frac1n\): fewer terms for the same accuracy.
  4. Reason 3: at a jump the sine series converges to the average 0 (not \(L^2\)) and overshoots nearby (Gibbs).

Model answer: “The Fourier cosine series. The even extension of \(x^2\) is continuous everywhere, while the odd extension has jumps of \(2L^2\) at \(x=\pm L,\pm3L,\dots\). Coefficients of a continuous piecewise-smooth function decay like \(1/n^2\), those of a function with jumps only like \(1/n\), and near a jump the partial sums overshoot (Gibbs) and give 0 at \(x=L\). So for a fixed number of terms the cosine series is closer.”

  1. (b) Even extension, period \(2L\): parabolas \(x^2\) on each \([-L+2kL,L+2kL]\), corners at odd multiples of \(L\).

    Even extension on \([-3L,3L]\), 3 periods; value \(L^2\) at \(x=\pm L,\pm3L\).

  2. \(b_n=0\). \(a_0=\frac2L\int_0^Lx^2dx=\frac{2L^2}3\Rightarrow\frac12a_0=\frac{L^2}3\).
  3. \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lx^2\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx=\frac{4L^2(-1)^n}{n^2\pi^2}\) (by parts twice; or scale the ready answer \(\int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx=\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2}\) with \(x=\frac{Lu}\pi\)).

In (a) the mark is for the reason (continuity / jumps / decay rate), not for the word “cosine”.

\[\begin{aligned}x^2&=\frac{L^2}3+\frac{4L^2}{\pi^2}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\\&\qquad(0\le x\le L)\end{aligned}\]

For comparison, the sine series has \(b_n=\frac{2L^2(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}+\frac{4L^2[(-1)^n-1]}{n^3\pi^3}\), size about \(\frac{2L^2}{n\pi}\). Part (c) is C4.4.

C4.4Parseval's identity to sum a series

2 parts · 2 sittings (May 2021, May 2017) · 16 marks · plus May 2015 Q4(iv), 4 marks, solved under C4.3 · Learn it · note §6

Type card · predictable
Looks like
“Apply Parseval's identity to the Fourier series above to find \(\sum\frac1{n^4}\)” (or \(\sum\frac1{(2n-1)^4}\)): the coefficients are \(\sim\frac1{n^2}\) and the target has \(n^4\).
Do
1. Write Parseval in the form below for the period used. 2. Left side: mean of \(f^2\) over one period (an easy integral). 3. Right side: \((\frac12a_0)^2+\frac12\sum(a_n^2+b_n^2)\) with your coefficients. 4. Solve for the sum.
Watch
The constant enters squared and without the \(\frac12\): \((\frac12a_0)^2\), which is \(A_0^2\) on the May sheet. Square the whole coefficient, \((\frac{4}{n^2\pi^2})^2=\frac{16}{n^4\pi^4}\).
Ready answer · Parseval's identity (not on the sheet) \[\begin{aligned}\frac1T\int_a^{a+T}f(t)^2\,dt&=\Big(\frac{a_0}2\Big)^2\\&\quad+\frac12\sum_{n=1}^\infty\big(a_n^2+b_n^2\big)\end{aligned}\]

Half-range on \([0,L]\): \(\frac1L\int_0^Lf^2\,dx=(\frac{a_0}2)^2+\frac12\sum a_n^2\) (cosine series) or \(=\frac12\sum b_n^2\) (sine series).

In words: the average of \(f^2\) over a period = (average of \(f\))\(^2\) + half the sum of the squared amplitudes of all the harmonics.

◆Before the exam

  • Sketch first, always: 2–3 periods, the range asked for, values at the ends labelled, open/filled dots at jumps.
  • Check symmetry before integrating: even → no \(b_n\); odd (or odd after subtracting a constant) → no \(a_n\). Say it in words.
  • Period \(T\ne2\pi\)? Then \(\omega=\frac{2\pi}T\) and every harmonic is \(\cos\frac{n\pi t}L\), \(\sin\frac{n\pi t}L\) with \(L=\frac T2\).
  • The constant term is \(\frac12a_0\) = average of \(f\) over a period (May sheet: \(A_0\)). Quick check: area of one period ÷ \(T\).
  • Know \(\int_0^\pi x\cos nx\), \(\int_0^\pi x\sin nx\), \(\int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx\) and the \((-1)^n\), \(\sin\frac{n\pi}2\), \(\cos\frac{n\pi}2\) patterns by heart (ready answer).
  • “Show it has the form …”: get the general \(a_n,b_n\), then split odd/even \(n\) and match term by term.
  • Series sums: choose \(t=0\), \(\pi\) or \(\frac\pi2\) to match the signs; at a jump use the average (ready answer). Check against \(\frac{\pi^2}6,\frac{\pi^2}8,\frac{\pi^2}{12},\frac\pi4\).
  • Half-range: the period of the extension is \(2L\) (twice the given interval); cosine series for “even”, sine series for “odd”. The cosine series usually converges faster.
  • Parseval: \(\frac1T\int f^2=(\frac12a_0)^2+\frac12\sum(a_n^2+b_n^2)\); target \(\sum\frac1{n^4}=\frac{\pi^4}{90}\).
  • Budget about 20 minutes for a 12-mark Fourier part. If the coefficients refuse to simplify into the given form, re-check the symmetry, the period and the limits before redoing the algebra.
