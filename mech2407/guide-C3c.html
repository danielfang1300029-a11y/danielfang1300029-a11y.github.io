◆Exam format and formula sheet for this chapter

  • December (main format): 3 hours, five 25-mark questions, answer any four (Q5, PDEs, is compulsory). This chapter sits in Q2–Q4, usually as a 10–17-mark part (a) or (b) that opens with a 2–5-mark div/curl line (type C3a.1). Typical slots: Dec 2025 Q2(a) potential, Q3(b) Gauss, Q4(a) flux; Dec 2024 Q3(a) Gauss, Q4(a) potential; Dec 2023 Q3(a) Gauss, Q4(a) hole field; Dec 2022 Q3(a) hole field, Q4(a) Stokes + Gauss; Dec 2018 and Dec 2021 also a 5–6-mark potential in Q1(c).
  • Dec 2021 Q3 and Q4 are missing from the scans, so we cannot say what vector-calculus parts that paper had beyond Q1(c). Every other December paper (2014–2018, 2022–2025) has at least one C3c part.
  • Time budget: 25 marks ≈ 45 min per question, so ≈ 1.8 min per mark: a 10–13-mark “verify Gauss/Stokes” ≈ 20 min (about half on each side), a 4-mark potential ≈ 7 min.
  • What the December formula sheet gives (same sheet 2014–2025): the surface integral over \(z=f(x,y)\), \[\begin{aligned}\iint_\Sigma g\,dS&=\iint_R g\big(x,y,f\big)\\&\qquad\times\sqrt{f_x^2+f_y^2+1}\,dx\,dy;\end{aligned}\] the line integrals \(\int_Cg\,ds\) and \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\int\vb F(t)\cdot\dot{\vb r}(t)\,dt\); the flux through \(z=f(x,y)\) with upward normal, \[\iint_\Sigma\vb F\cdot\vb n\,dS=\iint_R\big[-Mf_x-Nf_y+P\big]dx\,dy;\] and the statements of Gauss' theorem \(\iiint_V\operatorname{div}\vb F\,dV=\iint_\Sigma\vb F\cdot\vb n\,dS\) and Stokes' theorem \(\iint_\Sigma\operatorname{curl}\vb F\cdot\vb n\,dS=\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\).
  • What it leaves out (so they are Ready-answer boxes on this page): the curl and div formulas and the curl test (C3c.1), the potential recipe and “work = \(\varphi(B)-\varphi(A)\)” (C3c.1), the hole field and its \(2\pi\) (C3c.2), \(dS\) and outward normals on spheres, cylinders and planes (C3c.3), the closed-surface bookkeeping for Gauss (C3c.4) and the orientation (right-hand) rule for Stokes (C3c.5). Also not given: the conditions of the theorems (continuous partial derivatives, simply connected region), which C3c.2 asks about.
  • Notation: December papers write \(\vb F\), \(\vb n\), \(dS\), \(\Sigma\); May papers write \(\vec F\), \(\hat n\), \(d\sigma\), \(S\). Same things. “Irrotational” = curl \(=\vb 0\); “conservative” = \(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\) for some scalar potential \(\varphi\).
  • May format (other semester; useful practice, different rules): 2015–2019 five 20-mark questions, all compulsory, with Q3 = (a) conservative field (C3c.1) + (b) Green's theorem (now out of syllabus) + (c) Stokes (C3c.5); a formula sheet with the projection formula \(d\sigma=\frac{|\nabla f|}{|\nabla f\cdot\vb p|}dA\) and Green's theorem. 2021–2024: Q3 has three 10-mark parts from this chapter; no formula sheet. 2025–2026: eight 10-mark questions, three of them from this chapter (work done, Stokes/Green, Gauss). May questions say “work done”, “circulation”, “outward flux” rather than “verify”.
  • Exam-format changes: “verify Gauss/Stokes” (both sides) is the December style every year; “use the theorem to find the flux/circulation” (one side, the easier one) is the May style, and Dec 2022 Q4(a) asked for both the Stokes and the Gauss short cut for one flux. Green's theorem appeared only in May papers and is out of the 2026 syllabus (guide-prev).

C3c.1Conservative fields: curl test, constants, scalar potential, work

22 parts · 18 sittings (Dec 2025, 2024, 2021, 2018, 2017, 2016, 2015; May 2015–2026 except 2020) · 144 marks · Learn it · note §1 curl test · note §2

Every one of these is the same three moves (curl, potential, end-point values), but the question is dressed differently each year: unknown constants, a differential form \(\int M\,dx+N\,dy+P\,dz\), a curved or two-piece path, “work done”. Hence a procedure rather than a single card.

Decision procedure
  1. “Show \(\vb F\) is conservative / irrotational” → compute \(\nabla\times\vb F\) (ready answer). In 2D only the \(\kk\)-component \(N_x-M_y\) exists. Then say: “\(\nabla\times\vb F=\vb 0\) on \(\R^2\) (or \(\R^3\)), which is simply connected, so \(\vb F\) is conservative.” Dec 2018/2021 Q1(c) accept “by finding its potential” instead: a \(\varphi\) with \(\nabla\varphi=\vb F\) is the proof.
  2. Unknown constants (\(a\), \(b\), \(k\), \(\alpha\), \(\beta\)) → write each curl component, then make it zero for all \(x,y,z\): collect the coefficient of each monomial (\(xy\), \(y^2\), \(yz\sin xz\), …) and set it to 0. If powers carry the constant (\(y^ae^{xy}\)), substitute one convenient point and solve.
  3. Find \(\varphi\) → \(\varphi=\int M\,dx+g(y,z)\); differentiate in \(y\), match \(N\) to get \(g_y\); integrate; then the same in \(z\) (ready answer). Finish with a one-line check \(\nabla\varphi=\vb F\).
  4. Field given as a differential \(\int_A^B M\,dx+N\,dy+P\,dz\) → it is \(\int\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) with \(\vb F=(M,N,P)\). Look for a potential first, even if no one asks: these are always exact.
  5. Work / line integral from \(A\) to \(B\) with a conservative field → \(W=\varphi(B)-\varphi(A)\), whatever the path (straight, circular, two segments, half circle). State “path-independent since \(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\)”. Closed path → 0.
  6. Curl \(\ne\vb 0\), a “general \(k\)”, or “do NOT use the Fundamental Theorem” → parametrise the path: that is type C3b.1.
  7. Denominator \(x^2+y^2\) (a hole at the origin) → curl \(=\vb 0\) is not enough: go to C3c.2.

Cues: “irrotational”, “conservative”, “scalar potential”, “simply connected region”, “work done … from \(P\) to \(Q\)”, “hence, or otherwise”, an integrand written as \(M\,dx+N\,dy+P\,dz\).

Traps:

  • Writing \(\varphi=\int M\,dx+C\) with a plain constant: the “constant” is a function \(g(y,z)\) and usually is not zero.
  • Integrating each component and adding them: terms like \(xyz\) get counted three times. Integrate once, then only add what is missing.
  • Setting the curl to zero at one point only: it must vanish for all \(x,y,z\) (compare coefficients).
  • Sign and order: \(W=\varphi(\text{end})-\varphi(\text{start})\); with \(\ln y\) or \(1/x\) in \(\varphi\), make sure the path stays where they are defined.
Ready answer · curl and the curl test (not on the sheet)

For \(\vb F=M\,\ii+N\,\jj+P\,\kk\):

\[\begin{aligned}\nabla\times\vb F&=\big(P_y-N_z\big)\,\ii\\&\quad+\big(M_z-P_x\big)\,\jj\\&\quad+\big(N_x-M_y\big)\,\kk.\end{aligned}\]

In the plane (\(P=0\), no \(z\)): \(\nabla\times\vb F=(N_x-M_y)\,\kk\). If \(\nabla\times\vb F=\vb 0\) on a simply connected region (\(\R^2\), \(\R^3\), a half-plane, a ball) then \(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\) there.

In words: curl zero everywhere on a region with no holes means the field is the gradient of some potential; each curl component compares two cross-derivatives.

Ready answer · potential recipe and work \[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=\int M\,dx+g(y,z),\\ \varphi_y&=N\ \Rightarrow\ g(y,z)=\int(\dots)\,dy+h(z),\\ \varphi_z&=P\ \Rightarrow\ h(z).\end{aligned}\] \[\int_A^B\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\varphi(B)-\varphi(A).\]

In words: integrate the \(x\)-component in \(x\), let the other components tell you what the “constant” must be; then the work is the potential at the end minus the potential at the start, whatever path joins them.

Past-paper parts (December, newest first)

Dec 2025 Q2(a)(ii)–(iii)5 + 4 marks

Let \(\vb F(x,y,z)=(2xy+z)\,\ii\) \(+\,(x^2+z^2)\,\jj\) \(+\,(x+2yz)\,\kk\) be a vector field defined on \(\R^3\). (Part (i), C3a.1: \(\operatorname{curl}\vb F=\vb 0\).)

(ii) Find a scalar potential of \(\vb F\).

(iii) Use the scalar potential in 2(a)(ii), or otherwise, evaluate the work integral \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) where \(C\) is the straight line path from \((0,0,0)\) to \((1,2,3)\).

Hint

Integrate \(M=2xy+z\) in \(x\), then match \(N\) and \(P\). For (iii): end-point values.

Solution
  1. \(\varphi=\int(2xy+z)\,dx\) \(=x^2y+xz+g(y,z)\).
  2. \(\varphi_y=x^2+g_y\) must equal \(x^2+z^2\), so \(g_y=z^2\) and \(g=yz^2+h(z)\).
  3. \(\varphi_z=x+2yz+h'(z)\) must equal \(x+2yz\), so \(h'=0\). So \(\varphi=x^2y+xz+yz^2\) (+ any constant).
  4. (iii) \(\operatorname{curl}\vb F=\vb 0\) on \(\R^3\), so the integral is path-independent: \(\varphi(1,2,3)-\varphi(0,0,0)\) \(=2+3+18=23\).

In (iii) say why the end points suffice (\(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\)); a straight-line parametrisation \((t,2t,3t)\) gives the same 23 as a check.

\[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=x^2y+xz+yz^2,\\ \int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r&=23\end{aligned}\]
Dec 2024 Q4(a)(i)–(ii)4 + 4 marks

Consider a vector field defined in \(\R^3\) given by \(\vb F=y^ae^{xy}\,\ii+x^ae^{xy}\,\jj\) where \(a\gt0\) is a constant.

(i) Find the value of \(a\) such that \(\vb F\) is irrotational.

(ii) With \(a\) in Q4(a)(i), find a scalar potential of \(\vb F\).

Part (iii), a work integral by parametrisation, is type C3b.1: C3b guide.

Hint

No \(z\) and no \(\kk\)-component, so only \(N_x-M_y\) can be nonzero. It must vanish for every \((x,y)\): try one point such as \((1,2)\).

Solution
  1. \(P=0\) and \(M,N\) have no \(z\), so \(\nabla\times\vb F=(N_x-M_y)\,\kk\).
  2. \(N_x=(ax^{a-1}+x^ay)e^{xy}\), \(M_y=(ay^{a-1}+y^ax)e^{xy}\) (product rule).
  3. Need \(ax^{a-1}+x^ay=ay^{a-1}+y^ax\) for all \(x,y\). At \((1,2)\): \(a+2=(a+2)\,2^{a-1}\), so \(2^{a-1}=1\), \(a=1\).
  4. Check \(a=1\): both sides are \((1+xy)e^{xy}\), so the curl is \(\vb 0\).
  5. (ii) \(\varphi=\int ye^{xy}\,dx=e^{xy}+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=xe^{xy}+g_y=xe^{xy}\Rightarrow g_y=0\); \(\varphi_z=g_z=0\).

Show that \(a=1\) works for all \(x,y\) (step 4), not only that it solves the equation at one point.

\(a=1,\qquad \varphi=e^{xy}\ (+\text{const})\)
Dec 2021 Q1(c)6 marks

Show that the vector field \(\vb F(x,y)=(2xe^y+y)\,\ii+(x^2e^y+x-2y)\,\jj\) defined on the \(XY\)-plane is conservative by finding its scalar potential.

Hint

\(\varphi=\int(2xe^y+y)\,dx+g(y)\).

Solution
  1. \(\varphi=\int(2xe^y+y)\,dx\) \(=x^2e^y+xy+g(y)\).
  2. \(\varphi_y=x^2e^y+x+g'(y)\) must equal \(x^2e^y+x-2y\): \(g'=-2y\), \(g=-y^2\).
  3. \(\varphi=x^2e^y+xy-y^2\); check \(\varphi_x=2xe^y+y\), \(\varphi_y=x^2e^y+x-2y\). ✓
  4. \(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\) on the whole plane, so \(\vb F\) is conservative.

The conclusion sentence (“\(\vb F=\nabla\varphi\), hence conservative”) is what the question asks; don't stop at \(\varphi\).

\(\varphi(x,y)=x^2e^y+xy-y^2\ (+\text{const})\)
Dec 2018 Q1(c)5 marks

Show that the vector field \(\vb F(x,y)=e^xy\,\ii+(e^x+2y)\,\jj\) defined on the \(XY\)-plane is conservative by finding its scalar potential.

Hint

\(\int e^xy\,dx=e^xy\).

Solution
  1. \(\varphi=\int e^xy\,dx=e^xy+g(y)\).
  2. \(\varphi_y=e^x+g'(y)=e^x+2y\Rightarrow g=y^2\).
  3. \(\nabla(e^xy+y^2)=(e^xy,\ e^x+2y)=\vb F\) on \(\R^2\): conservative.

Optional cross-check worth one line: \(N_x=e^x=M_y\).

\(\varphi(x,y)=e^xy+y^2\ (+\text{const})\)
Dec 2017 Q3(a)(i)–(ii)4 + 4 marks

Given a vector field in \(\R^2\): \(\vb F=(x^2-y^2+x)\,\ii-(kxy+y)\,\jj\) where \(k\) is a real constant.

(i) Find the value of \(k\) such that \(\vb F\) is irrotational.

(ii) Find a scalar potential of \(\vb F\) with the value of \(k\) obtained in Q3(a)(i).

Part (iii), the integral along \(y=x^2\) for general \(k\), is type C3b.1: C3b guide.

Hint

\(N_x-M_y\) with \(N=-(kxy+y)\).

Solution
  1. \(N_x=-ky\), \(M_y=-2y\); \(\nabla\times\vb F=(2-k)y\,\kk\).
  2. Zero for all \(y\) ⇔ \(k=2\).
  3. (ii) \(\varphi=\int(x^2-y^2+x)\,dx\) \(=\frac{x^3}3-xy^2+\frac{x^2}2+g(y)\).
  4. \(\varphi_y=-2xy+g'\) must equal \(-2xy-y\), so \(g=-\frac{y^2}2\).

Keep the \(+x\) and \(-y\) pieces: they give the \(\frac{x^2}2\) and \(-\frac{y^2}2\) terms that are most often dropped.

\(k=2,\qquad \varphi=\dfrac{x^3}3-xy^2+\dfrac{x^2}2-\dfrac{y^2}2\)
Dec 2016 Q3(a)(i)5 marks

Given a vector field in \(\R^3\): \(\vb F=(\alpha y^2+4zx)\,\ii\) \(+\,y(\beta x+2z)\,\jj\) \(+\,(\alpha y^2+2x^2)\,\kk\) where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are constants. Find the conditions on \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) such that \(\vb F\) is irrotational.

Parts (ii) div (C3a.1) and (iii) the semicircle integral (C3b.1) are on other pages.

Hint

All three curl components; each is a multiple of \(y\).

Solution
  1. \(\ii\): \(P_y-N_z=2\alpha y-2y\).
  2. \(\jj\): \(M_z-P_x=4x-4x=0\).
  3. \(\kk\): \(N_x-M_y=\beta y-2\alpha y\).
  4. All zero for every \(y\): \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=2\alpha=2\).

Check (not required): \(\varphi=xy^2+2x^2z+y^2z\) has gradient \(\vb F\) with \(\alpha=1,\beta=2\).

\(\alpha=1,\quad\beta=2\)
Dec 2015 Q3(b)(ii)–(iii)4 + 5 marks

Consider a vector field on \(\R^2\): \(\vb F=(1+\alpha xy^2)\,\ii+\beta x^2y\,\jj\). (Part (i), C3a.1, asked when \(\vb F\) is solenoidal: \(\operatorname{div}\vb F=\alpha y^2+\beta x^2=0\), so \(\alpha=\beta=0\).)

(ii) Find the condition on \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) such that \(\vb F\) is irrotational.

(iii) With the condition in Q3(b)(ii), find a scalar potential for \(\vb F\). Hence, or otherwise, evaluate \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) where \(C\) is the straight line segment from \((0,0)\) to \((1,2)\). (Express the answer in terms of \(\alpha\).)

Hint

\(N_x-M_y=2\beta xy-2\alpha xy\).

Solution
  1. \(\nabla\times\vb F=(2\beta xy-2\alpha xy)\,\kk\) \(=2(\beta-\alpha)xy\,\kk\); zero for all \(x,y\) ⇔ \(\beta=\alpha\).
  2. \(\varphi=\int(1+\alpha xy^2)\,dx\) \(=x+\frac\alpha2x^2y^2+g(y)\); \(\varphi_y=\alpha x^2y+g'=\alpha x^2y\), so \(g'=0\).
  3. \(\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\varphi(1,2)-\varphi(0,0)\) \(=1+\frac\alpha2\cdot4=1+2\alpha\).

The answer must be “in terms of \(\alpha\)”: replace \(\beta\) by \(\alpha\) before integrating.

\[\begin{aligned}&\beta=\alpha,\quad\varphi=x+\tfrac12\alpha x^2y^2,\\ &\int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=1+2\alpha\end{aligned}\]

May 2026 Q410 marksMay format

Determine the work done by \(\vec F=(2x+2y)\,\hat i+(2x)\,\hat j\) along the right half of a circle of radius 2 centred at the origin.

Direction not stated. We take \((0,-2)\to(0,2)\) counter-clockwise; because \(\vec F\) is conservative and both ends give \(\varphi=0\), the answer is the same either way.

Hint

Test the curl first; a potential makes the half circle irrelevant.

Solution
  1. \(N_x-M_y=2-2=0\) on \(\R^2\) (simply connected), so \(\vec F\) is conservative.
  2. \(\varphi=\int(2x+2y)\,dx=x^2+2xy+g(y)\); \(\varphi_y=2x+g'=2x\Rightarrow g'=0\).
  3. Ends of the right half circle: \((0,-2)\) and \((0,2)\); \(\varphi(0,\pm2)=0\).
  4. \(W=\varphi(0,2)-\varphi(0,-2)=0\).

For 10 marks also show the parametrised check: \(\vb r=(2\cos t,2\sin t)\), \(-\frac\pi2\le t\le\frac\pi2\), gives \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=-8\sin t\cos t-8\sin^2t\) \(+8\cos^2t\), which integrates to 0.

\(W=0\)
May 2025 Q510 marksMay format

Determine the work done by \(\vec F=\left(2y+\frac1x\right)\hat i+(2x+\sin z)\,\hat j+(y\cos z)\,\hat k\) over a straight path from \((1,1,0)\) to \((e,2,\frac\pi2)\).

Hint

Curl is zero; \(\int\frac1x\,dx=\ln x\) (fine: \(x\gt0\) on the path).

Solution
  1. Curl: \(\ii\): \(\cos z-\cos z=0\); \(\jj\): \(0-0=0\); \(\kk\): \(2-2=0\). Conservative for \(x\gt0\).
  2. \(\varphi=\int(2y+\frac1x)\,dx\) \(=2xy+\ln x+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=2x+g_y\) must equal \(2x+\sin z\), so \(g=y\sin z+h(z)\); \(\varphi_z=y\cos z+h'\), so \(h'=0\).
  3. \(\varphi(e,2,\frac\pi2)=4e+1+2\), \(\varphi(1,1,0)=2\).

Mention that \(\ln x\) needs \(x\gt0\), which holds along the path from \(x=1\) to \(x=e\).

\[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=2xy+\ln x+y\sin z,\\ W&=4e+1\end{aligned}\]
May 2024 Q3(b)10 marksMay format

Evaluate

\[\begin{aligned}\int_C&(-\pi\sin(\pi x)-yz)\,dx\\&+(\pi\cos(\pi y)-xz)\,dy-xy\,dz\end{aligned}\]

where \(C\) is a path joined by two line segments: \((0,0,0)\) to \((1,2,0)\) then to \((1,2,3)\).

Hint

\(\vb F=(M,N,P)\) as read off the \(dx,dy,dz\). Try \(\varphi=\cos\pi x+\dots\)

Solution
  1. Curl: \(\ii\): \(-x-(-x)=0\); \(\jj\): \(-y-(-y)=0\); \(\kk\): \(-z-(-z)=0\). Conservative on \(\R^3\).
  2. \(\varphi=\int(-\pi\sin\pi x-yz)\,dx\) \(=\cos\pi x-xyz+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=-xz+g_y\), so \(g_y=\pi\cos\pi y\), \(g=\sin\pi y+h(z)\); \(\varphi_z=-xy+h'\), so \(h'=0\).
  3. Only the end points matter: \(\varphi(1,2,3)=\cos\pi+\sin2\pi-6=-7\), \(\varphi(0,0,0)=1\).

The two-segment path is a decoy: say “path-independent” and use only \((0,0,0)\) and \((1,2,3)\).

\[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=\cos\pi x+\sin\pi y-xyz,\\ \int_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r&=-8\end{aligned}\]
May 2023 Q3(a)10 marksMay format

Determine the work done by \(\vec F=(2xy+yz)\,\hat i\) \(+\,(x^2+xz)\,\hat j\) \(+\,(xy+2z)\,\hat k\) over the circular path from \((1,0,0)\) to \((0,1,0)\) then to \((0,0,1)\). This path lies on the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\).

Hint

Curl first. Only the start \((1,0,0)\) and the end \((0,0,1)\) matter.

Solution
  1. Curl: \(\ii\): \(x-x=0\); \(\jj\): \(y-y=0\); \(\kk\): \((2x+z)-(2x+z)=0\). Conservative on \(\R^3\).
  2. \(\varphi=\int(2xy+yz)\,dx\) \(=x^2y+xyz+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=x^2+xz+g_y\), so \(g_y=0\); \(\varphi_z=xy+g_z=xy+2z\), so \(g=z^2\).
  3. \(W=\varphi(0,0,1)-\varphi(1,0,0)=1-0\).

The middle point \((0,1,0)\) and the arcs play no part; one sentence on path independence earns the method mark.

\(\varphi=x^2y+xyz+z^2,\qquad W=1\)
May 2022 Q3(a)10 marksMay format

Evaluate

\[\begin{aligned}\int_A^B&\big(y\cos(xy)+2+z\big)\,dx\\&+\big(x\cos(xy)+2y-z\sin(yz)\big)\,dy\\&+\Big(x-y\sin(yz)+\frac1z\Big)\,dz\end{aligned}\]

where \(AB\) is a straight path from \(A(\pi,1,1)\) to \(B(2\pi,0,1)\).

Hint

\(\int y\cos(xy)\,dx=\sin(xy)\); the \(-z\sin(yz)\) term comes from \(\cos(yz)\).

Solution
  1. Curl, \(\ii\): \(P_y\) and \(N_z\) are both \(-\sin yz-yz\cos yz\), so 0. \(\jj\): \(1-1=0\). \(\kk\): \(N_x\) and \(M_y\) are both \(\cos xy-xy\sin xy\), so 0.
  2. \(\varphi=\sin xy+2x+xz+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=x\cos xy+g_y\), so \(g_y=2y-z\sin yz\) and \(g=y^2+\cos yz+h(z)\); \(\varphi_z=x-y\sin yz+h'\), so \(h=\ln z\).
  3. \(\varphi(B)=0+4\pi+2\pi+0+1+0\) \(=6\pi+1\).
  4. \(\varphi(A)=0+2\pi+\pi+1+\cos1+0\) \(=3\pi+1+\cos1\).

\(\ln z\) is fine because \(z=1\) along the whole path.

\[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=\sin xy+2x+xz+y^2\\&\quad+\cos yz+\ln z,\\ \int_A^B&=3\pi-\cos1\approx8.885\end{aligned}\]
May 2021 Q3(a)10 marksMay format

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_A^Be^{xy}\big(y\sin z\,dx+x\sin z\,dy+\cos z\,dz\big)\) where \(AB\) is a straight path from \(A(0,0,\pi)\) to \(B(2,\frac12,\frac\pi2)\).

Hint

Guess \(\varphi=e^{xy}\sin z\) and check its gradient.

Solution
  1. \(\vb F=e^{xy}(y\sin z,\ x\sin z,\ \cos z)\).
  2. \(\varphi=\int ye^{xy}\sin z\,dx\) \(=e^{xy}\sin z+g(y,z)\); \(\varphi_y=xe^{xy}\sin z+g_y\Rightarrow g_y=0\); \(\varphi_z=e^{xy}\cos z+g_z\Rightarrow g_z=0\).

C3c.2Fields with a hole at the origin

9 parts · 3 December sittings (Dec 2023 Q4(a), Dec 2022 Q3(a) = Dec 2018 Q4(b), Dec 2014 Q4(b)) · 42 marks (+15 for the 2018 repeat) · always a long December part (12–17 marks) · Learn it · note §2

Always the same field, \(\dfrac{y\,\ii-x\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}\) (sometimes with the radial field \(\dfrac{x\,\ii+y\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}\) beside it), but the questions change: potentials and where they live, a circle either way round, a square that may or may not contain the origin, and a written “why”. Hence a procedure.

Decision procedure
  1. “Find curl” → only \(N_x-M_y\). For \(\vb F=\frac{y\,\ii-x\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}\) both cross-derivatives equal \(\frac{x^2-y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\); for the radial field both equal \(\frac{-2xy}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\). Curl \(=\vb 0\) everywhere except \((0,0)\), where \(\vb F\) is undefined.
  2. “Find \(\varphi\) and the region where it is defined” → integrate \(M\) in \(x\). Radial: \(\frac12\ln(x^2+y^2)\), defined on all of \(\R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\). Swirl: write \(\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}=\frac{1/y}{1+(x/y)^2}\), \(u=\frac xy\), get \(\tan^{-1}\frac xy\): defined only where \(y\ne0\), i.e. on the half-plane \(y\gt0\) or \(y\lt0\). Say the whole \(x\)-axis is excluded, not just the origin.
  3. Closed curve that does NOT go round the origin → it lies in a half-plane (simply connected, curl \(=\vb 0\), a potential exists there): the integral is \(0\). Give the potential for that half-plane (e.g. \(-\tan^{-1}\frac yx\) for \(x\gt0\)).
  4. Closed curve that DOES go round the origin once → the swirl field gives \(\pm2\pi\) (ready answer): parametrise a circle and show \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=\pm1\). The radial field still gives \(0\) (its potential \(\frac12\ln(x^2+y^2)\) works all the way round).
  5. Non-circular curve round the origin (square) → integrate side by side; each side is \(\int\frac{c}{c^2+s^2}ds=\tan^{-1}\frac sc\); combine with \(\tan^{-1}\theta+\tan^{-1}\frac1\theta=\pm\frac\pi2\) (ready answer).
  6. “Explain / does this contradict Stokes?” → use the answer skeleton below.

Cues: \(x^2+y^2\) in every denominator; “\(\R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\)”; “determine the region where \(\varphi\) is defined”; “oriented clockwise”; “for the cases \(R\lt1\) and \(R\gt1\)”.

Traps:

  • Concluding “curl \(=\vb 0\), so every closed integral is 0”: false here, because the domain has a hole (not simply connected).
  • Sign of the \(2\pi\): \(\frac{y\,\ii-x\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}\) swirls clockwise, so an anticlockwise circle gives \(-2\pi\). Dec 2022 is clockwise (\(+2\pi\)), Dec 2018 and 2014 anticlockwise (\(-2\pi\)).
  • Stating the domain of \(\tan^{-1}\frac xy\) as “\((x,y)\ne(0,0)\)”: it fails on the whole line \(y=0\).
  • Using \(\tan^{-1}\theta+\tan^{-1}\frac1\theta=\frac\pi2\) when \(\theta\lt0\): there it is \(-\frac\pi2\) (that is exactly why the two square cases differ).
Answer skeleton · “Explain” / “Does this contradict Stokes' theorem?”

Draw: the curve with the origin marked as a hole (open dot), shaded inside.

Marker points: (1) Stokes' (or Green's) theorem needs \(\vb F\) and its first partial derivatives continuous on the curve and on a surface spanning it. (2) Every surface in the plane bounded by this curve contains \((0,0)\), where \(\vb F\) is undefined, so the theorem does not apply. (3) Equivalently, \(\R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\) is not simply connected, so curl \(=\vb0\) does not make \(\vb F\) conservative there. (4) Conclusion: no contradiction.

Model answer (2–3 marks): No. Stokes' theorem would give \(\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\iint_\Sigma(\nabla\times\vb F)\cdot\vb n\,dS=0\) only if \(\vb F\) had continuous partial derivatives on a surface \(\Sigma\) bounded by \(C\). Any such surface (e.g. the unit disc) contains the origin, where \(\vb F\) is not defined, so the hypothesis fails and the theorem says nothing. The nonzero value \(-2\pi\) shows that \(\vb F\) has no potential on the whole punctured plane, which is possible because that region is not simply connected.

Ready answer · the hole field (not on the sheet)

For \(\vb F=\dfrac{y\,\ii-x\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}\) (clockwise swirl) and a simple closed curve \(C\):

\[\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=\begin{cases}0,&O\text{ outside }C,\\ -2\pi,&O\text{ inside, anticlockwise},\\ +2\pi,&O\text{ inside, clockwise}.\end{cases}\]

Potentials (no single one on the whole punctured plane): \(\tan^{-1}\frac xy\) on \(y\ne0\); \(-\tan^{-1}\frac yx\) on \(x\ne0\). The radial field \(\frac{x\,\ii+y\,\jj}{x^2+y^2}=\nabla\big(\tfrac12\ln(x^2+y^2)\big)\) gives \(0\) round every closed curve.

Arctan identity: \(\tan^{-1}\theta+\tan^{-1}\frac1\theta=\frac\pi2\) for \(\theta\gt0\) and \(-\frac\pi2\) for \(\theta\lt0\).

In words: the swirl field is minus the gradient of the polar angle; going once round the origin changes the angle by \(2\pi\), so the circulation is \(\mp2\pi\); a loop that misses the origin brings the angle back to where it started, so it gives zero.

Looks like

Do

Surface side: list every piece of the closed surface with its outward \(\vb n\) and \(dS\) (table), compute each flux. 4. Add the pieces; state that the two sides agree.
Use instead of verify?
“Outward flux across the boundary” → volume side only. “Flux through an open surface” (a cap, the side of a cylinder) → close it with flat lids: flux(open) \(=\iiint\operatorname{div}\vb F\,dV-\) flux(lids) (ready answer).
Watch
The flat base is a piece of the surface: show its flux (often 0) with \(\vb n=-\kk\). Terms odd in \(x\) or \(y\) (\(\cos\theta\), \(\sin\theta\cos\theta\), \(\cos^3\theta\) over a full turn) vanish: say why.
Ready answer · Gauss set-up and standard integrals \[\begin{aligned}\iiint_V\operatorname{div}\vb F\,dV&=\sum_{\text{pieces }\Sigma_i}\iint_{\Sigma_i}\vb F\cdot\vb n_i\,dS,\\ \vb n_i&=\text{outward on each piece.}\end{aligned}\]

Ball / hemisphere of radius \(a\) (\(dV=\rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta\), \(dS=a^2\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta\)):

\[\begin{aligned}V_{\text{hemi}}&=\tfrac23\pi a^3,\quad \iiint_{\text{hemi}}z\,dV=\tfrac14\pi a^4,\\ \iint_{\text{hemi}}z^2\,dS&=\tfrac23\pi a^4,\quad \iint_{\text{sphere}}x^2\,dS=\tfrac43\pi a^4.\end{aligned}\]

Over a full turn: \(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\cos\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^3\theta=0\), \(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta=\pi\).

In words: the total outflow through every face of a closed surface equals the total “source strength” (divergence) inside; each face uses its own outward normal, and lids you add to close a surface must be subtracted again.

Ready answer · Stokes set-up \[\iint_\Sigma(\nabla\times\vb F)\cdot\vb n\,dS=\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\]

Orientation: walk along \(C\) with your head along \(\vb n\); \(\Sigma\) is on your left. Upward \(\vb n\) ↔ \(C\) anticlockwise seen from above. Any two surfaces with the same edge \(C\) (and the same orientation) give the same flux of the curl.

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Circle: }&\vb r=(R\cos t,\,R\sin t,\,z_0),\\ &\vb r'=(-R\sin t,\,R\cos t,\,0),\\ &\textstyle\oint(-y\,dx+x\,dy)=2\pi R^2.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the circulation round the edge equals the total “spin” (curl) passing through any surface that fills the edge in, counted with the normal that the right-hand rule ties to the direction of travel.

Given \(\vb F=xz\,\ii+yz\,\jj+\kk\). Let \(\Sigma\) be the part of the surface \(x^2+y^2+z^2=25\) lying above the plane \(z=3\) and \(\vb n\) be the upward unit normal vector on \(\Sigma\). Evaluate the flux integral \(\iint_\Sigma\operatorname{curl}(\vb F)\cdot\vb n\,dS\) using Stokes' Theorem.

Part (ii), the same flux by Gauss' theorem, is under C3c.4.

Hint

Edge: \(z=3\) in the sphere gives \(x^2+y^2=16\). Upward normal → anticlockwise. On the edge \(z=3\), \(dz=0\).

Solution
  1. Boundary \(C\): \(x^2+y^2=25-9=16\), \(z=3\); \(\vb r=(4\cos t,4\sin t,3)\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\) (anticlockwise, matching the upward normal).
  2. \(\vb F\cdot d\vb r=xz\,dx+yz\,dy+dz\) \(=3(x\,dx+y\,dy)\), since \(z=3\), \(dz=0\).
  3. \(x\,dx+y\,dy=(4\cos t)(-4\sin t)\,dt\) \(+(4\sin t)(4\cos t)\,dt=0\).
  4. So \(\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r=0\), and by Stokes the flux is 0.

State the orientation of \(C\) and why (upward normal); with this field the value is 0 either way, but the mark is for saying it.

Verify Stokes' Theorem for the vector field \(\vb F=(x^2+y-4)\,\ii+3xy\,\jj+(2xz+z^2)\,\kk\) defined on the surface of the circular paraboloid \(x^2+y^2+z=4\) above the \(XY\)-plane.

Hint

Edge: \(z=0\), \(x^2+y^2=4\). Take the upward normal, so \(C\) is anticlockwise. \(\Sigma\): \(z=f=4-x^2-y^2\).

Solution
  1. Orientation: \(\vb n\) upward, \(C\): \(\vb r=(2\cos t,2\sin t,0)\), anticlockwise.
  2. Line: on \(C\), \(F_1=4\cos^2t+2\sin t-4\), \(F_2=12\cos t\sin t\), \(F_3=0\); \(\vb r'=(-2\sin t,2\cos t,0)\).
  3. \(\vb F\cdot\vb r'=-8\cos^2t\sin t-4\sin^2t\) \(+8\sin t+24\cos^2t\sin t\). Over \(0\) to \(2\pi\) only \(-4\sin^2t\) survives: \(-4\pi\).
  4. Curl: \(\ii\): \(0-0\); \(\jj\): \(0-2z\); \(\kk\): \(3y-1\). So \(\nabla\times\vb F=(0,\,-2z,\,3y-1)\).
  5. Surface (sheet formula, \(f_x=-2x\), \(f_y=-2y\)): \(-Mf_x-Nf_y+P=-4yz+3y-1\) with \(z=4-x^2-y^2\).
  6. Over the disc \(x^2+y^2\le4\) the terms odd in \(y\) vanish; \(\iint(-1)\,dA=-4\pi\). Both sides \(-4\pi\). ✓

◆Before the exam

  • Know the curl formula by heart (not on the sheet); in 2D it is just \(N_x-M_y\). Unknown constants: make each curl component zero for all \(x,y,z\) (compare coefficients).
  • Potential: integrate \(M\) in \(x\), add \(g(y,z)\), match \(N\) then \(P\); finish with \(\nabla\varphi=\vb F\). Work \(=\varphi(\text{end})-\varphi(\text{start})\), whatever the path.
  • \(x^2+y^2\) in the denominator: curl \(=\vb0\) does not mean conservative. Swirl \(\frac{y\ii-x\jj}{x^2+y^2}\): \(\mp2\pi\) round the origin (anticlockwise \(-2\pi\)), 0 if the origin is outside (ready answer).
  • “Region where \(\tan^{-1}\frac xy\) is defined” = \(y\ne0\) (a half-plane), not just “not the origin”.
  • Have \(dS\) ready: sphere \(a^2\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta\), cylinder \(R\,d\theta\,dz\), graph \(\sqrt{f_x^2+f_y^2+1}\,dx\,dy\) (table).
  • Gauss verification: list every piece of the closed surface with its outward normal, including the flat base (\(\vb n=-\kk\)) even when its flux is 0.
  • Kill odd terms with a sentence: \(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\cos\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^3\theta=0\).
  • Stokes: state the orientation (upward \(\vb n\) ↔ anticlockwise from above). “Outward” on a bowl opening upwards means downward and clockwise.
  • “Choose \(\Sigma\) carefully”: replace a curved surface by the flat disc with the same edge.
  • Budget: about 20 minutes for a 10–13-mark verification, half per side. If the two sides disagree, check the orientation and the missing surface piece first.
