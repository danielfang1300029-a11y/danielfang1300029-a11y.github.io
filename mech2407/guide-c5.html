◆Exam format and formula sheet for this chapter

  • December (main format): 3 hours, five 25-mark questions, answer any four. Q5 is the PDE question and it is compulsory in every December paper we have except Dec 2015 (whose cover says only “answer four”). So Q5 is one of your four, whatever else you choose.
  • Q5 split: usually (a) 10 marks + (b) 15 marks; Dec 2021 was (a) 15 + (b) 10. Q5(a) was direct integration (out of syllabus, C5.7) in 2014–2018 and 2023, and a heat equation in 2021 (decay term, C5.6), 2022, 2024 and 2025 (C5.1). Q5(b) is a heat or wave equation every year: insulated-end heat (2015, 2023), non-zero ends (2022), fixed-end wave (2014, 2024, 2025), fixed + free wave (2017, 2018, 2021); only Dec 2016 Q5(b) (decay term, C5.6) is out of syllabus.
  • Time budget: 25 marks ≈ 45 min, so ≈ 18 min for a 10-mark (a) and ≈ 27 min for a 15-mark (b). The separation write-up takes about 8 minutes; practise it until it does.
  • What the December formula sheet gives (same sheet 2014–2025): the Fourier series of a \(T\)-periodic function, \(f(t)=\frac12a_0+\sum(a_n\cos n\omega t+b_n\sin n\omega t)\) with \(a_n=\frac2T\int f\cos n\omega t\,dt\), \(b_n=\frac2T\int f\sin n\omega t\,dt\); and reduction formulas for \(\int x^n\sin\alpha x\), \(\int x^n\cos\alpha x\), \(\int\sin^n\), \(\int\cos^n\), \(\int e^{\beta x}\sin^n\), \(\int e^{\beta x}\cos^n\) (use them for \(x^2\), \(x^3\) starting shapes).
  • What it leaves out: every separation-of-variables result. Nothing on the sheet tells you which shapes go with which ends, the time factors, the steady state, or the half-range coefficient formulas. You must write the method out (the answer skeleton in C5.1) and know the ready answers on this page by heart.
  • Convention used on this page (as in the note): the constant term of a cosine or full series is written \(a_0\) = the average of the start over the rod, \(a_0=\frac1L\int_0^Lf\) (ring: \(\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^Lf\)). That is the sheet's \(\frac12a_0\). Either is fine in the exam if you say which you use.
  • Letters change between papers. The diffusivity or wave speed may be \(c^2\), \(k\), \(k^2\), \(a^2\), or a number; the unknown may be \(u\), \(U\), \(\theta\), \(y\). The note calls the separation constant \(k\); because so many questions use \(k\) for the diffusivity or speed, this page writes it \(\lambda\) (useful case \(\lambda=-p^2\)). Never reuse a letter the question has taken.
  • May format (other semester; useful practice, different rules): 2015–2019 five 20-mark questions; 2021–2024 five questions (total 100); 2025–2026 eight questions (total 100). From 2021 there is no formula sheet. The PDE question is a word problem: “a string of length \(\pi\) and rigidity 900 fastened at one end only…”, “a thin circular ring…”, and often says “define and solve the boundary value problem”: you must first write the PDE, the boundary conditions and the initial conditions yourself (rigidity = \(c^2\), fastened end \(y=0\), free end \(y_x=0\), insulated end \(u_x=0\)). Many May starting shapes are already single allowed shapes, so the answer is read off without integrals.
  • Dec 2021 Q3 and Q4 are missing from the scans; they would not be PDE questions (Q5 is), so nothing is missing from this chapter.

C5.1Heat equation, both ends held at zero

3 parts · 3 sittings (Dec 2025, 2024, 2022, all Q5(a)) · 30 marks · Learn it · note §4 separation · note §3

Type card · predictable · which method? (all of Q5)
Looks like
“Given the conditions \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=f(x)\). Solve \(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\).” Ten marks, part (a).
Which method?
  • One time derivative \(u_t\) → heat: one time factor \(e^{-(\cdots)t}\) per shape. Two (\(u_{tt}\)) → wave: \(\cos\) and \(\sin\) in time, and you need the start shape and the start velocity (C5.4, C5.5).
  • Both ends \(u=0\) (held at 0 °C / fastened) → shapes \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), \(n\ge1\): sine series (this section, C5.4).
  • Both ends \(u_x=0\) (insulated / free) → shapes \(1,\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\): cosine series with the constant (C5.2, C5.5).
  • Ring, or \(u(-L)=u(L)\) and \(u_x(-L)=u_x(L)\) → \(1,\cos\frac{n\pi x}L,\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\): full Fourier series on \([-L,L]\) (C5.2).
  • One end \(u=0\), other end \(u_x=0\) → quarter-wave shapes \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\) (C5.5).
  • Ends held at non-zero temperatures → steady state \(u_E=Ax+B\) first, then a zero-end problem for the rest (C5.3).
  • Start is already a sum of allowed shapes (e.g. \(2a\sin\frac{3\pi x}5\) on a rod of length 5) → no integrals: read the answer off.
Do
Write the five-step separation (cases \(\lambda=0,\gt0,\lt0\)) → \(F_n=\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), \(G_n=e^{-(nc\pi/L)^2t}\) → superpose → \(B_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\) → write \(u\).
Watch
The time factor is \(e^{-(n\pi c/L)^2t}\) with the question's own constant: \(k\) (not \(k^2\)) in \(u_t=ku_{xx}\) gives \(e^{-k(n\pi/L)^2t}\). And state why \(\lambda=0\) and \(\lambda\gt0\) give only \(u\equiv0\) (method marks).

The four families of allowed shapes (first three of each; \(x\) in units of \(L\))

Top left: \(u=0\) at both ends → \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\). Top right: \(u_x=0\) at both ends → \(1,\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\). Bottom left: \(u(0)=0\), \(u_x(L)=0\) → \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\). Bottom right: ring on \([-L,L]\) → \(1,\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\) (solid) and \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\) (dashed). Moving pictures: note §0.

Answer skeleton · the separation write-up (every Q5 heat / wave part)
  1. Settings. Write PDE, BCs, IC(s). (May papers: “define the BVP” marks are here.)
  2. Separate. \(u=F(x)G(t)\) ⇒ \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}{c^2G}=\lambda\), a constant (left side depends on \(x\) only, right side on \(t\) only). Turn the BCs into conditions on \(F\) (e.g. \(F(0)=F(L)=0\)), because \(G\not\equiv0\).
  3. Three cases for \(F''=\lambda F\). \(\lambda=0\): \(F=Ax+B\). \(\lambda=p^2\gt0\): \(F=Ae^{px}+Be^{-px}\). \(\lambda=-p^2\lt0\): \(F=A\cos px+B\sin px\). Apply the BCs to each; keep the non-trivial ones and the allowed \(p_n\).
  4. Time equation with \(\lambda=-p_n^2\): heat \(G_n=e^{-c^2p_n^2t}\); wave \(G_n=C_n\cos cp_nt+D_n\sin cp_nt\).
  5. Superpose and fit the IC: \(u=\sum F_nG_n\); at \(t=0\) this is a Fourier (half-range) series of \(f\); get the coefficients by orthogonality; write the final \(u(x,t)\).

Marker points: the separation with a reason for “= constant”; all three cases with the BCs applied (with zero ends, two of them “trivial”); the allowed \(p_n\); the correct time factor; the superposition; the coefficient integral set up and evaluated; the final answer as one formula.

Model write-up (zero ends, length \(L\), exam length): “Let \(u=F(x)G(t)\). Then \(FG'=c^2F''G\), so \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}{c^2G}=\lambda\), constant. \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\) for all \(t\) gives \(F(0)=F(L)=0\). If \(\lambda=0\): \(F=Ax+B\), \(B=0\), \(AL=0\), so \(F\equiv0\). If \(\lambda=p^2\gt0\): \(A+B=0\), \(Ae^{pL}+Be^{-pL}=0\) give \(2A\sinh pL=0\), so \(A=B=0\). If \(\lambda=-p^2\lt0\): \(F=A\cos px+B\sin px\), \(F(0)=0\Rightarrow A=0\), \(F(L)=0\Rightarrow\sin pL=0\Rightarrow p=\frac{n\pi}L\), \(n=1,2,\dots\) Then \(G'=-c^2p^2G\Rightarrow G_n=e^{-(nc\pi/L)^2t}\). By superposition \(u=\sum_{n\ge1}B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}Le^{-(nc\pi/L)^2t}\), and \(u(x,0)=f(x)\) gives \(B_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\).”

Ready answer · heat equation, both ends at 0 (not on the sheet)

\(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\), \(0\lt x\lt L\); \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\); \(u(x,0)=f(x)\):

\[\begin{aligned}u(x,t)&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi c}L\right)^2t},\\ B_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

If the PDE is \(u_t=ku_{xx}\), the rate is \(k\big(\frac{n\pi}L\big)^2\). As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to0\) (the rod cools to the ends' temperature).

In words: write the start as a sum of half-wave sine shapes that fit between the cold ends; each shape then fades on its own, and the \(n\)-th one fades \(n^2\) times as fast as the first.

Ready answer · start already made of allowed shapes

If \(f(x)=\alpha\sin\frac{m\pi x}L+\beta\sin\frac{q\pi x}L\) (whole numbers \(m,q\)), then by orthogonality \(B_m=\alpha\), \(B_q=\beta\), every other \(B_n=0\):

\[\begin{aligned}u&=\alpha\sin\frac{m\pi x}L\,e^{-\left(\frac{m\pi c}L\right)^2t}\\&\quad+\beta\sin\frac{q\pi x}L\,e^{-\left(\frac{q\pi c}L\right)^2t}.\end{aligned}\]

To find \(m\): write the given \(\sin(\omega x)\) as \(\sin\frac{m\pi x}L\), so \(m=\frac{\omega L}\pi\); it must be a whole number (e.g. \(\sin2\pi x\) on \(L=5\) is \(m=10\)). The same works for cosine shapes (C5.2) and for quarter-wave shapes (C5.5), and for the wave equation with \(\cos\) in time.

In words: if the start is already a mix of the natural shapes, each one simply carries on with its own time factor; no integral is needed. Say “by comparing coefficients” (or “by orthogonality”).

Ready answer · sine coefficients that keep coming back

\(B_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\) for common starts on \([0,L]\):

\[\begin{aligned}f=a&:\ B_n=\frac{2a\,[1-(-1)^n]}{n\pi}\\ f=x&:\ B_n=\frac{2L(-1)^{n+1}}{n\pi}\\ f=x(L-x)&:\ B_n=\frac{4L^2[1-(-1)^n]}{n^3\pi^3}\\ \text{triangle}&\text{, height }h\text{ at }x=d:\\ B_n&=\frac{2hL^2\sin\frac{n\pi d}L}{d(L-d)\,n^2\pi^2}\end{aligned}\]

Cosine coefficients \(a_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\): \(f=x\): \(\frac{2L[(-1)^n-1]}{n^2\pi^2}\); \(f=x^2\): \(\frac{4L^2(-1)^n}{n^2\pi^2}\). Derivations: note C4 §5.

In words: \([1-(-1)^n]\) means “only odd \(n\), and then it is 2”; a constant or a straight line gives coefficients like \(\frac1n\), a smooth hump like \(\frac1{n^3}\), a corner like \(\frac1{n^2}\).

Dec 2025 Q5(a)10 marks

Given the conditions \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\), \(t\gt0\); \(u(x,0)=x(L-x)\), \(0\lt x\lt L\), where \(L\) is a constant. Solve the heat conduction equation \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=c^2\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\) where \(c\) is a constant.

Hint

Zero ends: the separation write-up gives sine shapes. Then \(B_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lx(L-x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\) by parts twice.

Solution
  1. \(u=F(x)G(t)\): \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}{c^2G}=\lambda\); \(F(0)=F(L)=0\).
  2. \(\lambda=0\): \(F=Ax+B\) ⇒ \(B=0\), \(A=0\) (trivial). \(\lambda=p^2\gt0\): \(A+B=0\), \(2A\sinh pL=0\) ⇒ trivial. \(\lambda=-p^2\): \(F=A\cos px+B\sin px\), \(A=0\), \(\sin pL=0\) ⇒ \(p=\frac{n\pi}L\).
  3. \(G'=-c^2p^2G\) ⇒ \(G_n=e^{-(nc\pi/L)^2t}\). Superpose: \(u=\sum B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}Le^{-(nc\pi/L)^2t}\).
  4. IC: \(B_n=\frac2L\int_0^Lx(L-x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\). By parts (boundary terms vanish because \(x(L-x)=0\) at both ends): \[\begin{aligned}&\int_0^Lx(L-x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\ &=\frac L{n\pi}\int_0^L(L-2x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\ &=\frac{2L^2}{n^2\pi^2}\int_0^L\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx\\ &=\frac{2L^3[1-(-1)^n]}{n^3\pi^3}.\end{aligned}\]
  5. \(B_n=\frac{4L^2[1-(-1)^n]}{n^3\pi^3}\): \(\frac{8L^2}{n^3\pi^3}\) for odd \(n\), 0 for even \(n\).

Most of the 10 marks are for steps 1–3 (the method); the integral is about 3 marks. Do not skip the cases to save time.

\[\begin{aligned}u(x,t)&=\frac{8L^2}{\pi^3}\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac1{n^3}\\&\qquad\times\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,e^{-\left(\frac{nc\pi}L\right)^2t}\end{aligned}\]

Equivalent: sum over \(m\ge1\) with \(n=2m-1\), coefficient \(\frac{8L^2}{(2m-1)^3\pi^3}\). Agrees with the note's exam taster.

Dec 2024 Q5(a)10 marks

Given the conditions \(u(0,t)=0\), \(u(L,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=a\sin\left(\dfrac{L\pi}{3}x\right)-a\sin(2\pi x)\), \(0\lt x\lt L\), where \(L\) and \(a\) are constants. Solve the boundary-initial value problem \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=k\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\) where \(k\) is a constant.

As printed, \(\sin\frac{L\pi x}3\) and \(\sin2\pi x\) are 0 at \(x=L\) only for special \(L\), so the start would not match the end condition. We read it as the evident intention \(u(x,0)=a\sin\frac{3\pi x}L-a\sin\frac{2\pi x}L\) (compare Dec 2022 Q5(a)). The as-printed answer is in the solution too; it has the same form.

Hint

Zero ends → sine shapes \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\). The start is already two of them: read off \(B_3\), \(B_2\). The separation constant must not be called \(k\) here.

Solution
  1. \(u=F(x)G(t)\): \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}{kG}=\lambda\); \(F(0)=F(L)=0\).
  2. Cases as in the skeleton: \(\lambda=0\) and \(\lambda\gt0\) give \(F\equiv0\); \(\lambda=-p^2\) gives \(F_n=\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\), \(p=\frac{n\pi}L\).
  3. \(G'=-kp^2G\) ⇒ \(G_n=e^{-k(n\pi/L)^2t}\); \(u=\sum B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}Le^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\).
  4. At \(t=0\): \(\sum B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L=a\sin\frac{3\pi x}L-a\sin\frac{2\pi x}L\). By orthogonality (compare coefficients) \(B_3=a\), \(B_2=-a\), all others 0.

Say “comparing coefficients / by orthogonality, \(B_3=a\), \(B_2=-a\), \(B_n=0\) otherwise”; integrating is not needed and wastes time.

\[\begin{aligned}u(x,t)&=a\sin\frac{3\pi x}L\,e^{-\frac{9\pi^2k}{L^2}t}\\&\quad-a\sin\frac{2\pi x}L\,e^{-\frac{4\pi^2k}{L^2}t}\end{aligned}\]

As printed (valid when \(\frac{L^2}3\) and \(2L\) are whole numbers, e.g. \(L=3\)): each term is the mode with \(\frac{n\pi}L=\frac{L\pi}3\), resp. \(2\pi\), so \(u=a\sin\frac{L\pi x}3\,e^{-kL^2\pi^2t/9}\) \(-\,a\sin(2\pi x)\,e^{-4\pi^2kt}\). Same pattern: each sine keeps its shape and gets \(e^{-k\omega^2t}\), \(\omega\) = its frequency in \(x\).

Dec 2022 Q5(a)10 marks

Consider the PDE \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=k\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\), \(k\gt0\). Solve the boundary-initial value problem with \(k=5\), \(u(0,t)=0\), \(u(5,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=2a\sin\left(\dfrac{3\pi}{5}x\right)-2b\sin(2\pi x)\) where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants.

Hint

\(L=5\): shapes \(\sin\frac{n\pi x}5\). Which \(n\) give \(\sin\frac{3\pi x}5\) and \(\sin2\pi x\)?

Solution
  1. \(u=FG\): \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}{5G}=\lambda\); \(F(0)=F(5)=0\). Cases (skeleton): only \(\lambda=-p^2\), \(p=\frac{n\pi}5\), \(F_n=\sin\frac{n\pi x}5\).
  2. \(G_n=e^{-5(n\pi/5)^2t}=e^{-n^2\pi^2t/5}\); \(u=\sum B_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}5e^{-n^2\pi^2t/5}\).
  3. \(\sin\frac{3\pi x}5\) is \(n=3\); \(\sin2\pi x=\sin\frac{10\pi x}5\) is \(n=10\). Comparing coefficients: \(B_3=2a\), \(B_{10}=-2b\), others 0.
  4. Rates: \(n=3\): \(\frac{9\pi^2}5\); \(n=10\): \(\frac{100\pi^2}5=20\pi^2\).

The step that earns the mark is spotting \(\sin2\pi x\) as mode \(n=10\) of a rod of length 5.

\[\begin{aligned}u(x,t)&=2a\sin\frac{3\pi x}5\,e^{-\frac{9\pi^2}5t}\\&\quad-2b\sin(2\pi x)\,e^{-20\pi^2t}\end{aligned}\]

C5.2Heat equation, insulated ends or a ring

6 parts · 5 sittings (Dec 2023, 2015; May 2025, 2023, 2017) · 90 marks · Learn it · note §5 ring · note §7

Type card · predictable · which method?
Looks like
\(u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0\) (“both ends insulated”), or a thin ring / “bar with \(u(-L,t)=u(L,t)\), \(u_x(-L,t)=u_x(L,t)\)”; then a start \(f(x)\).
Which method?
  • Rod, both ends insulated (\(u_x=0\) at \(0\) and \(L\)) → cosine series on \([0,L]\) including the constant shape from \(\lambda=0\) (ready answer).
  • Ring (\(x=-L\) and \(x=L\) the same point; periodic conditions) → full Fourier series on \([-L,L]\), cosines and sines (ready answer). Even start → only cosines.
  • Start already cosines (\(15+3\cos2\pi x\), \(200\cos4\pi x\)) → read off, after matching each frequency to an \(n\).
Do
1. Cases: \(\lambda=0\) now gives \(F=\) constant (keep it); \(\lambda\gt0\) trivial; \(\lambda=-p^2\) gives \(\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\) (ring: also \(\sin\)). 2. \(G_0=1\), \(G_n=e^{-(n\pi c/L)^2t}\). 3. \(a_0\) = average of \(f\); \(a_n\) by the integral. 4. Write \(u\).
Watch
Losing the constant term \(a_0\) (the final temperature) is the classic slip. In the \(\lambda=0\) case \(F=Ax+B\), \(F'=A=0\) leaves \(F=B\), not zero.
Ready answer · heat equation, both ends insulated

\(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\); \(u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0\); \(u(x,0)=f(x)\):

\[\begin{aligned}u(x,t)&=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi c}L\right)^2t},\\ a_0&=\frac1L\int_0^Lf\,dx,\\ a_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

As \(t\to\infty\), \(u\to a_0\): no heat leaves the rod, so it settles at its average starting temperature. (Sheet convention: \(a_0\) here = sheet's \(\frac12a_0\).)

In words: with sealed ends the heat just evens out: the rod ends at its average temperature, and every cosine ripple on top of that fades away.

Ready answer · thin ring (periodic ends)

\(u_t=c^2u_{xx}\), \(-L\lt x\lt L\); \(u(-L,t)=u(L,t)\), \(u_x(-L,t)=u_x(L,t)\); \(u(x,0)=f(x)\):

\[\begin{aligned}u&=a_0+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\Big(a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\\&\qquad+b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\Big)e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi c}L\right)^2t},\\ a_0&=\frac1{2L}\int_{-L}^Lf\,dx,\\ a_n&=\frac1L\int_{-L}^Lf\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx,\\ b_n&=\frac1L\int_{-L}^Lf\sin\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

The periodic BCs force \(A\sin pL=0\) and \(B\sin pL=0\) at once, so \(p=\frac{n\pi}L\) and both \(\cos px\) and \(\sin px\) survive. Circumference \(2L\).

In words: a ring has no ends, so any shape that repeats round it is allowed: the start is expanded in a full Fourier series round the ring, the average stays and every ripple fades.

Dec 2023 Q5(b)15 marks

Given the conditions \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}(0,t)=0\), \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}(1,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=1-x\), \(0\lt x\lt1\). Solve the boundary value problem \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\partial^2u}{\partial x^2}\).

Hint

Insulated ends, \(L=1\), \(c=1\): cosine series with the constant term. \(a_n=2\int_0^1(1-x)\cos n\pi x\,dx\).

Solution
  1. \(u=FG\): \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G'}G=\lambda\); \(F'(0)=F'(1)=0\).
  2. \(\lambda=0\): \(F=Ax+B\), \(F'=A=0\) ⇒ \(F=B\), constant (non-trivial), \(G'=0\) ⇒ \(G\) constant. \(\lambda=p^2\gt0\): \(F'=p(Ae^{px}-Be^{-px})\); \(A-B=0\) and \(2Ap\sinh p=0\) ⇒ trivial. \(\lambda=-p^2\): \(F'=-Ap\sin px+Bp\cos px\); \(F'(0)=0\Rightarrow B=0\); \(F'(1)=0\Rightarrow\sin p=0\Rightarrow p=n\pi\).
  3. \(G_n=e^{-n^2\pi^2t}\); \(u=a_0+\sum a_n\cos n\pi x\,e^{-n^2\pi^2t}\).
  4. \(a_0=\int_0^1(1-x)\,dx=\frac12\).

\[\begin{aligned}\theta(x,t)&=2\sin\frac x2\sin\frac t2\\&\quad+\frac{32}\pi\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac1{n^3}\sin\frac{nx}2\cos\frac{nt}2\end{aligned}\]

\(C_n\) is the bank value \(\frac{4L^2[1-(-1)^n]}{n^3\pi^3}\) with \(L=2\pi\).

C5.5Wave equation with free end(s)

6 parts · 6 sittings (Dec 2021, 2017; May 2026, 2024, 2022, 2021) + the Dec 2018 repeat · 110 marks · Learn it · note §7

Type card · predictable · which method?
Looks like
A string “fastened at one end only” / “one end free” (\(u(0,t)=0\), \(u_x(L,t)=0\)), or “both ends free” (\(u_x=0\) at both ends); May papers add “rigidity \(c^2\)”.
Which method?
  • One fixed + one free → quarter-wave shapes \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\), frequencies \(\frac{(2n-1)c\pi}{2L}\); coefficients over \([0,L]\) with \(\frac2L\) (ready answer).
  • Both free → \(1,\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\); the \(\lambda=0\) mode is \(A_0+B_0t\): a rest level plus a drift if the mean start velocity is not 0 (ready answer).
  • Start already allowed shapes (\(3\sin\frac x2\) on length \(\pi\); \(1+3\cos\frac x2+2\cos\frac{5x}2\) on length \(2\pi\)) → read off, after matching each frequency to an \(n\). Most May questions are this.
Do
Settings (fixed end \(y=0\), free end \(y_x=0\), at rest \(y_t(x,0)=0\)) → cases → \(F_n\), \(G_n\) → coefficients → \(u\).
Watch
For one free end the allowed \(p\) come from \(\cos pL=0\), so \(pL=\frac\pi2,\frac{3\pi}2,\dots\), not \(n\pi\). At \(x=L\): \(\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2=0\), \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2=(-1)^{n+1}\).
Ready answer · string fixed at \(x=0\), free at \(x=L\)

\(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\); \(u(0,t)=0\), \(u_x(L,t)=0\); \(u(x,0)=f\), \(u_t(x,0)=g\); \(\mu_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2L}\):

\[\begin{aligned}u&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\big(B_n\cos c\mu_nt+B_n^*\sin c\mu_nt\big)\sin\mu_nx,\\ B_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf(x)\sin\mu_nx\,dx,\\ B_n^*&=\frac2{Lc\mu_n}\int_0^Lg(x)\sin\mu_nx\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

From the cases: \(F=A\cos px+B\sin px\), \(F(0)=0\Rightarrow A=0\), \(F'(L)=Bp\cos pL=0\Rightarrow pL=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2\). Orthogonality: \(\int_0^L\sin\mu_nx\sin\mu_mx\,dx=\frac L2\delta_{nm}\). Same shapes for a heat rod with one end at 0 and the other insulated (rates \(e^{-c^2\mu_n^2t}\)).

In words: a fixed end must be a zero of the shape and a free end must be a flat top, so only odd numbers of quarter-waves fit on the string.

Ready answer · string with both ends free

\(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\); \(u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t)=0\); \(u(x,0)=f\), \(u_t(x,0)=g\); \(\lambda_n=\frac{cn\pi}L\):

\[\begin{aligned}u&=a_0+b_0t\\&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^\infty\big(a_n\cos\lambda_nt+b_n\sin\lambda_nt\big)\cos\frac{n\pi x}L,\\ a_0&=\frac1L\int_0^Lf,\quad b_0=\frac1L\int_0^Lg,\\ a_n&=\frac2L\int_0^Lf\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx,\\ b_n&=\frac2{cn\pi}\int_0^Lg\cos\frac{n\pi x}L\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

\(\lambda=0\) gives \(F=\)const and \(G''=0\), \(G=A_0+B_0t\): that is the \(a_0+b_0t\) term. At rest ⇒ \(b_0=b_n=0\).

In words: free ends behave like insulated ends: cosine shapes plus a constant level; if the string is thrown upward on average, the whole string also drifts at that average speed.

Dec 2021 Q5(b)10 marks

Also set: Dec 2018 Q5(b) (15 marks; writes \(a^2\), \(a\gt0\), for \(k^2\), otherwise identical)

Given the conditions \(U(0,t)=0\), \(U(x,0)=x^3\), \(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x}(\pi,t)=0\), \(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial t}(x,0)=0\). Solve the boundary value problem \(\dfrac{\partial^2U}{\partial t^2}=k^2\dfrac{\partial^2U}{\partial x^2}\), \(t\gt0\), \(0\le x\le\pi\), where \(k\) is positive.

Hint

Fixed at 0, free at \(\pi\): shapes \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)x}2\); at rest. \(\frac2\pi\int_0^\pi x^3\sin mx\,dx\) with \(m=\frac{2n-1}2\): the sheet's reduction formula three times.

Solution
  1. \(U=FG\): \(\frac{F''}F=\frac{G''}{k^2G}=\lambda\); \(F(0)=0\), \(F'(\pi)=0\).
  2. \(\lambda=0\): \(F=Ax+B\), \(B=0\), \(F'=A=0\): trivial. \(\lambda=p^2\): \(A+B=0\), \(F'(\pi)=2Ap\cosh p\pi=0\) ⇒ trivial. \(\lambda=-p^2\): \(A=0\), \(F'(\pi)=Bp\cos p\pi=0\) ⇒ \(p=\frac{2n-1}2\).
  3. \(G_n=C_n\cos\frac{(2n-1)kt}2+D_n\sin\frac{(2n-1)kt}2\); at rest ⇒ \(D_n=0\).
  4. With \(m=\frac{2n-1}2\): \[\begin{aligned}&\int x^3\sin mx\,dx\\&=-\frac{x^3\cos mx}m+\frac{3x^2\sin mx}{m^2}\\&\quad+\frac{6x\cos mx}{m^3}-\frac{6\sin mx}{m^4}.\end{aligned}\] At \(\pi\): \(\cos m\pi=0\), \(\sin m\pi=(-1)^{n+1}\); at 0 all terms vanish.
  5. \(b_n=\frac2\pi(-1)^{n+1}\Big(\frac{3\pi^2}{m^2}-\frac6{m^4}\Big)\), and \(\frac{3\pi^2}{m^2}=\frac{12\pi^2}{(2n-1)^2}\), \(\frac6{m^4}=\frac{96}{(2n-1)^4}\).

\(\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2=0\) kills two of the four boundary terms; write that line, it is what the marker looks for in the integral.

\[\begin{aligned}U(x,t)&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\frac{(2n-1)x}2\\&\qquad\times\cos\frac{(2n-1)kt}2,\\ b_n&=\frac{24(-1)^{n+1}}\pi\cdot\frac{\pi^2(2n-1)^2-8}{(2n-1)^4}\end{aligned}\]

\(b_1=24\pi-\frac{192}\pi\approx14.28\). Dec 2018 version: replace \(k\) by \(a\). Agrees with the note's exam taster.

Dec 2017 Q5(b)15 marks

Solve the boundary value problem \(\dfrac{\partial^2U}{\partial t^2}-k^2\dfrac{\partial^2U}{\partial x^2}=0\), with \(k\ne0\), \(t\gt0\), \(x\in[0,l]\), and \(U(0,t)=0\), \(U(x,0)=\dfrac{\pi}{2l}x+\sin x\), \(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial x}(l,t)=0\), \(\dfrac{\partial U}{\partial t}(x,0)=0\).

(You may find the following fact helpful: \(\int_a^bu\,dv=uv\big|_a^b-\int_a^bv\,du\).)

Hint

Fixed at 0, free at \(l\): \(\mu_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2l}\). Two integrals: \(\int_0^lx\sin\mu_nx\) (by parts) and \(\int_0^l\sin x\sin\mu_nx\) (product to sum). \(\sin x\) is not a mode unless \(\mu_n=1\) for some \(n\).

Solution
  1. Separation and cases as Dec 2021 Q5(b) with length \(l\): \(F_n=\sin\mu_nx\), \(\mu_n=\frac{(2n-1)\pi}{2l}\); \(G_n=\cos k\mu_nt\) (at rest). Note \(\cos\mu_nl=0\), \(\sin\mu_nl=(-1)^{n+1}\).
  2. \(b_n=\frac2l\int_0^l\big(\frac\pi{2l}x+\sin x\big)\sin\mu_nx\,dx\).
  3. By parts: \[\begin{aligned}\int_0^lx\sin\mu_nx\,dx&=\Big[-\frac{x\cos\mu_nx}{\mu_n}\Big]_0^l\\&\quad+\frac1{\mu_n}\int_0^l\cos\mu_nx\,dx\\&=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\mu_n^2}.\end{aligned}\]
  4. Product to sum (if \(\mu_n\ne1\)): \[\begin{aligned}&\int_0^l\sin x\sin\mu_nx\,dx\\&=\frac12\Big[\frac{\sin(\mu_n-1)l}{\mu_n-1}-\frac{\sin(\mu_n+1)l}{\mu_n+1}\Big]\\&=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}\cos l}{\mu_n^2-1},\end{aligned}\] since \(\sin(\mu_n\mp1)l=(-1)^{n+1}\cos l\).
  5. \(b_n=\frac2l(-1)^{n+1}\Big[\frac\pi{2l\mu_n^2}+\frac{\cos l}{\mu_n^2-1}\Big]\), and \(\frac\pi{2l\mu_n^2}=\frac{2l}{(2n-1)^2\pi}\).

State \(\cos\mu_nl=0\) and \(\sin\mu_nl=(-1)^{n+1}\) before simplifying; the rest is algebra.

\[\begin{aligned}U&=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2l}\\&\qquad\times\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi kt}{2l},\\ b_n&=(-1)^{n+1}\Big[\frac4{(2n-1)^2\pi}\\&\qquad+\frac{8l\cos l}{(2n-1)^2\pi^2-4l^2}\Big]\end{aligned}\]

Special case \(l=\frac{(2m-1)\pi}2\) (e.g. \(l=\frac\pi2\)): then \(\sin x\) is exactly mode \(m\) and \(\cos l=0\), so \(b_n=\frac{4(-1)^{n+1}}{(2n-1)^2\pi}\) plus an extra 1 in \(b_m\).

◆Before the exam

  • Q5 is compulsory: plan your 45 minutes around it, not around the other questions.
  • Read the end conditions first and pick the family: \(u=0\) both ends → sine; \(u_x=0\) both ends → cosine + constant; one of each → \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi x}{2L}\); ring → full series; non-zero temperatures → steady state first.
  • Write the separation in full every time: \(u=FG\), “= constant \(\lambda\)” with the reason, all three cases with the BCs applied. That is about half the marks, and it is not on the formula sheet.
  • Never call the separation constant \(k\) if the question uses \(k\) (Dec 2015, 2017, 2021, 2022, 2024). Use \(\lambda\).
  • Time factors: heat \(e^{-(\text{coefficient})p_n^2t}\); wave \(\cos cp_nt\), \(\sin cp_nt\) with \(c=\sqrt{\text{coefficient}}\). \(u_t=ku_{xx}\) → \(e^{-kp_n^2t}\); \(u_{tt}=ku_{xx}\) → \(\cos\sqrt k\,p_nt\).
  • Before integrating, check whether the start is already allowed shapes (match \(\sin\omega x\) to \(\frac{n\pi}L\)). 8 of the 19 past parts on this page need no integral at all.
  • Insulated / free ends: keep the constant \(a_0\) (the average); for a moving string also \(b_0t\) (the mean velocity).
  • Wave with a start velocity: the sine-in-time coefficient carries \(\frac1{cp_n}\) (Dec 2014: \(D_1=2\), not 1).
  • Know the coefficient bank (\(a\), \(x\), \(x(L-x)\), triangle) and the sheet's reduction formula for \(x^2\), \(x^3\); simplify with \(\cos n\pi=(-1)^n\) and, for quarter-waves, \(\cos\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2=0\), \(\sin\frac{(2n-1)\pi}2=(-1)^{n+1}\).
  • Finish with one formula for \(u(x,t)\), and check it quickly at \(t=0\) and at one end.
