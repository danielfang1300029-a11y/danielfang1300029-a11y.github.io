◆Exam format and formula sheet for this chapter

  • December (main format): 3 hours, five 25-mark questions, answer any four (Q5, PDEs, is compulsory). C3a is never a whole question: it sits inside the vector-calculus questions Q2–Q4, so it is optional, but almost every paper has some.
  • Where C3a.1 appears: as part (i) of a Q2–Q4 part, 2–5 marks: “find div(\(\vb F\))” before a Gauss verification (C3c.4), “find curl(\(\vb F\))” before a scalar potential (C3c.1) or a flux / Stokes part (C3c.3, C3c.5). Dec 2025 had three of these openers.
  • Where C3a.2 appears: as a whole part (b) of Q2, Q3 or Q4 worth 8–12 marks (Dec 2024 Q3(b), 2023 Q4(b), 2022 Q3(b), 2018 Q2(b), 2017 Q2(b), 2014 Q4(a)), or Q1(a) in Dec 2017 (polar form of a field).
  • Where C3a.3 appears: Q1(a)(i) of Dec 2022 and Dec 2017 (the same polar-unit-vector question) and Dec 2022 Q2(b)(iii)–(v) (a space curve after a tangent-plane part).
  • What the December formula sheet gives (same sheet 2014–2025): surface, line and flux integrals, Gauss’ and Stokes’ theorems, Fourier series and reduction formulas. Nothing from this chapter.
  • What it leaves out: the div and curl formulas, grad, every vector identity (product rules, curl grad \(=\vb 0\), div curl \(=0\)), the results for \(\vb r\) and \(r\), and the polar unit vectors. Learn the ready answers below by heart.
  • Missing papers: Dec 2021 Q3 and Q4 are missing from the scans, so 2021 may also have had a C3a part.
  • May format (other semester): no C3a part in any May paper 2015–2026; div and curl only appear inside May Gauss / Stokes questions (C3c). May 2015–2019: five 20-mark questions, all compulsory, with a formula sheet; May 2021–2024: all five, no sheet; May 2025–2026: eight 10-mark questions, no sheet.
  • Time budget: 25 marks ≈ 45 min per question, about 1.8 min a mark: a 3-mark div ≈ 5 min, an 8-mark “show that” ≈ 15 min.
  • Notation in the papers: div(\(\vb F\)) \(=\nabla\cdot\vb F\), curl(\(\vb F\)) \(=\nabla\times\vb F\), grad(\(\phi\)) \(=\nabla\phi\); \(\vb F=M\ii+N\jj+P\kk\); \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\), \(r=|\vb r|\).

C3a.1Compute div and curl of a given field

11 parts · 5 sittings (Dec 2025, 2024, 2023, 2016, 2015) · 37 marks · always the opening part of a Gauss, Stokes or potential question · Divergence · note §4 Curl · note §5

Type card · predictable
Looks like
“Find div(\(\vb F\)) / curl(\(\vb F\)) / \(\nabla\cdot\vb F\) / \(\nabla\times\vb F\)” for a polynomial or rational field (2–5 marks); once, “find \(\alpha,\beta\) such that \(\vb F\) is solenoidal”.
Do
1. Write down \(M,N,P\) (for a 2-D field \(P=0\)). 2. div \(=M_x+N_y+P_z\), a scalar. 3. curl: write the determinant, expand along the top row, one line per component. 4. Simplify; if it is \(0\) / \(\vb 0\), say “solenoidal” / “irrotational”, because the next part uses it.
Watch
The minus sign on the \(\jj\)-component of curl (or use the cyclic form \(P_y-N_z,\ M_z-P_x,\ N_x-M_y\), which has none). div is a number at each point, never a vector.
Ready answer · div, curl, grad (not on the sheet)

For \(\vb F=M\ii+N\jj+P\kk\) and a scalar \(\phi\):

\[\nabla\phi=\pd{\phi}{x}\ii+\pd{\phi}{y}\jj+\pd{\phi}{z}\kk\] \[\nabla\cdot\vb F=\pd Mx+\pd Ny+\pd Pz\] \[\nabla\times\vb F=\begin{vmatrix}\ii&\jj&\kk\\ \partial_x&\partial_y&\partial_z\\ M&N&P\end{vmatrix}\] \[\begin{aligned}\nabla\times\vb F&=(P_y-N_z)\,\ii+(M_z-P_x)\,\jj\\&\quad+(N_x-M_y)\,\kk\end{aligned}\]

2-D field \(M\ii+N\jj\): \(\nabla\times\vb F=(N_x-M_y)\,\kk\). Solenoidal means \(\nabla\cdot\vb F=0\); irrotational means \(\nabla\times\vb F=\vb 0\).

In words: grad = the vector of partial derivatives; div = sum of “each component differentiated by its own variable”; curl \(\ii\)-part = (\(z\)-component by \(y\)) minus (\(y\)-component by \(z\)), and the other two follow by turning \(x\to y\to z\to x\).

Ready answer · results for \(\vb r\) and \(r\)

\(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\), \(r=|\vb r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\), valid wherever \(r\ne0\):

\[\nabla\cdot\vb r=3,\qquad \nabla\times\vb r=\vb 0,\qquad \nabla r=\frac{\vb r}{r}\] \[\nabla(r^n)=n\,r^{n-2}\,\vb r,\qquad \nabla\Big(\frac1r\Big)=-\frac{\vb r}{r^3}\]

In words: the position field spreads out at rate 3 and does not rotate; the gradient of any function of distance points straight away from the origin, with size \(\frac{d}{dr}(r^n)=nr^{n-1}\). The one-line reason: \(\pd rx=\frac xr\).

Past-paper parts (December, newest first)

Dec 2025 Q2(a)(i)4 marks

Let \(\vb F(x,y,z)\) \(=(2xy+z)\,\ii+(x^2+z^2)\,\jj\) \(+(x+2yz)\,\kk\) be a vector field defined on \(\R^3\). Find curl(\(\vb F\)). (Next parts, C3c.1: find a scalar potential, then a work integral.)

Hint

\(M=2xy+z\), \(N=x^2+z^2\), \(P=x+2yz\). Expand the curl determinant one component at a time.

Solution
  1. \(\ii\): \(P_y-N_z=2z-2z=0\).
  2. \(\jj\): \(M_z-P_x=1-1=0\).
  3. \(\kk\): \(N_x-M_y=2x-2x=0\).
  4. So \(\vb F\) is irrotational (and, on \(\R^3\), conservative: the next part).

Show the determinant and each of the three differences; writing only “\(=\vb 0\)” loses most of the 4 marks.

\(\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}\vb F=\vb 0\)
Dec 2025 Q3(b)(i)3 marks

Let \(\vb F=-12\,\ii+3x\,\jj+3z\,\kk\). Find div(\(\vb F\)). (Next part, C3c.4: verify Gauss’ theorem over the upper hemisphere \(z=\sqrt{a^2-x^2-y^2}\), \(z\ge0\).)

Hint

Differentiate each component by its own variable only: \(M\) by \(x\), \(N\) by \(y\), \(P\) by \(z\).

Solution
  1. \(M_x=\pd{}{x}(-12)=0\).
  2. \(N_y=\pd{}{y}(3x)=0\) (\(3x\) does not depend on \(y\)).
  3. \(P_z=\pd{}{z}(3z)=3\).

The common slip is \(N_y=3\): \(N=3x\) is differentiated with respect to \(y\), not \(x\).

\(\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\vb F=3\)
Dec 2025 Q4(a)(i)3 marks

Given \(\vb F=z\,\ii+x\,\jj+y\,\kk\). Find \(\nabla\times\vb F\). (Next parts: the flux of \(\nabla\times\vb F\) through a triangle (C3c.3), then the boundary line integral (C3b.1).)

Hint

\(M=z\), \(N=x\), \(P=y\): each component depends on one variable only.

Solution
  1. \(\ii\): \(P_y-N_z=1-0=1\).
  2. \(\jj\): \(M_z-P_x=1-0=1\).
  3. \(\kk\): \(N_x-M_y=1-0=1\).

Keep the order in each difference; a sign slip in the \(\jj\)-component changes the flux in the next part.

\(\displaystyle\nabla\times\vb F=\ii+\jj+\kk\)
Dec 2024 Q3(a)(i)3 marks

Given a vector field \(\vb F=x^2\,\ii+y^2\,\jj+z^2\,\kk\). Find the divergence of \(\vb F\). (Next part, C3c.4: verify Gauss’ theorem over the cylinder \(x^2+y^2\le R^2\), \(0\le z\le H\).)

Hint

\(\nabla\cdot\vb F=M_x+N_y+P_z\).

Solution
  1. \(M_x=2x\), \(N_y=2y\), \(P_z=2z\).
  2. Add.

The answer is a scalar function; do not write it as \(2x\ii+2y\jj+2z\kk\).

\(\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\vb F=2x+2y+2z\)
Dec 2023 Q3(a)(i)3 marks

Given \(\vb F=x\,\ii+y\,\jj+z\,\kk\). Find the divergence of \(\vb F\). (Next part, C3c.4: verify Gauss’ theorem over the ball \(x^2+y^2+z^2\le R^2\).)

Hint

This is \(\vb r\) itself (ready answer), but show the three derivatives.

Solution
  1. \(M_x=1\), \(N_y=1\), \(P_z=1\).
  2. Add: \(1+1+1=3\).

Write the definition line \(\nabla\cdot\vb F=M_x+N_y+P_z\) first; it carries a mark.

\(\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\vb F=3\)
Dec 2023 Q4(a)(i)5 marks

Consider the vector field \(\vb F\) \(=\dfrac{y}{x^2+y^2}\,\ii\) \(-\dfrac{x}{x^2+y^2}\,\jj\) defined over \(\R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}\) (that is, the whole \(XY\)-plane with \((0,0)\) removed). Find div(\(\vb F\)) and curl(\(\vb F\)). (Next part, C3c.2: \(\oint_C\vb F\cdot d\vb r\) round a square that does or does not enclose the origin.)

Hint

Quotient rule on \(\dfrac{y}{x^2+y^2}\) and \(\dfrac{-x}{x^2+y^2}\); for a 2-D field the curl has only a \(\kk\)-component \(N_x-M_y\).

Solution
  1. \(M_x=\dfrac{-2xy}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\), \(N_y=\dfrac{2xy}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\); so \(\nabla\cdot\vb F=0\).
  2. \(M_y\) \(=\dfrac{(x^2+y^2)-y\cdot2y}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\) \(=\dfrac{x^2-y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\).
  3. \(N_x\) \(=-\dfrac{(x^2+y^2)-x\cdot2x}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\) \(=\dfrac{x^2-y^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}\).
  4. \(\nabla\times\vb F=(N_x-M_y)\,\kk=\vb 0\) (the \(\ii,\jj\) parts vanish since \(P=0\) and nothing depends on \(z\)).

Say “for \((x,y)\ne(0,0)\)”: the field is not defined at the origin, which is exactly why the next part’s answer depends on the square.

\[\begin{aligned}&\nabla\cdot\vb F=0,\qquad\nabla\times\vb F=\vb 0\\&\big((x,y)\ne(0,0)\big)\end{aligned}\]
Dec 2016 Q2(b)(i)–(ii)2 + 3 marks

Given \(\vb F=2x\,\ii+y\,\jj+x\,\kk\) defined over \(\R^3\). (i) Evaluate \(\nabla\cdot\vb F\). (ii) Evaluate \(\nabla\times\vb F\). (Next part, C3c.4: verify Gauss’ theorem over the unit ball.)

Hint

\(M=2x\), \(N=y\), \(P=x\). Only \(P_x\) is non-zero among the cross derivatives.

Solution
  1. (i) \(M_x+N_y+P_z=2+1+0=3\).
  2. (ii) \(\ii\): \(P_y-N_z=0-0=0\).
  3. \(\jj\): \(M_z-P_x=0-1=-1\).
  4. \(\kk\): \(N_x-M_y=0-0=0\).

The only non-zero entry is the \(\jj\)-component, the one with the sign trap: \(-(P_x-M_z)=-1\).

\(\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\vb F=3,\qquad\nabla\times\vb F=-\jj\)
Dec 2016 Q3(a)(ii)3 marks

Given a vector field in \(\R^3\): \(\vb F\) \(=(\alpha y^2+4zx)\,\ii\) \(+y(\beta x+2z)\,\jj\) \(+(\alpha y^2+2x^2)\,\kk\), where \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) are constants. (Part (i), C3c.1: the conditions on \(\alpha,\beta\) for \(\vb F\) to be irrotational.) Find the divergence of \(\vb F\).

Hint

Expand \(N=\beta xy+2yz\) first; \(P\) has no \(z\) in it.

Solution
  1. \(M_x=4z\).
  2. \(N_y=\pd{}{y}(\beta xy+2yz)=\beta x+2z\).
  3. \(P_z=\pd{}{z}(\alpha y^2+2x^2)=0\).

Leave the answer in terms of \(\beta\) (\(\alpha\) drops out); the question does not fix the constants here.

\(\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\vb F=\beta x+6z\)
Dec 2015 Q2(b)(i)5 marks

Given \(\vb F=xyz\,\ii+xz\,\kk\). Find div(\(\vb F\)) and curl(\(\vb F\)). (Next part, C3c.5: verify Stokes’ theorem on the paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\) cut by \(z=4\).)

Hint

\(M=xyz\), \(N=0\), \(P=xz\).

Solution
  1. div: \(M_x+N_y+P_z=yz+0+x\).
  2. \(\ii\): \(P_y-N_z=0-0=0\).
  3. \(\jj\): \(M_z-P_x=xy-z\).
  4. \(\kk\): \(N_x-M_y=0-xz=-xz\).

Set \(N=0\) explicitly in the determinant so the missing \(\jj\)-component of \(\vb F\) is not forgotten.

\[\begin{aligned}\nabla\cdot\vb F&=x+yz\\ \nabla\times\vb F&=(xy-z)\,\jj-xz\,\kk\end{aligned}\]
Dec 2015 Q3(b)(i)3 marks

Consider a vector field on \(\R^2\): \(\vb F\) \(=(1+\alpha xy^2)\,\ii+\beta x^2y\,\jj\). Find \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) such that \(\vb F\) is solenoidal. (Next parts, C3c.1: the condition for \(\vb F\) to be irrotational, a potential and a line integral.)

Hint

Solenoidal means \(\nabla\cdot\vb F=0\) at every \((x,y)\): compare coefficients.

Solution
  1. \(\nabla\cdot\vb F\) \(=\pd{}{x}(1+\alpha xy^2)\) \(+\pd{}{y}(\beta x^2y)\) \(=\alpha y^2+\beta x^2\).
  2. This must be \(0\) for all \(x,y\): the coefficients of \(y^2\) and of \(x^2\) are both zero.

Give the reason “for all \(x,y\)”; \(\alpha y^2+\beta x^2=0\) at one point does not make the field solenoidal.

\(\displaystyle\alpha=0,\quad\beta=0\qquad(\text{then }\vb F=\ii)\)

C3a.2Vector identities and proofs with \(\vb r\), \(r\), \(\phi\vb F\) (incl. polar form)

14 parts · 6 sittings (Dec 2024, 2023, 2022, 2018, 2017, 2014) · 69 marks · a whole part (b) of Q2–Q4, 8–12 marks · Identities and product rules · note §6 \(\nabla\) as an operator · note §3

Decision procedure
  1. If you see “prove” with a general \(\phi\) and \(\vb F=M\ii+N\jj+P\kk\) → go component-wise: write the left side’s \(\ii\)-component (for a div: the whole scalar), use the ordinary product rule, regroup into the right side’s \(\ii\)-component, then write “similarly for \(\jj,\kk\) (cycle \(x\to y\to z\), \(M\to N\to P\))”.
  2. If you see \(\vb r\), \(r\), constant vectors \(\vb u,\vb v\) → do not expand into \(x,y,z\) at once: split with the product rules (often the ones you proved in the earlier part: “use (i)”), then use the \(\vb r\)-results (\(\nabla\cdot\vb r=3\), \(\nabla r^n=nr^{n-2}\vb r\), \(\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)=\vb v\), …).
  3. If you see a radial field \(h(r)\,\vb r\) → curl is \(\vb 0\) always; div \(=rh'(r)+3h\). A condition such as div \(=0\) becomes a separable ODE in \(r\); use the given value to fix the constant.
  4. If you see “show that … is a constant vector” → it is (almost always) \(\vb 0\): simplify one component to \(0\) and say “similarly”, or cancel whole terms with an identity. End with “\(=\vb 0\), a constant vector”.
  5. If you see \(\vb u_r,\vb u_\theta\) or “in polar coordinates” → write \(\ii,\jj\) in terms of \(\vb u_r,\vb u_\theta\) and substitute; for div, apply the given chain-rule formulas for \(\pd{}{x},\pd{}{y}\) to \(P\) and \(Q\), then expand the right-hand side and compare.
  6. If an identity route stalls or you doubt a sign → fall back on components with \(\pd rx=\frac xr\). It always works; it is just longer.

Cues: “prove”, “show that”, “evaluate” with \(\phi\), \(M,N,P\), \(\vb r\), \(r\), \(\vb u\), \(\vb v\) and no numbers; an earlier part that proves a product rule is the tool for the next part.

Traps:

  • \(\nabla(r^n)\) is a vector, \(nr^{n-2}\vb r\), not \(nr^{n-1}\).
  • Order matters: \(\nabla\phi\times\vb F\) \(=-\vb F\times\nabla\phi\); \((\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb F\) is an operator applied to \(\vb F\), not \(\vb F(\nabla\cdot\vb F)\).
  • Results with \(r\) in a denominator hold only for \(\vb r\ne\vb 0\): say so.
  • In a “prove” part, checking one example, or quoting the very identity you were asked to prove, earns nothing.
Ready answer · product rules (not on the sheet)

\(\phi,\psi\) scalar fields; \(\vb F,\vb G\) vector fields; numbering as in the note.

\[\begin{aligned}&2.\ \ \nabla(\phi\psi)=\phi\nabla\psi+\psi\nabla\phi\\ &4.\ \ \nabla\cdot(\phi\vb F)=\phi\,\nabla\cdot\vb F+\vb F\cdot\nabla\phi\\ &6.\ \ \nabla\times(\phi\vb F)=\nabla\phi\times\vb F+\phi\,\nabla\times\vb F\\ &7.\ \ \nabla\cdot(\vb F\times\vb G)=\vb G\cdot(\nabla\times\vb F)\\&\qquad\qquad-\vb F\cdot(\nabla\times\vb G)\\ &8.\ \ \nabla\times(\vb F\times\vb G)=\vb F(\nabla\cdot\vb G)-\vb G(\nabla\cdot\vb F)\\&\qquad\qquad+(\vb G\cdot\nabla)\vb F-(\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb G\end{aligned}\]

Beyond the handout: \(\nabla(\vb F\cdot\vb G)\) \(=(\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb G\) \(+(\vb G\cdot\nabla)\vb F\) \(+\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb G)\) \(+\vb G\times(\nabla\times\vb F)\); with \(\vb G=\vb F\): \(\tfrac12\nabla(\vb F\cdot\vb F)\) \(=(\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb F\) \(+\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb F)\).

In words: differentiate one factor at a time and keep the kind of product (dot stays dot, cross stays cross, with the factors in the same order).

Ready answer · the two zero identities \[\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=\vb 0,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vb F)=0\]

In words: a gradient field never rotates, and a curl field has no sources (both because mixed partial derivatives are equal).

Ready answer · \(\vb r\) with constant \(\vb v\), and radial fields \[\begin{aligned}\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)&=\vb v,\qquad(\vb v\cdot\nabla)\vb r=\vb v\\ \nabla\cdot(\vb v\times\vb r)&=0,\qquad\nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)=2\vb v\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}\nabla\times\big(h(r)\vb r\big)&=\vb 0\\ \nabla\cdot\big(h(r)\vb r\big)&=r\,h'(r)+3h(r)\end{aligned}\] \[\nabla\cdot\Big(\frac{\vb r}{r^3}\Big)=0,\qquad\nabla^2\Big(\frac1r\Big)=0\qquad(\vb r\ne\vb 0)\] \[\nabla^2(r^n)=n(n+1)\,r^{n-2}\] \[\vb a\times(\vb b\times\vb c)=\vb b(\vb a\cdot\vb c)-\vb c(\vb a\cdot\vb b)\]

In words: a constant vector dotted with \(\vb r\) has that vector as its gradient; the spinning field \(\vb v\times\vb r\) has no sources and curl twice its axis; any field pointing straight out from the origin is curl-free, and the inverse-square field \(\vb r/r^3\) is source-free away from the origin. The last one is “BAC minus CAB”.

Ready answer · polar form of a 2-D field \[\begin{aligned}\ii&=\cos\theta\,\vb u_r-\sin\theta\,\vb u_\theta\\ \jj&=\sin\theta\,\vb u_r+\cos\theta\,\vb u_\theta\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}P\ii+Q\jj&=v\,\vb u_r+w\,\vb u_\theta,\\ v&=P\cos\theta+Q\sin\theta,\\ w&=-P\sin\theta+Q\cos\theta\end{aligned}\] \[\operatorname{div}\vb F=\pd vr+\frac vr+\frac1r\pd w\theta\]

In words: \(v\) and \(w\) are the outward and the anticlockwise parts of the field (\(v=\vb F\cdot\vb u_r\), \(w=\vb F\cdot\vb u_\theta\)); the polar divergence has an extra \(\frac vr\) because outward-pointing arrows spread apart as \(r\) grows.

Past-paper parts (December, newest first)

Dec 2024 Q3(b)(i)–(iii)4 + 4 + 4 marks

Let \(\phi\) be a scalar field, \(\vb F=M\ii+N\jj+P\kk\) be a vector field, \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\), and \(r=|\vb r|\).

(i) Prove that \(\nabla\cdot(\phi\vb F)\) \(=\phi\,\nabla\cdot\vb F\) \(+\vb F\cdot\nabla\phi\).
(ii) Prove that \(\operatorname{curl}(\phi\vb F)\) \(=\operatorname{grad}(\phi)\times\vb F\) \(+\phi\operatorname{curl}(\vb F)\).
(iii) Find \(\operatorname{div}\Big(\dfrac{\vb r}{r^3}\Big)\).

Hint

(i), (ii): \(\phi\vb F=\phi M\ii+\phi N\jj+\phi P\kk\); product rule on each derivative. (iii): use (i) with \(\phi=r^{-3}\), \(\vb F=\vb r\).

Solution
  1. (i) \(\nabla\cdot(\phi\vb F)\) \(=\pd{(\phi M)}x+\pd{(\phi N)}y\) \(+\pd{(\phi P)}z\).
  2. Product rule: \(=\big(\phi M_x+M\phi_x\big)\) \(+\big(\phi N_y+N\phi_y\big)\) \(+\big(\phi P_z+P\phi_z\big)\).
  3. Regroup: \(=\phi\,(M_x+N_y+P_z)\) \(+(M\phi_x+N\phi_y+P\phi_z)\) \(=\phi\,\nabla\cdot\vb F\) \(+\vb F\cdot\nabla\phi\). \(\blacksquare\)
  4. (ii) \(\ii\)-component of \(\nabla\times(\phi\vb F)\): \(\pd{(\phi P)}y-\pd{(\phi N)}z\) \(=\phi\,(P_y-N_z)+(\phi_yP-\phi_zN)\).
  5. \(\ii\)-component of \(\phi\,\nabla\times\vb F\) is \(\phi\,(P_y-N_z)\); \(\ii\)-component of \(\nabla\phi\times\vb F\) \(=(\phi_x,\phi_y,\phi_z)\times(M,N,P)\) is \(\phi_yP-\phi_zN\). They add to the line above.
  6. Similarly for the \(\jj\)- and \(\kk\)-components (cycle \(x\to y\to z\), \(M\to N\to P\)). \(\blacksquare\)
  7. (iii) By (i) with \(\phi=r^{-3}\), \(\vb F=\vb r\): \(\nabla\cdot\big(r^{-3}\vb r\big)\) \(=r^{-3}\,\nabla\cdot\vb r\) \(+\vb r\cdot\nabla(r^{-3})\).
  8. \(\nabla\cdot\vb r=3\), \(\nabla(r^{-3})=-3r^{-5}\vb r\): so \(=\dfrac3{r^3}\) \(-\dfrac{3\,\vb r\cdot\vb r}{r^5}\) \(=\dfrac3{r^3}-\dfrac{3r^2}{r^5}=0\).

In (ii) show the \(\ii\)-component of all three terms explicitly; “similarly” is accepted for \(\jj,\kk\) only after one full component.

\(\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\Big(\frac{\vb r}{r^3}\Big)=0\quad(\vb r\ne\vb 0)\)
Dec 2023 Q4(b)8 marks

Let \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\) and \(\vb F\) \(=M(x,y,z)\,\ii+N(x,y,z)\,\jj\) \(+P(x,y,z)\,\kk\). Show that

\[(\vb F\cdot\nabla)(\vb F)+\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb F)-\tfrac12\nabla(\vb F\cdot\vb F)\]

is a constant vector. Notice that the operator \(\vb F\cdot\nabla\equiv M\pd{}{x}\) \(+N\pd{}{y}+P\pd{}{z}\).

Hint

Work out the \(\ii\)-component of each of the three terms in terms of \(M,N,P\) and their derivatives, and add.

Solution
  1. \(\ii\)-component of \((\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb F\): \(MM_x+NM_y+PM_z\).
  2. \(\nabla\times\vb F\) has components \(P_y-N_z\), \(M_z-P_x\), \(N_x-M_y\), so the \(\ii\)-component of \(\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb F)\) is \(N(N_x-M_y)-P(M_z-P_x)\).
  3. \(\ii\)-component of \(\tfrac12\nabla(M^2+N^2+P^2)\): \(MM_x+NN_x+PP_x\).
  4. Sum (terms cancel in pairs):\[\begin{aligned}&MM_x+NM_y+PM_z\\&+NN_x-NM_y-PM_z+PP_x\\&-MM_x-NN_x-PP_x=0.\end{aligned}\]
  5. Similarly the \(\jj\)- and \(\kk\)-components are \(0\) (cycle \(x\to y\to z\), \(M\to N\to P\)). So the expression is \(\vb 0\), a constant vector.

Name the constant: “\(=\vb 0\)”. Showing one component cancel term by term earns most of the marks.

\[\begin{aligned}&(\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb F+\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb F)\\&\quad-\tfrac12\nabla(\vb F\cdot\vb F)=\vb 0\end{aligned}\]

Equivalent: the expression is zero because \(\tfrac12\nabla(\vb F\cdot\vb F)\) \(=(\vb F\cdot\nabla)\vb F\) \(+\vb F\times(\nabla\times\vb F)\) (ready answer), but that identity is what the question wants you to show.

Dec 2022 Q3(b)(i)–(ii)3 + 5 marks

Let \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\) and \(r=|\vb r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\). A vector field \(\vb V\) is given by \(\vb V=h(r)\,\vb r\). (i) Evaluate \(\nabla\times\vb V\). (ii) If \(\nabla\cdot\vb V=0\), find \(h(r)\) given that \(h(1)=1\).

Hint

Product rules with \(\phi=h(r)\); chain rule: \(\nabla h(r)=h'(r)\,\nabla r\) \(=h'(r)\,\dfrac{\vb r}{r}\).

Solution
  1. (i) \(\nabla\times(h\vb r)\) \(=\nabla h\times\vb r\) \(+h\,\nabla\times\vb r\) \(=h'(r)\,\dfrac{\vb r\times\vb r}{r}\) \(+h\cdot\vb 0\) \(=\vb 0\) (\(\vb r\times\vb r=\vb 0\)).
  2. (ii) \(\nabla\cdot(h\vb r)\) \(=\nabla h\cdot\vb r+h\,\nabla\cdot\vb r\) \(=h'(r)\,\dfrac{\vb r\cdot\vb r}{r}+3h\) \(=r\,h'+3h\).
  3. Set \(rh'+3h=0\): \(\dfrac{dh}{h}=-\dfrac{3\,dr}{r}\) \(\Rightarrow\ln|h|=-3\ln r+c\) \(\Rightarrow h=Cr^{-3}\).
  4. \(h(1)=C=1\).

The step \(\nabla h(r)=h'(r)\,\vb r/r\) is the key mark in both parts; state it.

\[\nabla\times\vb V=\vb 0,\qquad h(r)=\frac1{r^3}\]
Dec 2018 Q2(b)(i)–(ii)5 + 6 marks

Let \(\vb v=a\ii+b\jj+c\kk\) be a constant vector and \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\), \(r=|\vb r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\).

(i) Show that \(\operatorname{div}\big[\operatorname{grad}\big((\vb r\cdot\vb r)(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\big)\big]\) \(=10\,(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\).
(ii) Prove that \(\operatorname{curl}\Big(\dfrac{\vb v\times\vb r}{r^3}\Big)\) \(=\dfrac{3\vb r}{r^5}(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\) \(-\dfrac{\vb v}{r^3}\).

Hint

(i) Gradient of a product, then div of each piece with rule 4. (ii) Rule 6 with \(\phi=r^{-3}\) and \(\vb F=\vb v\times\vb r\); you need \(\nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)\) and BAC−CAB.

Solution
  1. (i) \(\nabla\big(r^2(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\big)\) \(=(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\nabla(r^2)\) \(+r^2\nabla(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\) \(=2(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\,\vb r+r^2\vb v\).
  2. \(\nabla\cdot\big(2(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\vb r\big)\) \(=2\big[(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\,\nabla\cdot\vb r+\vb r\cdot\nabla(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\big]\) \(=2\big[3\,\vb r\cdot\vb v+\vb r\cdot\vb v\big]\) \(=8\,\vb r\cdot\vb v\).
  3. \(\nabla\cdot(r^2\vb v)\) \(=\vb v\cdot\nabla(r^2)\) \(+r^2\,\nabla\cdot\vb v\) \(=2\,\vb r\cdot\vb v+0\). Total \(10\,\vb r\cdot\vb v\). \(\blacksquare\)
  4. (ii) \(\vb v\times\vb r\) \(=(bz-cy)\ii\) \(+(cx-az)\jj\) \(+(ay-bx)\kk\); its curl has \(\ii\)-component \(\pd{}{y}(ay-bx)-\pd{}{z}(cx-az)=2a\); similarly \(2b,2c\): \(\nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)=2\vb v\).
  5. Rule 6: \(\nabla\times\big(r^{-3}(\vb v\times\vb r)\big)\) \(=\nabla(r^{-3})\times(\vb v\times\vb r)\) \(+r^{-3}\cdot2\vb v\), with \(\nabla(r^{-3})=-3r^{-5}\vb r\).
  6. BAC−CAB: \(\vb r\times(\vb v\times\vb r)\) \(=r^2\vb v-(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\vb r\).
  7. So the curl \(=-\dfrac{3}{r^5}\big[r^2\vb v-(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\vb r\big]\) \(+\dfrac{2\vb v}{r^3}\) \(=\dfrac{3(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\vb r}{r^5}\) \(-\dfrac{\vb v}{r^3}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Quote each rule as you use it (“by \(\nabla\times(\phi\vb F)\) \(=\nabla\phi\times\vb F\) \(+\phi\nabla\times\vb F\)”); the marks are for the chain of justified steps.

\[\begin{aligned}&\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}\big(r^2(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\big)=10\,(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\\ &\nabla\times\frac{\vb v\times\vb r}{r^3}=\frac{3(\vb r\cdot\vb v)\,\vb r}{r^5}-\frac{\vb v}{r^3}\end{aligned}\]
Dec 2017 Q1(a)(iii)–(iv)4 + 6 marks

Let \(x=r\cos\theta\), \(y=r\sin\theta\). Earlier parts: (i), C3a.3: the polar unit vectors are \(\vb u_r=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj\), \(\vb u_\theta\) \(=-\sin\theta\,\ii+\cos\theta\,\jj\); (ii), C1.1: for \(f(x,y)\) and \(g(r,\theta)=f(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\),

\[\begin{aligned}\pd fx&=\cos\theta\pd gr-\frac{\sin\theta}r\pd g\theta,\\ \pd fy&=\sin\theta\pd gr+\frac{\cos\theta}r\pd g\theta.\end{aligned}\]

(iii) Given \(\vb F(x,y)=M(x,y)\,\ii+N(x,y)\,\jj\) and \(M(x,y)=P(r,\theta)\), \(N(x,y)=Q(r,\theta)\). Show that \(\vb G(r,\theta)\) \(=\vb F(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\) can be expressed as \(\vb G(r,\theta)\) \(=v(r,\theta)\,\vb u_r\) \(+w(r,\theta)\,\vb u_\theta\), where \(v=P\cos\theta+Q\sin\theta\), \(w=-P\sin\theta+Q\cos\theta\).

(iv) Use Q1(ii) and Q1(iii) to show that \(\operatorname{div}\big(\vb G(r,\theta)\big)\) \(=\pd vr+\dfrac vr+\dfrac1r\pd w\theta\). (Notice that \(\operatorname{div}(\vb G(r,\theta))\) is \(\operatorname{div}(\vb F(x,y))\) in polar coordinates.)

Hint

(iii) Solve (i) for \(\ii\) and \(\jj\). (iv) \(\operatorname{div}\vb F=M_x+N_y\): use (ii) with \(f=M\), \(g=P\) and with \(f=N\), \(g=Q\); then expand the right-hand side.

Solution
  1. (iii) From (i): \(\cos\theta\,\vb u_r\) \(-\sin\theta\,\vb u_\theta\) \(=\ii\) and \(\sin\theta\,\vb u_r\) \(+\cos\theta\,\vb u_\theta\) \(=\jj\) (check by expanding; \(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1\)).
  2. \(\vb G=P\ii+Q\jj\) \(=P(\cos\theta\,\vb u_r-\sin\theta\,\vb u_\theta)\) \(+Q(\sin\theta\,\vb u_r+\cos\theta\,\vb u_\theta)\) \(=(P\cos\theta+Q\sin\theta)\,\vb u_r\) \(+(-P\sin\theta+Q\cos\theta)\,\vb u_\theta\) \(=v\,\vb u_r+w\,\vb u_\theta\). \(\blacksquare\)
  3. (iv) By (ii): \(M_x\) \(=\cos\theta\,P_r\) \(-\dfrac{\sin\theta}{r}P_\theta\), \(N_y\) \(=\sin\theta\,Q_r\) \(+\dfrac{\cos\theta}{r}Q_\theta\).
  4. Right-hand side: \(v_r=P_r\cos\theta+Q_r\sin\theta\); \(w_\theta\) \(=-P_\theta\sin\theta-P\cos\theta\) \(+Q_\theta\cos\theta-Q\sin\theta\).
  5. So \(\dfrac1r w_\theta\) \(=\dfrac{-P_\theta\sin\theta+Q_\theta\cos\theta}{r}\) \(-\dfrac vr\), and the \(\pm\dfrac vr\) cancel.
  6. \(v_r+\dfrac vr+\dfrac1rw_\theta\) \(=\cos\theta\,P_r+\sin\theta\,Q_r\) \(-\dfrac{\sin\theta}rP_\theta\) \(+\dfrac{\cos\theta}rQ_\theta\) \(=M_x+N_y=\operatorname{div}\vb F\). \(\blacksquare\)

In (iv) do not forget the product rule in \(w_\theta\): the terms \(-P\cos\theta-Q\sin\theta=-v\) are what cancel \(\frac vr\).

\(\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\vb G=\pd vr+\frac vr+\frac1r\pd w\theta\)
Dec 2017 Q2(b)8 marks

Let \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\). Show that

\[\nabla\times\Big(\kk\times\nabla\Big(\frac1{|\vb r|}\Big)\Big)+\nabla\Big(\kk\cdot\nabla\Big(\frac1{|\vb r|}\Big)\Big)\]

is a constant vector.

Hint

\(\nabla(1/r)=-\vb r/r^3\). Work out \(\kk\times\nabla(1/r)\) and \(\kk\cdot\nabla(1/r)\) as explicit fields, then take curl and grad.

Solution
  1. \(\nabla\big(\tfrac1r\big)\) \(=-\dfrac{\vb r}{r^3}\); \(\kk\times\vb r=-y\,\ii+x\,\jj\). So \(\vb A=\kk\times\nabla\big(\tfrac1r\big)\) \(=\dfrac{y}{r^3}\ii-\dfrac{x}{r^3}\jj\) and \(\kk\cdot\nabla\big(\tfrac1r\big)\) \(=-\dfrac{z}{r^3}\).
  2. Use \(\pd{}{x}r^{-3}=-\dfrac{3x}{r^5}\) (and the same for \(y,z\)). Curl of \(\vb A\): \(\ii\): \(0-\pd{}{z}\Big(-\dfrac x{r^3}\Big)\) \(=-\dfrac{3xz}{r^5}\); \(\jj\): \(\pd{}{z}\Big(\dfrac y{r^3}\Big)-0\) \(=-\dfrac{3yz}{r^5}\).
  3. \(\kk\): \(\pd{}{x}\Big(-\dfrac x{r^3}\Big)\) \(-\pd{}{y}\Big(\dfrac y{r^3}\Big)\) \(=-\dfrac2{r^3}+\dfrac{3(x^2+y^2)}{r^5}\).
  4. \(\nabla\Big(-\dfrac z{r^3}\Big)\) \(=\dfrac{3xz}{r^5}\ii+\dfrac{3yz}{r^5}\jj\) \(+\Big(-\dfrac1{r^3}+\dfrac{3z^2}{r^5}\Big)\kk\).
  5. Add: \(\ii,\jj\) cancel; \(\kk\): \(-\dfrac3{r^3}\) \(+\dfrac{3(x^2+y^2+z^2)}{r^5}\) \(=-\dfrac3{r^3}+\dfrac3{r^3}=0\). So the sum is \(\vb 0\) for \(\vb r\ne\vb 0\), a constant vector.

Keep the \(r^{-5}\) terms unsimplified until the last line; the cancellation \(x^2+y^2+z^2=r^2\) is the point of the question.

\[\begin{aligned}&\nabla\times\Big(\kk\times\nabla\tfrac1r\Big)+\nabla\Big(\kk\cdot\nabla\tfrac1r\Big)\\&\quad=\vb 0\quad(\vb r\ne\vb 0)\end{aligned}\]

Equivalent, with identities: for \(\vb A=\nabla(1/r)\), rule 8 gives \(\nabla\times(\kk\times\vb A)\) \(=\kk(\nabla\cdot\vb A)\) \(-(\kk\cdot\nabla)\vb A\), and \(\nabla(\kk\cdot\vb A)\) \(=(\kk\cdot\nabla)\vb A\) \(+\kk\times(\nabla\times\vb A)\); with \(\nabla\cdot\vb A=\nabla^2(1/r)=0\) and \(\nabla\times\vb A=\vb 0\) the sum is \(\vb 0\).

Dec 2014 Q4(a)(i)–(iii)3 + 4 + 5 marks

Let \(\vb u\) and \(\vb v\) be constant vectors, \(\vb r=x\ii+y\jj+z\kk\) and \(r=|\vb r|=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\).

(i) Evaluate \(\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)\).
(ii) Evaluate \(\nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)\).
(iii) Prove that \(\vb u\cdot\nabla\Big[\vb v\cdot\nabla\Big(\dfrac1r\Big)\Big]\) \(=\dfrac{3(\vb u\cdot\vb r)(\vb v\cdot\vb r)}{r^5}\) \(-\dfrac{\vb u\cdot\vb v}{r^3}\).

Hint

Write \(\vb v=(v_1,v_2,v_3)\) for (i), (ii). For (iii) work from the inside out: first \(\vb v\cdot\nabla(1/r)\), a scalar; then its gradient (product rule, using (i)); then dot with \(\vb u\).

Solution
  1. (i) \(\vb v\cdot\vb r=v_1x+v_2y+v_3z\), so \(\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)=(v_1,v_2,v_3)\) \(=\vb v\).
  2. (ii) \(\vb v\times\vb r\) has components \(v_2z-v_3y\), \(v_3x-v_1z\), \(v_1y-v_2x\); \(\ii\)-component of the curl: \(\pd{}{y}(v_1y-v_2x)-\pd{}{z}(v_3x-v_1z)\) \(=2v_1\); similarly \(2v_2,2v_3\).
  3. (iii) \(\vb v\cdot\nabla\big(\tfrac1r\big)\) \(=\vb v\cdot\Big(-\dfrac{\vb r}{r^3}\Big)\) \(=-\dfrac{\vb v\cdot\vb r}{r^3}\).
  4. \(\nabla\Big(-\dfrac{\vb v\cdot\vb r}{r^3}\Big)\) \(=-\dfrac{\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)}{r^3}\) \(-(\vb v\cdot\vb r)\nabla(r^{-3})\) \(=-\dfrac{\vb v}{r^3}\) \(+\dfrac{3(\vb v\cdot\vb r)\,\vb r}{r^5}\), by (i) and \(\nabla r^{-3}=-3r^{-5}\vb r\).
  5. Dot with \(\vb u\): \(-\dfrac{\vb u\cdot\vb v}{r^3}\) \(+\dfrac{3(\vb u\cdot\vb r)(\vb v\cdot\vb r)}{r^5}\). \(\blacksquare\)

(iii) is built to use (i): cite it when you write \(\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)=\vb v\).

\[\begin{aligned}\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)&=\vb v\\ \nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)&=2\vb v\end{aligned}\]

C3a.3Vector functions and space curves: parametrise, tangent vector, polar unit vectors

4 parts · 1 sitting (Dec 2022; Q1(a)(i) repeats Dec 2017 Q1(a)(i)) · 13 marks · Learn it · note §1

Type card · predictable
Looks like
“Show that \(\vb u_r=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj\), \(\vb u_\theta=\dots\)” from a figure; or “find a parametrisation \(\vb r(t)\) of the curve where two surfaces meet, its tangent vector at a point, and how it sits relative to a surface”.
Do
Polar: \(\vb u_r=\) position vector ÷ its length; \(\vb u_\theta=\vb u_r\) turned \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise (angle \(\theta+\frac\pi2\)). Curve: 1. put \(x=t\); 2. solve the two surface equations for \(y,z\); 3. differentiate each component; 4. put in the \(t\) of the point; 5. compare with the surface normal \(\nabla h\) by a dot product.
Watch
Check the point is on the curve at your \(t\) (here \(t=x\)-coordinate), and in “discuss the relation” give the geometric conclusion, not just “dot product \(=0\)”.
Ready answer · polar unit vectors and tangent vectors \[\vb u_r=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj,\qquad\vb u_\theta=-\sin\theta\,\ii+\cos\theta\,\jj\] \[\frac{d\vb u_r}{d\theta}=\vb u_\theta,\qquad\frac{d\vb u_\theta}{d\theta}=-\vb u_r\] \[\begin{aligned}\text{tangent: }&\vb r'(t_0)=x'(t_0)\ii+y'(t_0)\jj+z'(t_0)\kk\\ \text{tangent line: }&\vb r(t_0)+s\,\vb r'(t_0)\end{aligned}\]

In words: \(\vb u_r\) points straight away from the origin and \(\vb u_\theta\) points the way you move when \(\theta\) increases; the tangent vector of a curve is the velocity of the moving point. If the tangent is perpendicular to a surface’s normal, the curve touches (is tangent to) the surface there.

Past-paper parts (December, newest first)

Dec 2022 Q1(a)(i)4 marks

Also set: Dec 2017 Q1(a)(i) (3 marks, same wording and figure)

Let \(x=r\cos\theta\) and \(y=r\sin\theta\) be the transformation formulae between Cartesian coordinates \((x,y)\) and polar coordinates \((r,\theta)\). Let \(\vb u_r\) and \(\vb u_\theta\) be the unit vectors in positive directions at \((r,\theta)\) as shown in Figure Q1. Show that \(\vb u_r=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj\), \(\vb u_\theta\) \(=-\sin\theta\,\ii+\cos\theta\,\jj\).

Figure Q1 (redrawn): \(\vb u_r\) continues outward along the radius; \(\vb u_\theta\) is perpendicular to it, pointing the way \(\theta\) increases.

Hint

\(\vb u_r\) is the position vector \(x\ii+y\jj\) divided by its length. \(\vb u_\theta\) is a unit vector at angle \(\theta+\frac\pi2\) to the \(X\)-axis.

Solution
  1. The position vector of the point is \(x\ii+y\jj\) \(=r\cos\theta\,\ii+r\sin\theta\,\jj\), of length \(r\), pointing outward along the radius.
  2. So \(\vb u_r=\dfrac{x\ii+y\jj}{r}\) \(=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj\).
  3. \(\vb u_\theta\) is a unit vector perpendicular to \(\vb u_r\), in the direction of increasing \(\theta\): its angle with the \(X\)-axis is \(\theta+\frac\pi2\).
  4. \(\vb u_\theta\) \(=\cos\big(\theta+\tfrac\pi2\big)\ii\) \(+\sin\big(\theta+\tfrac\pi2\big)\jj\) \(=-\sin\theta\,\ii+\cos\theta\,\jj\).
  5. Check: \(|\vb u_\theta|=1\) and \(\vb u_r\cdot\vb u_\theta\) \(=-\cos\theta\sin\theta\) \(+\sin\theta\cos\theta\) \(=0\).

Use the figure: say why each vector has that direction (along the radius; \(90^\circ\) anticlockwise from it), not just the formulas.

\[\begin{aligned}\vb u_r&=\cos\theta\,\ii+\sin\theta\,\jj\\ \vb u_\theta&=-\sin\theta\,\ii+\cos\theta\,\jj\end{aligned}\]

Equivalent method: \(\vb u_r\) \(=\pd{\vb R}{r}\Big/\Big|\pd{\vb R}{r}\Big|\), \(\vb u_\theta\) \(=\pd{\vb R}{\theta}\Big/\Big|\pd{\vb R}{\theta}\Big|\) with \(\vb R=r\cos\theta\,\ii+r\sin\theta\,\jj\) (\(|\partial\vb R/\partial\theta|=r\)).

Dec 2022 Q2(b)(iii)–(v)4 + 3 + 2 marks

Given the surface \(\Sigma\): \(h(x,y,z)=xyz-x^2-6y+6=0\). (Earlier parts, C1.3: (i) a normal vector to \(\Sigma\) at \((1,1,1)\) is \(\nabla h(1,1,1)=-\ii-5\jj+\kk\); (ii) the tangent plane there is \(x+5y-z=5\).)

(iii) A curve \(C\) is obtained from the intersection of two surfaces: \(x^2-1=(y+z)(y-z)\), \(y+z=\dfrac2x\). A parametrization of \(C\) is given by the position vector \(\vb r(t)=t\,\ii+y(t)\,\jj+z(t)\,\kk\). Find \(y(t)\) and \(z(t)\). (Hint: Let \(x=t\) and solve \(y\) and \(z\) in terms of \(t\).)

(iv) Show that the tangent vector of \(C\) at \((1,1,1)\) is given by \(\dfrac{d\vb r}{dt}(1,1,1)\) \(=\ii-\tfrac12\jj-\tfrac32\kk\).

(v) By considering Q2(b)(i) and Q2(b)(iv), discuss the relation between the surface \(\Sigma\) and the curve \(C\) at \((1,1,1)\).

Hint

Divide the first equation by the second to get \(y-z\); then you have \(y+z\) and \(y-z\). In (v), dot the tangent vector with the normal.

Solution
  1. (iii) With \(x=t\): \(y+z=\dfrac2t\) and \(y-z=\dfrac{t^2-1}{y+z}=\dfrac{t(t^2-1)}2\).
  2. Add and subtract, halve: \(y=\dfrac1t+\dfrac{t(t^2-1)}4\), \(z=\dfrac1t-\dfrac{t(t^2-1)}4\). At \(t=1\): \(y=z=1\), so \((1,1,1)\) is at \(t=1\).
  3. (iv) \(y'(t)=-\dfrac1{t^2}+\dfrac{3t^2-1}4\), \(z'(t)=-\dfrac1{t^2}-\dfrac{3t^2-1}4\).
  4. At \(t=1\): \(x'=1\), \(y'=-1+\frac12=-\frac12\), \(z'=-1-\frac12=-\frac32\): \(\vb r'(1)=\ii-\frac12\jj-\frac32\kk\). \(\blacksquare\)
  5. (v) \((1,1,1)\) lies on \(\Sigma\), since \(h(1,1,1)=1-1-6+6=0\), and on \(C\). \(\vb r'(1)\cdot\nabla h\) \(=(1)(-1)+(-\tfrac12)(-5)+(-\tfrac32)(1)\) \(=-1+\tfrac52-\tfrac32\) \(=0\).
  6. So the tangent to \(C\) is perpendicular to the normal of \(\Sigma\): it lies in the tangent plane \(x+5y-z=5\). \(C\) touches \(\Sigma\) at \((1,1,1)\) (meets it tangentially there, not cutting across it).

For (v) the 2 marks need both the computation (dot product \(=0\)) and the geometric statement (the curve is tangent to the surface at that point).

\[\begin{aligned}y(t)&=\frac1t+\frac{t(t^2-1)}4\\ z(t)&=\frac1t-\frac{t(t^2-1)}4\\ \vb r'(1)&\perp\nabla h(1,1,1)\\ &\Rightarrow C\text{ is tangent to }\Sigma\end{aligned}\]

Equivalent forms: \(y=\dfrac{t^4-t^2+4}{4t}\), \(z=\dfrac{4+t^2-t^4}{4t}\).

◆Before the exam

  • Nothing from this chapter is on the formula sheet: be able to write div, curl (determinant) and grad from memory.
  • Curl \(=(P_y-N_z,\ M_z-P_x,\ N_x-M_y)\): the cyclic form avoids the \(\jj\) sign slip.
  • div is a scalar, curl a vector; for a 2-D field the curl is \((N_x-M_y)\,\kk\).
  • Say “irrotational” / “solenoidal” when you get \(\vb 0\) / \(0\): the next part (potential, Gauss) builds on it.
  • \(\nabla\cdot\vb r=3\), \(\nabla\times\vb r=\vb 0\), \(\nabla r^n=nr^{n-2}\vb r\), \(\nabla(\vb v\cdot\vb r)=\vb v\), \(\nabla\times(\vb v\times\vb r)=2\vb v\).
  • Product rules: \(\nabla\cdot(\phi\vb F)=\phi\nabla\cdot\vb F+\vb F\cdot\nabla\phi\), \(\nabla\times(\phi\vb F)=\nabla\phi\times\vb F+\phi\nabla\times\vb F\).
  • “Prove” with general \(M,N,P\): one full \(\ii\)-component, then “similarly for \(\jj,\kk\)”.
  • “Constant vector” almost always means \(\vb 0\); write “\(=\vb 0\), a constant vector” as the last line.
  • Anything with \(r\) in a denominator: add “for \(\vb r\ne\vb 0\)”.
  • Polar: \(\vb u_r=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\), \(\vb u_\theta=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\); \(\ii=\cos\theta\,\vb u_r-\sin\theta\,\vb u_\theta\), \(\jj=\sin\theta\,\vb u_r+\cos\theta\,\vb u_\theta\).
