MATH1851 Exam Guide · Laplace Transforms

The short version

What to expect

Laplace is the most predictable part of the paper. Every recent paper asks the same few things in the same places: a handful of short transforms and inverses in Q9, then one ODE solved by Laplace in Q10. Many questions come back word for word. If you can do each type in the cards below, you are ready. There are no surprises to prepare for.

How to practise: try a question, open the Hint only if stuck, check against the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind a method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Q9–Q10 (10% each) on papers up to May 2026; Q17–Q18 (10 pts each) in Dec 2025. Q9 = short parts; Q10 = a second-order initial value problem.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. Laplace stayed full-working, but the paper is faster-paced, so the routine parts here need to be quick.
  • Formula sheet: most papers print a short table (tⁿ, sin, cos, the tⁿf rule, both shifts, convolution), not always all of it. Dec 2025 printed none. No paper prints \(\mathcal L(y')=sY-y(0)\), \(\mathcal L(y'')=s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0)\) or the cover-up rule. Learn the whole table.
  • Notation: step function \(S(t-a)\) in the slides, \(H(t-a)\) in the papers; same thing.

LT.1Forward transforms20 recent · 40 all

Looks like"Find the Laplace transform of" \(t^ne^{at}\), \(t\sin bt\), \(e^{at}\cos bt\), …

DoTable entry, then first shift: \(e^{at}f(t)\to F(s-a)\). Times \(t\): \(tf(t)\to -F^{\prime}(s)\).

WatchSign from \(-\frac{d}{ds}\). Shift every \(s\), including the one on top.

LT.2Inverse by partial fractions11 recent · 17 all

Looks likeDistinct factors \((s-a)(s-b)(s-c)\) at the bottom; top \(1\), \(s\) or \(s^2\).

DoSplit into \(\frac{A}{s-a}+\cdots\); cover-up \(A=\frac{P(a)}{(a-b)(a-c)}\); \(\frac{A}{s-a}\to Ae^{at}\).

WatchPut the root into the top too. \(\frac1{s+2}\to e^{-2t}\).

LT.3Inverse with a delay10 recent · 16 all

Looks likeInverse of \(e^{-as}F(s)\).

DoInvert \(F\) to \(f(t)\), then answer \(f(t-a)\,S(t-a)\).

WatchReplace every \(t\) by \(t-a\); keep the step function.

LT.4Repeated or squared factors4 recent · 10 all

Looks like\((s-a)^n\), \((s^2+a^2)^2\) or \((s+\beta)^2+b^2\) at the bottom.

Do\(\frac1{(s-a)^n}\to\frac{t^{n-1}e^{at}}{(n-1)!}\); repeated: \(\frac{A_1}{s-a}+\frac{A_2}{(s-a)^2}\); \((s^2+a^2)^2\): \(-\frac{d}{ds}\) or convolution; \((s+\beta)^2+b^2\): first shift.

WatchCover-up does not work for repeated factors.

LT.5Convolution9 recent · 17 all

Looks likeThe symbol \(*\): compute \(f*g\) or \(\mathcal L(f*g)\).

Do\(f*g=\int_0^t f(\tau)g(t-\tau)\,d\tau\), or \(\mathcal L(f*g)=F(s)G(s)\), then invert. Shortcut: \(t^m*t^n=\frac{m!\,n!}{(m+n+1)!}t^{m+n+1}\).

WatchIntegrate in \(\tau\), not \(t\). The question may want \(f*g\) itself or only its transform.

LT.6–7Solving an ODE by Laplace20 recent · 34 all

Looks like"Solve … by Laplace transform" with \(y(0)\) (and \(y^{\prime}(0)\)) given.

DoTransform: \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime})=sY-y(0)\), \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime\prime})=s^2Y-sy(0)-y^{\prime}(0)\). Solve for \(Y\), split, invert.

WatchSigns of the initial-value terms. Not on the formula sheet.

LT.8–9Integral equations, derivatives, theory4 recent · 7 all

Looks like\(y(t)\) inside \(\int_0^t\cdots d\tau\); "\(\mathcal L(y^{\prime\prime\prime})\) in terms of \(Y\)"; "what is convolution".

DoSpot a convolution: \(\int_0^t y(\tau)\,d\tau=y*1\to\frac{Y}{s}\). Transform, solve for \(Y\), invert.

Watch\(\int_0^t y(\tau)(t-\tau)\,d\tau=y*t\to\frac{Y}{s^2}\).

LT.1Forward transforms: shifts, multiplying by \(t\), \(t^n\)

Chapter note · forward transforms and shifts · method in the table

Ready answer · comes back again and again \[t\sin bt\ \to\ \frac{2bs}{(s^2+b^2)^2}\] \[t\cos bt\ \to\ \frac{s^2-b^2}{(s^2+b^2)^2}\] \[t^ne^{at}\ \to\ \frac{n!}{(s-a)^{n+1}}\] \[\begin{aligned}&te^{at}\sin bt\\&\quad\to\ \frac{2b(s-a)}{\big[(s-a)^2+b^2\big]^2}\end{aligned}\]

The first two come from \(\mathcal L\big(tf(t)\big)=-F^{\prime}(s)\); the last two from the first shift \(e^{at}f(t)\to F(s-a)\). Together they answer 31 of the 40 LT.1 parts below. The papers want one line of working: name the rule you used.

Frequency: 12 of 12 recent papers; 10 of 10 older papers. Usually two or three one-line parts at the start of Q9 (Q17 in Dec 2025).

(\(a,b,c,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are distinct real numbers.) Find the Laplace transform of \(t\cos(\gamma t)\).

Hint

Type LT.1, forward transform, multiplying by \(t\). First step: write down \(\mathcal L(\cos\gamma t)\) from the table, then use \(\mathcal L\big(tf(t)\big)=-\dfrac{d}{ds}F(s)\).

Solution

1. Table. \(\mathcal L(\cos\gamma t)=\dfrac{s}{s^2+\gamma^2}\).

2. Multiply by \(t\) means take \(-\dfrac{d}{ds}\) (quotient rule):

\[\begin{aligned}\mathcal L(t\cos\gamma t)&=-\frac{d}{ds}\,\frac{s}{s^2+\gamma^2}\\&=-\frac{(s^2+\gamma^2)-s\cdot 2s}{(s^2+\gamma^2)^2}\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\mathcal L(t\cos\gamma t)=\frac{s^2-\gamma^2}{(s^2+\gamma^2)^2}}\]

Marker: the minus sign in \(-F^{\prime}(s)\) must flip \(\gamma^2-s^2\) into \(s^2-\gamma^2\); a wrong sign loses the mark.

Equivalent forms. For the other sittings replace \(\gamma\) by \(b\) or \(a\): \(\dfrac{s^2-b^2}{(s^2+b^2)^2}\), \(\dfrac{s^2-a^2}{(s^2+a^2)^2}\).

Find the inverse Laplace transform of \[\frac{s^2}{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)},\] where \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are real and distinct.

Hint

Type LT.2, inverse by partial fractions. The top \(s^2\) has lower degree than the cubic bottom, and the three factors are different. First step: write the expression as \(\dfrac{A}{s-a}+\dfrac{B}{s-b}+\dfrac{C}{s-c}\) and find \(A\) by covering up \(s-a\) and putting \(s=a\) into what remains, including the top.

Solution

1. Partial fractions. Since \(\deg s^2=2\lt 3\) and \(a,b,c\) are distinct,

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{s^2}{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\\&\quad=\frac{A}{s-a}+\frac{B}{s-b}+\frac{C}{s-c}.\end{aligned}\]

2. Cover-up (allowed because no factor is repeated):

\[A=\frac{a^2}{(a-b)(a-c)},\] \[B=\frac{b^2}{(b-a)(b-c)},\] \[C=\frac{c^2}{(c-a)(c-b)}.\]

3. Invert each term with \(\mathcal L(e^{kt})=\frac1{s-k}\) and linearity:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}f(t)&=\frac{a^2e^{at}}{(a-b)(a-c)}\\&\quad+\frac{b^2e^{bt}}{(b-a)(b-c)}\\&\quad+\frac{c^2e^{ct}}{(c-a)(c-b)}\end{aligned}}\]

Ready answer · comes back again and again \[(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau\] \[\mathcal L(f*g)=F(s)\,G(s)\] \[t^m*t^n=\frac{m!\,n!}{(m+n+1)!}\,t^{m+n+1}\] \[\mathcal L\big(t^m*t^n\big)=\frac{m!\,n!}{s^{m+n+2}}\]

Read the question word: "find the Laplace transform of \(f*g\)" wants \(F(s)G(s)\) only (one line). "Evaluate", "simplify" or "calculate" \(f*g\) wants a function of \(t\): multiply the transforms and invert, or integrate directly. The \(t^m*t^n\) lines cover 9 of the 17 LT.5 parts.

Frequency: 9 of 12 recent papers (all but May 2024, Dec 2023 and May 2019); 8 of 10 older papers. Usually one short part in Q9; May 2022 and May 2018 put it in Q10.

(\(a,b,c,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are distinct real numbers.) Find the Laplace transform of \(t*\sin(\alpha t)\), where \(*\) is the convolution operator.

Hint

Type LT.5, transform of a convolution. First step: \(\mathcal L(f*g)=F(s)G(s)\); write down \(\mathcal L(t)\) and \(\mathcal L(\sin\alpha t)\).

Solution

1. Transform each factor. \(\mathcal L(t)=\dfrac1{s^2}\), \(\mathcal L(\sin\alpha t)=\dfrac{\alpha}{s^2+\alpha^2}\).

2. Convolution theorem: multiply.

\[\boxed{\mathcal L\big(t*\sin\alpha t\big)=\frac{\alpha}{s^2\,(s^2+\alpha^2)}}\]

Marker: quote \(\mathcal L(f*g)=F(s)G(s)\); there is no need to compute the convolution integral.

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\Big(\dfrac1{s^2}-\dfrac{1}{s^2+\alpha^2}\Big)\). Dec 2017: \(\dfrac{c}{s^2(s^2+c^2)}\).

(\(a,b,c,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are distinct real numbers.) Solve the first order equation by Laplace transform (\(y_0\) = a real number): \[\frac{dy}{dt}+cy=t,\qquad y(0)=y_0.\]

Hint

Type LT.6, first-order equation by Laplace. First step: transform both sides, using \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime})=sY-y_0\) and \(\mathcal L(t)=\frac1{s^2}\), then solve for \(Y\).

Solution

1. Transform both sides with \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime})=sY-y(0)\):

\[sY-y_0+cY=\frac{1}{s^2}.\]

2. Solve for \(Y\) (assume \(c\ne0\)):

\[Y=\frac{y_0}{s+c}+\frac{1}{s^2(s+c)}.\]

3. Partial fractions. \(s^2\) is a repeated factor, so use \(\frac{A}{s}+\frac{B}{s^2}+\frac{C}{s+c}\). Cover-up gives \(B=\frac1c\) (cover \(s^2\), put \(s=0\)) and \(C=\frac1{c^2}\) (cover \(s+c\), put \(s=-c\)). Comparing \(s^2\) terms: \(A+C=0\), so \(A=-\frac1{c^2}\).

\[\frac{1}{s^2(s+c)}=-\frac{1}{c^2s}+\frac{1}{cs^2}+\frac{1}{c^2(s+c)}.\]

4. Invert with \(\frac1{s^2}\to t\), \(\frac1{s+c}\to e^{-ct}\):

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=\Big(y_0+\frac1{c^2}\Big)e^{-ct}\\&\quad+\frac{t}{c}-\frac{1}{c^2}\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: the repeated factor \(s^2\) needs both \(\frac{A}{s}\) and \(\frac{B}{s^2}\); cover-up alone cannot give \(A\).

Check. \(y(0)=y_0+\frac1{c^2}-\frac1{c^2}=y_0\). ✓ If \(c=0\) (not excluded by the stem), \(y=y_0+\frac{t^2}{2}\).

Consider the differential equation as an initial value problem: \[\frac{d^2y}{dt^2}-5\frac{dy}{dt}+6y=6t,\quad t\gt 0,\]\[y(0)=1,\qquad y^{\prime}(0)=1.\]Part (b): solve the problem again independently using the Laplace transform, i.e., without any input from part (a) of the problem. The answers from (a) and (b) should match.

Other part of this question: (a), the same problem by undetermined coefficients, is in the O4 guide (O4.3).

Hint

Type LT.7, second-order IVP by Laplace. The left side factors as \((s-2)(s-3)Y\). First step: transform both sides with \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime\prime})=s^2Y-sy(0)-y^{\prime}(0)\) and \(\mathcal L(y^{\prime})=sY-y(0)\), and transform the right side.

Solution

1. Transform both sides.

\[\begin{aligned}&\big(s^2Y-s-1\big)-5\big(sY-1\big)+6Y\\&\quad=\frac{6}{s^2}\end{aligned}\]

2. Solve for \(Y\). Collect the \(Y\) terms and factor \(s^{2} - 5 s + 6=(s-2)(s-3)\):

\[\begin{aligned}&(s-2)(s-3)Y\\&\quad=s - 4+\frac{6}{s^2}\end{aligned}\]

Over one denominator,

\[Y=\frac{s^{3} - 4 s^{2} + 6}{s^2(s-2)(s-3)}.\]

3. Partial fractions. \(s^2\) is repeated, so write \(\frac{A}{s}+\frac{B}{s^2}+\frac{C}{s-2}+\frac{D}{s-3}\). Cover-up:

\[\begin{aligned}B&=\frac{N(0)}{(0-2)(0-3)}=\frac{6}{6}=1,\\C&=\frac{N(2)}{2^2(2-3)}=\frac{-2}{-4}=\frac{1}{2},\\D&=\frac{N(3)}{3^2(3-2)}=\frac{-3}{9}=- \frac{1}{3}.\end{aligned}\]

Here \(N(s)\) is the top. For \(A\), compare the \(s^3\) terms: \(A+C+D=1\) (the \(s^3\) coefficient of \(N\)), so \(A=\frac{5}{6}\).

\[Y=\frac{5}{6s}+\frac{1}{s^2}+\frac{1}{2(s-2)}-\frac{1}{3(s-3)}.\]

4. Invert with \(\frac1s\to1\), \(\frac1{s^2}\to t\), \(\frac1{s-r}\to e^{rt}\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac{5}{6}+t+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2t}-\tfrac{1}{3}e^{3t}}\]

Check. \(y(0)=1\) ✓, \(y^{\prime}(0)=1\) ✓.

The fractions are genuine here (the only paper where they appear).

Solution

1. Transform. The integral is \(y*1\), so

\[Y=\frac{2}{s^3}+\frac{Y}{s}.\]

2. Solve for \(Y\). \(Y\big(1-\frac1s\big)=\frac2{s^3}\), so \(Y\cdot\frac{s-1}{s}=\frac{2}{s^3}\) and

\[Y=\frac{2}{s^2(s-1)}.\]

3. Partial fractions. \(\frac{A}{s}+\frac{B}{s^2}+\frac{C}{s-1}\): cover-up gives \(B=-2\), \(C=2\); \(s^2\) terms give \(A+C=0\), so \(A=-2\).

\[Y=\frac{2}{s-1}-\frac2s-\frac2{s^2}.\]

4. Invert:

\[\boxed{y=2e^{t}-2t-2}\]

May 2022 Q9(d)Q9: 10%; part marks not shown

What is the convolution operator? Why is that important in Laplace transform?

Other parts of this question: (a) is under LT.1, (b) under LT.2 and LT.3, (c) is LT.6.

Hint

Type LT.9, theory (uses LT.5). First step: write the defining integral of \(f*g\), then state the convolution theorem.

Solution

1. Definition. For functions \(f,g\) defined for \(t\ge0\),

\[(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau.\]

Word form: a weighted running total of \(f\) up to time \(t\), where the weight \(g(t-\tau)\) depends on how long ago each value occurred.

2. Why it matters: the convolution theorem.

\[\boxed{\mathcal L(f*g)=F(s)\,G(s)}\]

So a product in the \(s\)-world is a convolution in the \(t\)-world: \(\mathcal L^{-1}(FG)=f*g\). This lets you

  • invert a product such as \(\frac{1}{s(s^2+c^2)}\) as \(1*\frac{\sin ct}{c}\) without partial fractions;
  • solve integral equations such as \(y=1+\int_0^ty(\tau)\,d\tau\) (see Dec 2018 Q9(c));
  • Beyond the handouts write the solution of an ODE for any right side \(f(t)\) as \(f\) convolved with the solution for a unit impulse.

Any two of these uses, with the definition and the theorem, make a full answer.
