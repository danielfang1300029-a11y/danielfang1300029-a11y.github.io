MATH1851 Exam Guide · Curve Sketching and Optimisation

The short version

What to expect

Curve sketching is in 11 of the 13 recent papers (all except Dec 2023 and May 2026), usually as a whole Part I question or as two or three parts of one. The function changes every time, but the job does not: find where the graph goes up or down (sign of \(f'\)), where it bends up or down (sign of \(f''\)), mark the special points, then draw. Every question is one of five types, and each type below has a standard procedure: numbered steps of the form "if you see this, do that". Follow them in order and you never have to invent an approach in the exam.

How to practise: use Start here to decide what you are given (a formula, a graph of the derivative, a list of conditions, a word problem or a graph). Work the question with the type's procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end; for sketch questions compare your drawing with ours. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas (critical points, concavity, the derivative tests), use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: a Part I long question (Q2–Q5, 5–10%) on most papers: a full sketch from a formula (May 2025 Q2, Dec 2021 Q2, May 2023 Q4), a function read from the graph of its derivative (May 2019 Q1, Dec 2019 Q3, Dec 2020 Q1, May 2024 Q3), or an optimisation word problem (May 2022 Q5, Dec 2024 Q4). Dec 2025 has one multiple-choice graph question (MC9) and one optimisation part (Q13(c)).
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. Graph-reading questions now come as quick multiple choice; sketches and optimisation are still written, and the marks are for the reasons: the sign table, the test you used, the limits behind each asymptote, "justify completely".
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard integrals and derivatives (\(\int\sec x\,dx\), \(\int\tan x\,dx\), derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}\), \(\cos^{-1}\), \(\tan^{-1}\)) and the Laplace and ODE material. Nothing for this topic: no first or second derivative test, no sketching checklist, no limits such as \(xe^{-x}\to0\) as \(x\to\infty\). Learn them.
  • Wording: \(\exp(x)\) means \(e^x\). "Possible points of inflection" means candidates (where \(f''=0\) or \(f''\) does not exist). "Indicate clearly" means label each feature on the sketch with its coordinates. "No graph paper will be provided" means a neat freehand sketch is enough; the shape and the labels score, not the scale.

L9.1Full analysis of a given formula, then sketch

Chapter note · increasing, decreasing and the first derivative test · Chapter note · concavity and inflection points · Chapter note · curve sketching checklist

Frequency: 5 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2020, Dec 2021, May 2023, May 2025); 6 of 10 older papers. The most common L9 type: 13 parts in all.

Recognise it

You are given the formula of the function itself and asked for some of: extreme points, where it increases or decreases, where it is concave up or down, inflection points, intercepts, asymptotes, a sketch.

Standard procedure

Do only the steps the question asks for, but always in this order: later steps use earlier ones. A critical point is an \(x\) in the domain where \(f'(x)=0\) or \(f'(x)\) does not exist.

  1. Domain. Exclude where a denominator is 0, a square root has a negative inside, a \(\ln\) has a non-positive inside.
    • 1a. If the function is built from a given graph or from pieces, treat each piece on its own interval. \(u(x+2)-2\) is the graph of \(u\) moved 2 left and 2 down; keep every open and filled dot. Asked: Dec 2020.
  2. Intercepts. \(y\)-intercept \(f(0)\) if 0 is in the domain; \(x\)-intercepts by solving \(f(x)=0\) (an \(e^{(\dots)}\) factor is never 0).
  3. Asymptotes. Vertical: at each excluded point, find both one-sided limits. Horizontal: find \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\) and \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}\) separately. For "polynomial \(\times\, e^{(\dots)}\)" forms such as \(xe^{2x}\) as \(x\to-\infty\), write it as a quotient and use L'Hôpital's Rule.
  4. First derivative, fully factorised. Find the critical points, then a sign table of \(f'\) on the intervals between them (and between excluded points): \(f'\gt0\) increasing, \(f'\lt0\) decreasing.
  5. Classify each critical point (first derivative test): \(f'\) changes \(+\to-\) local max, \(-\to+\) local min, no change neither. Give coordinates \((x,f(x))\), exact and decimal.
  6. Second derivative, fully factorised. Sign table of \(f''\): \(f''\gt0\) concave up, \(f''\lt0\) concave down. An inflection point is a point on the curve where the concavity changes.
    • 6a. If the question gives \(f''\) (often with an unknown root), use it as given: only the signs of its factors matter. Asked: May 2025.
    • 6b. If \(f''\) never changes sign (for example \(f''=-\frac{2}{9}x^{-4/3}\)), say so: no inflection point, even where \(f''\) does not exist. Asked: May 2013.
    • Stop here if no sketch is asked. Asked: May 2023, May 2019.
  7. Extra asks. Absolute max/min on an interval: compare \(f\) at critical points and endpoints, and the limits at any open end. Number of roots: Intermediate Value Theorem on each interval where \(f\) is monotonic (only increasing or only decreasing). Where two curves meet: solve \(f(x)=g(x)\) and factorise. Asked: Dec 2021, Dec 2016 (a), Dec 2016 (c).
  8. Sketch. Draw dashed asymptotes, plot intercepts, extrema and inflection points with coordinates, then join them following the two sign tables. Label every feature. Asked: May 2025, Dec 2021, Dec 2020, and 6 older parts.
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2025 Q2(a) (Q2: 10%; part marks not shown). Let \(g(x)=\left(3-\dfrac1x\right)\exp\left(-\dfrac x2\right)\). Sketch the graph of \(y=g(x)\). Indicate clearly where the graph is increasing and where it is concave up. Find the intercept(s) and asymptote(s). [Hint: Compute \(g'(x)\), and you may use without giving a proof that \(g''(x)=\dfrac{e^{-x/2}}{4x^3}(x-\alpha)(3x^2+bx+c)\) where \(\alpha=1.84\cdots\) and \(b^2\lt12c\).]

Step 1 Domain: all \(x\ne0\).

The \(\frac1x\) excludes 0. That single point will give a vertical asymptote and splits every sign table.

Step 2 \(e^{-x/2}\gt0\), so \(g(x)=0\iff3-\frac1x=0\iff x=\frac13\). No \(y\)-intercept, since 0 is not in the domain.

Saying "no \(y\)-intercept" with the reason counts as finding the intercepts.

Step 3 Near 0: as \(x\to0^+\), \(3-\frac1x\to-\infty\) and \(e^{-x/2}\to1\), so \(g\to-\infty\). As \(x\to0^-\), \(3-\frac1x\to+\infty\), so \(g\to+\infty\). Vertical asymptote \(x=0\).

At the ends: as \(x\to\infty\), \(3-\frac1x\to3\) and \(e^{-x/2}\to0\), so \(g\to0\): horizontal asymptote \(y=0\) on the right. As \(x\to-\infty\), \(e^{-x/2}\to\infty\), so \(g\to\infty\): no asymptote on the left.

Do each side separately. Nothing here is indeterminate, so the Product Law is enough; no L'Hôpital.

Step 4 Product rule, then one fraction:

\[\begin{aligned}g'(x)&=e^{-x/2}\Big[\frac1{x^2}-\frac12\Big(3-\frac1x\Big)\Big]\\&=\frac{e^{-x/2}\,(2+x-3x^2)}{2x^2}\\&=-\frac{e^{-x/2}\,(3x+2)(x-1)}{2x^2}.\end{aligned}\]

Critical points \(x=-\frac23\) and \(x=1\); \(x=0\) is excluded. The signs of \(-(3x+2)(x-1)\) (the rest is positive):

\(x\)\((-\infty,-\frac23)\)\((-\frac23,0)\)\((0,1)\)\((1,\infty)\)
\(g'\)\(-\)\(+\)\(+\)\(-\)
\(g\)decreasingincreasingincreasingdecreasing

The markers want the factorised \(g'\) and the table: that is what "indicate clearly where the graph is increasing" means.

Step 5 Local min at \(x=-\frac23\): \(g=\frac92e^{1/3}\approx6.28\). Local max at \(x=1\): \(g=2e^{-1/2}\approx1.21\).

Sign change \(-\to+\) gives a min, \(+\to-\) a max. Both are only local: \(g\) goes to \(\pm\infty\) near 0.

Step 6a Use the given \(g''\). Since \(b^2-12c\lt0\), the quadratic \(3x^2+bx+c\) has no real roots and is always positive (it opens upward). \(e^{-x/2}\gt0\) too, so \(g''\) has the sign of \(\dfrac{x-\alpha}{x^3}\):

\(x\)\((-\infty,0)\)\((0,\alpha)\)\((\alpha,\infty)\)
\(g''\)\(+\)\(-\)\(+\)
\(g\)concave upconcave downconcave up

Inflection point at \(x=\alpha\approx1.84\) (\(g(\alpha)\approx0.98\)). The concavity also changes at \(x=0\), but 0 is not in the domain, so it is not an inflection point.

This is why the hint says \(b^2\lt12c\): it tells you the quadratic never changes sign, so you never need \(b\) and \(c\). (They are about 4.53 and 4.34.)

Step 8 Sketch:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{increasing on }[-\tfrac23,0),\ (0,1]\\&\text{concave up on }(-\infty,0),\ (\alpha,\infty)\\&x\text{-intercept }(\tfrac13,0)\\&\text{asymptotes }x=0,\ y=0\end{aligned}}\]

Common slip: a horizontal asymptote on the left as well. \(e^{-x/2}\) blows up as \(x\to-\infty\).

Traps
  • Sign slips in \(f'\). Keep the minus sign and the \(e^{(\dots)}\) factor outside, and factorise completely before the table. In May 2023 Q4, \(y'=-4e^{-x}x(2x-3)\): forgetting the minus swaps every max and min.
  • Asymptotes on one side only. May 2025 Q2(a) has \(y=0\) only as \(x\to\infty\); Dec 2021 Q2(a) and May 2015 Q2(a) only as \(x\to-\infty\). Write the limit for each side.
  • "Inflection" where the function is not defined, or where nothing changes. \(x=0\) is not an inflection point in May 2025 Q2(a) or May 2016 Q2(b) (not in the domain), nor in May 2013 Q2(a) (\(f''\lt0\) on both sides).

May 2023 Q46%

For the following function, find all the extremum points and identify the intervals where the function is increasing, decreasing, concave up and concave down. \[y=4e^{-x}(2x^2+x+1)\]

Hint

Type L9.1, stop after step 6. First step: product rule, then take \(4e^{-x}\) out as a common factor and factorise the quadratic that is left.

Solution

Step 1 Domain: all real \(x\).

Step 4 First derivative:

\[\begin{aligned}y'&=4e^{-x}\big[(4x+1)-(2x^2+x+1)\big]\\&=4e^{-x}(3x-2x^2)\\&=-4e^{-x}\,x(2x-3).\end{aligned}\]

Critical points \(x=0,\ \frac32\). Signs: \(y'\lt0\) on \((-\infty,0)\), \(y'\gt0\) on \((0,\frac32)\), \(y'\lt0\) on \((\frac32,\infty)\).

Step 5 Local min \((0,4)\); local max \(\big(\frac32,28e^{-3/2}\big)\approx(1.5,\,6.25)\).

Step 6 Second derivative (same trick on \(4e^{-x}(3x-2x^2)\)):

\[\begin{aligned}y''&=4e^{-x}\big[(3-4x)-(3x-2x^2)\big]\\&=4e^{-x}(2x^2-7x+3)\\&=4e^{-x}(2x-1)(x-3).\end{aligned}\]

\(y''\gt0\) on \((-\infty,\frac12)\) and \((3,\infty)\); \(y''\lt0\) on \((\frac12,3)\). Both are sign changes, so inflection points at \(x=\frac12\) and \(x=3\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{min }(0,4)\\&\text{max }(\tfrac32,\,28e^{-3/2})\\&\text{increasing on }[0,\tfrac32];\ \text{decreasing on}\\&\quad(-\infty,0],\ [\tfrac32,\infty)\\&\text{concave up on }(-\infty,\tfrac12),\ (3,\infty);\\&\text{concave down on }(\tfrac12,3)\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: "extremum points" means points: give both coordinates, and say which is the max and which the min.

Equivalent forms. \(28e^{-3/2}\approx6.248\). Inflection points \(\big(\frac12,8e^{-1/2}\big)\approx(0.5,4.85)\) and \((3,88e^{-3})\approx(3,4.38)\).

Dec 2021 Q2(a)Q2: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(a,b\) be two constants. Consider the function \[f(x)=\begin{cases}\left(x^2+\dfrac ax\right)\sin\left(x^2+\dfrac bx\right) & x\gt0,\\[2mm] -xe^{2x} & x\le0.\end{cases}\] Sketch the graph of \(y=f(x)\) for \(x\le0\) only and locate the absolute extrema, point of inflection(s) and asymptote(s) of the graph. [Show your calculation how to find them.]

Hint

Type L9.1, on the half-line \((-\infty,0]\), with step 7 (absolute extrema). First step: find \(\lim_{x\to-\infty}(-xe^{2x})\) by writing it as \(\dfrac{-x}{e^{-2x}}\).

Solution

Step 2 \(f(0)=0\); for \(x\lt0\), \(-x\gt0\) and \(e^{2x}\gt0\), so \(f(x)\gt0\).

Step 3 As \(x\to-\infty\), \(-xe^{2x}=\dfrac{-x}{e^{-2x}}\) has form \(\frac{\infty}{\infty}\). L'Hôpital:

\[\begin{aligned}&\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{-1}{-2e^{-2x}}\\&=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\frac{e^{2x}}{2}=0.\end{aligned}\]

Horizontal asymptote \(y=0\) as \(x\to-\infty\). No vertical asymptote (\(f\) is continuous on \((-\infty,0]\)).

Step 4–5 \(f'(x)=-e^{2x}-2xe^{2x}=-e^{2x}(1+2x)\). Zero at \(x=-\frac12\); \(f'\gt0\) for \(x\lt-\frac12\), \(f'\lt0\) on \((-\frac12,0)\). Local max \(f(-\frac12)=\frac1{2e}\).

Step 6 \(f''(x)=-2e^{2x}-2e^{2x}(1+2x)=-4e^{2x}(1+x)\). Concave up on \((-\infty,-1)\), concave down on \((-1,0)\): inflection point \((-1,e^{-2})\).

Step 7 Absolute extrema. \(f\) increases up to \(x=-\frac12\) and decreases after, so \(\frac1{2e}\) is the largest value. Since \(f(x)\gt0\) for \(x\lt0\) and \(f(0)=0\), the smallest value is \(0\), at the endpoint \(x=0\). (The values tend to 0 on the left but never reach it.)

Step 8 Sketch:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{abs max }\tfrac1{2e}\text{ at }x=-\tfrac12\\&\text{abs min }0\text{ at }x=0\\&\text{inflection }(-1,e^{-2})\\&\text{asymptote }y=0\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: the question says "show your calculation": the L'Hôpital limit for the asymptote and the sign tables carry the marks.

Equivalent forms. \(\frac1{2e}\approx0.184\); \(e^{-2}\approx0.135\).

May 2019 Q3(a)Q3: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(h(t)=\sqrt t\,e^{-t}\) be defined for \(t\ge0\). The function \(h(t)\) attains a local maximum at \(t=a\) and has an inflection point at \(t=b\). Evaluate \(a\) and \(b\).

Parts (b) and (c) are in the L16 and L15 guides.

Hint

Type L9.1, steps 4–6 only. First step: write \(h'(t)\) over the common denominator \(2\sqrt t\).

Solution

Step 4 For \(t\gt0\):

\[h'(t)=\frac{e^{-t}}{2\sqrt t}-\sqrt t\,e^{-t}=\frac{e^{-t}(1-2t)}{2\sqrt t}.\]

Step 5 \(h'\gt0\) on \((0,\frac12)\), \(h'\lt0\) on \((\frac12,\infty)\): local max at \(t=\frac12\).

Step 6 Differentiate again and simplify (sympy check):

\[h''(t)=\frac{e^{-t}(4t^2-4t-1)}{4t^{3/2}}.\]

\(4t^2-4t-1=0\) gives \(t=\frac{1\pm\sqrt2}2\). Only \(\frac{1+\sqrt2}2\gt0\) is in the domain, and \(h''\) changes from \(-\) to \(+\) there.

\[\boxed{a=\tfrac12,\qquad b=\tfrac{1+\sqrt2}{2}}\]

Marker: reject the negative root \(\frac{1-\sqrt2}2\) explicitly: it is outside \(t\ge0\).

Equivalent forms. \(b=\frac12+\frac1{\sqrt2}\approx1.207\).

Dec 2016 Q3(a)Q3: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Let \(0\lt a\lt1\) and \(f(x)=x^3-ax^2+1\). Determine the different ranges of \(x\) on which the function \(f(x)\) is increasing/decreasing. Find the number of roots of \(f(x)\) and determine the maxima and minima of \(y=f(x)\) in the interval \([-1,1]\). Give clear justifications to your answer.

Hint

Type L9.1, steps 4–5 and 7. First step: \(f'(x)=x(3x-2a)\); then check the sign of the local minimum value.

Solution

Step 4 \(f'(x)=3x^2-2ax=x(3x-2a)\), zero at \(0\) and \(\frac{2a}3\). Increasing on \((-\infty,0]\) and \([\frac{2a}3,\infty)\); decreasing on \([0,\frac{2a}3]\).

Step 5 Local max \(f(0)=1\); local min \(f(\frac{2a}3)=1-\frac{4a^3}{27}\), which is \(\gt0\) because \(a\lt1\).

Step 7 Roots. On \([0,\infty)\), \(f\ge1-\frac{4a^3}{27}\gt0\): no roots. On \((-\infty,0]\), \(f\) is increasing, and \(f(-1)=-a\lt0\lt1=f(0)\). By the Intermediate Value Theorem there is a root in \((-1,0)\), and only one because \(f\) is increasing there. So exactly one root.

Step 7 Max and min on \([-1,1]\). Compare the critical points and the endpoints:

\[\begin{aligned}&f(-1)=-a,\quad f(0)=1,\\&f(\tfrac{2a}3)=1-\tfrac{4a^3}{27},\quad f(1)=2-a.\end{aligned}\]

Since \(0\lt a\lt1\): \(2-a\gt1\gt1-\frac{4a^3}{27}\gt0\gt-a\).

L9.2Get a function's shape from the graph or formula of its derivative

Chapter note · sign of f′ and monotonicity · Chapter note · concavity from f′ · Chapter note · area functions

Frequency: 4 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2019, Dec 2020, May 2024); 1 of 10 older papers. 8 parts in all; recent papers ask it more than older ones.

Recognise it

You are handed the derivative, not the function: "\(U'(x)=u(x)\)" with a graph of \(u\), "\(G\) is an antiderivative of \(h\)", "\(\frac{du}{dx}=\dots\)", "\(f'(x)=g(x)\)". The questions are about the original function.

Standard procedure

Call the function you are asked about \(F\) and the given one \(f=F'\). The whole question runs on two lines: \(F\) goes up where \(f\) is positive; \(F\) bends up (concave up) where \(f\) is increasing.

  1. Mark the \(x\)-axis. Zeros of \(f\) (does the sign change or not?), points where \(f\) is undefined or jumps (\(F'\) does not exist there), turning points of \(f\) (its local max and min), and kinks of \(f\).
  2. Up or down. Sign of \(f\) on each interval: \(f\gt0\) \(F\) increasing, \(f\lt0\) \(F\) decreasing.
  3. Extrema of \(F\). \(f\) changes \(+\to-\): local max; \(-\to+\): local min. This holds at a jump of \(f\) too, because \(F\) is continuous (\(F\) has a corner there). \(f=0\) without a sign change (\(f\) just touches 0) gives no extremum. Asked: Dec 2019 (a).
  4. Concavity. Where is \(f\) increasing or decreasing?
    • 4a. \(f\) is a formula: \(F''=f'\); differentiate and factorise, then a sign table. Asked: May 2024 (a).
    • 4b. \(f\) is a graph: read where it rises and falls; its turning points split the intervals. Asked: Dec 2019 (b).
  5. Inflection points of \(F\): where \(f\) switches between increasing and decreasing (a turning point of \(f\)) and \(F\) has a tangent line there. A jump of \(f\) gives a corner of \(F\), not an inflection point; a kink of \(f\) where \(f\) keeps rising changes nothing. Asked: May 2019, Dec 2020.
  6. Sketch \(F\). If values are needed, use \(F(b)=F(a)+\int_a^bf\): the signed area under the graph of \(f\) (areas below the axis count negative). Start at the given point, then draw interval by interval from the tables: horizontal tangent where \(f=0\), a corner where \(f\) jumps. Asked: Dec 2019 (c), May 2024 (b), May 2018.

Definition used on this page: an inflection point is a point of the graph with a tangent line where the concavity changes. This is the course's own definition (L9 handout: \(f''(c)=0\) or does not exist, a tangent line, and a change in concavity), so the corners noted below as "concavity changes, but a corner" are not inflection points.

Graph of y = u(x) (redrawn on a unit grid; the graph continues off the left edge).

Step 1 On \([-2,6]\): \(u\) jumps at \(x=2\) (open circles at \((2,3)\) and \((2,-1)\)), is zero at \(x=3\), has a smooth max at \(x=0\) and a kink at \(x=4\).

Step 3 At \(x=2\), \(u\) changes \(+\to-\) (by a jump): local max of \(U\), a corner. At \(x=3\), \(u\) changes \(-\to+\): local min.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{i. inc. on }[-2,2],[3,6];\ \text{dec. on }[2,3]\\&\quad\text{local max }x=2,\ \text{local min }x=3\\&\text{ii. up on }(-2,0),(2,6);\ \text{down on }(0,2)\\&\quad\text{inflection point at }x=0\end{aligned}}\]

Endpoints: if your course counts endpoint extrema, \(x=-2\) is also a local min and \(x=6\) a local max. Mention them in one line; it costs nothing.

Traps
  • Mixing the two readings. Increasing/decreasing of \(F\) comes from the sign of \(f\); concavity of \(F\) from the slope of \(f\). Dec 2019 Q3(a) and (b) ask both from the same graph.
  • Jumps of \(f\). They give corners of \(F\): an extremum if the sign changes (May 2019 \(x=2\), Dec 2020 \(x=3\)), but not a smooth inflection.
  • \(f\) touching zero. In Dec 2019, \(h\) touches 0 at \(x=2\) without changing sign: \(G\) has a flat inflection there, not a max or min.

L9.3Sketch from a list of sign conditions

Chapter note · curve sketching from f′ and f″

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2020); 1 of 10 older papers (May 2017). Short (5%) and quick if you use a table.

Recognise it

No formula: just a list such as "\(f(-2)=8\), \(f'(x)\gt0\) for \(|x|\gt2\), \(f''(x)\lt0\) for \(x\lt0\)", and "sketch a curve that satisfies all of these".

Standard procedure
  1. One number line. Mark every \(x\)-value that appears in any condition. Turn \(|x|\gt2\) into \(x\lt-2\) or \(x\gt2\), and \(|x|\lt2\) into \(-2\lt x\lt2\).
  2. Two rows. Sign of \(f'\) and sign of \(f''\) on each interval.
  3. Shape per interval, one of four: rising and bending up, rising and bending down, falling and bending up, falling and bending down.
  4. Special points. \(f'\) changes sign: local max or min (horizontal tangent if \(f'=0\) is given). \(f''\) changes sign: inflection point. Plot the given values.
  5. Join the pieces in order inside the domain, and label every extremum and inflection point with its \(x\)-value (and \(y\)-value if known). Asked: May 2020, May 2017.
Traps
  • Values that must fit. In May 2017, \(f(0)=0\) sits in the middle of an increasing stretch, so the local min at \(x=-3\) is below the axis and the local max at \(x=1\) above it.
  • Inflection at a given point. In May 2020 the concavity changes at \(x=0\), so \((0,4)\) is the inflection point: the curve must cross its tangent there, not turn.

L9.4Optimisation word problems

Chapter note · applied optimisation · Chapter note · absolute extrema on an interval

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2022, Dec 2024, Dec 2025); 0 of 10 older papers. New since 2022 and now in most December papers.

Recognise it

A short story with "maximise", "minimise", "largest", "least", "minimal area": a box, a rectangle inside a region, a line cutting off a triangle. No function is given; you build it.

Standard procedure
  1. Draw and name. A picture with every length labelled by a letter.
  2. Target. Write the quantity \(Q\) to be made largest or smallest in those letters.
  3. One variable. Use the condition in the question (fixed volume, a point on the line, a corner on the curve) to remove all letters but one. Write the domain of that variable.
  4. Critical points. Solve \(Q'=0\) inside the domain.
  5. Prove it is the absolute max or min, not just a critical point.
    • 5a. Only one critical point and \(Q''\) has one sign on the whole domain (or at the point, with one critical point): \(Q''\gt0\) gives the absolute min, \(Q''\lt0\) the absolute max. Asked: Dec 2024.
    • 5b. Closed interval: compare \(Q\) at the endpoints and the critical points. Asked: Dec 2025.
    • 5c. Open interval: find the limits of \(Q\) at both ends (for a minimum, \(Q\to\infty\) at both ends). Asked: May 2022.
  6. Answer what was asked: all the dimensions, or the equation of the line, or the value, with units.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2024 Q4 (10%). A rectangular box shaped house has a square floor. Three times as much heat (per square foot) enters through the roof as through the walls, and no heat enters or leaves through the floor. What dimensions for a house of volume 12,000 cubic feet minimise heat entry?

Step 1 Floor \(x\) ft by \(x\) ft, height \(h\) ft (\(x,h\gt0\)). Walls: four rectangles \(x\times h\), total \(4xh\). Roof: \(x^2\).

Write the areas next to the picture: the floor is not used at all.

Step 2 Let \(k\gt0\) be the heat per square foot through the walls; through the roof it is \(3k\). Total heat:

\[H=k(4xh)+3k(x^2).\]

Word equation: heat = (heat per sq ft) × (area), added over walls and roof. \(k\) is an unknown positive constant; it does not change where the minimum is.

Step 3 Volume: \(x^2h=12000\), so \(h=\dfrac{12000}{x^2}\) and

\[H(x)=k\Big(3x^2+\frac{48000}{x}\Big),\qquad x\gt0.\]

State the domain \(x\gt0\): step 5 needs it.

Step 4 \(H'(x)=k\Big(6x-\dfrac{48000}{x^2}\Big)=0\iff x^3=8000\iff x=20.\)

Step 5a \(H''(x)=k\Big(6+\dfrac{96000}{x^3}\Big)\gt0\) for all \(x\gt0\). \(H\) is concave up on its whole domain with one critical point, so \(x=20\) gives the absolute minimum.

"\(H''(20)\gt0\)" alone only proves a local minimum; with one critical point on an interval it is also absolute. Say that sentence.

Step 6 \(h=\dfrac{12000}{20^2}=30\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{floor }20\text{ ft}\times20\text{ ft}\\&\text{height }30\text{ ft}\end{aligned}}\]

Common slip: answering "\(x=20\)" only. "Dimensions" means all three lengths.

Traps
  • Answering a different question. Dec 2024 and Dec 2025 ask for the dimensions, May 2022 for the equation of the line, not the area or the critical value.
  • Half-lengths. In Dec 2025 the corner is \((x,1-x^2)\), so the rectangle is \(2x\) wide and \(2(1-x^2)\) high.
  • No justification. May 2022 says "justify completely": a critical point is not yet a minimum; use step 5a or 5c.

L9.5Read f, f′, f″ at a point from a graph

Chapter note · what f′ and f″ say about a graph

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, multiple choice); never on older papers. Expect more of these as Part I moves to multiple choice.

Recognise it

A graph of \(f\) and options comparing \(f(a)\), \(f'(a)\) and \(f''(a)\) (or their signs) at one point.

Standard procedure
  1. Signs first. \(f(a)\): above or below the axis. \(f'(a)\): rising or falling. \(f''(a)\): bending up or down. Cross out every option with a wrong sign.
  2. Height \(f(a)\): read it off the grid.
  3. Slope \(f'(a)\): read two points a small step \(s\) either side, \(f'(a)\approx\dfrac{f(a+s)-f(a-s)}{2s}\).
  4. Bending \(f''(a)\): the change in slope per unit, \(f''(a)\approx\dfrac{f(a+s)-2f(a)+f(a-s)}{s^2}\).
  5. Compare and pick the option. "Not enough information" is right only if the graph cannot settle the order. Asked: Dec 2025.
