MATH1851 Exam Guide · Definite Integrals and the FTC

The short version

What to expect

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (FTC) is in 9 of the 13 recent papers and in 7 of the 10 older ones. It rarely gets a whole question of its own. Instead it turns up as one or two parts inside other questions: "differentiate this integral" in the differentiation question, "find this limit" where the top is an integral, a long sum with \(\frac1n\) in front, or a graph whose areas are given. The functions change every time, but the moves do not. Every part is one of five types, and each type below has a standard procedure: numbered steps of the form "if you see this, do that". Two results recur so often that they are given as Ready answers: the derivative of \(\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}f(t)\,dt\), and the Riemann-sum template. The statement of the FTC itself has been asked 4 times, word for word; a model answer is in L12.5.

How to practise: use Start here to name the type. Then work the question with the type's procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind the theorem, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Q1 "Find each of the following limits" (a limit with an integral on top, 4%); Q2 "Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\)" or "Differentiate" (an integral with \(x\) in the limits, 4%); longer Part I questions built on a graph, a table or an unknown \(F\) (Dec 2020 Q2, Dec 2021 Q4, May 2019 Q5, May 2025 Q3–Q4). Dec 2025: multiple-choice MC3, MC7, MC10 and Q12(c). Older papers often had one whole question: "State the FTC; find \(F'(x)\); evaluate the limit of a sum".
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, May 2025 Q3(a) scores true/false statements "answer only", and May 2026 made Q1–3 answers only. Only the final value scores there. On written papers the stem says "showing all the reasoning" or "write down the names of the theorems you have used", and the conditions (continuity of the integrand) carry marks.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard integrals and derivatives (\(\int\sec x\,dx\), \(\int\tan x\,dx\), derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}\), \(\cos^{-1}\), \(\tan^{-1}\)), a Laplace table and a variation-of-parameters hint. No paper prints the FTC, the rule for differentiating \(\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}f(t)\,dt\), the average-value formula or the Riemann-sum definition of the integral. Learn them.
  • Notation quirks: the dummy letter changes (\(t\), \(u\), even \(x\) inside \(\int_0^tF(x)\,dx\)); sums start at odd places (\(k=n\) to \(2n-1\), \(r=n+1\) to \(2n\)); \(\exp(x)\) means \(e^x\); brackets are sometimes missing from the integrand (May and Dec 2023 Q1(c)).

L12.1Differentiate an integral with x in the limits

Chapter note · FTC Part I · Chapter note · chain rule

Frequency: 5 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, Dec 2023, May 2023, Dec 2021, May 2020); 4 of 10 older papers (May 2018, Dec 2017, May 2016, Dec 2015). It is also the first move inside every L12.2 limit.

Recognise it

A function defined as an integral, \(F(x)=\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}f(t)\,dt\), with \(x\) in one or both limits, and the question asks for \(F'(x)\), \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) or a limit that is really a derivative.

Standard procedure
  1. Find where \(x\) sits.
    • Only in the limits: go to step 2.
    • 1a. Also inside the integrand as a separate factor, e.g. \(\int_0^x x\sin(t^2)\,dt\): pull it out in front, \(x\int_0^x\sin(t^2)\,dt\), and use the product rule in step 3.
    • 1b. Mixed with \(t\), e.g. \(\sin\big((x+t)^2\big)\): substitute \(u=x+t\) (\(x\) is a constant inside the integral) so that \(x\) moves into the limits only. Asked: May 2016.
  2. Name the pieces. Integrand \(f(t)\), lower limit \(a(x)\), upper limit \(b(x)\). Check that \(f\) is continuous on the interval between \(a(x)\) and \(b(x)\) (watch \(\frac1t\), square roots, zero denominators).
  3. Apply the Ready answer \(F'(x)=f\big(b(x)\big)\,b'(x)-f\big(a(x)\big)\,a'(x)\).
    • 3a. Both limits depend on \(x\). Asked: Dec 2025, Dec 2023, May 2023, May 2016, Dec 2015.
    • 3b. One limit is a constant: its term is \(0\). Asked: May 2020, May 2018, Dec 2017.
    • 3c. The question hides a derivative: \(\lim_{t\to c}\frac{G(t)-G(c)}{t-c}\) with \(G(t)=\int_0^tF\) is \(G'(c)=F(c)\). Asked: Dec 2021.
  4. Simplify, then substitute if a value such as \(F'(0)\) is asked. Asked: May 2018.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2023 Q2(b), 4%. Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\), using any method you choose, and showing all the reasoning.

\[y=\int_x^{x^3}\frac{e^t}{t}\,dt\]

Step 1 \(x\) appears only in the limits.

If \(x\) were also inside the integrand we would have to move it out first (step 1a or 1b).

Step 2 \(f(t)=\dfrac{e^t}{t}\), \(a(x)=x\), \(b(x)=x^3\). \(f\) is continuous for \(t\ne0\), and for \(x\ne0\) the numbers \(x\) and \(x^3\) have the same sign, so \(t=0\) is never between them.

FTC Part I needs a continuous integrand. One line on this shows the marker you know the condition.

Step 3a Split at a constant, say \(1\) (take \(-1\) if \(x\lt0\)):

\[y=\int_1^{x^3}\frac{e^t}{t}\,dt-\int_1^{x}\frac{e^t}{t}\,dt.\]

FTC Part I with the chain rule on each piece:

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{e^{x^3}}{x^3}\cdot3x^2-\frac{e^x}{x}\cdot1.\]

"Showing all the reasoning": the split and the names "FTC Part I" and "chain rule" are what earn the method marks. Skipping straight to the answer is fine only on answers-only papers.

Step 4 Simplify \(\frac{3x^2}{x^3}=\frac3x\):

\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{3e^{x^3}-e^x}{x}}\]

Common slip: forgetting the factor \(b'(x)=3x^2\), or getting the sign of the lower-limit term wrong.

Traps
  • Forgetting the chain-rule factor. \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^{x^2}t^4e^t\,dt\) is \(x^8e^{x^2}\cdot2x\), not \(x^8e^{x^2}\) (Dec 2017 Q3(b)). Dec 2025 MC3 option (C) is exactly this mistake.
  • Sign of the lower limit. The lower-limit term is subtracted; MC3 option (B) has the signs swapped.
  • \(x\) inside the integrand. May 2016 Q5(b) \(\int_0^x\sin\big((x+t)^2\big)dt\) is not \(\sin(4x^2)\): substitute first (step 1b).
Ready answer · differentiating an integral

If \(f\) is continuous on an interval containing all values of \(a(x)\) and \(b(x)\), and \(a\), \(b\) are differentiable, then

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{d}{dx}\int_{a(x)}^{b(x)}f(t)\,dt\\&\quad=f\big(b(x)\big)\,b'(x)-f\big(a(x)\big)\,a'(x).\end{aligned}\]

In words: put the top limit into the integrand and multiply by the derivative of the top limit; subtract the same thing for the bottom limit.

Why: pick a constant \(c\) and write the integral as \(\int_c^{b(x)}f-\int_c^{a(x)}f\). FTC Part I gives \(\frac{d}{du}\int_c^uf(t)\,dt=f(u)\); the chain rule does the rest. On written papers, show this split once.

Let \(F(x)=\displaystyle\int_{x^2}^{x^3}\cos(t^2)\,dt\). What is \(F'(x)\)?

(A) \(3x^2\cos(x^6)-2x\cos(x^4)\)
(B) \(2x\cos(x^4)-3x^2\cos(x^6)\)
(C) \(\cos(x^6)-\cos(x^4)\)
(D) \(\dfrac{\sin(x^6)-\sin(x^4)}{x}\)
(E) \(\cos(x^3)-\cos(x^2)\)

Hint

Type L12.1, both limits variable (step 3a). First step: \(f(t)=\cos(t^2)\), \(a=x^2\), \(b=x^3\); put each limit into \(f\) and multiply by its derivative.

Solution

Step 2 \(f(t)=\cos(t^2)\) is continuous everywhere; \(b(x)=x^3\), \(a(x)=x^2\).

Step 3a \(F'(x)=\cos\big((x^3)^2\big)\cdot3x^2-\cos\big((x^2)^2\big)\cdot2x\).

May 2023 Q2(b)4%

Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\), using any method you choose, and showing all the reasoning. \[y=\int_x^{x^2}\frac{e^t}{\cos t+1}\,dt\]

Hint

Type L12.1, both limits variable (step 3a). First step: \(f(t)=\frac{e^t}{\cos t+1}\), \(a=x\), \(b=x^2\); note where \(f\) is continuous.

Solution

Step 2 \(f(t)=\dfrac{e^t}{\cos t+1}\) is continuous wherever \(\cos t\ne-1\), i.e. away from \(t=\pm\pi,\pm3\pi,\dots\). Work with \(x\) for which no such point lies between \(x\) and \(x^2\) (for example \(|x|\lt\sqrt\pi\)).

Step 3a Split at \(0\): \(y=\int_0^{x^2}f-\int_0^{x}f\). FTC Part I and the chain rule:

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{e^{x^2}}{\cos(x^2)+1}\cdot2x-\frac{e^x}{\cos x+1}.\]
\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{2x\,e^{x^2}}{\cos(x^2)+1}-\frac{e^x}{\cos x+1}}\]

Marker: name FTC Part I and the chain rule; the factor \(2x\) is where most marks are lost.

Equivalent forms. Using \(1+\cos\theta=2\cos^2\frac\theta2\): \(x\,e^{x^2}\sec^2\frac{x^2}2-\frac12e^x\sec^2\frac x2\).

Let \(F\) be a differentiable function on \((-\infty,\infty)\) such that \(F(-1)=2\), \(F(0)=1\), \(F(1)=\frac12\), \(F(2)=\frac43\), \(F(3)=3\). Evaluate, if exists, the following limit. [Show your calculation.] \[\lim_{t\to2}\frac1{t-2}\left(\int_0^tF(x)\,dx-\int_0^2F(x)\,dx\right)\]

Part (a) (state the FTC) is under L12.5; part (ii) is under L12.3.

Hint

Type L12.1, a hidden derivative (step 3c). First step: call \(G(t)=\int_0^tF(x)\,dx\) and recognise the limit as a difference quotient of \(G\) at \(2\).

Solution

Step 2 \(F\) is differentiable, hence continuous on \(\mathbb R\). Let \(G(t)=\int_0^tF(x)\,dx\).

Step 3c The limit is \(\displaystyle\lim_{t\to2}\frac{G(t)-G(2)}{t-2}=G'(2)\), by the definition of the derivative.

L12.2Limit of a ratio involving integrals: FTC then L'Hôpital

Chapter note · FTC Part I · Chapter note · L'Hôpital's Rule

Frequency: 6 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, May 2025, Dec 2023, May 2023, May 2020, May 2019); 0 of 10 older papers. A recent favourite: usually Q1(c) of the limits question.

Recognise it

A limit of a quotient where the top (or both top and bottom) is an integral whose limit contains the variable, e.g. \(\dfrac{\int_0^{x^2}(\dots)\,dt}{x^4}\). You cannot work out the integral, but you can differentiate it.

Standard procedure
  1. Find the limits of top and bottom separately. \(\int_a^af=0\), so \(\int_0^{x}(\dots)\to0\) as \(x\to0\) when the integrand is continuous. As \(x\to\infty\), use what the question gives ("given that … tends to infinity").
  2. Name the form. \(\frac00\) or \(\frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty}\): go to step 3. Anything else is not indeterminate: answer directly.
  3. L'Hôpital. Differentiate top and bottom separately; differentiate each integral with the L12.1 Ready answer \(f(b)\,b'-f(a)\,a'\). Say why both are differentiable (continuous integrand, FTC Part I).
  4. Finish the new limit.
    • 4a. Substitute (continuity), simplify trig, or use \(\frac{\sin u}{u}\to1\), \(\frac{e^u-1}{u}\to1\). If still \(\frac00\), go back to step 3. Asked: Dec 2025, Dec 2023 (= May 2023), May 2020.
    • 4b. The variable is \(n\) (a sequence): first replace \(n\) by a real variable \(x\); L'Hôpital needs a function. The limit as \(x\to\infty\) is then the limit of the sequence. Asked: May 2025.
    • 4c. An unknown function \(F\) that tends to \(\infty\): divide top and bottom by \(F\) and use \(\frac{\sin F}{F}\to0\) (bounded over large). Asked: May 2019.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2023 Q1(c), 4% (same question as May 2023 Q1(c)). Find the limit, or explain why it does not exist, showing all the reasoning. If the limit involves infinity explain whether it is \(\infty\) or \(-\infty\).

\[\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\int_0^{x^2}\left(e^{\sin t}-1\right)dt}{x^4}\]

Both papers print the integrand without brackets, "\(e^{\sin t}-1\,dt\)". It is read as \(\int_0^{x^2}(e^{\sin t}-1)\,dt\): the other reading, \(\int_0^{x^2}e^{\sin t}dt-1\), makes the top tend to \(-1\) and the question trivial.

Step 1 Top: the integrand is continuous and \(x^2\to0\), so \(\int_0^{x^2}(\dots)\to\int_0^0(\dots)=0\). Bottom: \(x^4\to0\).

Write the reason the integral tends to \(0\); "showing all the reasoning" includes the form check.

Step 2 Form \(\frac00\): go on.

Step 3 Top: FTC Part I + chain rule, \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^{x^2}(e^{\sin t}-1)\,dt=(e^{\sin x^2}-1)\cdot2x\). Bottom: \(4x^3\). By L'Hôpital's Rule,

\[\lim_{x\to0}\frac{(e^{\sin x^2}-1)\,2x}{4x^3}=\lim_{x\to0}\frac{e^{\sin x^2}-1}{2x^2}\]

provided this new limit exists.

This is L12.1 inside a limit. The factor \(2x\) is easy to lose.

Step 4a Still \(\frac00\). Rather than differentiate again, split into standard limits (\(u=\sin x^2\to0\)):

\[\frac{e^{\sin x^2}-1}{2x^2}=\frac12\cdot\frac{e^{u}-1}{u}\cdot\frac{\sin x^2}{x^2}\to\frac12\cdot1\cdot1.\]
\[\boxed{\tfrac12}\]

A second L'Hôpital also works but is longer. Either way, name the form each time.

Traps
  • Losing the chain-rule factor when differentiating \(\int_0^{x^2}\): Dec/May 2023 Q1(c) then gives \(\infty\) or a wrong constant.
  • L'Hôpital on a sequence. May 2025 Q4(c) has \(n\to\infty\); say "replace \(n\) by \(x\)" before differentiating.
  • Forgetting what \(F\) does. In May 2019 Q5(c), the \(\sin F(x)\) term only disappears because \(F(x)\to\infty\) (part (a)); say so.

Dec 2025 MC102 pts (multiple choice)

Let \(g\) be a continuously differentiable function with \(g(1)=6\) and \(g'(1)=3\). What is \[\lim_{x\to1}\frac{\int_1^xg(t)\,dt}{g(x)-6}\,?\]

(A) \(0\) (B) \(\frac12\) (C) \(1\) (D) \(2\) (E) The limit does not exist

Hint

Type L12.2. First step: both top and bottom tend to \(0\) (why?); then differentiate each.

Solution

Step 1–2 Top \(\to\int_1^1g=0\). \(g\) is differentiable, hence continuous, so bottom \(\to g(1)-6=0\). Form \(\frac00\).

Step 3 FTC Part I (\(g\) continuous): top' \(=g(x)\); bottom' \(=g'(x)\). L'Hôpital:

\[\lim_{x\to1}\frac{g(x)}{g'(x)}.\]

Step 4a \(g\) and \(g'\) are continuous ("continuously differentiable"), so the limit is \(\frac{g(1)}{g'(1)}=\frac63=2\).

\[\boxed{\text{(D)}\ \ 2}\]

May 2025 Q4(c)Q4: 12%; part marks not shown

Evaluate \[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\int_n^{2n}t\sin\left(\frac1t\right)dt.\] [Hint: You may given that \(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_n^{2n}t\sin\left(\frac1t\right)dt=\infty\).]

Part (a) is in the L17 guide; part (b) is under L12.3.

Hint

Type L12.2, form \(\frac\infty\infty\), with a sequence (step 4b). First step: replace \(n\) by a real \(x\) and write the quotient \(\dfrac{\int_x^{2x}t\sin(1/t)\,dt}{x}\).

Solution

Step 1–2 Replace \(n\) by a real \(x\gt0\). Top \(\to\infty\) (given), bottom \(x\to\infty\): form \(\frac\infty\infty\).

Step 3 \(t\sin\frac1t\) is continuous for \(t\gt0\). FTC Part I + chain rule:

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{d}{dx}\int_x^{2x}t\sin\tfrac1t\,dt\\&\quad=2x\sin\tfrac1{2x}\cdot2-x\sin\tfrac1x.\end{aligned}\]

Bottom' \(=1\).

Step 4a–b With \(u=\frac1{2x}\to0^+\) and \(v=\frac1x\to0^+\): \(2x\sin\frac1{2x}=\frac{\sin u}{u}\to1\) and \(x\sin\frac1x=\frac{\sin v}{v}\to1\). So the limit is \(2\cdot1-1=1\). The function limit as \(x\to\infty\) is \(1\), so the sequence limit is \(1\).

L12.3Long sum with 1/n in front as n → ∞: turn it into an integral (Riemann sum)

Chapter note · Riemann sums and the definite integral · Chapter note · sigma notation and area

Frequency: 6 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, May 2025, May 2024, Dec 2021, Dec 2020, May 2019); 4 of 10 older papers (Dec 2017, May 2017, May 2016, Dec 2015). The most common L12 type. After the sum becomes an integral, the integral itself usually needs an L13 or L14 technique.

Recognise it

\(\lim_{n\to\infty}\) of a sum with \(n\) terms (or \(2n\), or "\(+\cdots+\)"), a factor like \(\frac1n\), \(\frac1{n^2}\), \(\frac1{n^5}\) or \(\sin\frac1n\) in front, and \(\frac kn\) (or \(\frac rn\)) inside a function. Sometimes the hint says "Riemann sum" or "area under the curve".

Standard procedure
  1. Get exactly one \(\frac1n\) in front (it will be the width \(\Delta x\)). Share the other powers of \(n\) with the terms: \(\frac1{n^2}\sum r(\dots)=\frac1n\sum\frac rn(\dots)\), \(\frac1{n^5}\sum k^4=\frac1n\sum\left(\frac kn\right)^4\).
    • 1a. Something that only behaves like \(\frac1n\), e.g. \(\sin\frac1n\): write it as \(\left(n\sin\frac1n\right)\cdot\frac1n\); the bracket \(\to1\). A log of a product: \(\ln(a_1a_2\cdots)=\ln a_1+\ln a_2+\cdots\).
    • 1b. A denominator like \(4n+k\): factor it, \(4n+k=2n\left(2+\frac{k}{2n}\right)\), so the width is \(\frac1{2n}\).
  2. Find the points \(x_k\): the expression that goes up by the width each term. Write \(x_k=a+k\,\Delta x\); the function applied to it is \(f\).
  3. Read off \([a,b]\) from the first and last \(x_k\). Check: (number of terms) \(\times\,\Delta x=b-a\).
    • 3a. \(k=1\) to \(n\), \(x_k=\frac kn\): \([0,1]\). Asked: Dec 2025, May 2024, Dec 2017, May 2016, Dec 2015.
    • 3b. Shifted start or extra terms (\(k=n\) to \(2n-1\); \(r=n+1\) to \(2n\); \(r=1\) to \(2n\)): the interval moves or stretches. Asked: May 2025, Dec 2021, May 2017.
    • 3c. After step 1a or 1b (width \(\frac1{2n}\), or a factor to remove first). Asked: Dec 2020, May 2019.
  4. Write the limit as \(\int_a^bf(x)\,dx\). Say "\(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), so its Riemann sums converge to the integral". Left or right endpoints do not matter.
  5. Evaluate the integral (FTC Part II; by parts or substitution if needed).
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2025 Q4(b), Q4: 12%. Evaluate the limit. [Hint: Think about Riemann sum.]

\[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=n}^{2n-1}\ln\frac kn\]

Step 1 One \(\frac1n\) is already in front. \(\Delta x=\frac1n\).

If the front factor were \(\frac1{n^2}\) or similar, we would first move the extra \(n\)'s inside.

Step 2 \(x_k=\frac kn\) goes up by \(\frac1n\) each term, and \(f(x)=\ln x\).

Step 3b First point \(x_n=\frac nn=1\), last \(x_{2n-1}=2-\frac1n\). That is \(n\) points spaced \(\frac1n\) apart, the left endpoints of \(n\) strips covering \([1,2]\). Check: \(n\times\frac1n=1=2-1\).

The interval is not \([0,1]\) here: read it from the actual first and last terms. This is where most marks are lost.

Step 4 \(\ln x\) is continuous on \([1,2]\), so the Riemann sums converge:

\[\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\sum_{k=n}^{2n-1}\ln\frac kn=\int_1^2\ln x\,dx.\]

Step 5 By parts (\(u=\ln x\), \(dv=dx\)):

\[\begin{aligned}\int_1^2\ln x\,dx&=\big[x\ln x-x\big]_1^2\\&=(2\ln2-2)-(0-1).\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{2\ln2-1}\]

Check: \(2\ln2-1\approx0.386\); the sum at \(n=2000\) is \(0.3861\).

Traps
  • Assuming \([0,1]\). May 2025 Q4(b) is \([1,2]\), Dec 2021 Q4(b)(ii) is \([2,3]\) (not \([1,2]\)), May 2017 Q3(b) is \([0,2]\). Always read the first and last terms.
  • Wrong width. In May 2019 Q4(c) the points go up by \(\frac1{2n}\), so the front factor must be \(\frac1{2n}\).
  • Leaving a factor that tends to 1. Dec 2020 Q5(b) has \(\sin\frac1n\), not \(\frac1n\): write \(n\sin\frac1n\to1\) explicitly.
Ready answer · the Riemann-sum template

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), then

\[\begin{aligned}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{b-a}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\!\left(a+\frac{(b-a)k}{n}\right)\\&\quad=\int_a^bf(x)\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

In words: width of each strip \(\times\) the sum of the heights at the points \(a+\frac{(b-a)k}n\) tends to the integral. Most exam sums are the case \(a=0\), \(b=1\): \(\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^nf\!\left(\frac kn\right)\to\int_0^1f(x)\,dx\).

Starting at \(k=0\) instead of \(k=1\) (left endpoints), or adding or dropping one term, does not change the limit: one term is at most \(\frac{\max|f|}{n}\to0\).

Recognise it

A graph of a function (and often a table of areas under it), and a new function defined as its integral, \(M(x)=\int_0^xm(t)\,dt\). Questions ask for values of \(M\), \(M'\), where \(M\) rises or falls, or how many times \(M\) takes a value.

Standard procedure
  1. Sign the areas. \(\int_n^{n+1}m=+A(n)\) where the graph is above the axis, \(-A(n)\) where it is below.
  2. Tabulate \(M\) at the whole numbers. \(M(0)=0\). For \(x\gt0\) add the signed pieces from \(0\) to \(x\). For \(x\lt0\), \(M(x)=-\int_x^0m\) (swapping the limits flips the sign).

  4. Shape and equations. \(M\) is increasing where \(m\gt0\) and decreasing where \(m\lt0\). For "how many solutions of \(M(x)=c\)": split into pieces where \(M\) is monotonic; on each piece the IVT gives a solution if \(c\) lies between the end values, and monotonicity says there is only one. Asked: Dec 2020 (c).
Traps
  • Unsigned areas. The table gives areas "without sign"; on \([-1,1]\) the graph is below the axis, so those pieces count negative.
  • Negative \(x\). \(M(-1)=\int_0^{-1}m=-\int_{-1}^0m=+1.75\), not \(-1.75\): two minus signs.
  • Not justifying the count. Dec 2020 Q2(c) says "explain concisely": name the IVT for "at least one" and monotonicity for "at most one".

L12.5State the FTC; proofs, inequalities and average values with integrals

Chapter note · the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus · Chapter note · average value

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, May 2025, Dec 2021); 5 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018, Dec 2017, May 2016, Dec 2015, May 2015). Half of these parts are "State the FTC", asked word for word 4 times.

Recognise it

"State the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus"; "show that … for all \(x\)" with integrals on both sides; "determine all continuous functions such that \(\int\dots=\int\dots\)"; "average value", "show there exists \(c\) such that …"; true/false statements about a function known only through \(f'\) and a table.

Standard procedure
  1. Name the task.
    • 1a. "State the FTC": write the Ready answer below, both parts, with the conditions, and stop. Asked: Dec 2021, Dec 2018, Dec 2017, Dec 2015.
    • 1b. An integral of something that is a derivative (\(\int\sqrt{1+g}\) when \(2f'=\sqrt{1+g}\)), with values in a table: step 2.
    • 1c. An equation or inequality between integrals that must hold for all \(x\): step 3.
    • 1d. "Average value", "there exists \(c\) with \(f(c)=\) the average": step 4.
  2. FTC Part II. Check the integrand is a continuous derivative \(F'\); then \(\int_a^bF'(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)\). Judge the other statements in the same list with the theorem they need (IVT, MVT, critical points), using only the table. Asked: May 2025.
  3. Differentiate. FTC Part I turns \(\int_a^x(\dots)\,dt\) into the integrand at \(x\). Pull any factor of \(x\) out of the integral first.
    • 3a. An equation: differentiate both sides to get an equation for the unknown function at each \(x\); solve it; use continuity (IVT) to rule out switching between solutions; check in the original. Asked: May 2015.
    • 3b. An inequality: let \(H(x)=\text{LHS}-\text{RHS}\), note \(H=0\) at the starting point, and show \(H'\ge0\). Then \(H\) is increasing, so \(H(x)\ge0\). Asked: May 2016.
  4. Average value \(f_{\text{avg}}=\dfrac1{b-a}\displaystyle\int_a^bf(x)\,dx\). For "there exists \(c\)": \(f\) is continuous and \(f_{\text{avg}}\) lies between two values of \(f\) on \([a,b]\), so the IVT gives \(c\) with \(f(c)=f_{\text{avg}}\) (this is the Mean Value Theorem for integrals). Asked: Dec 2025.
Ready answer · model answer to "State the FTC"

Let \(f\) be continuous on \([a,b]\).

Part I. The function \(g(x)=\displaystyle\int_a^xf(t)\,dt\), \(a\le x\le b\), is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\), and \(g'(x)=f(x)\).

Part II. If \(F\) is any antiderivative of \(f\) on \([a,b]\), that is \(F'=f\), then

\[\int_a^bf(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).\]

In words: Part I, the rate at which the area under \(f\) grows is the height \(f(x)\). Part II, to find an integral, find any antiderivative and subtract its values at the two ends.

The marks are in the conditions: "\(f\) continuous on \([a,b]\)" and "\(F'=f\)". A statement without them is incomplete. "Without proof" means no proof is wanted.
