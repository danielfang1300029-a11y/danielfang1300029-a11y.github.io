The short version

What to expect

A region question is in 10 of the 13 recent papers and in all 10 older papers. Usually you are given two curves (or one curve and an axis), asked for the area of the region between them or, more often, the volume of the solid you get by spinning the region about a line. The curves change every year, but the method does not: sketch → find where the curves meet → choose the slice → measure radius and height from the axis line → integrate. Every type below turns that into numbered steps.

The one real decision is the slice. With the curves given as \(y=f(x)\): spinning about a horizontal line (\(x\)-axis or \(y=k\)) → discs/washers (L16.2); spinning about a vertical line (\(y\)-axis or \(x=k\)) → shells (L16.3). Both then integrate in \(x\), so you never have to solve the curves for \(x\).

How to practise: use Start here to pick the type, then work the question with the type's procedure open; check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind the formulas, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: recent papers put it in Part I Q5 (8–10%), usually paired with a surface-area or arc-length part on the same region (Dec 2023, May 2023, Dec 2024, May 2024, May 2026; Dec 2025 Q13 in the free-response block). It is also the last part of a curve-sketching question (May 2019 Q3, May 2025 Q2) or stands alone (May 2020 Q5, 5%; May 2022 Q4, 7%). Older papers: "sketch the region" then one or two volumes.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. So far volume questions have stayed written (Dec 2025 Q13, May 2026 Q5), so the set-up line (\(V=\pi\int\dots\) with radii) carries marks; but the rest of the paper is faster now, so do not spend long on the integration.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard derivatives and integrals, and the Laplace table. They print no area, disc, washer or shell formula. Learn the four in the Ready answer box.
  • Wording: "revolving / rotating \(R\) about (around, by) the line …"; "disk method" means discs or washers; older papers sometimes fix the method ("by the shell method", May 2015; "use the disk method", Dec 2018) and then the other method scores nothing. "Finite region" or "bounded region" means: find the closed-off piece yourself.

L16.1Area between curves (and "sketch the finite region")

Chapter note · area between curves

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2023, May 2023); 5 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018, May 2016 twice, Dec 2015, May 2015, May 2014). 8 parts, of which 4 only ask for the sketch; the sketch is then used in a volume part.

Recognise it

"Find the area of the region enclosed by …", or "Sketch the graphs … and mark the finite region (R)". The region is bounded by two curves, or by one curve, the \(x\)-axis and perhaps a line \(x=c\).

Standard procedure
  1. Sketch both boundaries and shade the region. For a curve with a square root, square both sides to see if it is part of a circle or ellipse.
  2. Find where the boundaries meet: solve \(f(x)=g(x)\) (or \(f(x)=0\) for the \(x\)-axis), and add any given line \(x=c\). These \(x\)-values are the limits \(a\) and \(b\).
    • If the part only asks you to sketch and mark the region: label the meeting points and the region, and stop. Asked: Dec 2018, May 2016 Q4, Dec 2015, May 2014.
  3. Decide which boundary is on top by testing one \(x\) between \(a\) and \(b\). If the curves cross between \(a\) and \(b\), split the interval there.
  4. Integrate top − bottom: \(\displaystyle A=\int_a^b\big(\text{top}-\text{bottom}\big)\,dx\).
    • 4a. Polynomials: integrate term by term. Asked: May 2015.
    • 4b. A square root of a quadratic: the curve is half a circle or ellipse. Use a trig substitution (\(x-\text{centre}=\text{radius}\cdot\sin\theta\)), and check with the geometry (\(\tfrac12\pi ab\)). Asked: May 2023, Dec 2023.
    • 4c. A product such as \(x\ln x\): integration by parts. Asked: May 2016 Q2.
  5. Check: the area is positive and roughly fits a rectangle drawn around the region.
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2023 Q5(a), 4%. Let \(D\) be the region bounded by the curve \(y=\sqrt{x(1-x)}\) \((0\le x\le1)\) and the \(x\)-axis. Compute the area of \(D\).

Step 1 Squaring, \(y^2=x-x^2\), i.e. \(\left(x-\tfrac12\right)^2+y^2=\tfrac14\). With \(y\ge0\) this is the upper half of the circle with centre \(\left(\tfrac12,0\right)\) and radius \(\tfrac12\).

Completing the square inside the root is the quickest way to see the shape, and it tells you the answer before you integrate.

Step 2 \(\sqrt{x(1-x)}=0\) at \(x=0\) and \(x=1\): the limits.

Step 3 The curve is on top (\(y\ge0\)); the \(x\)-axis is the bottom.

Step 4b \(\displaystyle A=\int_0^1\sqrt{\tfrac14-\left(x-\tfrac12\right)^2}\,dx\). Put \(x-\tfrac12=\tfrac12\sin\theta\), \(dx=\tfrac12\cos\theta\,d\theta\); \(x=0\to\theta=-\tfrac\pi2\), \(x=1\to\theta=\tfrac\pi2\):

\[\begin{aligned}A&=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\tfrac12\cos\theta\cdot\tfrac12\cos\theta\,d\theta\\&=\tfrac14\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\\&=\tfrac14\cdot\tfrac\pi2=\tfrac\pi8.\end{aligned}\]

\(\cos\theta\ge0\) on \(\left[-\tfrac\pi2,\tfrac\pi2\right]\), so \(\sqrt{\cos^2\theta}=\cos\theta\) with no sign trouble. Use \(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12(1+\cos2\theta)\) for the last integral.

Step 5 Half a disc of radius \(\tfrac12\): \(\tfrac12\pi\left(\tfrac12\right)^2=\tfrac\pi8\). ✓

\[\boxed{\dfrac{\pi}{8}}\]

A 4% part: the geometry alone, clearly explained, usually earns the marks; the integral is the safe way to show it.

Traps
  • Wrong left end. May 2016 Q2(c) asks for the region "in the first quadrant" under \(y=x\ln x\) up to \(x=e\). \(x\ln x\lt0\) on \((0,1)\), so the region starts at \(x=1\). Integrating from 0 gives \(\frac{e^2}{4}\), which is wrong.
  • Sketch-only parts still need detail. Dec 2018 Q2(a) says "Identify (R) carefully!": label both meeting points with coordinates and shade only the finite piece.
  • Top and bottom swapped. In May 2015 Q4(a) \(4+x^2\) is on top between \(-2\) and \(2\) (test \(x=0\)), even though \(2x^2\) grows faster. Swapping them gives \(-\frac{32}{3}\).
Ready answer · half an ellipse

For \(a,b\gt0\):

\[\int_{-a}^{a}b\sqrt{1-\frac{x^2}{a^2}}\;dx=\frac{\pi ab}{2}.\]

It is the area of half an ellipse with half-axes \(a\) and \(b\) (a circle when \(a=b\)). It gives Dec 2023 Q5(a) (\(a=2,b=1\): \(\pi\)) and May 2023 Q5(a) (circle of radius \(\frac12\): \(\frac\pi8\)) in one line. On a written paper, show the trig substitution and use this to check.

Solution

L16.2Volume by discs or washers (axis y = k)

Chapter note · discs and washers

Frequency: 6 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, Dec 2025, Dec 2024, Dec 2023, May 2023, May 2019); 4 of 10 older papers (May 2018, May 2017, May 2014, May 2013). The most common L16 type.

Recognise it

The region is given by curves \(y=f(x)\) and it is spun about a horizontal line: the \(x\)-axis or \(y=k\). Vertical strips are then perpendicular to the axis, and each one sweeps out a disc or a washer (a disc with a hole).

Standard procedure
  1. Sketch the region and draw the axis line \(y=k\) dashed. Check that the region lies on one side of the axis line. If the region is symmetric about the axis (an ellipse, the astroid), keep only the half on one side: the other half sweeps the same solid.
  2. Limits: solve for the meeting points (or read off the given lines \(x=a\), \(x=b\)). If everything is symmetric about the \(y\)-axis, you may integrate from 0 and double.
  3. Slice: a vertical strip at \(x\), width \(dx\). Spun about \(y=k\) it gives a disc if the strip touches the axis line, a washer if there is a gap.
  4. Radii, measured from the axis line: \(R(x)=\) distance from \(y=k\) to the far edge of the strip, \(r(x)=\) distance to the near edge.
    • 4a. Strip touches the axis: disc, \(r=0\), \(R=|f(x)-k|\) (just \(f(x)\) for the \(x\)-axis). Asked: May 2026, Dec 2024 (= Dec 2023), May 2023, May 2019, May 2018.
    • 4b. Gap between the region and the axis: washer. Above the axis \(R=\text{top}-k\), \(r=\text{bottom}-k\); below it \(R=k-\text{bottom}\), \(r=k-\text{top}\). Asked: Dec 2025, May 2017, May 2014, May 2013.
  5. Integrate: \(\displaystyle V=\pi\int_a^b\big(R^2-r^2\big)\,dx\). Expand \(R^2-r^2\) first; it often simplifies a lot.
  6. Check: \(V\gt0\); compare with a known solid (sphere, ellipsoid) or with the Pappus check in L16.3.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2025 Q13(a), Part I free response (Q13: 10 pts). Let \(\mathcal R\) be the region bounded by \(y_1=1-x^2\) and \(y_2=x^2-1\). Find the volume of the solid generated when \(\mathcal R\) is rotated about the line \(y=2\).

Step 1 \(y_1\) opens down from \((0,1)\); \(y_2\) opens up from \((0,-1)\). The region is the lens between them. Its highest point is \(y=1\lt2\), so the whole region is below the axis line \(y=2\).

The sketch tells you which curve is far from the axis. Here it is the lower curve, which surprises many students.

Step 2 \(1-x^2=x^2-1\iff x=\pm1\).

Step 3 A vertical strip runs from \(y_2\) up to \(y_1\le1\), so there is a gap up to \(y=2\): a washer.

Step 4b Region below the axis:

\[\begin{aligned}R&=2-y_2=2-(x^2-1)=3-x^2,\\ r&=2-y_1=2-(1-x^2)=1+x^2.\end{aligned}\]

Write both radii as "axis minus curve". The marker looks for exactly these two expressions.

Step 5 Expand first: \(R^2-r^2=(9-6x^2+x^4)-(1+2x^2+x^4)=8-8x^2\). So

\[\begin{aligned}V&=\pi\int_{-1}^{1}(8-8x^2)\,dx\\&=2\pi\Big[8x-\tfrac{8x^3}{3}\Big]_0^1=2\pi\cdot\tfrac{16}{3}.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\dfrac{32\pi}{3}}\]

Step 6 Pappus check: area \(=\int_{-1}^1 2(1-x^2)\,dx=\frac83\), centre of the region at \(y=0\), distance 2 from the axis, so \(V=2\pi\cdot2\cdot\frac83=\frac{32\pi}3\). ✓

Common slip: using \(R=1-x^2\) and \(r=x^2-1\) (distances from the \(x\)-axis). That is the volume about the \(x\)-axis, which is a different (and here invalid) question.

Traps
  • Radius from the wrong line. Measure from \(y=k\), not from the \(x\)-axis: Dec 2025 (\(y=2\)) needs \(R=3-x^2\); May 2018 (\(y=-1\)) needs \(R=g(x)+1\).
  • \(R^2-r^2\) is not \((R-r)^2\). May 2014: \(\pi\int\big[(6-x)^2-x^4\big]dx\), not \(\pi\int(6-x-x^2)^2dx\).
  • Counting a symmetric region twice. May 2026's astroid and Dec 2024's ellipse lie on both sides of the axis; spin only the upper half. Doubling gives \(\frac{64\pi}{105}\) and \(\frac{16\pi}{3}\), both wrong.
Ready answer · the four volume formulas with a shifted axis

Washers about \(y=k\), vertical strips:

\[V=\pi\int_a^b\big(R(x)^2-r(x)^2\big)\,dx\]

Washers about \(x=k\), horizontal strips (curves written as \(x=\dots(y)\)):

\[V=\pi\int_c^d\big(R(y)^2-r(y)^2\big)\,dy\]

Shells about \(x=k\), vertical strips:

\[V=2\pi\int_a^b|x-k|\,\big(\text{top}-\text{bottom}\big)\,dx\]

Shells about \(y=k\), horizontal strips:

\[V=2\pi\int_c^d|y-k|\,\big(\text{right}-\text{left}\big)\,dy\]

In words: volume = π × ∫ (far radius² − near radius²) for washers; volume = 2π × ∫ (distance to axis) × (strip length) for shells. Every radius and distance is measured from the axis line. A disc is a washer with near radius 0.

Ellipsoid. Spinning \(y=b\sqrt{1-\frac{x^2}{a^2}}\) \((-a\le x\le a)\) about the \(x\)-axis gives

\[V=\frac43\pi ab^2.\]

Recurs: Dec 2024 Q5(b) = Dec 2023 Q5(b) (\(a=2,b=1\): \(\frac{8\pi}3\)) and May 2023 Q5(b) (sphere, \(a=b=\frac12\): \(\frac\pi6\)). On written papers show the integral and use this to check.

L16.3Volume by shells (axis x = k)

Chapter note · cylindrical shells

Frequency: 4 of 13 recent papers (May 2025, May 2024, May 2022, May 2020); 4 of 10 older papers (May 2016, Dec 2015, May 2015, May 2013 twice), 5 parts.

Recognise it

The region is given by curves \(y=f(x)\) and it is spun about a vertical line: the \(y\)-axis or \(x=k\). Vertical strips are then parallel to the axis and each sweeps out a thin cylinder (a shell, like a tin can with no top or bottom). Shells are the only sensible choice when \(f\) cannot be solved for \(x\): \(\sin(x^2-1)\), \(\left(3-\frac1x\right)e^{-x/2}\), \(\cosh x\).

Standard procedure
  1. Sketch the region and draw the axis line \(x=k\) dashed. If the axis cuts through the region and the region is symmetric about it, keep only the half on one side: the other half sweeps the same solid.
  2. Limits: \(x\)-values of the meeting points or the given lines. For a "bounded region" next to an asymptote, the left end is where the curve crosses the \(x\)-axis.
  3. Slice: a vertical strip at \(x\), width \(dx\), parallel to the axis. Spun about \(x=k\) it is a shell.
  4. Radius and height: height \(=\) top \(-\) bottom of the strip; radius \(=\) distance from the strip to the axis line.
    • 4a. The \(y\)-axis: radius \(x\). Asked: May 2025, May 2022, May 2020, Dec 2015, May 2013 Q4, May 2013 Q5.
    • 4b. A shifted line \(x=k\): radius \(x-k\) if the region is to the right of the axis, \(k-x\) if to the left. Asked: May 2024 (\(x+1\)), May 2016 (\(2-x\)), May 2015 (\(x+3\)).
  5. Integrate: \(\displaystyle V=2\pi\int_a^b(\text{radius})(\text{height})\,dx\). Polynomials: expand. Otherwise use the substitution \(u=\) the inside of the function (May 2020, May 2022) or integration by parts (May 2025, May 2013 Q5).
  6. Check: \(V\gt0\); Pappus (below) or the washer method in \(y\) when the curves are easy to invert.
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2024 Q5(a) (Q5: 10%). Let \(E\) be the region enclosed between the curves \(y=x^3\) and \(y=\sqrt x\). Evaluate the volume of the solid \(S\) generated by revolving the region \(E\) about the line \(x=-1\).

The green strip and its radius are what you should draw on your own sketch: it makes the radius \(x+1\) obvious.

Step 2 \(x^3=\sqrt x\Rightarrow x^6=x\Rightarrow x(x^5-1)=0\): \(x=0\) or \(1\).

Step 3 Vertical strips are parallel to the vertical axis: shells.

Step 4b Radius \(=x-(-1)=x+1\); height \(=\sqrt x-x^3\).

Using radius \(x\) gives the volume about the \(y\)-axis: the most common lost mark on this question.

Step 5 Expand and integrate:

\[\begin{aligned}V&=2\pi\int_0^1(x+1)\big(x^{1/2}-x^3\big)dx\\&=2\pi\int_0^1\big(x^{3/2}+x^{1/2}-x^4-x^3\big)dx\\&=2\pi\left(\tfrac25+\tfrac23-\tfrac15-\tfrac14\right)=2\pi\cdot\tfrac{37}{60}.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\dfrac{37\pi}{30}}\]

Step 6 Washers in \(y\): \(\pi\int_0^1\big[(1+y^{1/3})^2-(1+y^2)^2\big]dy=\frac{37\pi}{30}\). ✓

The check uses the second formula in the Ready answer: far edge \(x=y^{1/3}\), near edge \(x=y^2\), both measured from \(x=-1\).

Traps
  • Radius for a shifted axis. \(x=-1\) → \(x+1\) (May 2024); \(x=2\) → \(2-x\) (May 2016); \(x=-3\) → \(x+3\) (May 2015). Plain \(x\) is only right for the \(y\)-axis.
  • Axis through a symmetric region. In May 2013 Q5(c) the region \(-a\le x\le a\) is cut in half by the \(y\)-axis. Integrate from 0 to \(a\) only. \(2\pi\int_{-a}^{a}x\cosh x\,dx=0\) and \(2\pi\int_{-a}^{a}|x|\cosh x\,dx\) is double the answer.
  • Where the "bounded region" starts. May 2025: \(g\to-\infty\) as \(x\to0^+\), so the bounded region begins where \(g=0\), at \(x=\frac13\), not at \(x=0\).
Ready answer · Pappus check and the torus Beyond the handouts

Spinning a flat region of area \(A\) about a line that does not cut it gives

\[V=2\pi\,d\,A,\]

where \(d\) is the distance from the region's centre of mass (its balance point) to the axis. For a region symmetric about a line, the centre lies on that line.

In words: volume = (distance the centre travels in one turn) × (area).

It gives May 2022 Q4 (circle of radius 1, centre 2 from the axis: \(2\pi\cdot2\cdot\pi=4\pi^2\); in general a torus is \(2\pi^2Rr^2\)), May 2015 Q4(c) (\(2\pi\cdot3\cdot\frac{32}3=64\pi\)) and Dec 2025 Q13(a) (\(2\pi\cdot2\cdot\frac83=\frac{32\pi}3\)). Use it to check; on a written paper the integral is what scores. The shell formulas with a shifted axis are in the L16.2 Ready answer.

L16.4Set up only: write the integral, compare methods

Chapter note · choosing discs or shells

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 3 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018, Dec 2017, Dec 2016), 6 parts. Recent papers always ask for the value, but the set-up line below is still the first mark of every L16.2 and L16.3 question.

Recognise it

"Write down the integral … (No need to evaluate the integral.)", "use the shell method … repeat by using the disk method", or "mark clearly the regions" before writing an area or volume integral.

Standard procedure
  1. Sketch the region and draw the axis line dashed.
  2. Meeting points: solve \(f(x)=g(x)\). Note every crossing between the outer limits: the region falls into pieces there. If the part only asks to mark the regions, shade each piece, label the meeting points and stop.
  3. Slice with the method asked (if none is named: horizontal axis → washers in \(x\), vertical axis → shells in \(x\)).
    • 3a. Shells in \(x\): \(2\pi\int(\text{radius})(\text{height})\,dx\), radius measured from the axis line. Asked: Dec 2018 (i), Dec 2017, Dec 2016 (d).
    • 3b. "Disk method" about a vertical axis: solve each boundary for \(x\) in terms of \(y\) and use horizontal strips, \(\pi\int(R^2-r^2)\,dy\). Split the \(y\)-range wherever the left or right edge changes to a different curve. Asked: Dec 2018 (ii).
    • 3c. Curves that cross: one integral per piece, deciding top/bottom (or near/far from the axis) on each piece. Asked: Dec 2016 (b), (c), (d).
  4. Write the integral(s) in full: \(\pi\) or \(2\pi\), both limits, the integrand, \(dx\) or \(dy\). Do not evaluate.
  5. Check on scrap paper: if both methods are asked, they must give the same number; for a letter like \(a\), try \(a=\frac12\).
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2018 Q2(b) (Q2: 10%). (R) is the finite region enclosed between \(y=x(4-x)\) and \(y=x\). Let \(W\) be the volume of the solid of revolution obtained by revolving (R) around the \(y\)-axis. (i) Use the shell method to write down the integral for \(W\). (No need to evaluate the integral.) (ii) Repeat part (i) by using the disk method.

Step 1–2 From part (a): the curves meet at \((0,0)\) and \((3,3)\); the parabola is on top for \(0\le x\le3\). The parabola's highest point is \((2,4)\).

Step 3a (i) Shells. Radius \(x\), height \(x(4-x)-x=3x-x^2\):

\[W=2\pi\int_0^3x\left(3x-x^2\right)dx.\]

Shells are the natural method here: no solving for \(x\), one integral.

Step 3b (ii) Disks. Solve for \(x\): the line is \(x=y\); the parabola \(x^2-4x+y=0\) gives \(x=2\pm\sqrt{4-y}\) (minus for the left half, plus for the right half). A horizontal strip at height \(y\):

  • \(0\le y\le3\): from the left half of the parabola to the line, so \(r=2-\sqrt{4-y}\), \(R=y\);
  • \(3\le y\le4\): from the left half to the right half of the parabola, so \(r=2-\sqrt{4-y}\), \(R=2+\sqrt{4-y}\).

At \(y=3\) the right edge stops being the line and becomes the parabola (the point \((3,3)\)), so the integral must split there. Missing the split is the usual lost mark.

Step 4 Writing \(s=\sqrt{4-y}\) for short:

\[\begin{aligned}W={}&\pi\int_0^3\Big[y^2-(2-s)^2\Big]dy\\&+\pi\int_3^4\Big[(2+s)^2-(2-s)^2\Big]dy\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{(i) }2\pi\int_0^3x(3x-x^2)\,dx\\&\text{(ii) the two integrals above}\end{aligned}}\]

Step 5 Both evaluate to \(\frac{27\pi}{2}\) (sympy). The second integrand simplifies to \(8\sqrt{4-y}\).

Traps
  • No split in \(y\). Dec 2018 (ii): one integral from 0 to 4 with the line as the right edge is wrong above \(y=3\). The parabola also has two halves, \(x=2\pm\sqrt{4-y}\).
  • No split at a crossing. Dec 2016 (b): \(\int_0^1(f-g)\,dx\) subtracts one region's area from the other's. Split at \(x=a\), or use \(|f-g|\).
  • Incomplete integral. "Write down the integral" marks need \(\pi\) or \(2\pi\), limits and \(dx\)/\(dy\). Using a method other than the one named scores nothing.

L16.5Volume by slicing and rates of filling

Chapter note · volume by slicing

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 1 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018). One question in all; the procedure below covers it.

Recognise it

A solid or container described by its cross-sections (here: a disc of radius \(r(h)\) at height \(h\)), often being filled, with a question about how fast the depth rises.

Standard procedure
  1. Area of one slice at height \(s\): \(A(s)\) (for a circular slice \(A(s)=\pi r(s)^2\)).
  2. Add up the slices: \(\displaystyle V(h)=\int_0^hA(s)\,ds\). Use a different letter \(s\) inside, since \(h\) is the upper limit.
  3. Differentiate: by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, \(\frac{dV}{dh}=A(h)\). By the chain rule, \(\frac{dV}{dt}=A(h)\,\frac{dh}{dt}\), where \(t\) is time.
  4. Use the given rate: set \(\frac{dV}{dt}=\) the filling rate and solve \(\displaystyle\frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{\text{filling rate}}{A(h)}\) at the given depth. Asked: Dec 2018.
