MATH1851 Exam Guide · Non-homogeneous Linear ODEs

The short version

What to expect

This is one of the most predictable topics on the paper. A typical recent paper has three parts here, about 5% each: one undetermined coefficients part with no clash, one where the trial solution clashes with the complementary function, and one variation of parameters part (usually a Cauchy–Euler equation). The equations change only in their numbers. If you can do each type in the cards below, you are ready.

The only real decision is which method. Look at the right-hand side (the forcing term, the part without \(y\)) and at what the question tells you to use. The cards below are arranged around that choice.

How to practise: try a question, open the Hint only if stuck, check against the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind a method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Part II, Q7–Q8 (10% each) on papers up to May 2026, usually Q7(b), Q8(a) and Q8(b); Q15(b)–Q16 (10 pts each) in Dec 2025; Q5–Q6 in Dec 2019. Papers from 2013–2016 also used half of Q10 for "solve again by undetermined coefficients" next to a Laplace part.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. These ODE parts stayed full-working, but the paper is faster-paced, so the routine trial-and-compare work needs to be quick.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print a variation-of-parameters hint. They do not print the table of trial solutions, the "multiply by \(x\)" rule or the auxiliary-equation cases. Learn the trial-solution table.
  • Notation: \(\exp(x)\) means \(e^x\). "Complementary function(s)" \(=y_c\), the general solution of the equation with right-hand side \(0\); "particular solution / particular integral" \(=y_p\). Read the question: "find a particular solution" needs only \(y_p\); "general solution" or "solve" needs \(y=y_c+y_p\) with two constants.

O4.1Undetermined coefficients, no clash14 recent · 23 all

Looks like\(ay^{\prime\prime}+by^{\prime}+cy=\) polynomial, \(e^{kx}\), \(\sin\omega x\), \(\cos\omega x\), or a sum.

Do\(y_c\) from \(am^2+bm+c=0\); trial \(y_p\) from the table; substitute, compare coefficients; \(y=y_c+y_p\).

Watch\(\sin\omega x\) needs both \(A\cos\omega x+B\sin\omega x\); a constant on the right needs its own constant in the trial.

O4.2Undetermined coefficients with a clash13 recent · 23 all

Looks likeAs O4.1, but a forcing term already appears in \(y_c\): \(e^{kx}\) with \(k\) a root, or \(\sin\omega x\) in \(y^{\prime\prime}+\omega^2y\).

DoMultiply that piece of the trial by \(x\) (simple root) or \(x^2\) (double root). Then substitute as usual.

WatchCheck the roots first; multiply only the clashing piece, not the whole trial.

O4.3Initial conditions, or solve two ways0 recent · 6 all

Looks like\(y(0)\), \(y^{\prime}(0)\) given; or "solve by undetermined coefficients" next to a Laplace part.

DoGeneral solution \(y_c+y_p\) first, then two equations from \(y(0)\) and \(y^{\prime}(0)\) for \(C_1,C_2\).

WatchApply the conditions to \(y_c+y_p\), never to \(y_c\) alone.

O4.4Variation of parameters6 recent · 10 all

Looks like"by variation of parameters"; or a right-hand side not in the table (\(\sec 2x\)).

Do\(y_1,y_2\) from \(y_c\); \(W=y_1y_2^{\prime}-y_2y_1^{\prime}\); \(y_p=-y_1\int\frac{y_2f}{W}dx+y_2\int\frac{y_1f}{W}dx\).

Watch\(f\) is the right-hand side after making the \(y^{\prime\prime}\) coefficient \(1\).

O4.5Cauchy–Euler equations7 recent · 13 all

Looks like\(x^2y^{\prime\prime}+bxy^{\prime}+cy=x^k\) (powers of \(x\) in front of each derivative).

Do\(y_c\): try \(y=x^m\), so \(m(m-1)+bm+c=0\). Then VoP with \(f=x^{k}/x^2\), or try \(y_p=Ax^k\).

WatchDivide by \(x^2\) before VoP; the papers print a "Caution" about this every time.

O4.6D-operator method0 recent · 1 all

Looks like"Use the D operator method".

DoWrite \(P(D)y=e^{kx}\); \(y_p=\frac{e^{kx}}{P(k)}\) if \(P(k)\ne0\), else \(\frac{xe^{kx}}{P^{\prime}(k)}\).

WatchThe case \(P(k)=0\) (a clash) is the one that is asked.

Clash rule (type O4.2): if a piece of the trial already solves the equation with right-hand side \(0\), multiply that piece by \(x\). For \(e^{kx}\): \(k\) a simple root of the auxiliary equation \(\Rightarrow\) times \(x\); \(k\) a double root \(\Rightarrow\) times \(x^2\). For \(\sin\omega x,\cos\omega x\): roots \(\pm i\omega\) \(\Rightarrow\) times \(x\).

Complementary function, from the roots of \(am^2+bm+c=0\): two real roots \(m_1\ne m_2\): \(C_1e^{m_1x}+C_2e^{m_2x}\); double root \(m\): \((C_1+C_2x)e^{mx}\); complex roots \(\alpha\pm i\beta\): \(e^{\alpha x}(C_1\cos\beta x+C_2\sin\beta x)\).

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q7(b)Q7: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following differential equation: \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+4\dfrac{dy}{dx}+5y=\cos 3x\]

Hint

Type O4.1, undetermined coefficients. The roots of \(m^2+4m+5=0\) are complex with real part \(-2\), so \(\cos3x\) does not clash. First step: try \(y_p=A\cos3x+B\sin3x\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2+4m+5=0\Rightarrow m=-2\pm i\), so \(y_c=e^{-2x}(C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x)\).

2. Trial. \(y_p=A\cos3x+B\sin3x\), so \(y_p^{\prime}=-3A\sin3x+3B\cos3x\) and \(y_p^{\prime\prime}=-9y_p\).

3. Substitute and compare.

\[\begin{aligned}&(-9+5)(A\cos3x+B\sin3x)\\&\quad+4(-3A\sin3x+3B\cos3x)=\cos3x\end{aligned}\]

\(\cos3x\): \(-4A+12B=1\); \(\sin3x\): \(-12A-4B=0\Rightarrow B=-3A\). So \(-40A=1\): \(A=-\frac1{40}\), \(B=\frac3{40}\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=e^{-2x}(C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x)\\&\quad-\tfrac1{40}\cos3x+\tfrac3{40}\sin3x\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: state \(y_c\), the trial and the two coefficient equations; the answer must contain both arbitrary constants.

May 2025 Q8(a)Q8: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following differential equation: \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-3\dfrac{dy}{dx}+2y=2\sin x+1\]

Hint

Type O4.1, undetermined coefficients with a sum on the right. Roots \(1,2\): no clash. First step: trial \(y_p=A\cos x+B\sin x+C\) (one piece per forcing term).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2-3m+2=(m-1)(m-2)=0\), so \(y_c=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{2x}\).

2. Trial. \(y_p=A\cos x+B\sin x+C\).

3. Substitute and compare. \(y_p^{\prime\prime}-3y_p^{\prime}+2y_p=(A-3B)\cos x+(3A+B)\sin x+2C\).

\(A-3B=0\), \(3A+B=2\), \(2C=1\). So \(B=\frac15\), \(A=\frac35\), \(C=\frac12\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{2x}\\&\quad+\tfrac35\cos x+\tfrac15\sin x+\tfrac12\end{aligned}}\]

Dec 2024 Q7(b)5%

Find the general solution to the differential equation: \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+5\dfrac{dy}{dx}+16y=12x+e^x\]

Hint

Type O4.1. Polynomial plus exponential; the auxiliary roots are complex, so nothing clashes. First step: trial \(y_p=Ax+B+Ce^{x}\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2+5m+16=0\Rightarrow m=\dfrac{-5\pm\sqrt{25-64}}{2}=-\dfrac52\pm\dfrac{\sqrt{39}}{2}i\).

\[\begin{aligned}y_c=e^{-5x/2}\Big(&C_1\cos\tfrac{\sqrt{39}}{2}x\\&+C_2\sin\tfrac{\sqrt{39}}{2}x\Big)\end{aligned}\]

2. Trial. \(y_p=Ax+B+Ce^{x}\).

3. Substitute and compare. \(y_p^{\prime\prime}+5y_p^{\prime}+16y_p=16Ax+(5A+16B)+22Ce^{x}\).

\(16A=12\Rightarrow A=\frac34\); \(5A+16B=0\Rightarrow B=-\frac{15}{64}\); \(22C=1\Rightarrow C=\frac1{22}\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=e^{-5x/2}\Big(C_1\cos\tfrac{\sqrt{39}}{2}x\\&\qquad+C_2\sin\tfrac{\sqrt{39}}{2}x\Big)\\&\quad+\tfrac34x-\tfrac{15}{64}+\tfrac1{22}e^{x}\end{aligned}}\]

May 2024 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the second order differential equation: \[y^{\prime\prime}-4y^{\prime}-5y=6x+3.\]

Hint

Type O4.1, polynomial forcing; roots \(5,-1\), no clash. First step: trial \(y_p=Ax+B\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2-4m-5=(m-5)(m+1)\), so \(y_c=C_1e^{5x}+C_2e^{-x}\).

2. Trial. \(y_p=Ax+B\): \(y_p^{\prime}=A\), \(y_p^{\prime\prime}=0\).

3. Compare. \(-4A-5(Ax+B)=6x+3\): \(-5A=6\Rightarrow A=-\frac65\); \(-4A-5B=3\Rightarrow \frac{24}5-5B=3\Rightarrow B=\frac9{25}\).

\[\boxed{y=C_1e^{5x}+C_2e^{-x}-\tfrac65x+\tfrac9{25}}\]

May 2023 Q7(b)5%

Solve the second order differential equation: \[y^{\prime\prime}-y=\cos3x.\]

Hint

Type O4.1, trig forcing; roots \(\pm1\), no clash. First step: trial \(y_p=A\cos3x+B\sin3x\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2-1=0\Rightarrow m=\pm1\), so \(y_c=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{-x}\).

2. Trial. \(y_p=A\cos3x+B\sin3x\); then \(y_p^{\prime\prime}-y_p=-10A\cos3x-10B\sin3x\).

3. Compare. \(-10A=1\), \(-10B=0\): \(A=-\frac1{10}\), \(B=0\).

\[\boxed{y=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{-x}-\tfrac1{10}\cos3x}\]

\(B=0\) because there is no \(y^{\prime}\) term; still write both terms in the trial.

May 2022 Q7(b)5%

Solve the second order differential equation: \[y^{\prime\prime}-4y^{\prime}-5y=\cos(2x).\]

Hint

Type O4.1; roots \(5,-1\). First step: trial \(y_p=A\cos2x+B\sin2x\) and collect the \(\cos2x\) and \(\sin2x\) terms.

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(y_c=C_1e^{5x}+C_2e^{-x}\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=C_1e^{5x}+C_2e^{-x}\\&\quad-\tfrac{9}{145}\cos2x-\tfrac{8}{145}\sin2x\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: most errors are in \(4y^{\prime}\): \(-4(-2A\sin2x)=+8A\sin2x\).

Dec 2020 Q8(a)Q8: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the differential equation: \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-4\dfrac{dy}{dx}+3y=6x+2\sin x\]

Hint

Type O4.1, polynomial plus sine; roots \(1,3\). First step: trial \(y_p=Ax+B+C\cos x+D\sin x\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2-4m+3=(m-1)(m-3)\), so \(y_c=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{3x}\).

2. Polynomial part. \(Ax+B\): \(-4A+3Ax+3B=6x\Rightarrow A=2\), \(3B=8\Rightarrow B=\frac83\).

3. Trig part. \(C\cos x+D\sin x\) gives \((2C-4D)\cos x+(4C+2D)\sin x=2\sin x\). So \(C=2D\), \(10D=2\): \(D=\frac15\), \(C=\frac25\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=C_1e^{x}+C_2e^{3x}+2x+\tfrac83\\&\quad+\tfrac25\cos x+\tfrac15\sin x\end{aligned}}\]

Use \(C\) and \(D\) (or \(\alpha,\beta\)) in the trial so they are not confused with the arbitrary constants \(C_1,C_2\).

May 2020 Q7(b)5 marks

Solve the second order differential equation (\(a\) is a real, positive parameter): \[y^{\prime\prime}-a^2y=\sin 2x.\]

Hint

Type O4.1 with a parameter. Roots \(\pm a\) are real, so \(\sin2x\) never clashes. First step: trial \(y_p=A\cos2x+B\sin2x\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \(m^2-a^2=0\Rightarrow m=\pm a\) (distinct, since \(a\gt0\)), so \(y_c=C_1e^{ax}+C_2e^{-ax}\).

2. Trial. \(y_p=A\cos2x+B\sin2x\): \(y_p^{\prime\prime}-a^2y_p=-(4+a^2)(A\cos2x+B\sin2x)\).

3. Compare. \(A=0\), \(B=-\dfrac{1}{a^2+4}\).

\[\boxed{y=C_1e^{ax}+C_2e^{-ax}-\frac{\sin2x}{a^2+4}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(y_c=K_1\cosh ax+K_2\sinh ax\) is the same family.

May 2016 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following ODE using the method of undetermined coefficients: \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+9y=x+\cos x\sin(2x).\]

Hint

Type O4.1, but the forcing must be rewritten first. First step: product-to-sum, \(\cos x\sin2x=\frac12(\sin3x+\sin x)\); then check each piece against the roots \(\pm3i\).

Solution

1. Rewrite the forcing. \(\sin A\cos B=\frac12[\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)]\) gives \(\cos x\sin2x=\frac12\sin3x+\frac12\sin x\).

2. Complementary function. \(m=\pm3i\), so \(y_c=C_1\cos3x+C_2\sin3x\). The \(\frac12\sin3x\) piece clashes.

3. Trial. \(y_p=(Ax+B)+(C\cos x+D\sin x)+x(E\cos3x+F\sin3x)\).

4. Compare piece by piece. \(9Ax+9B=x\Rightarrow A=\frac19,B=0\). \(8(C\cos x+D\sin x)=\frac12\sin x\Rightarrow C=0,D=\frac1{16}\). For the clash piece, \(y^{\prime\prime}+9y=-6E\sin3x+6F\cos3x=\frac12\sin3x\Rightarrow E=-\frac1{12},F=0\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}y&=C_1\cos3x+C_2\sin3x+\tfrac{x}{9}\\&\quad+\tfrac1{16}\sin x-\tfrac{1}{12}x\cos3x\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: the hidden clash is the point of the question; a trial \(E\cos3x+F\sin3x\) without \(x\) gives \(0=\frac12\sin3x\).

Dec 2015 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find the general solution of the differential equation \[\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}+10\dfrac{dy}{dx}+25y=2\cos x.\]

Hint

Type O4.1; double root \(-5\), but \(\cos x\) does not clash with it. First step: trial \(y_p=A\cos x+B\sin x\).

Solution

1. Complementary function. \((m+5)^2=0\), so \(y_c=(C_1+C_2x)e^{-5x}\).

2. Trial. \((24A+10B)\cos x+(24B-10A)\sin x=2\cos x\).

O4.2Undetermined coefficients with a clash: multiply by x or x²

Chapter note · when the trial clashes · trial-solution table

Frequency: 12 of 13 recent papers (all but May 2022); 8 of 10 older papers. Recent papers often ask two clash parts, or one clash part plus a variation-of-parameters part with a clash (O4.4).

Ready answer · the clash results Beyond the handouts

For \(ay^{\prime\prime}+by^{\prime}+cy=e^{kx}\) with \(P(m)=am^2+bm+c\):

\[\begin{aligned}&P(k)\ne0:\quad y_p=\frac{e^{kx}}{P(k)}\\&k\text{ a simple root}:\quad y_p=\frac{xe^{kx}}{P^{\prime}(k)}=\frac{xe^{kx}}{2ak+b}\\&k\text{ a double root}:\quad y_p=\frac{x^2e^{kx}}{2a}\end{aligned}\]

For \(y^{\prime\prime}+\omega^2y\):

\[\begin{aligned}&=\sin\omega x:\quad y_p=-\frac{x\cos\omega x}{2\omega}\\&=\cos\omega x:\quad y_p=\frac{x\sin\omega x}{2\omega}\end{aligned}\]

Use these to check an answer in seconds. In the exam, still write the trial (with its \(x\) or \(x^2\)) and the substitution; the marks are for that working.

Clash questions asked by another method are elsewhere: variation of parameters with a clash in O4.4 (May 2024 Q8(b), May 2023 Q8(b), Dec 2018 Q8(b)); the D-operator clash May 2018 Q8(a) in O4.6.

O4.4Variation of parameters, constant coefficients

Chapter note · variation of parameters · method in the table

Ready answer · the VoP formulas

Write the equation as \(y^{\prime\prime}+p\,y^{\prime}+q\,y=f(x)\) (coefficient of \(y^{\prime\prime}\) equal to \(1\)). Take two solutions \(y_1,y_2\) of the equation with \(f=0\). Then

\[W=y_1y_2^{\prime}-y_1^{\prime}y_2,\] \[u_1^{\prime}=-\frac{y_2\,f}{W},\qquad u_2^{\prime}=\frac{y_1\,f}{W},\] \[y_p=u_1y_1+u_2y_2.\]

Recent formula sheets print a VoP hint, but learn these anyway: the sign of \(u_1^{\prime}\) and the order inside \(W\) are where marks go.

Ready answer · asked three times

For \(y^{\prime\prime}-(a+b)y^{\prime}+ab\,y=e^{ax}\) with \(a\ne b\): \(y_1=e^{ax}\), \(y_2=e^{bx}\), \(W=(b-a)e^{(a+b)x}\), and

\[u_1=\frac{x}{a-b},\qquad u_2=-\frac{e^{(a-b)x}}{(a-b)^2},\] \[\begin{aligned}y&=C_1e^{ax}+C_2e^{bx}+\frac{x\,e^{ax}}{a-b}.\end{aligned}\]

Swap \(a\) and \(b\) for the \(e^{bx}\) version: \(y_p=\dfrac{x\,e^{bx}}{b-a}\). Numeric cases: May 2024 (\(e^{9x}\), roots 9 and 2) and Dec 2018 (\(e^{2x}\), roots 2 and 7) below.

Frequency: 6 of 13 recent papers (May 2024, Dec 2023, May 2023, May 2022, Dec 2020, Dec 2019); 4 of 10 older papers. Together with O4.5, every recent paper except May 2019 has a VoP part.

2. Wronskian. \(W=e^{x}\cdot6e^{6x}-e^{x}\cdot e^{6x}=5e^{7x}\).

3. Find \(u_1,u_2\) with \(f=e^{2x}\):

\[u_1^{\prime}=-\frac{e^{6x}e^{2x}}{5e^{7x}}=-\frac{e^{x}}{5}\;\Rightarrow\;u_1=-\frac{e^{x}}{5},\] \[u_2^{\prime}=\frac{e^{x}e^{2x}}{5e^{7x}}=\frac{e^{-4x}}{5}\;\Rightarrow\;u_2=-\frac{e^{-4x}}{20}.\]

4. Assemble. \(y_p=-\tfrac15e^{2x}-\tfrac1{20}e^{2x}\).

\[\boxed{y_p=-\frac{e^{2x}}{4}}\]

Check. Trial \(Ae^{2x}\): \((4-14+6)A=1\), \(A=-\frac14\). ✓

O4.5Cauchy–Euler with a right side: VoP or an Axm trial

Chapter note · VoP for Cauchy–Euler · Chapter note · Cauchy–Euler homogeneous · method in the table

Ready answer · every part has this form

All 13 parts are \(x^2y^{\prime\prime}+\alpha xy^{\prime}+\beta y=g(x)\), \(x\gt 0\), and all but one have \(g=x^k\).

  1. Divide by \(x^2\) first. The VoP formulas need the coefficient of \(y^{\prime\prime}\) to be 1, so \(f=\dfrac{g}{x^2}\), e.g. \(g=x^6\Rightarrow f=x^4\). Most papers print a warning about this; it is the trap.
  2. Homogeneous solutions: try \(x^m\): \(m(m-1)+\alpha m+\beta=0\). Distinct roots: \(y_1=x^{m_1}\), \(y_2=x^{m_2}\), \(W=(m_2-m_1)x^{m_1+m_2-1}\). Repeated root: \(y_1=x^{m}\), \(y_2=x^{m}\ln x\), \(W=x^{2m-1}\).
  3. Answer when \(k\) is not a root: \[y_p=\frac{x^k}{(k-m_1)(k-m_2)}.\] This is exactly what the trial \(y_p=Ax^k\) gives, so use it to check your VoP.

Beyond the handouts When \(k\) is a root (VoP still works; these let you check): \(k=m_1\ne m_2\Rightarrow y_p=\dfrac{x^{k}\ln x}{m_1-m_2}\); \(k=m\) a repeated root \(\Rightarrow y_p=\tfrac12x^{m}(\ln x)^2\).

Frequency: 7 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, Dec 2025, May 2025, Dec 2024, Dec 2021, May 2020, Dec 2019); 6 of 10 older papers. Recent papers always use \(g=x^k\) with \(k\) not a root; the clashing cases (\(k\) a root) are all 2013–2017.

O4.6D-operator method

Chapter note · D-operator method · method in the table

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 1 of 10 older papers (May 2018). Low priority: if it comes back, undetermined coefficients (O4.2) gives the same particular solution, but the question may insist on this method.

Ready answer · the two rules used

Write \(D=\dfrac{d}{dx}\), so the equation is \(\varphi(D)\,y=f\) and \(y_p=\dfrac{1}{\varphi(D)}f\).

  • Shift rule: \(\dfrac{1}{\varphi(D)}\big[e^{ax}V\big]=e^{ax}\dfrac{1}{\varphi(D+a)}V\). Use it when \(\varphi(a)=0\).
  • \(\dfrac1D\) means integrate (no constant); \(\dfrac{1}{D-c}\) on a polynomial: expand \(-\dfrac1c\left(1+\dfrac Dc+\dfrac{D^2}{c^2}+\cdots\right)\).

Beyond the handouts Shortcut for a simple clash: if \(\varphi(D)=(D-a)\psi(D)\) with \(\psi(a)\ne0\), then \(\dfrac{1}{\varphi(D)}e^{ax}=\dfrac{x\,e^{ax}}{\psi(a)}\). Use it to check.

Use the 'D operator' method to solve the equation: \[y^{\prime\prime}+2y^{\prime}=3e^{-2x}.\]

Hint

Type O4.6, D-operator method; the right side \(e^{-2x}\) clashes with a homogeneous solution. First step: write the left side as \(D(D+2)y\), find the complementary function, then move \(e^{-2x}\) to the front with the shift rule (\(D\to D-2\)).

Solution

1. Operator form. \((D^2+2D)y=D(D+2)y=3e^{-2x}\).

2. Complementary function. \(m(m+2)=0\), \(m=0,-2\): \(y_c=C_1+C_2e^{-2x}\).

3. Shift rule with \(a=-2\) (since \(\varphi(-2)=0\)), writing \(3e^{-2x}=e^{-2x}\cdot3\):
