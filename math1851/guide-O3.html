MATH1851 Exam Guide · Homogeneous Linear ODEs (constant coefficients, Cauchy–Euler)

The short version

What to expect

This is the most mechanical topic in the course. A homogeneous linear ODE is one with \(0\) on the right-hand side. You guess a trial solution, get a polynomial equation for \(m\), find its roots, and write the answer from a fixed table (the Ready answer below). There are only three kinds of root, and the papers just rotate through them. If you can do each type in the cards below, you are ready.

It is also never optional. Stand-alone parts have not appeared since Dec 2023, but every non-homogeneous question in the O4 guide starts with exactly this step (the "complementary function"). So it must be fast and error-free.

How to practise: try a question, open the Hint only if stuck, check against the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind a method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: a part (a) or (b) of the Part II ODE questions, Q7 or Q8 on the papers up to Dec 2023, part marks are rarely printed (Dec 2023 Q7(b): 5% of the 10%). One part on 5 of 13 recent papers and on 5 of 10 older ones.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. The ODE questions stayed full-working, but the paper is faster-paced, so this routine step needs to be quick.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard derivatives and integrals, a Laplace table and a variation-of-parameters hint. Nothing for this topic: no characteristic equation, no table of root cases, no Cauchy–Euler trial \(y=x^m\). Learn the Ready answer.
  • Notation: papers write both \(\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\) and \(y^{\prime\prime}\). "Complementary function" means the general solution of the homogeneous equation. Cauchy–Euler questions assume \(x\gt 0\) (sometimes printed, sometimes not), so \(\ln x\) is defined.

Types at a glance

Read the form, pick the trial. Counts are question parts, 12 in all (2013–2026); recent = the 13 papers May 2019–May 2026.

O3.1Second order, constant coefficients2 recent · 2 all

Looks like\(ay^{\prime\prime}+by^{\prime}+cy=0\) with plain numbers \(a,b,c\) in front.

DoTry \(y=e^{mx}\), solve \(am^2+bm+c=0\), use the root-case table.

WatchComplex roots \(\alpha\pm\beta i\): \(e^{\alpha x}(c_1\cos\beta x+c_2\sin\beta x)\), real only.

O3.2Higher order, constant coefficients2 recent · 4 all

Looks like\(y^{\prime\prime\prime}\) or \(y^{(4)}\), number coefficients, maybe \(y(0),y^{\prime}(0),y^{\prime\prime}(0)\).

DoTrial \(e^{mx}\); factor (take out \(m\), test \(m=\pm1,\pm2,\dots\)). One constant per root.

WatchRoot \(m=0\) gives a constant; a double root needs an extra \(x\); 3×3 systems for initial conditions.

O3.3Cauchy–Euler2 recent · 6 all

Looks like\(x^2y^{\prime\prime}+axy^{\prime}+by=0\): the power of \(x\) matches the number of dashes.

DoTry \(y=x^m\), solve \(m(m-1)+am+b=0\), use the Cauchy–Euler column.

WatchIt is \(m(m-1)\), not \(m^2\). Repeated root: \(x^m\ln x\). Complex: \(\cos(\beta\ln x)\), not \(\cos\beta x\).

Non-homogeneous versions (right-hand side not \(0\)) are in the O4 guide; Dec 2019 Q5(a) and May 2013 Q7(a) also use O3.1, and Dec 2019 Q5(b) and Dec 2016 Q8(a) also use O3.3, for their complementary functions.

Ready answer · the three root cases

Chapter note · root cases

Ready answer · every question in this guide ends here

Constant coefficients, \(ay^{\prime\prime}+by^{\prime}+cy=0\), trial \(y=e^{mx}\), characteristic equation \(am^2+bm+c=0\):

  • Distinct real roots \(m_1\ne m_2\): \[y=c_1e^{m_1x}+c_2e^{m_2x}\]
  • Repeated root \(m\): \[y=(c_1+c_2x)e^{mx}\]
  • Complex roots \(\alpha\pm\beta i\): \[y=e^{\alpha x}\left(c_1\cos\beta x+c_2\sin\beta x\right)\]

Cauchy–Euler, \(ax^2y^{\prime\prime}+bxy^{\prime}+cy=0\) with \(x\gt 0\), trial \(y=x^m\), indicial equation \(am(m-1)+bm+c=0\):

  • Distinct real roots \(m_1\ne m_2\): \[y=c_1x^{m_1}+c_2x^{m_2}\]
  • Repeated root \(m\): \[y=(c_1+c_2\ln x)\,x^{m}\]
  • Complex roots \(\alpha\pm\beta i\): \[y=x^{\alpha}\left[c_1\cos(\beta\ln x)+c_2\sin(\beta\ln x)\right]\]

Higher order: one term per root. A root repeated \(k\) times gives \(e^{mx},xe^{mx},\dots,x^{k-1}e^{mx}\) (Cauchy–Euler: \(x^m,x^m\ln x,\dots,x^m(\ln x)^{k-1}\)). The answer has as many constants as the order of the equation.

The Cauchy–Euler column is the constant-coefficient column with \(x\) replaced by \(\ln x\): put \(x=e^t\) and the equation becomes constant-coefficient in \(t\).

O3.1Second order, constant coefficients: characteristic equation

Chapter note · constant coefficients · root-case table

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2020); 0 of 10 older papers as a stand-alone part. It is also the first step of every O4 question.

Past-paper questions

Dec 2020 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following differential equation: \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+2\frac{dy}{dx}+5y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.1, second order with constant coefficients. First step: put \(y=e^{mx}\) to get the characteristic equation \(m^2+2m+5=0\), and look at its discriminant to see which root case you are in.

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. With \(y=e^{mx}\): \(m^2+2m+5=0\).

2. Roots. Discriminant \(4-20=-16\lt 0\), so complex:

\[m=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{-16}}{2}=-1\pm 2i.\]

3. Complex case with \(\alpha=-1\), \(\beta=2\):

\[\boxed{y=e^{-x}\left(c_1\cos 2x+c_2\sin 2x\right)}\]

Marker: the expected answer is the real form; do not stop at \(c_1e^{(-1+2i)x}+c_2e^{(-1-2i)x}\).

Equivalent forms. \(Ae^{-x}\cos(2x-\phi)\) with constants \(A,\phi\).

May 2019 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following differential equation: \[\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-6\frac{dy}{dx}+8y=0.\]

Part (b) of this question is under O3.3.

Hint

Type O3.1, second order with constant coefficients. First step: put \(y=e^{mx}\) and factorise the characteristic equation \(m^2-6m+8=0\).

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. With \(y=e^{mx}\): \(m^2-6m+8=0\).

2. Roots. \((m-2)(m-4)=0\), so \(m=2\) or \(m=4\): distinct real.

3. Distinct real case:

\[\boxed{y=c_1e^{2x}+c_2e^{4x}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(e^{2x}\left(c_1+c_2e^{2x}\right)\).

Older papers ask this type only inside non-homogeneous questions: May 2013 Q7(a) (and Dec 2019 Q5(a)) are in the O4 guide.

O3.2Higher order, constant coefficients (with initial conditions)

Chapter note · higher-order equations · root-case table

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2019, Dec 2021); 2 of 10 older papers (Dec 2015, Dec 2017). Every cubic or quartic so far has factorised with small whole-number roots.

Past-paper questions

Dec 2021 Q8(a)Q8: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the differential equation: \[\frac{d^4y}{dx^4}-\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.2, higher order with constant coefficients. First step: put \(y=e^{mx}\) to get \(m^4-m^2=0\), then take out the common factor \(m^2\). Count how many times each root appears.

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. With \(y=e^{mx}\): \(m^4-m^2=0\).

2. Factorise: \(m^2(m-1)(m+1)=0\), so \(m=0\) (twice), \(m=1\), \(m=-1\).

3. Write one term per root. The double root \(0\) gives \(e^{0x}=1\) and \(xe^{0x}=x\); the others give \(e^{x}\) and \(e^{-x}\). Four constants for a fourth-order equation:

\[\boxed{y=c_1+c_2x+c_3e^{x}+c_4e^{-x}}\]

Marker: the double root \(m=0\) must produce both \(c_1\) and \(c_2x\); writing only one constant leaves three, not four.

Equivalent forms. \(c_1+c_2x+A\cosh x+B\sinh x\).

Dec 2019 Q8(b)Q8: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following 3rd order differential equation: \[\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}-3\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+2\frac{dy}{dx}=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.2, higher order with constant coefficients. First step: the characteristic equation \(m^3-3m^2+2m=0\) has no constant term, so take out a factor \(m\) and factorise the quadratic left over.

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. With \(y=e^{mx}\): \(m^3-3m^2+2m=0\).

2. Factorise: \(m(m-1)(m-2)=0\), so \(m=0,1,2\): three distinct real roots.

3. One term per root (\(m=0\) gives the constant \(e^{0x}=1\)):

\[\boxed{y=c_1+c_2e^{x}+c_3e^{2x}}\]

Older format (2013–2018)

Dec 2017 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find the solution of the following differential equation, which satisfies the given initial conditions \(y(0)=1\), \(y^{\prime}(0)=0\), \(y^{\prime\prime}(0)=0\): \[y^{\prime\prime\prime}-4y^{\prime\prime}+7y^{\prime}-6y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.2, higher order with initial conditions. First step: get the characteristic equation \(m^3-4m^2+7m-6=0\) and test small whole numbers that divide \(6\) to find one root; then divide to get a quadratic.

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. \(m^3-4m^2+7m-6=0\). Try \(m=2\): \(8-16+14-6=0\), so \(m-2\) is a factor.

2. Factorise: \((m-2)(m^2-2m+3)=0\). The quadratic has discriminant \(4-12=-8\), so \(m=1\pm\sqrt2\,i\).

3. General solution (one real root, one complex pair):

\[y=c_1e^{2x}+e^{x}\left(c_2\cos\sqrt2x+c_3\sin\sqrt2x\right).\]

4. Initial conditions. Differentiating and putting \(x=0\):

\[\begin{aligned}y(0)&=c_1+c_2=1,\\ y^{\prime}(0)&=2c_1+c_2+\sqrt2c_3=0,\\ y^{\prime\prime}(0)&=4c_1-c_2+2\sqrt2c_3=0.\end{aligned}\]

5. Solve. Put \(c_2=1-c_1\) into the other two: \(c_1+\sqrt2c_3=-1\) and \(5c_1+2\sqrt2c_3=1\). Subtract twice the first from the second: \(3c_1=3\). So \(c_1=1\), \(c_2=0\), \(c_3=-\sqrt2\).

\[\boxed{y=e^{2x}-\sqrt2\,e^{x}\sin\sqrt2x}\]

Check. \(y(0)=1\); \(y^{\prime}(0)=2-\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=0\); \(y^{\prime\prime}(0)=4-2\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2=0\). ✓

Marker: show the general solution first, then the three equations for the constants, then the values.

Equivalent forms. \(e^{x}\left(e^{x}-\sqrt2\sin\sqrt2x\right)\).

Dec 2015 Q8(a)Q8: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the differential equation \[\frac{d^3y}{dx^3}-\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-49\frac{dy}{dx}+49y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.2, higher order with constant coefficients. First step: in \(m^3-m^2-49m+49=0\), group the first two terms and the last two terms and take out common factors.

Solution

1. Characteristic equation. \(m^3-m^2-49m+49=0\).

2. Factorise by grouping: \(m^2(m-1)-49(m-1)=(m-1)(m-7)(m+7)=0\), so \(m=1,7,-7\).

3. One term per root:

\[\boxed{y=c_1e^{x}+c_2e^{7x}+c_3e^{-7x}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(c_1e^{x}+A\cosh 7x+B\sinh 7x\).

O3.3Cauchy–Euler equations: try y = xm

Chapter note · Cauchy–Euler · root-case table

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2023); 4 of 10 older papers (May 2014, Dec 2015, May 2016, Dec 2018). All three root cases have been asked, plus one third-order question with initial conditions.

Ready answer · the indicial equation

With \(y=x^m\): \(xy^{\prime}=mx^m\), \(x^2y^{\prime\prime}=m(m-1)x^m\), \(x^3y^{\prime\prime\prime}=m(m-1)(m-2)x^m\). So

\[\begin{aligned}&x^2y^{\prime\prime}+axy^{\prime}+by=0\\&\quad\Longrightarrow\ m^2+(a-1)m+b=0.\end{aligned}\]

Every second-order paper question had leading coefficient \(1\) except May 2016 (\(4x^2\)): multiply the whole \(m(m-1)\) term by it.

Past-paper questions

Dec 2023 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; this part 5%

Find the general solution to the differential equation: \[x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-7x\frac{dy}{dx}+16y=0\qquad(x\gt 0).\]

Hint

Type O3.3, Cauchy–Euler. First step: put \(y=x^m\) to get \(m(m-1)-7m+16=0\), simplify, and see whether the quadratic is a perfect square.

Solution

1. Indicial equation. With \(y=x^m\): \(m(m-1)-7m+16=0\), i.e. \(m^2-8m+16=0\).

2. Roots. \((m-4)^2=0\): repeated root \(m=4\).

3. Repeated case (Cauchy–Euler, so \(\ln x\), not \(x\)):

\[\boxed{y=x^4\left(c_1+c_2\ln x\right)}\]

Marker: \(c_1x^4+c_2x\cdot x^4\) is the constant-coefficient pattern and is wrong here.

Equivalent forms. \(c_1x^4+c_2x^4\ln x\).

May 2019 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Find the general solution to the following Cauchy-Euler equation: \[x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-7x\frac{dy}{dx}+7y=0.\]

Part (a) of this question is under O3.1.

Hint

Type O3.3, Cauchy–Euler. First step: put \(y=x^m\), so \(x^2y^{\prime\prime}=m(m-1)x^m\) and \(xy^{\prime}=mx^m\); collect into a quadratic in \(m\) and factorise.

Solution

1. Indicial equation. \(m(m-1)-7m+7=0\), i.e. \(m^2-8m+7=0\).

2. Roots. \((m-1)(m-7)=0\), so \(m=1\) or \(m=7\): distinct real.

3. Distinct real case (\(x\gt 0\)):

\[\boxed{y=c_1x+c_2x^{7}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(x\left(c_1+c_2x^6\right)\).

Older format (2013–2018)

Dec 2018 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find the solution of the following Cauchy-Euler equation, which satisfies the given conditions \(y(1)=1\), \(y^{\prime}(1)=-7\), \(y^{\prime\prime}(1)=44\): \[x^3y^{\prime\prime\prime}+7x^2y^{\prime\prime}-2xy^{\prime}-10y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.3, third-order Cauchy–Euler with conditions at \(x=1\). First step: put \(y=x^m\), using \(x^3y^{\prime\prime\prime}=m(m-1)(m-2)x^m\); expand to a cubic in \(m\) and test small whole numbers that divide \(10\).

Solution

1. Indicial equation. \(m(m-1)(m-2)+7m(m-1)-2m-10=0\), which expands to

\[m^3+4m^2-7m-10=0.\]

2. Factorise. \(m=2\) works (\(8+16-14-10=0\)), and \(m=-1\) works (\(-1+4+7-10=0\)). So \((m-2)(m+1)(m+5)=0\): \(m=2,-1,-5\).

3. General solution (\(x\gt 0\)):

\[y=c_1x^2+c_2x^{-1}+c_3x^{-5}.\]

4. Conditions at \(x=1\) (every power of \(x\) equals \(1\) there):

\[\begin{aligned}y(1)&=c_1+c_2+c_3=1,\\ y^{\prime}(1)&=2c_1-c_2-5c_3=-7,\\ y^{\prime\prime}(1)&=2c_1+2c_2+30c_3=44.\end{aligned}\]

5. Solve. Third minus twice the first: \(28c_3=42\), so \(c_3=\frac32\). First plus second: \(3c_1-4c_3=-6\), so \(c_1=0\). Then \(c_2=1-0-\frac32=-\frac12\).

\[\boxed{y=-\frac{1}{2x}+\frac{3}{2x^{5}}}\]

Check. \(y(1)=-\frac12+\frac32=1\); \(y^{\prime}(1)=\frac12-\frac{15}2=-7\); \(y^{\prime\prime}(1)=-1+45=44\). ✓

Marker: show the indicial cubic and its factors, then the general solution, before fitting the constants.

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{3-x^4}{2x^5}\).

May 2016 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Cauchy-Euler equation: \[4x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}+16x\frac{dy}{dx}+9y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.3, Cauchy–Euler with leading coefficient \(4\). First step: put \(y=x^m\) to get \(4m(m-1)+16m+9=0\), expand, and check for a perfect square.

Solution

1. Indicial equation. \(4m(m-1)+16m+9=0\), i.e. \(4m^2+12m+9=0\).

2. Roots. \((2m+3)^2=0\): repeated root \(m=-\frac32\).

3. Repeated case (\(x\gt 0\)):

\[\boxed{y=x^{-3/2}\left(c_1+c_2\ln x\right)}\]

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{c_1+c_2\ln x}{x\sqrt{x}}\).

Dec 2015 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Cauchy-Euler equation \[x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-17x\frac{dy}{dx}+41y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.3, Cauchy–Euler. First step: put \(y=x^m\) to get a quadratic in \(m\); it does not factorise, so use the quadratic formula and check the sign of the discriminant.

Solution

1. Indicial equation. \(m(m-1)-17m+41=0\), i.e. \(m^2-18m+41=0\).

2. Roots. Discriminant \(324-164=160\gt 0\), so two distinct real (irrational) roots:

\[m=\frac{18\pm\sqrt{160}}{2}=9\pm2\sqrt{10}.\]

3. Distinct real case (\(x\gt 0\)):

\[\boxed{y=c_1x^{9+2\sqrt{10}}+c_2x^{9-2\sqrt{10}}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(x^{9}\left(c_1x^{2\sqrt{10}}+c_2x^{-2\sqrt{10}}\right)\).

May 2014 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Cauchy-Euler equation \[x^2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}-5x\frac{dy}{dx}+10y=0.\]

Hint

Type O3.3, Cauchy–Euler. First step: put \(y=x^m\) to get \(m^2-6m+10=0\) and find its roots with the quadratic formula.

Solution

1. Indicial equation. \(m(m-1)-5m+10=0\), i.e. \(m^2-6m+10=0\).

2. Roots. Discriminant \(36-40=-4\lt 0\), so \(m=3\pm i\): complex with \(\alpha=3\), \(\beta=1\).

3. Complex case (\(x\gt 0\)):

\[\boxed{y=x^{3}\left[c_1\cos(\ln x)+c_2\sin(\ln x)\right]}\]

Marker: the argument is \(\ln x\), not \(x\); \(x^3(c_1\cos x+c_2\sin x)\) is wrong.

Equivalent forms. \(Ax^3\cos(\ln x-\phi)\).

Cauchy–Euler with a non-zero right-hand side (Dec 2019 Q5(b), Dec 2016 Q8(a)) is in the O4 guide (O4.5).
