MATH1851 Exam Guide · Substitution and Integration by Parts

The short version

What to expect

Substitution or integration by parts is asked in all 13 recent papers (and 5 of the 10 older ones). It usually comes as two or three parts of the "Evaluate each of the following integrals" question, and it is also the last step of many Riemann-sum, area, volume and ODE questions. The integrands change every time, so there are no answers to memorise. What does not change is the decision: every integral here is one of four types, and each type below comes with a standard procedure, numbered steps of the form "if you see this, do that; if that fails, do this".

How to practise: first use Start here to read the integrand and find the type. Then work the question with the type's procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end. Always finish by differentiating your answer: if you get the integrand back, it is right. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind the methods, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Q3 "Evaluate each of the following integrals, showing all the reasoning" (10–16%, 3–5% per part) on May 2020, May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023 and Dec 2024; inside Q4 or Q5 on May 2019, Dec 2019, May 2024 and May 2025; Dec 2020 Q2 and Dec 2019 Q3 with a graph and a table. The same methods finish many Riemann-sum limits (L12 guide), area and volume questions (L16 guide) and integrating-factor ODEs (O1 guide).
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice (MC8 here), and May 2026 made Q3 answers only ("Evaluate the following integrals using any method", five integrals). Only the final answer scores there, so a lost minus sign or a missing \(+C\) costs the part: differentiate to check. On written papers the stem says "showing all the reasoning": write the substitution, the \(du\) line and the new limits, because those lines carry the marks.
  • Formula sheet: Dec 2025 prints \(\int\sec x\,dx\), \(\int\tan x\,dx\) and the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\), \(\tan^{-1}x\); May 2026 prints no derivative or integral formulas at all. No paper prints the integration-by-parts formula, the LIATE order, \(\int\ln x\,dx\) or \(\int\frac{dx}{ax+b}\). Learn \(\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\) and \(\int\ln x\,dx=x\ln x-x+C\).
  • Wording to watch: "Use the substitution \(x=\frac\pi4-u\)" or "Use the substitution \(t=\dots\)" means use that one (May 2025 Q5). "Write down the appropriate substitutions (No need to carry out the integration)" (Dec 2018) wants only the substitution. Papers mix notations: \(\exp(x)=e^x\), \(\arctan x=\tan^{-1}x\), \(\sin^{-1}x=\arcsin x\).

L13.1Substitution: let u be the inside, change the limits

Chapter note · substitution · Chapter note · substitution in definite integrals

Frequency: 10 of 13 recent papers (every one except Dec 2021, Dec 2023, Dec 2025); 4 of 10 older papers. The most common L13 type: 16 parts.

Recognise it

Some "inside" expression \(g(x)\) appears together with its derivative \(g'(x)\) (up to a constant factor): \(x^2\) next to \(x^3\), \(\frac1x\) next to \(\ln x\), \(\sin x\) next to \(\cos x\), \(e^x\) next to \(e^x\). Or a root \(\sqrt x\) sits inside another function, or the question names the substitution.

Standard procedure
  1. Tidy. Split sums into separate integrals; write \(\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\); write \(\sinh,\cosh\) in exponentials; simplify fractions of exponentials.
  2. Choose \(u\).
    • 2a. An inside \(g(x)\) whose derivative is also a factor (or the inside is linear, \(ax+b\)): \(u=g(x)\). Asked: May 2022, May 2023, May 2019, Dec 2019, May 2020, May 2024, Dec 2024, and 4 older papers.
    • 2b. A root with nothing to pair it with (\(\sin^{-1}\sqrt x\), \(\frac{\tan^{-1}\sqrt x}{\sqrt x}\)), or the question prints a substitution: \(u=\) the root (so \(x=u^2\)), or use exactly the printed one. Asked: May 2026, May 2025, May 2020.
    • 2c. \(h(g(t))\,g'(t)\) with \(h\) known only from a graph or a table: \(u=g(t)\), the inside of \(h\). Asked: Dec 2019, Dec 2020.
    • If the question only asks for the substitution, write \(u=\dots\) and \(du=\dots\) and stop. Asked: Dec 2018.
  3. Write \(du=g'(x)\,dx\) and replace every \(x\) and \(dx\). Keep the constant: \(u=3-2x\) gives \(dx=-\frac12du\). If an \(x\) is left that you cannot write in terms of \(u\), go back to step 2: wrong \(u\).
  4. Definite integral: change the limits at once (\(x=a\Rightarrow u=g(a)\)) and never go back to \(x\).
  5. Integrate in \(u\).
    • 5a. A standard integral (\(u^n\), \(\frac1u\), \(e^u\), \(\sin u\)). Done. Asked: most parts; see the coverage table.
    • 5b. Still a product, or \(\sin^{-1}u\), \(\tan^{-1}u\) on its own: integrate by parts (L13.2). Asked: May 2026, Dec 2024, May 2020, May 2018.
    • 5c. \(\int_p^q h(u)\,du\) for a graph: add the areas above the axis and subtract those below. Asked: Dec 2019, Dec 2020.
  6. Indefinite integral: put \(x\) back, add \(+C\), keep \(|\dots|\) inside \(\ln\). Differentiate your answer to check.
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2019 Q4(b) (Q4: 10%). Let \(g(x)=4\ln x+\dfrac1{\ln x}\). (Assume \(g\) is defined on its natural domain.) Evaluate

\[\int_2^4\frac{g(x)}{x}\,dx.\]

Step 1 Write the integrand out: \(\dfrac{g(x)}{x}=\dfrac{4\ln x}{x}+\dfrac{1}{x\ln x}\).

Seeing the whole integrand shows that \(\ln x\) and \(\frac1x\) appear together in both terms.

Step 2a The inside is \(\ln x\) and its derivative \(\frac1x\) is a factor: put \(u=\ln x\).

This is the "inside and its derivative" pattern. One substitution handles both terms.

Step 3 \(du=\dfrac1x\,dx\). The integrand becomes \(\left(4u+\dfrac1u\right)du\): no \(x\) left.

Step 4 New limits: \(x=2\Rightarrow u=\ln2\); \(x=4\Rightarrow u=\ln4=2\ln2\).

The marker looks for the new limits written next to the substitution. Changing them now means you never have to go back to \(x\).

Step 5a Integrate:

\[\begin{aligned}&\int_{\ln2}^{2\ln2}\Big(4u+\frac1u\Big)du\\&=\Big[2u^2+\ln u\Big]_{\ln2}^{2\ln2}\\&=2(4-1)(\ln2)^2+\ln\frac{2\ln2}{\ln2}\\&=6(\ln2)^2+\ln2.\end{aligned}\]

\(u\gt0\) on \([\ln2,2\ln2]\), so \(\ln|u|=\ln u\). Simplify \(\ln(2\ln2)-\ln(\ln2)\) to \(\ln2\).

\[\boxed{6(\ln2)^2+\ln2}\]

Common slip: keeping the old limits 2 and 4 with the new variable \(u\).

Traps
  • Limits. In a definite integral, change the limits with the substitution (May 2019 Q4(b), Dec 2019 Q3(d), May 2015 Q3(a)(i)). With \(u=\frac1x\) the new limits come out upside down: keep them, then flip using the minus sign.
  • Lost constants. \(u=3-2x\) gives \(-\frac12\) (Dec 2019 Q4(a)); \(u=2x^3+2\) gives \(\frac16\) (May 2022 Q3(b)); \(u=2t^2-5\) gives \(\frac14\) (Dec 2019 Q3(d)).
  • Signed areas. In Dec 2019 Q3(d) and Dec 2020 Q2(b) the areas are given without sign. A region below the axis counts negative in the integral.
Ready answer · two forms that recur \[\begin{aligned}\int\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx&=\ln|f(x)|+C,\\\int F'(ax+b)\,dx&=\frac1aF(ax+b)+C.\end{aligned}\]

The first is substitution \(u=f(x)\) done once and for all: May 2023 Q3(b) (\(f=1-\cos x\)), May 2020 Q3(a) (\(\tan x=-\frac{(\cos x)'}{\cos x}\)), May 2024 Q4(b) (\(f=2-e^{-2x}\)). The second is any linear inside: Dec 2019 Q4(a). On written papers still show \(u\) and \(du\) once.

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q3(e)Q3: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Evaluate the following integral using any method. \[\int_0^1\sin^{-1}\sqrt x\,dx\]

Parts (a)–(c) of this question are in the L14 guide; part (d) is under L13.4. This part is also listed under L13.2.

Hint

Type L13.1 then L13.2, a root inside \(\sin^{-1}\) (step 2b). First step: let \(\theta=\sin^{-1}\sqrt x\), i.e. \(x=\sin^2\theta\), and find \(dx\) and the new limits.

Solution

Step 2b–3 Put \(x=\sin^2\theta\), \(0\le\theta\le\frac\pi2\). Then \(\sin^{-1}\sqrt x=\theta\) and \(dx=2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta=\sin2\theta\,d\theta\).

Step 4 \(x=0\Rightarrow\theta=0\); \(x=1\Rightarrow\theta=\frac\pi2\).

\[\int_0^1\sin^{-1}\sqrt x\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/2}\theta\sin2\theta\,d\theta.\]

Step 5b By parts with \(u=\theta\), \(dv=\sin2\theta\,d\theta\), \(v=-\frac12\cos2\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}&\Big[-\tfrac12\theta\cos2\theta\Big]_0^{\pi/2}+\tfrac12\int_0^{\pi/2}\cos2\theta\,d\theta\\&=\tfrac\pi4+\Big[\tfrac14\sin2\theta\Big]_0^{\pi/2}=\tfrac\pi4+0.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\dfrac\pi4}\]

Marker: answers only; \(\frac\pi4\approx0.785\) is all that is written.

Equivalent forms. \(0.785\ldots\). Other route: \(u=\sqrt x\) gives \(\int_0^12u\sin^{-1}u\,du\); by parts this needs \(\int_0^1\frac{u^2}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}du=\frac\pi4\) (a trig substitution, L14), and gives the same \(\frac\pi2-\frac\pi4\).

May 2025 Q5(a)Q5: 8%; part marks not shown

Use the substitution \(t=\sqrt{\dfrac{x+1}{x+2}}\) to evaluate \[\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x+1)(x+2)}}.\]

Part (b) is under L13.4.

Hint

Type L13.1, printed substitution (step 2b). First step: square, \(t^2=\frac{x+1}{x+2}\), solve for \(x\) in terms of \(t\), then write \(x+1\), \(x+2\) and \(dx\) in terms of \(t\).

Solution

The integrand needs \((x+1)(x+2)\gt0\): \(x\gt-1\) or \(x\lt-2\). The working below is for \(x\gt-1\), where \(0\lt t\lt1\).

Step 2b \(t^2(x+2)=x+1\), so

\[x=\frac{2t^2-1}{1-t^2}=-2+\frac1{1-t^2}.\]

Step 3 Then \(x+2=\dfrac1{1-t^2}\), \(x+1=\dfrac{t^2}{1-t^2}\), \(\sqrt{(x+1)(x+2)}=\dfrac{t}{1-t^2}\), and \(dx=\dfrac{2t}{(1-t^2)^2}\,dt\). So

\[\begin{aligned}&\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{(x+1)(x+2)}}\\&=\int\frac{2t}{(1-t^2)^2}\cdot\frac{1-t^2}{t}\,dt\\&=\int\frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2}.\end{aligned}\]

Step 5a Partial fractions \(\dfrac2{1-t^2}=\dfrac1{1+t}+\dfrac1{1-t}\):

\[\begin{aligned}\int\frac{2\,dt}{1-t^2}&=\ln(1+t)-\ln(1-t)+C\\&=\ln\frac{1+t}{1-t}+C.\end{aligned}\]

Step 6 Back to \(x\), with \(t=\sqrt{\frac{x+1}{x+2}}\).

\[\boxed{\ln\frac{1+t}{1-t}+C,\quad t=\sqrt{\frac{x+1}{x+2}}}\]

Marker: the question names \(t\), so the \(dx\)-in-\(t\) line must be shown; another method would not follow the instruction.

Equivalent forms. Since \(\frac{1+t}{1-t}=\frac{(1+t)^2}{1-t^2}=(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x+2})^2\), the answer is \(2\ln\!\big(\sqrt{x+1}+\sqrt{x+2}\big)+C\). For \(x\lt-2\) (where \(t\gt1\)) the same steps give \(\ln\frac{t-1}{t+1}+C=-2\ln\!\big(\sqrt{-x-1}+\sqrt{-x-2}\big)+C\).

Dec 2024 Q3(b)5%

Compute \[\int x\left(\frac{1}{(1-x^2)^{3/2}}+\ln(x+1)\right)dx.\]

Part (a) is in the L14 guide. This part is also listed under L13.2.

Hint

Type L13.1 + L13.2. First step: expand into two integrals. The first has the inside \(1-x^2\) with its derivative \(-2x\) (substitution); the second is \(x\ln(x+1)\) (by parts, \(u=\ln(x+1)\)).

Solution

Step 1 Split: \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x\,dx}{(1-x^2)^{3/2}}+\int x\ln(x+1)\,dx\), for \(-1\lt x\lt1\).

Step 2a–5a First integral: \(u=1-x^2\), \(du=-2x\,dx\):

\[-\frac12\int u^{-3/2}\,du=u^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.\]

Step 5b Second integral, by parts with \(u=\ln(x+1)\), \(dv=x\,dx\). Choose \(v=\frac{x^2-1}{2}\) (any antiderivative of \(x\) works; this one cancels \(x+1\)):

\[\begin{aligned}&\int x\ln(x+1)\,dx\\&=\frac{x^2-1}2\ln(x+1)-\int\frac{x^2-1}{2(x+1)}\,dx\\&=\frac{x^2-1}2\ln(x+1)-\int\frac{x-1}{2}\,dx\\&=\frac{x^2-1}2\ln(x+1)-\frac{x^2}4+\frac x2.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}+\frac{x^2-1}{2}\ln(x+1)\\&\quad-\frac{x^2}4+\frac x2+C\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: with \(v=\frac{x^2}2\) instead, you meet \(\int\frac{x^2}{x+1}dx\) and must divide first (\(x-1+\frac1{x+1}\)); the answer is the same.

Equivalent forms. \((1-x^2)^{-1/2}+\frac{x^2}2\ln(x+1)-\frac12\ln(x+1)-\frac{x^2}4+\frac x2+C\). Checked by differentiating (sympy).

May 2024 Q4(b)Q4: 10%; part marks not shown

Evaluate \[\int_0^1\frac{e^{-x}}{\cosh x+3\sinh x}\,dx.\] [Hint: Use the definition of \(\sinh x\) and \(\cosh x\).]

Part (a) is under L13.2; part (c), a Riemann sum, is in the L12 guide.

Hint

Type L13.1, after tidying (step 1). First step: replace \(\cosh x\) and \(\sinh x\) by exponentials and simplify the integrand to one fraction in \(e^{2x}\).

Solution

Beyond the handouts The hyperbolic functions are defined by \(\sinh x=\dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}2\) ("hyperbolic sine") and \(\cosh x=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}2\) ("hyperbolic cosine"). They are not in the calculus handouts; the question's hint says to use these definitions.

Step 1 \(\cosh x+3\sinh x=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}+3e^x-3e^{-x}}2=2e^x-e^{-x}\). Multiply top and bottom by \(e^{-x}\):

\[\frac{e^{-x}}{2e^x-e^{-x}}=\frac{e^{-2x}}{2-e^{-2x}}.\]

Step 2a–3 \(u=2-e^{-2x}\), \(du=2e^{-2x}\,dx\): the top is half of \(du\).

Step 4 \(x=0\Rightarrow u=1\); \(x=1\Rightarrow u=2-e^{-2}\).

Step 5a

\[\frac12\int_1^{2-e^{-2}}\frac{du}{u}=\frac12\ln\!\big(2-e^{-2}\big)-\frac12\ln1.\]
\[\boxed{\tfrac12\ln\!\big(2-e^{-2}\big)}\]

Equivalent forms. \(\frac12\ln\frac{2e^2-1}{e^2}=\frac12\ln(2e^2-1)-1\approx0.3115\).

May 2020 Q3(d)4 marks (Q3: 16%)

Evaluate the following integral, showing all reasoning. \[\int\frac{\arctan\sqrt x}{\sqrt x}\,dx\]

Also listed under L13.2.

Hint

Type L13.1 then L13.2. First step: \(u=\sqrt x\); note that \(\frac{dx}{\sqrt x}\) is exactly \(2\,du\).

Solution

Step 2b–3 \(u=\sqrt x\), \(du=\dfrac{dx}{2\sqrt x}\), so \(\dfrac{dx}{\sqrt x}=2\,du\):

\[\int\frac{\arctan\sqrt x}{\sqrt x}\,dx=2\int\arctan u\,du.\]

Step 5b By parts with \(dv=du\), \(v=u\):

\[\begin{aligned}&2\int\arctan u\,du\\&=2u\arctan u-2\int\frac{u}{1+u^2}\,du\\&=2u\arctan u-\ln(1+u^2)+C.\end{aligned}\]

Step 6 Put \(u=\sqrt x\) back.

\[\boxed{2\sqrt x\arctan\sqrt x-\ln(1+x)+C}\]

Equivalent forms. \(2\sqrt x\tan^{-1}\sqrt x-\ln(1+x)+C\). No absolute value is needed: \(1+x\gt0\).

L13.2Integration by parts: choose u by LIATE

Chapter note · integration by parts · Chapter note · repeated parts, ln and inverse trig

Frequency: 7 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, Dec 2024, May 2024, Dec 2023, May 2023, Dec 2021, May 2020); 2 of 10 older papers. 6 parts are by parts only; 4 more start with a substitution and are solved under L13.1.

Recognise it

A product of two different kinds of function where substitution fails (the \(x\) in \(xe^{x}\) is not the derivative of the exponent), or a lone \(\ln(\dots)\), \((\ln x)^n\), \(\sin^{-1}\), \(\tan^{-1}\) that you cannot integrate directly.

Standard procedure

The rule: \(\displaystyle\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\); for limits, \(\displaystyle\int_a^bu\,dv=\Big[uv\Big]_a^b-\int_a^bv\,du\). It is not on the formula sheet.

  1. Try substitution first. If an inside and its derivative are both there, use L13.1 instead: \(xe^{-x^2}\) is substitution, \(xe^{-x}\) is by parts.
  2. Choose \(u\) by LIATE: Logarithm, Inverse trig, Algebraic (powers of \(x\)), Trig, Exponential. \(u\) is the factor that comes first in this list; \(dv\) is everything else. A lone \(\ln\) or inverse-trig function: \(u=\) it, \(dv=dx\).
  3. Find \(du\) and \(v\). Any antiderivative works for \(v\); pick the constant that helps (\(v=x+A\) for \(\ln(x+A)\), \(v=\frac{x^2-1}2\) for \(x\ln(x+1)\)).
  4. Write \(uv-\int v\,du\), with the limits on \(uv\) as well for a definite integral. Keep the minus sign in brackets when \(v\) is negative (\(v=-\frac1x\), \(v=-e^{-x}\)).
  5. Look at the new integral \(\int v\,du\).
    • 5a. It is standard: integrate and finish. Asked: May 2024, Dec 2021, May 2020, May 2017, and the second step of May 2026, Dec 2024, May 2020 (d), May 2018.
    • 5b. It is the same kind with the power one lower (\((\ln x)^3\to(\ln x)^2\), \(x^2e^x\to xe^x\)): repeat steps 2–4 on it until the power is gone. Asked: Dec 2023, May 2023.
    • 5c. It is the original integral again (e.g. \(e^x\sin x\) after two rounds): move it to the left side and solve for it. Never asked on these papers.
  6. Finish. Evaluate \(uv\) at both limits (or add \(+C\)), simplify, and differentiate your answer to check.

Dec 2023 Q3(a), 4%. Evaluate the following integral, showing all the reasoning.

\[\int_e^{e^2}(\ln x)^3\,dx\]

Step 2 A lone power of \(\ln x\): \(u=(\ln x)^3\), \(dv=dx\).

L comes first in LIATE. Differentiating lowers the power of \(\ln x\); integrating \(dx\) costs nothing.

Step 3 \(du=\dfrac{3(\ln x)^2}{x}\,dx\), \(v=x\).

Step 4 \(\displaystyle\int(\ln x)^3dx=x(\ln x)^3-3\int(\ln x)^2\,dx\).

The \(x\) from \(v\) cancels the \(\frac1x\) from \(du\): that is why this choice works.

Step 5b Same kind, power 2. Repeat twice (\(u=(\ln x)^2\), then \(u=\ln x\), each with \(dv=dx\)):

\[\begin{aligned}\int(\ln x)^2dx&=x(\ln x)^2-2\int\ln x\,dx\\&=x(\ln x)^2-2x\ln x+2x.\end{aligned}\]

So

\[\begin{aligned}&\int(\ln x)^3dx\\&=x\big[(\ln x)^3-3(\ln x)^2+6\ln x-6\big]+C.\end{aligned}\]

Write each round on its own line; the marker gives credit round by round.

Step 6 At \(x=e^2\) (\(\ln x=2\)): \(e^2(8-12+12-6)=2e^2\). At \(x=e\) (\(\ln x=1\)): \(e(1-3+6-6)=-2e\).

\[2e^2-(-2e)=2e^2+2e.\]
\[\boxed{2e^2+2e}\]

Common slip: the lower limit gives a negative value, so subtracting it adds \(2e\).

L13.3By parts with an unknown function, and reduction formulas

Chapter note · by parts with an unknown function · Chapter note · reduction formulas

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2025, Dec 2020, Dec 2019); 3 of 10 older papers (May 2018, Dec 2016, May 2015).

Recognise it

You cannot find an antiderivative because the function is unknown: only some values \(f(0)\), \(f(1)\), \(f'(0)\), …, a graph, or a relation like \(f''=3ff'\) are given. Or the integral carries a letter \(n\) in a power, \(I_n=\int(\dots)^n\,dx\), and you are asked for a formula linking \(I_n\) and \(I_{n-1}\).

Standard procedure
  1. List what you know: the given values of \(f,f'\) (at which points), any relation between \(f,f',f''\), or the definition of \(I_n\). The answer can only use these.
  2. Choose \(dv\) and \(u\). Unknown function: \(dv=\) its highest derivative (\(f''(x)\,dx\), \(m'(t)\,dt\)), because integrating just removes a dash; \(u=\) the known factor (\(x\), \(t\), \(1-u\)). Reduction formula: \(u=\) the factor carrying the power \(n\), \(dv=\) the rest (often just \(dx\)).
  3. Integrate by parts. Work out the boundary term \(\big[uv\big]_a^b\) with the given values.
  4. Deal with the integral that is left.
    • 4a. It is \(\int_a^bf'(x)\,dx\): it equals \(f(b)-f(a)\) (Fundamental Theorem of Calculus), or \(\int_a^bm\) from signed areas. Asked: Dec 2025, Dec 2020.
    • 4b. It contains a higher derivative you are given a relation for: substitute the relation, then use \(\int f^kf'\,dx=\frac{f^{k+1}}{k+1}\). Asked: May 2018.
    • 4c. "Show that" an identity: transform each side separately by parts until both read the same. Asked: Dec 2019.
    • 4d. Reduction formula: rewrite the new integrand with an identity that brings back the original (\(x^2=(x^2+9)-9\), \(x=1-(1-x)\)), split into \(I_n\) and \(I_{n-1}\), and solve for \(I_n\). Asked: Dec 2016, May 2015.
  5. Using a reduction formula: apply it again and again down to \(I_0\), which you can integrate; the product of factors usually cancels (telescopes). Asked: May 2015 (ii).
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2025 MC8, 2 pts. Let \(f(x)\) be a twice-differentiable function with \(f(0)=2\), \(f(1)=3\), \(f'(0)=-1\), \(f'(1)=4\). Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^1x\,f''(x)\,dx\).
(A) \(1\) (B) \(2\) (C) \(3\) (D) \(4\) (E) Not enough information given

Step 1 Known: \(f\) and \(f'\) at \(0\) and \(1\). Nothing about \(f''\) itself.

So the \(f''\) must disappear: by parts is the only way to turn it into \(f'\) and \(f\).

Step 2 \(dv=f''(x)\,dx\), \(v=f'(x)\); \(u=x\), \(du=dx\).

The other way round (\(dv=x\,dx\)) would give \(\int\frac{x^2}2f'''(x)\,dx\): even less known.

Step 3

\[\begin{aligned}&\int_0^1xf''(x)\,dx\\&=\Big[xf'(x)\Big]_0^1-\int_0^1f'(x)\,dx\\&=1\cdot4-0\cdot(-1)-\int_0^1f'(x)\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

Step 4a \(\displaystyle\int_0^1f'(x)\,dx=f(1)-f(0)=3-2=1\). So the integral is \(4-1=3\).

This is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: \(f\) is an antiderivative of \(f'\).

\[\boxed{3\quad\text{(C)}}\]

Common slip: choosing (E). The data are exactly enough once you integrate by parts.

Traps
  • Wrong \(dv\). Integrating the known factor (\(x\), \(t\)) instead of the unknown derivative leaves a higher derivative you know nothing about (Dec 2025 MC8, Dec 2020 Q2(b)(1)).
  • Boundary terms. Check whether \(\big[uv\big]\) vanishes and say why: in May 2015 Q3(b) it is \(0\) only because \(n\ge1\); in May 2018 Q5(b)(ii) \(f(0)^{n+1}=0\) because \(n+1\ge1\).
  • Reduction formulas. After splitting, \(I_n\) appears on both sides. Collect it before dividing: Dec 2016 Q2(c) gives \((2n+1)I_n=\dots\), hence the condition \(n\ne-\frac12\).

\[\begin{aligned}&\int_0^xuf'(u)\,du\\&=\Big[uf(u)\Big]_0^x-\int_0^xf(u)\,du\\&=xf(x)-\int_0^xf(u)\,du.\end{aligned}\]

So the right side is

\[\begin{aligned}&\int_0^1f-\int_0^xf+xf(x)\\&=xf(x)+\int_x^1f(u)\,du.\end{aligned}\]

Step 4c Both sides equal \(xf(x)+\displaystyle\int_x^1f(u)\,du\).

\[\boxed{\text{LHS}=xf(x)+\int_x^1f(u)\,du=\text{RHS}}\]

Marker: say why by parts is allowed: \(f'\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), so \(f\) and \(f'\) are integrable there.

Check. Tested in sympy with \(f(x)=e^x\sin3x\) and \(f(x)=x^5-2x\): both sides agree.

L13.4The x → a − x symmetry trick

Chapter note · the x → a − x substitution

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, May 2025); never on the older papers. New, and both times the paper gave the substitution or the identity.

Recognise it

A definite integral \(\int_0^a\) of something with no antiderivative you know (\(\ln(1+\tan x)\), \(\ln\sin x\)), and the question prints the substitution \(x=a-u\) or the identity \(\int_0^af(x)\,dx=\int_0^af(a-x)\,dx\).

Standard procedure

Call the integral \(I\). The aim is an equation for \(I\), not an antiderivative.

  1. Substitute \(x=a-u\). \(dx=-du\); \(x=0\Rightarrow u=a\), \(x=a\Rightarrow u=0\). The minus sign flips the limits back: \(I=\int_0^af(a-u)\,du\).
  2. Simplify \(f(a-u)\) with an identity: \(\tan\big(\frac\pi4-u\big)=\dfrac{1-\tan u}{1+\tan u}\), \(\sin\big(\frac\pi2-u\big)=\cos u\).
  3. Combine the two forms of \(I\).
    • 3a. The new integrand is "constant minus the old one": \(I=ac-I\), so \(I=\frac{ac}2\). Asked: May 2025.
    • 3b. Adding the two forms gives a rescaled copy of \(I\) (\(\ln\sin2x\)): substitute \(t=2x\), use the symmetry again to write it in terms of \(I\), then solve the equation for \(I\). Asked: May 2026.
Traps
  • Dropping the minus sign from \(dx=-du\): you get \(I=-\int_0^af(a-u)\,du\) and the equation stops making sense.
  • Looking for an antiderivative. \(\ln(1+\tan x)\) and \(\ln\sin x\) have none in elementary functions; the trick is the whole question (May 2025 Q5(b), May 2026 Q3(d)).

May 2026 Q3(d)Q3: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Part (e) is under L13.1; parts (a)–(c) are in the L14 guide.

Hint

Type L13.4, step 3b. First step: the hint turns \(I\) into \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\cos x\,dx\); add the two forms and use \(\sin x\cos x=\frac12\sin2x\).

Solution

\(\ln\sin x\to-\infty\) as \(x\to0^+\), so this is an improper integral; it converges, and the steps below are valid for it.

Step 1–2 With \(a=\frac\pi2\): \(I=\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\cos x\,dx\).

Step 3b Add the two forms:

\[\begin{aligned}2I&=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln(\sin x\cos x)\,dx\\&=\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\big(\tfrac12\sin2x\big)\,dx\\&=-\frac\pi2\ln2+\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\sin2x\,dx.\end{aligned}\]

Step 3b Put \(t=2x\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\ln\sin2x\,dx=\frac12\int_0^{\pi}\ln\sin t\,dt\). Since \(\sin(\pi-t)=\sin t\), the parts over \([0,\frac\pi2]\) and \([\frac\pi2,\pi]\) are equal, so this is \(\frac12\cdot2I=I\).

Hence \(2I=-\frac\pi2\ln2+I\).

\[\boxed{-\frac\pi2\ln2}\]
