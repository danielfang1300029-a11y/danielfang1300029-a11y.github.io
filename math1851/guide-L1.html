MATH1851 Exam Guide · Limits

The short version

What to expect

Basic limits (no L'Hôpital, no infinity) are a small topic in the exam: 7 parts in 23 papers, usually the first, easiest parts of Q1 ("Find the following limits"). May 2026 had three of them in a row. They are quick marks, but each one hides a small trap: a limit that is not \(\frac00\) at all, an absolute value that changes sign, or a one-sided approach that decides the answer. Every past part is one of the four types below, and each type has a short standard procedure. The first move is always the same: substitute and look at what you get.

How to practise: use Start here to name the type, work the question with the procedure open, then check with the Hint and the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind each step, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: the opening parts of Q1 "Find the following limits" (May 2026 Q1(a)–(c), Dec 2021 Q1(a), May 2015 Q1(a)(i), May 2016 Q1(a)(i)), or a short "Does the limit exist? Why?" part (Dec 2018 Q1(b)). The later parts of the same Q1 are usually L'Hôpital limits (L10 guide).
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Q1–3 answers only ("a number, \(\infty\), \(-\infty\), or non-existence"). There only the final value scores, so a sign slip with an absolute value costs the whole part. On written papers the stem says "explain carefully why the limit does not exist" and "indicate clearly the theorems you have used": name the limit laws you use.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trigonometric identities (and on most papers the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}\), \(\cos^{-1}\), \(\tan^{-1}\)); nothing else for this topic. No paper prints the limit laws, the definition of \(|x|\) or the standard limits such as \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1\).
  • Wording to watch: "non-existence" is one of the allowed answers on May 2026; \(t\to a^-\) with "where \(a\lt0\)" (Dec 2021) means the sign of every piece must be worked out from \(t\lt a\lt0\).

L1.1Plug in first: the limit isn't actually indeterminate

Chapter note · limit laws · Chapter note · direct substitution

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2026); 0 of 10 older papers. Rare as a type of its own, but step 1 below is the first move in every limit question.

Recognise it

The expression looks like a famous limit (\(\frac{\sin x}{x}\), a quotient, a root), but the point is one where every piece is defined and the bottom is not 0.

Standard procedure
  1. Substitute the value into the top and the bottom separately.
  2. Look at the bottom.
    • 2a. Bottom \(\ne0\) and every piece defined there: the function is continuous at that point, so the limit is the value. Say why (Quotient Law; \(\sin\), polynomials and roots are continuous). Stop. Asked: May 2026.
    • 2b. Top \(\ne0\), bottom \(=0\): an infinite limit or no limit. Go to the L3 guide (type L3.1).
    • 2c. \(\frac00\): go to L1.2 (algebra) or, with \(e^x\), \(\ln\) or trig, to the L10 guide (L'Hôpital).
Traps
  • Reflex answers. \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1\) only when \(x\to0\). In May 2026 Q1(a), \(x\to\frac\pi2\), and the answer is \(\frac2\pi\), not 1.
  • L'Hôpital on a non-\(\frac00\) form gives a wrong number: here it would give \(\frac{\cos(\pi/2)}{1}=0\).

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q1(a)Q1: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the following limit. Your answer should be a number, \(\infty\), \(-\infty\), or "non-existence." \[\lim_{x\to\frac\pi2}\frac{\sin x}{x}\]

Other parts of this question: (b) is under L1.3, (c) under L1.2; (d) and (e) are in the L10 guide.

Hint

Type L1.1, not indeterminate. First step: substitute \(x=\frac\pi2\) into the top and the bottom separately and check whether the bottom is 0.

Solution

Step 1 Substitute. Top: \(\sin\frac\pi2=1\). Bottom: \(\frac\pi2\ne0\).

Step 2a \(\sin x\) and \(x\) are continuous and the bottom is not 0, so by the Quotient Law the limit is the value:

\[\lim_{x\to\frac\pi2}\frac{\sin x}{x}=\frac{1}{\pi/2}=\frac2\pi.\]
\[\boxed{\tfrac{2}{\pi}}\]

Marker: answers only, so only the value scores; "1" (the reflex \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\) answer) scores nothing.

Equivalent forms. \(\frac2\pi\approx0.637\).

L1.20/0 with polynomials or roots: factor, rationalise or combine fractions

Chapter note · removing the 0/0 by algebra

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2026); 1 of 10 older papers (May 2015).

Recognise it

Substituting gives \(\frac00\) (or \(\infty-\infty\) from two fractions), and the expression contains only polynomials and square roots: no \(e^x\), \(\ln\) or trig.

Standard procedure
  1. Substitute and write down the form. \(\frac00\) means the top and bottom share a hidden factor \((x-a)\). Two fractions that each blow up (\(\infty-\infty\)) also count: combine them first (2c).
  2. Expose the factor \((x-a)\).
    • 2a. Polynomials: factor top and bottom (\(a\) is a root of both) and cancel \((x-a)\). Cancelling is allowed because \(x\ne a\) inside a limit.
    • 2b. A square root: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (\(A-\sqrt B\) becomes \(A+\sqrt B\)). The difference of squares \((A-\sqrt B)(A+\sqrt B)=A^2-B\) removes the root; then factor and cancel as in 2a. Asked: May 2026.
    • 2c. A difference of fractions: put everything over one common denominator, simplify the top, then factor and cancel as in 2a. Asked: May 2015.
  3. Substitute again. A number with a non-zero bottom is the answer (Quotient Law). Still \(\frac00\): repeat step 2. A non-zero number over 0: an infinite limit, see the L3 guide (L3.1).
Traps
  • Calling \(\infty-\infty\) "does not exist". In May 2015 Q1(a)(i) both fractions blow up at \(t=2\), but after combining the limit is \(\frac34\).
  • Sign slips in the conjugate step. In May 2026 Q1(c) the top becomes \(4-(w^2-5)=9-w^2\), and \(9-w^2=(3-w)(3+w)\), not \((w-3)(w+3)\). One sign wrong turns \(\frac32\) into \(-\frac32\), and on an answers-only paper that is zero marks.

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q1(c)Q1: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the following limit. Your answer should be a number, \(\infty\), \(-\infty\), or "non-existence." \[\lim_{w\to-3}\frac{2-\sqrt{w^2-5}}{w+3}\]

Other parts of this question: (a) is under L1.1, (b) under L1.3; (d) and (e) are in the L10 guide.

Hint

Type L1.2, \(\frac00\) with a square root (step 2b). First step: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate \(2+\sqrt{w^2-5}\).

Solution

Step 1 Form. At \(w=-3\): \(\sqrt{9-5}=2\), so the top \(\to2-2=0\); the bottom \(\to0\). Form \(\frac00\).

Step 2b Conjugate.

Write \(R=\sqrt{w^2-5}\). Then

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{2-R}{w+3}&=\frac{(2-R)(2+R)}{(w+3)(2+R)}\\&=\frac{4-(w^2-5)}{(w+3)(2+R)}\\&=\frac{(3-w)(3+w)}{(w+3)(2+R)}\\&=\frac{3-w}{2+R}\quad(w\ne-3).\end{aligned}\]

Step 3 Substitute again: \(\dfrac{3-(-3)}{2+2}=\dfrac64\).

\[\boxed{\tfrac32}\]

Marker: answers only, so only \(\frac32\) scores; the sign of \(3-w\) at \(w=-3\) is where most errors happen.

Equivalent forms. \(1.5\).

Older format (2013–2018)

May 2015 Q1(a)(i)Q1: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Either evaluate the limit or explain carefully why the limit does not exist. Indicate clearly the theorems you have used in your argument. \[\lim_{t\to2}\left(\frac{t}{t-2}-\frac{3t+2}{t^2-4}\right)\]

Other parts of this question: (ii) \(\lim_{x\to0}(2x+\cos x)^{1/\sin x}\) is in the L10 guide; (iii) \(\lim_{x\to0^+}e^{-(\ln x)^3}\) is in the L7 guide.

Hint

Type L1.2, a difference of fractions (step 2c). First step: \(t^2-4=(t-2)(t+2)\), so use \((t-2)(t+2)\) as the common denominator.

Solution

Step 1 Form. As \(t\to2\) both fractions blow up (top \(\to2\) and \(8\), bottoms \(\to0\)): form \(\infty-\infty\). Combine.

Step 2c One fraction, for \(t\ne\pm2\):

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{t(t+2)-(3t+2)}{(t-2)(t+2)}\\&=\frac{t^2-t-2}{(t-2)(t+2)}\\&=\frac{(t-2)(t+1)}{(t-2)(t+2)}=\frac{t+1}{t+2}.\end{aligned}\]

Step 3 Substitute again. \(\frac{t+1}{t+2}\) is continuous at 2 (bottom \(4\ne0\)), so by the Quotient Law the limit is \(\frac{3}{4}\).

\[\boxed{\tfrac34}\]

Equivalent forms. \(0.75\).

L1.3Absolute values and one-sided limits

Chapter note · one-sided limits · Chapter note · absolute values and piecewise functions

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, Dec 2021); 1 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018). The most common L1 type: 3 parts in all.

Recognise it

An absolute value \(|\dots|\) whose inside is 0 at the point, a one-sided arrow \(x\to a^{+}\) or \(x\to a^{-}\), or a function defined by cases (a different formula on each side of a point).

Standard procedure

\(x\to a^{+}\) ("from the right") means only \(x\gt a\) counts; \(x\to a^{-}\) ("from the left") means only \(x\lt a\). Recall \(|E|=E\) when \(E\ge0\) and \(|E|=-E\) when \(E\lt0\).

  1. Find where each \(|\dots|\) (or each case) switches: where the inside is 0, or where the case boundary is.
  2. Fix the side. If the question gives \(a^{+}\) or \(a^{-}\), use only that side. If it is two-sided and a switch happens at the point, do both sides.
  3. Decide the sign of every expression inside \(|\dots|\) on that side, using the side and any given condition (such as \(a\lt0\)). Write each \(|E|\) as \(E\) or \(-E\).
  4. Simplify and substitute. With the \(|\;|\) gone, cancel and evaluate as in L1.1 or L1.2. Asked: May 2026, Dec 2021.
    • 4a. Two-sided: equal left and right limits give the limit; different ones mean it does not exist.
    • 4b. A composition \(h(g(x))\) with \(h\) defined by cases: the limit of \(g\) is not enough. Find which case of \(h\) is used, i.e. which side of the switch \(g(x)\) lands on for \(x\) near the point, then use that formula. Asked: Dec 2018.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2021 Q1(a), part of Q1 (10%). In each of the following, either evaluate the limit or explain carefully why the limit does not exist. Indicate clearly the theorems you have used in your argument.

\[\lim_{t\to a^-}\frac{a+|t|}{|a-t|}\quad\text{where }a\lt0.\]

Step 1 The two absolute values switch at \(t=0\) (for \(|t|\)) and at \(t=a\) (for \(|a-t|\)).

Substituting \(t=a\) gives \(\frac{a+|a|}{0}=\frac{a-a}{0}=\frac00\) (because \(a\lt0\), \(|a|=-a\)). So the \(|\;|\) signs must be removed before anything else.

Step 2 One-sided, \(t\to a^-\): only \(t\lt a\) counts. Together with \(a\lt0\): \(t\lt a\lt0\).

Write this chain of inequalities down. It is the whole argument, and the marker looks for it.

Step 3 Signs. \(t\lt0\), so \(|t|=-t\). \(t\lt a\), so \(a-t\gt0\) and \(|a-t|=a-t\).

\(t=0\), the other switch, is on the far side of \(a\), so it does not matter here.

Step 4 Simplify. For \(t\lt a\):

\[\frac{a+|t|}{|a-t|}=\frac{a-t}{a-t}=1.\]

The function equals the constant 1 for every \(t\lt a\), so its left-hand limit at \(a\) is 1.

Theorem used: the limit of a constant is that constant (and two functions that agree for all \(t\) near \(a\) on the chosen side have the same one-sided limit).

\[\boxed{\lim_{t\to a^-}\frac{a+|t|}{|a-t|}=1}\]

Check: from the right (\(a\lt t\lt0\)) the same expression is \(\frac{a-t}{t-a}=-1\), so only the one-sided limit exists. Sympy with \(a=-1,-5,-\frac13\) gives 1 from the left and \(-1\) from the right.

Traps
  • Answering the two-sided question. In May 2026 Q1(b) and Dec 2021 Q1(a) the two-sided limit does not exist (left and right differ), but only one side is asked. "Non-existence" would score nothing.
  • Forgetting \(a\lt0\). In Dec 2021 Q1(a), writing \(|t|=t\) gives \(\frac{a+t}{a-t}\to\frac{2a}{0}\), a wrong "infinite" answer.
  • Composition with a piecewise function. In Dec 2018 Q1(b), \(h\) has no limit at 0, yet \(h(x^2)\) does: \(x^2\) never goes below 0.

Coverage: the procedure solves all 3 past parts: May 2026 Q1(b) and Dec 2021 Q1(a) end at step 4 (one-sided), Dec 2018 Q1(b) at step 4b. A two-sided limit whose left and right values differ (so the answer is "does not exist") has never been asked in this type; that comparison appears in the L4 guide (continuity at a join).

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q1(b)Q1: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the following limit. Your answer should be a number, \(\infty\), \(-\infty\), or "non-existence." \[\lim_{t\to-3^+}\frac{t+3}{|2t+6|}\]

Other parts of this question: (a) is under L1.1, (c) under L1.2; (d) and (e) are in the L10 guide.

Hint

Type L1.3, absolute value, one-sided. First step: for \(t\gt-3\), decide the sign of \(2t+6\).

Solution

Step 1–2 \(|2t+6|\) switches at \(t=-3\). Only \(t\gt-3\) counts.

Step 3 For \(t\gt-3\): \(2t+6=2(t+3)\gt0\), so \(|2t+6|=2(t+3)\).

Step 4 For \(t\gt-3\):

\[\frac{t+3}{|2t+6|}=\frac{t+3}{2(t+3)}=\frac12.\]
\[\boxed{\tfrac12}\]

Marker: answers only. The two-sided limit does not exist (from the left the value is \(-\frac12\)), but the question is one-sided, so the answer is \(\frac12\).

Equivalent forms. \(0.5\).

In each of the following, either evaluate the limit or explain carefully why the limit does not exist. Indicate clearly the theorems you have used in your argument. \[\lim_{t\to a^-}\frac{a+|t|}{|a-t|}\quad\text{where }a\lt0.\]

Other parts of this question: (b) is in the L10 guide, (c) in the L7 guide, (d) in the L2 guide.

Hint

Type L1.3, absolute values, one-sided. First step: from \(t\to a^-\) and \(a\lt0\), write down the order of \(t\), \(a\) and \(0\), then the sign of \(t\) and of \(a-t\).

Solution: worked in full in the worked example above. Answer \(1\).

Older format (2013–2018)

Dec 2018 Q1(b)Q1: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Let \[h(x)=\begin{cases}0, & \text{if } x\ge0,\\ 1, & \text{if } x\lt0.\end{cases}\] Does \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}h(x^2)\) exist? Why?

Part (a) of this question is in the L2 guide.

Hint

Type L1.3, a composition with a piecewise function (step 4b). First step: for every real \(x\), which case of \(h\) does \(x^2\) fall into?

Solution

Step 1 \(h\) switches at 0: \(h(s)=0\) for \(s\ge0\), \(h(s)=1\) for \(s\lt0\).

Step 4b Which case? For every real \(x\), \(x^2\ge0\), so \(h(x^2)=0\). The function \(x\mapsto h(x^2)\) is the constant 0.

Step 4a From both sides it is constantly 0, so the limit exists and

\[\lim_{x\to0}h(x^2)=\lim_{x\to0}0=0.\]
\[\boxed{\text{Yes; }\lim_{x\to0}h(x^2)=0}\]

Marker: the "why" is the sentence "\(x^2\ge0\) for all \(x\), so \(h(x^2)=0\)". Note that \(\lim_{s\to0}h(s)\) itself does not exist (left 1, right 0), so "limit of the inside, then apply \(h\)" is not a valid argument here.

L1.4Limits involving an unknown function: use the limit facts you're given

Chapter note · limit laws

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 1 of 10 older papers (May 2016). Never asked on a recent paper.

Recognise it

\(f\) has no formula. You are told the limit of some expression containing \(f(x)\), and asked for the limit of \(f(x)\) (or of another expression in \(f\)).

Standard procedure
  1. Name the given expression, say \(g(x)\), with \(g(x)\to L\) (the given value).
  2. Solve for \(f(x)\) in terms of \(g(x)\) and known functions, for \(x\) near the point (\(x\ne a\)).
  3. Simplify the known pieces so that each one has a limit: cancel any factor that would give \(0\cdot\infty\) (as in L1.2).
  4. Take limits with the limit laws (Sum and Product Laws need every piece to have a finite limit; say so). Asked: May 2016.
Traps
  • Splitting the given limit. In May 2016 Q1(a)(i), \(\frac{x}{x^2-4}\) has no limit at 2, so "\(\lim\frac{f(x)-3}{x-2}=4+\lim\frac{x}{x^2-4}\)" is not allowed. Solve for \(f(x)\) first.
  • Answering 3. Guessing \(f(x)\to3\) "because the top must go to 0" is wrong here: the bracket contains a term that blows up.

Past-paper questions

Older format (2013–2018)

May 2016 Q1(a)(i)Q1: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2}f(x)\) if \[\lim_{x\to2}\left(\frac{f(x)-3}{x-2}-\frac{x}{x^2-4}\right)=4.\]

Other parts of this question: (ii) \(\lim_{x\to0^+}x^{0.7}\ln(e^x-1)\) is in the L10 guide; (iii) \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^4+3x}-\sqrt{x^4-x^2}\right)\) is in the L3 guide.

Hint

Type L1.4, unknown function. First step: call the bracket \(g(x)\) and solve \(g(x)=\frac{f(x)-3}{x-2}-\frac{x}{x^2-4}\) for \(f(x)\).

Solution

Step 1 Let \(g(x)=\dfrac{f(x)-3}{x-2}-\dfrac{x}{x^2-4}\), so \(g(x)\to4\) as \(x\to2\).

Step 2 Solve for \(f\), for \(x\ne\pm2\):

\[f(x)=3+(x-2)\,g(x)+\frac{(x-2)\,x}{x^2-4}.\]

Step 3 Simplify: \(\dfrac{(x-2)x}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\dfrac{x}{x+2}\to\dfrac24=\dfrac12\).

Step 4 Limit laws: \((x-2)\to0\) and \(g(x)\to4\), so by the Product Law \((x-2)g(x)\to0\cdot4=0\). By the Sum Law,

\[\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=3+0+\frac12=\frac72.\]
\[\boxed{\tfrac72}\]

Marker: name the Product and Sum Laws, and note each piece has a finite limit before you use them.

Check with \(f(x)=3+(x-2)\left(4+(x-2)+\frac{x}{x^2-4}\right)\): the bracket tends to 4 and sympy gives \(f(x)\to\frac72\). ✓

Equivalent forms. \(3.5\).
