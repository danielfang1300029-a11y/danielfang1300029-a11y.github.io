MATH1851 Exam Guide · ODE Substitutions (homogeneous, Bernoulli, Riccati, shift, reduction of order)

The short version

What to expect

Almost every recent paper has one 5-mark part that says "Solve the differential equation" for a first-order equation that is not linear and not separable as it stands. The whole job is to pick the right substitution. After the substitution the equation becomes separable or linear, which you already know how to finish. There are only five substitutions, and each has a clear look. If you can do each type in the cards below, you are ready.

How to practise: try a question, open the Hint only if stuck, check against the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind a method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Part II, Q6 or Q7 part (a) or (b), usually 5 of the question's 10%; Dec 2025 Q15(a); May 2026 Q7(a). 11 of the 13 recent papers have one (May 2025 has two).
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. These ODE parts stayed full working, but the paper is faster, so the substitution step should be automatic.
  • Hints are disappearing: papers up to May 2024 often printed the substitution ("use \(u=y^{-3}\)", "try \(y=vx\)", "\(y=\frac2x+\frac1u\)"). Dec 2024, May 2025, Dec 2025 and May 2026 printed none. Learn to choose it yourself.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard derivatives and integrals, and a hint for variation of parameters. Nothing for this topic: not \(u=y^{1-n}\), not the integrating factor \(e^{\int P\,dx}\), not the Riccati form. Notation: the hints call \(y/x\) by \(v\), \(w\) or \(\vartheta\); all the same thing.

Types at a glance

Match the equation to a card. Counts are question parts, 19 in all, 2013–2026 (recent = 13 papers, May 2019–May 2026).

O2.1Homogeneous: put y = vx3 recent · 6 all

Looks like\(\frac{dy}{dx}=\) a fraction, same total power in every term, e.g. \(\frac{x^2+2y^2}{2xy}\).

Do\(y=vx\), \(\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}\); separate; put \(v=\frac yx\) back.

Watch\(M\,dx+N\,dy=0\): rearrange to \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) first.

O2.2Bernoulli: put u = y1−n4 recent · 7 all

Looks like\(\frac{dy}{dx}+P(x)\,y=Q(x)\,y^n\): one extra power of \(y\).

Do\(u=y^{1-n}\) gives \(\frac{du}{dx}+(1-n)Pu=(1-n)Q\); integrating factor.

WatchCoefficient of \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) equal to 1 first; \((1-n)\) multiplies both sides.

O2.3Riccati: put y = y₁ + 1/u3 recent · 3 all

Looks like\(y^2\), \(y\) and a term with no \(y\); often a hint \(y=y_1+\frac1u\).

DoSubstitute; \(y_1\) terms cancel; times \(-u^2\): linear in \(u\).

WatchNo hint (May 2020): try \(y_1=\frac kx\) and find \(k\).

O2.4Shift: put u = y − g(x)2 recent · 2 all

Looks likeRight side \(=y^2-2g\,y+g^2+g^{\prime}=(y-g)^2+g^{\prime}\), e.g. \(y^2-2xy+x^2+1\).

Do\(u=y-g\) gives \(\frac{du}{dx}=u^2\), so \(u=-\frac1{x+C}\).

WatchSpot the square: the last term must be \(g^{\prime}\).

O2.5Reduction of order: put y₂ = c(x)y₁0 recent · 1 all

Looks likeSecond-order linear, "observe that \(y_1\) is one solution".

Do\(y_2=c(x)\,y_1\); \(c\) terms cancel; solve first order for \(c^{\prime}\).

WatchFinal answer \(y=C_1y_1+C_2y_2\), not \(y_2\) alone.

O2.1Homogeneous equations: put y = vx

Chapter note · homogeneous equations · method in the table

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2020, Dec 2023); 3 of 10 older papers.

Ready answer · the same fraction comes back \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^2+ky^2}{kxy}\ (x\gt 0)\] \[\Longrightarrow\ y^2=\frac2k\,x^2\left(\ln x+C\right)\]

\(k=2\): May 2019 and Dec 2023; \(k=3\): Dec 2020. After \(y=vx\) the equation is always \(x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac1{kv}\). Still show the substitution in the exam.

Past-paper questions

Dec 2023 Q6(b)5% (Q6: 10%)

Also asked: May 2019 Q6(b) (Q6: 10%; part marks not shown), written as \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{x^2+2y^2}{2xy}\ (x\gt 0)\), titled "Solve the 'homogeneous' first order differential equation" with the hint "try \(y=vx\)".

Solve the differential equation: \[(x^2+2y^2)\,dx-2xy\,dy=0\qquad(x\gt 0).\]

Hint

Type O2.1, homogeneous. Rearranged, \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{x^2+2y^2}{2xy}\): every term top and bottom has total power 2. First step: put \(y=vx\), so \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\), and divide top and bottom by \(x^2\).

Solution

1. Rearrange. \(2xy\,dy=(x^2+2y^2)\,dx\), so

\[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^2+2y^2}{2xy}.\]

2. Substitute \(y=vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\):

\[v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+2v^2}{2v}\] \[x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+2v^2}{2v}-v=\frac{1}{2v}.\]

3. Separate and integrate (\(x\gt 0\), so \(\ln x\) needs no modulus):

\[\int 2v\,dv=\int\frac{dx}{x}\ \Rightarrow\ v^2=\ln x+C.\]

4. Put \(v=\frac yx\) back:

\[\boxed{y^2=x^2\left(\ln x+C\right)}\]

Marker: the substitution line \(\frac{dy}{dx}=v+x\frac{dv}{dx}\) and the return to \(x,y\) both carry marks.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\pm x\sqrt{\ln x+C}\); \(y^2=x^2\ln(Ax)\) with \(A\gt 0\); \(\frac{y^2}{x^2}-\ln x=C\).

Dec 2020 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the following differential equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x^2+3y^2}{3xy}\qquad(x\gt 0).\]

Hint

Type O2.1, homogeneous. Every term top and bottom has total power 2. First step: \(y=vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\), then divide top and bottom by \(x^2\).

Solution

1. Substitute \(y=vx\):

\[v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+3v^2}{3v}\] \[x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{1+3v^2}{3v}-v=\frac{1}{3v}.\]

2. Separate and integrate:

\[\int 3v\,dv=\int\frac{dx}{x}\ \Rightarrow\ \tfrac32v^2=\ln x+C.\]

3. Put \(v=\frac yx\) back:

\[\boxed{y^2=\tfrac23\,x^2\left(\ln x+C\right)}\]

Equivalent forms. \(3y^2=2x^2\ln x+Cx^2\); \(y=\pm x\sqrt{\tfrac23(\ln x+C)}\).

Older format (2013–2018)

May 2018 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the homogeneous equation (Hint: use a substitution of \(y=\vartheta(x)\,x\)): \[(y^2+xy)\,dx-x^2\,dy=0.\]

Hint

Type O2.1, homogeneous. Rearranged, \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y^2+xy}{x^2}\). First step: put \(y=\vartheta x\) (written \(v\) below), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\).

Solution

1. Rearrange: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y^2+xy}{x^2}=\left(\dfrac yx\right)^2+\dfrac yx\).

2. Substitute \(y=vx\):

\[v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=v^2+v\ \Rightarrow\ x\frac{dv}{dx}=v^2.\]

3. Separate (for \(v\ne 0\)) and integrate:

\[\int\frac{dv}{v^2}=\int\frac{dx}{x}\ \Rightarrow\ -\frac1v=\ln|x|+C.\]

4. Put \(v=\frac yx\) back: \(-\dfrac xy=\ln|x|+C\).

\[\boxed{y=-\frac{x}{\ln|x|+C}}\]

Dividing by \(v^2\) lost \(v=0\), i.e. \(y=0\), which is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{x}{C-\ln|x|}\) (renamed constant); \(\dfrac xy+\ln|x|=C\).

May 2017 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following differential equation (Hint: you may use the substitution \(v=y/x\)): \[(x-y)\,dy=y\,dx.\]

Hint

Type O2.1, homogeneous. \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{y}{x-y}\); divide top and bottom by \(x\). First step: \(y=vx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}\).

Solution

1. Rearrange and substitute \(y=vx\):

\[v+x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v}{1-v}\] \[x\frac{dv}{dx}=\frac{v}{1-v}-v=\frac{v^2}{1-v}.\]

2. Separate and integrate:

\[\int\frac{1-v}{v^2}\,dv=\int\frac{dx}{x}\] \[-\frac1v-\ln|v|=\ln|x|+C.\]

3. Put \(v=\frac yx\) back. \(-\dfrac xy-\ln|y|+\ln|x|=\ln|x|+C\); the \(\ln|x|\) cancels:

\[\boxed{\frac xy+\ln|y|=C}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution (lost when dividing by \(v^2\)).

Equivalent forms. \(x=y\,(C-\ln|y|)\); \(ye^{x/y}=A\).

Dec 2015 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following equation (one way is to use \(y=wx\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{y^2-x^2}{2xy}.\] For various possible integration constants, sketch the shapes of the solution curves and comment on the main features.

Hint

Type O2.1, homogeneous. First step: \(y=wx\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=w+x\dfrac{dw}{dx}\). For the sketch, complete the square in \(x\) in your answer.

Solution

1. Substitute \(y=wx\):

\[w+x\frac{dw}{dx}=\frac{w^2-1}{2w}\] \[x\frac{dw}{dx}=\frac{w^2-1}{2w}-w=-\frac{1+w^2}{2w}.\]

2. Separate and integrate:

\[\int\frac{2w}{1+w^2}\,dw=-\int\frac{dx}{x}\] \[\ln(1+w^2)=-\ln|x|+c\] \[1+w^2=\frac{A}{x}.\]

3. Put \(w=\frac yx\) back and multiply by \(x^2\):

\[\boxed{x^2+y^2=Ax}\]

4. Sketch. Completing the square: \(\left(x-\frac A2\right)^2+y^2=\frac{A^2}{4}\), a circle with centre \(\left(\frac A2,0\right)\) and radius \(\frac{|A|}2\).

5. Main features.

  • Every curve is a circle through the origin with its centre on the \(x\)-axis, so all touch the \(y\)-axis at \(O\). \(A\gt 0\): right of the \(y\)-axis; \(A\lt 0\): left.
  • Each solution \(y(x)\) is the upper or lower half: \(y=\pm\sqrt{Ax-x^2}\), for \(x\) between \(0\) and \(A\).
  • Where a circle meets the \(x\)-axis (\(y=0\)) the slope \(\frac{y^2-x^2}{2xy}\) is undefined: vertical tangent, the solution ends there.
  • The slope is \(0\) where \(y^2=x^2\): each circle's highest and lowest points lie on the dashed lines \(y=\pm x\).

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{x}=A\); \(y^2=Ax-x^2\).

O2.2Bernoulli equations: put u = y1−n

Chapter note · Bernoulli equations · method in the table

Frequency: 4 of 13 recent papers (May 2023, May 2025, Dec 2025, May 2026); 3 of 10 older papers. The most common type on this page, and on all three latest papers.

Ready answer · every Bernoulli question \[\frac{dy}{dx}+P(x)\,y=Q(x)\,y^n,\quad u=y^{1-n}\] \[\Longrightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}+(1-n)P\,u=(1-n)\,Q\]

Then integrating factor \(e^{\int(1-n)P\,dx}\). In the exam show the steps: divide by \(y^n\), write \(\frac{du}{dx}=(1-n)y^{-n}\frac{dy}{dx}\), substitute.

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q7(a)Q7: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[x^2\frac{dy}{dx}=(x^3+x^2e^x)\,y^2+xy.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=2\). Divide by \(x^2\) and move the \(y\) term left: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac yx=(x+e^x)\,y^2\). First step: divide by \(y^2\) and put \(u=y^{-1}\).

Solution

1. Standard form (divide by \(x^2\)):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac1x\,y=(x+e^x)\,y^2.\]

2. Substitute. Divide by \(y^2\): \(y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac1x\,y^{-1}=x+e^x\). With \(u=y^{-1}\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[-\frac{du}{dx}-\frac ux=x+e^x\] \[\frac{du}{dx}+\frac ux=-x-e^x.\]

3. Integrating factor \(e^{\int\frac1x dx}=x\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}(xu)=-x^2-xe^x\] \[xu=-\frac{x^3}{3}-(x-1)e^x+C,\]

using \(\int xe^x\,dx=(x-1)e^x\) (by parts).

4. Back to \(y=\frac1u\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac{x}{C-\frac{x^3}{3}-(x-1)e^x}}\]

Marker: the linear equation in \(u\), the integrating factor, and the return to \(y\) are the three checkpoints.

\(y=0\) is also a solution (lost when dividing by \(y^2\)).

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac xy=C-\dfrac{x^3}{3}-(x-1)e^x\); \(y=\dfrac{3x}{C-x^3-3(x-1)e^x}\) (renamed constant).

Dec 2025 Q15(a)Q15: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[x^3\frac{dy}{dx}+x^2y=(1+x^4)\,y^{\frac52}.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=\frac52\). Divide by \(x^3\) first. First step: divide by \(y^{5/2}\) and put \(u=y^{1-\frac52}=y^{-\frac32}\).

Solution

\(y^{5/2}\) and the powers of \(x\) below need \(x\gt 0\), \(y\gt 0\); the paper does not say so.

1. Standard form (divide by \(x^3\)):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac1x\,y=\left(x^{-3}+x\right)y^{\frac52}.\]

2. Substitute. Divide by \(y^{5/2}\): \(y^{-\frac52}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac1x\,y^{-\frac32}=x^{-3}+x\). With \(u=y^{-\frac32}\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-\dfrac32y^{-\frac52}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[-\frac23\frac{du}{dx}+\frac ux=x^{-3}+x\] \[\frac{du}{dx}-\frac{3}{2x}\,u=-\frac32\left(x^{-3}+x\right).\]

3. Integrating factor \(e^{-\frac32\ln x}=x^{-\frac32}\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(x^{-\frac32}u\right)=-\frac32\left(x^{-\frac92}+x^{-\frac12}\right)\] \[x^{-\frac32}u=\frac37x^{-\frac72}-3x^{\frac12}+C.\]

4. Multiply by \(x^{3/2}\), back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y^{-\frac32}=\frac{3}{7x^2}-3x^2+Cx^{\frac32}}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\left(\dfrac{3}{7x^2}-3x^2+Cx^{3/2}\right)^{-2/3}\); \(\dfrac{7x^2}{y^{3/2}}=3-21x^4+Cx^{7/2}\) (renamed constant).

May 2025 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}+y=xy^4.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=4\). First step: divide by \(y^4\) and put \(u=y^{-3}\).

Solution

1. Substitute. Divide by \(y^4\): \(y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+y^{-3}=x\). With \(u=y^{-3}\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-3y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[-\frac13\frac{du}{dx}+u=x\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}-3u=-3x.\]

2. Integrating factor \(e^{-3x}\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{-3x}u\right)=-3xe^{-3x}\] \[e^{-3x}u=xe^{-3x}+\tfrac13e^{-3x}+C\]

(by parts). So \(u=x+\frac13+Ce^{3x}\).

3. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y^{-3}=x+\tfrac13+Ce^{3x}}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\left(x+\frac13+Ce^{3x}\right)^{-1/3}\); \(\dfrac{3}{y^3}=3x+1+Ce^{3x}\) (renamed constant).

May 2023 Q7(a)5% (Q7: 10%)

Solve the differential equation (Hint: use a substitution of \(u=y^{-3}\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac13y=e^xy^4.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=4\). First step: divide by \(y^4\) and use \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-3y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\).

Solution

1. Substitute. Divide by \(y^4\): \(y^{-4}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac13y^{-3}=e^x\). With \(u=y^{-3}\):

\[-\frac13\frac{du}{dx}+\frac13u=e^x\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}-u=-3e^x.\]

2. Integrating factor \(e^{-x}\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{-x}u\right)=-3\ \Rightarrow\ e^{-x}u=-3x+C.\]

3. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y^{-3}=(C-3x)\,e^{x}}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y^3=\dfrac{e^{-x}}{C-3x}\); \(y=\dfrac{e^{-x/3}}{\sqrt[3]{C-3x}}\).

Dec 2016 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Bernoulli equation (\(u=y^{1-n}\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac yx=xy^2.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=2\). First step: divide by \(y^2\) and put \(u=y^{-1}\).

Solution

1. Substitute. \(y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac1x\,y^{-1}=x\); with \(u=y^{-1}\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-y^{-2}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[-\frac{du}{dx}+\frac ux=x\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}-\frac ux=-x.\]

2. Integrating factor \(e^{-\ln x}=\frac1x\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac ux\right)=-1\ \Rightarrow\ \frac ux=-x+C.\]

3. Back to \(y\): \(u=Cx-x^2\).

\[\boxed{y=\frac{1}{Cx-x^2}}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{1}{x(C-x)}\); \(\dfrac1{xy}+x=C\).

May 2016 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Bernoulli equation (Hint: \(u=y^{1-n}\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}=5y-5xy^3.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=3\). Standard form \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}-5y=-5xy^3\). First step: divide by \(y^3\) and put \(u=y^{-2}\).

Solution

1. Standard form: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}-5y=-5xy^3\).

2. Substitute. \(y^{-3}\dfrac{dy}{dx}-5y^{-2}=-5x\); with \(u=y^{-2}\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=-2y^{-3}\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[-\frac12\frac{du}{dx}-5u=-5x\] \[\frac{du}{dx}+10u=10x.\]

3. Integrating factor \(e^{10x}\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(e^{10x}u\right)=10xe^{10x}\] \[e^{10x}u=xe^{10x}-\tfrac1{10}e^{10x}+C\]

(by parts), so \(u=x-\frac1{10}+Ce^{-10x}\).

4. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y^{2}=\frac{1}{x-\frac1{10}+Ce^{-10x}}}\]

\(y=0\) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(y^{-2}=x-\frac1{10}+Ce^{-10x}\); \(y^2=\dfrac{10}{10x-1+Ce^{-10x}}\) (renamed constant).

May 2013 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the Bernoulli equation (\(u=y^{1-n}\)): \[x\frac{dy}{dx}-y=\frac1y.\]

Hint

Type O2.2, Bernoulli with \(n=-1\) (\(\frac1y=y^{-1}\)). Divide by \(x\) first. First step: put \(u=y^{1-(-1)}=y^2\).

Solution

1. Standard form: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac1x\,y=\dfrac1x\,y^{-1}\).

2. Substitute. Multiply by \(y\) (that is, divide by \(y^{-1}\)): \(y\dfrac{dy}{dx}-\dfrac{y^2}{x}=\dfrac1x\). With \(u=y^2\), \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}\):

\[\frac12\frac{du}{dx}-\frac ux=\frac1x\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}-\frac2x\,u=\frac2x.\]

3. Integrating factor \(e^{-2\ln x}=x^{-2}\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac u{x^2}\right)=\frac2{x^3}\ \Rightarrow\ \frac u{x^2}=-\frac1{x^2}+C.\]

4. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y^2=Cx^2-1}\]

Check. The equation is also separable: \(\frac{y}{y^2+1}\,dy=\frac{dx}{x}\) gives the same answer.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\pm\sqrt{Cx^2-1}\); \(\dfrac{1+y^2}{x^2}=C\).

O2.3–4Riccati and shift substitutions (equations with y²)

Chapter note · Riccati equations · Chapter note · shift substitution · method in the table

Frequency: Riccati (O2.3): 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2020, May 2022, May 2024); none older. Shift (O2.4): 2 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2024, May 2025); none older. Both are equations with a \(y^2\) term plus a term without \(y\), so Bernoulli does not apply.

Ready answer · Riccati after the substitution Beyond the handouts \[\frac{dy}{dx}=P\,y^2+Q\,y+R,\quad y=y_1+\frac1u\] \[\Longrightarrow\ \frac{du}{dx}+(2Py_1+Q)\,u=-P\]

Here \(y_1\) is a known solution. In all three past papers \(2Py_1+Q\) came out as a constant or \(\frac2x\), so the linear step is short. Use this to check your line; the marker wants the substitution shown.

Ready answer · shift \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\big(y-g(x)\big)^2+g^{\prime}(x)\] \[\Longrightarrow\ y=g(x)-\frac{1}{x+C}\ \text{ (and } y=g(x)\text{)}\]

\(g=x\): May 2025; \(g=e^x\): Dec 2024. The right side is given expanded, \(y^2-2gy+g^2+g^{\prime}\); recognise the square.

Past-paper questions · Riccati (O2.3)

May 2024 Q7(a)Q7: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the Riccati's equation (Hint: use a substitution of \(y=\frac2x+\frac1u\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}+y=\frac12xy^2-\frac{2}{x^2}.\]

Hint

Type O2.3, Riccati with given \(y_1=\frac2x\). First step: write \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac2{x^2}-\dfrac{1}{u^2}\dfrac{du}{dx}\) and substitute everything; the terms without \(u\) should cancel.

Solution

1. Substitute \(y=\frac2x+\frac1u\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac2{x^2}-\dfrac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}\). Left side:

\[-\frac2{x^2}-\frac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}+\frac2x+\frac1u.\]

Right side, using \(y^2=\frac4{x^2}+\frac4{xu}+\frac1{u^2}\):

\[\frac2x+\frac2u+\frac{x}{2u^2}-\frac2{x^2}.\]

2. Cancel \(-\frac2{x^2}\) and \(\frac2x\) (this works because \(y_1=\frac2x\) is a solution):

\[-\frac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}+\frac1u=\frac2u+\frac{x}{2u^2}.\]

3. Multiply by \(-u^2\): a linear equation.

\[\frac{du}{dx}+u=-\frac x2.\]

4. Integrating factor \(e^{x}\): \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(e^xu)=-\dfrac x2e^x\), so \(e^xu=-\dfrac12(x-1)e^x+C\) and

\[u=\frac{1-x}{2}+Ce^{-x}.\]

5. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac2x+\frac{2}{1-x+Ce^{-x}}}\]

Marker: the cancelling in Step 2 is the check that you substituted correctly; if it fails, recheck \(y^2\).

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac2x+\dfrac{1}{\frac{1-x}{2}+Ce^{-x}}\) (the constant here is half the boxed one). \(y=\frac2x\) itself is the solution for \(C\to\infty\).

May 2022 Q6(b)5% (Q6: 10%)

Solve the Riccati's equation (Hint: use a substitution of \(y=-\frac1x+\frac1u\)): \[\frac{dy}{dx}=-y^2+\frac{2}{x^2}.\]

Hint

Type O2.3, Riccati with given \(y_1=-\frac1x\). First step: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac1{x^2}-\dfrac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}\); substitute and cancel the terms without \(u\).

Solution

1. Substitute \(y=-\frac1x+\frac1u\). Left: \(\dfrac1{x^2}-\dfrac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}\). Right, with \(y^2=\frac1{x^2}-\frac2{xu}+\frac1{u^2}\):

\[\begin{aligned}&-\frac1{x^2}+\frac2{xu}-\frac1{u^2}+\frac2{x^2}\\&\quad=\frac1{x^2}+\frac2{xu}-\frac1{u^2}.\end{aligned}\]

2. Cancel \(\frac1{x^2}\), multiply by \(-u^2\):

\[\frac{du}{dx}=-\frac2x\,u+1\] \[\frac{du}{dx}+\frac2x\,u=1.\]

3. Integrating factor \(x^2\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}(x^2u)=x^2\ \Rightarrow\ x^2u=\frac{x^3}{3}+C\] \[u=\frac{x^3+3C}{3x^2}.\]

4. Back to \(y\) (renaming \(3C\) as \(C\)):

\[\boxed{y=-\frac1x+\frac{3x^2}{x^3+C}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{2x^3-C}{x\,(x^3+C)}\); \(y=-\frac1x+\dfrac{1}{\frac x3+\frac{C}{x^2}}\). \(y=-\frac1x\) is the case \(C\to\infty\).

May 2020 Q6(b)5 marks

Solve the differential equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}+y=xy^2-\frac{1}{x^2}.\]

No substitution hint was printed on this paper. The equation is printed correctly as it stands: \(y_1=\frac1x\) solves it (found below, checked with sympy), so it is a Riccati equation like May 2024 Q7(a).

Hint

Type O2.3, Riccati without a given \(y_1\). The \(-\frac1{x^2}\) suggests trying \(y_1=\frac kx\). First step: substitute \(y=\frac kx\) and choose \(k\) so both sides agree; then put \(y=y_1+\frac1u\).

Solution

1. Find a solution \(y_1=\frac kx\). Left: \(-\frac k{x^2}+\frac kx\). Right: \(\frac{k^2}{x}-\frac1{x^2}\). Matching: \(k=1\) (both \(k=k^2\) and \(-k=-1\)). So \(y_1=\frac1x\).

2. Substitute \(y=\frac1x+\frac1u\), \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac1{x^2}-\dfrac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}\):

\[\begin{aligned}&-\frac1{x^2}-\frac{u^{\prime}}{u^2}+\frac1x+\frac1u\\&\quad=\frac1x+\frac2u+\frac{x}{u^2}-\frac1{x^2}.\end{aligned}\]

3. Cancel, multiply by \(-u^2\):

\[\frac{du}{dx}+u=-x.\]

4. Integrating factor \(e^x\): \(e^xu=-(x-1)e^x+C\), so \(u=1-x+Ce^{-x}\).

5. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac1x+\frac{1}{1-x+Ce^{-x}}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{1+Ce^{-x}}{x\,(1-x+Ce^{-x})}\); \(y=\frac1x+\dfrac{e^x}{(1-x)e^x+C}\). \(y=\frac1x\) is the case \(C\to\infty\).

May 2025 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation \[\frac{dy}{dx}=y^2-2xy+x^2+1.\]

Hint

Type O2.4, shift. \(y^2-2xy+x^2=(y-x)^2\). First step: put \(u=y-x\), so \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}-1\).

Solution

1. Spot the square: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=(y-x)^2+1\).

2. Substitute \(u=y-x\): \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}-1=u^2\).

3. Separate (for \(u\ne 0\)):

\[\int\frac{du}{u^2}=\int dx\ \Rightarrow\ -\frac1u=x+C.\]

4. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y=x-\frac{1}{x+C}}\]

Marker: \(u=0\), i.e. \(y=x\), is also a solution; say so.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{x^2+Cx-1}{x+C}\); \(\dfrac{1}{x-y}=x+C\).

Dec 2024 Q7(a)5% (Q7: 10%)

Solve the differential equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}=y^2-2e^xy+e^{2x}+e^x.\]

Hint

Type O2.4, shift. \(y^2-2e^xy+e^{2x}=(y-e^x)^2\). First step: put \(u=y-e^x\), so \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}-e^x\).

Solution

1. Spot the square: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=(y-e^x)^2+e^x\).

2. Substitute \(u=y-e^x\): \(\dfrac{du}{dx}=\dfrac{dy}{dx}-e^x=u^2\).

3. Separate (for \(u\ne 0\)): \(-\dfrac1u=x+C\).

4. Back to \(y\):

\[\boxed{y=e^x-\frac{1}{x+C}}\]

\(y=e^x\) (from \(u=0\)) is also a solution.

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{1}{e^x-y}=x+C\); \(y=e^x+\dfrac{1}{C-x}\) (renamed constant).

May 2017 Q7(b)Q7: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Observe that \(y_1=x\) is one solution to the differential equation \[x^2y^{\prime\prime}+x(x-2)\,y^{\prime}-(x-2)\,y=0.\] Find the general solution to the above equation using the method of reduction of order. (Hint: you may consider to attempt \(y_2=c(x)\,y_1\) to get another independent solution.)

Hint

Type O2.5, reduction of order. First step: put \(y_2=c\,x\), so \(y_2^{\prime}=c^{\prime}x+c\) and \(y_2^{\prime\prime}=c^{\prime\prime}x+2c^{\prime}\), into the equation; every term with \(c\) alone should cancel.

Solution

1. Substitute \(y_2=cx\), \(y_2^{\prime}=c^{\prime}x+c\), \(y_2^{\prime\prime}=c^{\prime\prime}x+2c^{\prime}\):

\[\begin{aligned}&x^2(c^{\prime\prime}x+2c^{\prime})\\&+x(x-2)(c^{\prime}x+c)\\&-(x-2)\,cx=0.\end{aligned}\]

2. Simplify. The \(c\) terms cancel (because \(y_1=x\) is a solution):

\[x^3c^{\prime\prime}+\left(2x^2+x^3-2x^2\right)c^{\prime}=0\] \[c^{\prime\prime}+c^{\prime}=0\quad(x\ne 0).\]

3. Solve for \(c^{\prime}\) (first order in \(w=c^{\prime}\)): \(w=e^{-x}\) (any non-zero multiple will do), so \(c=-e^{-x}\).

4. Second solution: \(y_2=-xe^{-x}\); drop the constant factor: \(y_2=xe^{-x}\), which is not a multiple of \(x\), so it is independent.

5. General solution:

\[\boxed{y=C_1x+C_2\,xe^{-x}}\]

Marker: the question asks for the general solution; \(y_2\) alone is not the final answer.

Equivalent forms. \(y=x\left(C_1+C_2e^{-x}\right)\).
