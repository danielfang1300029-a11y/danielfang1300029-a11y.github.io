MATH1851 Exam Guide · First-Order ODEs (separable, linear, exact)

The short version

What to expect

This is one of the most predictable parts of the paper. Every recent paper has asked a linear equation (solve with an integrating factor), and most have paired it with an exact equation. A separable equation turns up only now and then. The equations are short and many come back with only the numbers changed. If you can do each type in the cards below, you are ready. The only real decision is which method the equation needs, and the cards answer that.

How to practise: try a question, open the Hint only if stuck, check against the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind a method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy by substituting back into the equation.

Exam format

  • Where: the first ODE question: Q6 (sometimes also Q7(a)) at 10% on papers up to May 2025; Q14 (10 pts) in Dec 2025; Q6 (10 pts, Part II) in May 2026. Usually part (a) is linear and part (b) is exact, about 5% each.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. These ODE parts stayed full-working, but the paper is faster-paced, so a 5% linear or exact part should take a few minutes.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities, a few standard integrals and derivatives, and a hint for variation of parameters (a second-order method). Nothing for this page: no integrating-factor formula \(e^{\int P\,dx}\), no exactness test \(M_y=N_x\). Learn both, and your basic integrals (by parts is often needed after the integrating factor).
  • Notation: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) and \(y^{\prime}\) mean the same. Exact equations are printed as \(M\,dx+N\,dy=0\); the answer is usually left as \(F(x,y)=C\) (implicit form).

O1.1Separable equations: split and integrate

Chapter note · separable equations · method in the cards

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2023); 3 of 10 older papers. Rare now, but it is also the way out when an "exact-looking" equation fails the exact test (Dec 2018 Q6(b)).

Past-paper questions

May 2023 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; this part 5%

Solve the differential equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}=e^{-y}(2x-4).\]

Other part of this question: (a) is under O1.2.

Hint

Type O1.1, separable. The right side is (function of \(y\)) × (function of \(x\)). First step: multiply both sides by \(e^{y}\) so all the \(y\) sits with \(dy\).

Solution

1. Separate. Multiply by \(e^{y}\,dx\):

\[e^{y}\,dy=(2x-4)\,dx.\]

2. Integrate both sides (one constant):

\[e^{y}=x^2-4x+C.\]

3. Solve for \(y\) (take \(\ln\) of both sides):

\[\boxed{y=\ln\left(x^2-4x+C\right)}\]

Marker: the \(+C\) must go in before taking \(\ln\); \(\ln(x^2-4x)+C\) is wrong.

Equivalent forms. \(e^{y}=x^2-4x+C\); \(y=\ln\left((x-2)^2+K\right)\) with \(K=C-4\). Valid where \(x^2-4x+C\gt 0\).

Dec 2018 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find the general solution of the following equation: \[(y^2\sin x)\,dx+\left(\frac1x-\frac yx\right)dy=0.\]

Other part of this question: (a) is under O1.2.

Hint

Type O1.1, separable (it looks like an exact question but is not exact). First step: the \(dy\) bracket is \(\dfrac{1-y}{x}\), so multiply the whole equation by \(\dfrac{x}{y^2}\) to put only \(x\) with \(dx\) and only \(y\) with \(dy\).

Solution

1. Not exact. With \(M=y^2\sin x\) and \(N=\dfrac{1-y}{x}\): \(M_y=2y\sin x\) but \(N_x=-\dfrac{1-y}{x^2}\). These differ, so do not look for \(F\) directly.

2. Separate. Multiply by \(\dfrac{x}{y^2}\) (for \(y\ne0\)):

\[x\sin x\,dx+\frac{1-y}{y^2}\,dy=0.\]

3. Integrate. By parts, \(\int x\sin x\,dx=\sin x-x\cos x\). Also \(\int\left(\dfrac1{y^2}-\dfrac1y\right)dy=-\dfrac1y-\ln|y|\).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\sin x-x\cos x-\frac1y-\ln|y|=C\\&\text{and also } y=0\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: state why it is not exact before separating; mention \(y=0\), which the division by \(y^2\) removes (it does satisfy the equation).

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac1y+\ln|y|+x\cos x-\sin x=K\) (\(K=-C\)).

Dec 2017 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following differential equation, and find the solution satisfying the given condition \(y(\pi)=e^2\): \[y^{-1}\,dy+ye^{\cos x}\sin x\,dx=0.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.2.

Hint

Type O1.1, separable, with an initial condition. First step: divide the whole equation by \(y\) so that \(dy\) has only \(y^{-2}\) and \(dx\) has only \(x\).

Solution

1. Separate. Divide by \(y\):

\[y^{-2}\,dy=-e^{\cos x}\sin x\,dx.\]

2. Integrate. Since \(\dfrac{d}{dx}e^{\cos x}=-\sin x\,e^{\cos x}\),

\[-\frac1y=e^{\cos x}+C,\qquad y=-\frac{1}{e^{\cos x}+C}.\]

3. Use \(y(\pi)=e^2\). \(\cos\pi=-1\), so \(-e^{-2}=e^{-1}+C\), giving \(C=-e^{-1}-e^{-2}\).

\[\boxed{y=\frac{1}{e^{-1}+e^{-2}-e^{\cos x}}}\]

Check. At \(x=\pi\): \(y=\dfrac{1}{e^{-2}}=e^2\). ✓

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{e^2}{e+1-e^{2+\cos x}}\); general solution \(\dfrac1y=C-e^{\cos x}\).

May 2015 Q8(a)Q8: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following ODE using the method of separable equations: \[x\frac{dy}{dx}-512y=0.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is in the O4 guide.

Hint

Type O1.1, separable. First step: rewrite as \(\dfrac{dy}{y}=\dfrac{512}{x}\,dx\).

Solution

1. Separate (for \(y\ne0\), \(x\ne0\)):

\[\frac{dy}{y}=512\,\frac{dx}{x}.\]

2. Integrate: \(\ln|y|=512\ln|x|+c\), so \(|y|=e^{c}|x|^{512}\).

3. Tidy the constant. \(\pm e^{c}\) and the lost solution \(y=0\) combine into one constant \(C\):

\[\boxed{y=Cx^{512}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(\ln|y|=512\ln|x|+c\) (but this form leaves out \(y=0\)).

O1.2Linear first order: integrating factor

Chapter note · linear equations and the integrating factor · method in the cards

Frequency: 13 of 13 recent papers; 10 of 10 older papers. Every paper has one.

Ready answer · the form that keeps coming back

15 of the 24 parts are, after tidying, \(x\,y^{\prime}+k\,y=c\,x^{n}\) (or a sum of such right sides). Then \(\mu=x^{k}\) and

\[\begin{aligned}&(x^ky)^{\prime}=c\,x^{n+k-1}\\&\Rightarrow\ y=\frac{c\,x^{n}}{n+k}+\frac{C}{x^{k}}\quad(n+k\ne0).\end{aligned}\]

If \(n+k=0\): \(y=\dfrac{c\ln|x|+C}{x^{k}}\) (May 2022). Negative \(k\) works the same: \(xy^{\prime}-y\) has \(\mu=\frac1x\).

Use it to check your answer in seconds. In the exam still show standard form, \(\mu\), \((\mu y)^{\prime}=\mu Q\) and the integration: those steps carry the marks. \(e^{k\ln|x|}=|x|^k\); dropping the absolute value is fine because any constant multiple of \(\mu\) works.

Past-paper questions

Every solution below follows the same steps: standard form \(y^{\prime}+P(x)y=Q(x)\) → \(\mu=e^{\int P\,dx}\) → \((\mu y)^{\prime}=\mu Q\) → integrate with \(+C\) → divide by \(\mu\).

May 2026 Q6(a)Q6: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[x\frac{dy}{dx}+2y=x^2\ln(x).\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: divide by \(x\) to get \(y^{\prime}+\frac2x y=x\ln x\), then find \(\mu=e^{\int 2/x\,dx}\). The last integral needs integration by parts.

Solution

1. Standard form. Divide by \(x\) (\(x\gt 0\) because of \(\ln x\)):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac2xy=x\ln x.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{\int\frac2x\,dx}=e^{2\ln x}=x^2.\)

3. Multiply by \(\mu\). The left side becomes a product derivative:

\[\left(x^2y\right)^{\prime}=x^3\ln x.\]

4. Integrate by parts (\(u=\ln x\), \(dv=x^3dx\)):

\[\begin{aligned}x^2y&=\frac{x^4}{4}\ln x-\int\frac{x^3}{4}\,dx\\&=\frac{x^4\ln x}{4}-\frac{x^4}{16}+C.\end{aligned}\]

5. Divide by \(x^2\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac{x^2\ln x}{4}-\frac{x^2}{16}+\frac{C}{x^2}}\]

Marker: write \(\mu\) explicitly and show \((x^2y)^{\prime}=x^3\ln x\); the \(+C\) goes in before dividing by \(x^2\).

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{x^2(4\ln x-1)}{16}+Cx^{-2}\).

Dec 2025 Q14(a)Q14: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Solve the following differential equations: (a) \[x\frac{dy}{dx}+3y=2x^3\quad(x\gt 0).\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: divide by \(x\) to get \(y^{\prime}+\frac3x y=2x^2\); the integrating factor is \(e^{\int 3/x\,dx}\).

Solution

1. Standard form.

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac3xy=2x^2.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{3\ln x}=x^3.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(x^3y\right)^{\prime}=2x^5\\\Rightarrow\ &x^3y=\frac{x^6}{3}+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Divide by \(x^3\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{C}{x^3}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{x^6+3C}{3x^3}\). Fits the ready answer with \(k=3,\ c=2,\ n=3\).

May 2025 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[x\left(\frac{dy}{dx}+1\right)=x^3-3y.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is in the O2 guide.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: expand and move the \(y\) term left: \(xy^{\prime}+3y=x^3-x\). Then divide by \(x\).

Solution

1. Rearrange. Expand: \(xy^{\prime}+x=x^3-3y\), so \(xy^{\prime}+3y=x^3-x\). Divide by \(x\):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac3xy=x^2-1.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{\int\frac3x\,dx}=x^3.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}\left(x^3y\right)^{\prime}&=x^5-x^3\\ x^3y&=\frac{x^6}{6}-\frac{x^4}{4}+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Divide by \(x^3\):

\[\boxed{y=\frac{x^3}{6}-\frac{x}{4}+\frac{C}{x^3}}\]

Marker: the \(x\cdot1\) from the bracket is easy to drop; it gives the \(-\frac{x}{4}\) term.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{2x^6-3x^4+12C}{12x^3}\).

Dec 2024 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; this part 5%

Solve the differential equation: \[x\frac{dy}{dx}-y=x^2\sin(x).\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: divide by \(x\): \(y^{\prime}-\frac1x y=x\sin x\). Note the minus sign when you find \(\mu\).

Solution

1. Standard form.

\[\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac1xy=x\sin x.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{-\int\frac1x\,dx}=e^{-\ln x}=\dfrac1x.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(\frac yx\right)^{\prime}=\sin x\\\Rightarrow\ &\frac yx=-\cos x+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Multiply by \(x\):

\[\boxed{y=-x\cos x+Cx}\]

Marker: \(\mu=\frac1x\), not \(x\): the coefficient of \(y\) is \(-\frac1x\).

Equivalent forms. \(y=x(C-\cos x)\).

Calculate the integrating factor and solve the equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac3xy=5x.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.3.

Hint

2. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(x^3y\right)^{\prime}=5x^4\\\Rightarrow\ &x^3y=x^5+C.\end{aligned}\]

May 2022 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; this part 5%

Calculate the integrating factor and solve the equation: \[x^2\frac{dy}{dx}+xy=1.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is in the O2 guide; Q7(a) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: divide by \(x^2\): \(y^{\prime}+\frac1x y=\frac1{x^2}\). The integral at the end gives a logarithm.

Solution

1. Standard form.

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac1xy=\frac1{x^2}.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{\ln x}=x.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(xy\right)^{\prime}=\frac1x\\\Rightarrow\ &xy=\ln|x|+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Divide by \(x\):

\[\boxed{\mu=x,\quad y=\frac{\ln|x|+C}{x}}\]

Marker: \(\mu=x\) must be stated; dividing by \(x^2\) first is the step most often skipped.

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{\ln x}{x}+\dfrac{C}{x}\) for \(x\gt 0\). This is the \(n+k=0\) case of the ready answer.

Dec 2018 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following differential equation, and find the solution satisfying the given condition \(y(1)=e-1\): \[\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac yx=xe^x.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is under O1.1.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: \(\mu=e^{-\int 1/x\,dx}=\frac1x\). Find the general solution, then put \(x=1\).

Solution

1. Integrating factor. \(P=-\frac1x\), so \(\mu=\dfrac1x.\)

2. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(\frac yx\right)^{\prime}=e^x\\\Rightarrow\ &y=xe^x+Cx.\end{aligned}\]

3. Use \(y(1)=e-1\). \(e+C=e-1\), so \(C=-1\).

\[\boxed{y=xe^x-x}\]

Check. \(y(1)=e-1\). ✓

Equivalent forms. \(y=x(e^x-1)\); general solution \(y=xe^x+Cx\).

May 2018 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Calculate the integrating factor and solve the equation: \[\frac{x^2}{2}\frac{dy}{dx}-xy=1.\]

Other parts: (b) is in the O2 guide; Q7(a) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: multiply by \(\frac{2}{x^2}\): \(y^{\prime}-\frac2x y=\frac2{x^2}\).

Solution

1. Standard form. Multiply by \(\frac2{x^2}\):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac2xy=\frac2{x^2}.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\mu=e^{-2\ln x}=x^{-2}.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(\frac{y}{x^2}\right)^{\prime}=\frac2{x^4}\\\Rightarrow\ &\frac{y}{x^2}=-\frac{2}{3x^3}+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Multiply by \(x^2\):

\[\boxed{\mu=\frac1{x^2},\quad y=-\frac{2}{3x}+Cx^2}\]

Marker: the \(\frac{x^2}{2}\) in front must be divided out, including the \(\frac12\).

Equivalent forms. \(y=\dfrac{3Cx^3-2}{3x}\).

Dec 2017 Q6(b)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following differential equation: \[\cos x\frac{dy}{dx}+y\sin x=2x\cos^2x.\]

Other part of this question: (a) is under O1.1.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: divide by \(\cos x\): \(y^{\prime}+(\tan x)\,y=2x\cos x\); then \(\mu=e^{\int\tan x\,dx}\).

Solution

1. Standard form.

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+(\tan x)\,y=2x\cos x.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\int\tan x\,dx=-\ln|\cos x|\), so \(\mu=e^{-\ln|\cos x|}=\sec x.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}&\left(y\sec x\right)^{\prime}=2x\\\Rightarrow\ &y\sec x=x^2+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Multiply by \(\cos x\):

\[\boxed{y=(x^2+C)\cos x}\]

Marker: show \(\mu=\sec x\) from \(\int\tan x\,dx\); sign errors here give \(\mu=\cos x\), which is wrong.

Equivalent forms. \(y=x^2\cos x+C\cos x\).

May 2017 Q6(a)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Solve the following differential equation: \[\left(x+\frac1x\right)\frac{dy}{dx}+4y=8.\]

Other part of this question: (b) is in the O2 guide.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: \(x+\frac1x=\frac{x^2+1}{x}\), so dividing gives \(y^{\prime}+\frac{4x}{x^2+1}y=\frac{8x}{x^2+1}\).

Solution

1. Standard form. Divide by \(\frac{x^2+1}{x}\):

\[\frac{dy}{dx}+\frac{4x}{x^2+1}y=\frac{8x}{x^2+1}.\]

2. Integrating factor. \(\int\frac{4x}{x^2+1}dx=2\ln(x^2+1)\), so \(\mu=(x^2+1)^2.\)

3. Multiply and integrate.

\[\begin{aligned}\left((x^2+1)^2y\right)^{\prime}&=8x(x^2+1)\\(x^2+1)^2y&=2(x^2+1)^2+C.\end{aligned}\]

4. Divide by \((x^2+1)^2\):

\[\boxed{y=2+\frac{C}{(x^2+1)^2}}\]

Equivalent forms. It is also separable: \(\frac{dy}{8-4y}=\frac{x\,dx}{x^2+1}\) gives the same answer.

Solve the first order equation \[\frac{dy}{dx}-\frac yx=x^2\cos x.\]

Other parts: Dec 2016 (b) and May 2016 (b) are in the O2 guide; May 2016 (c) is under O1.3.

Hint

Type O1.2, linear first order. First step: \(\mu=\frac1x\). The integral \(\int x\cos x\,dx\) needs integration by parts.

Solution

1. Integrating factor. \(P=-\frac1x\), so \(\mu=\dfrac1x.\)

2. Multiply.

\[\left(\frac yx\right)^{\prime}=x\cos x.\]

3. Integrate by parts (\(u=x\), \(dv=\cos x\,dx\)):

\[\frac yx=x\sin x+\cos x+C.\]

4. Multiply by \(x\):

\[\boxed{y=x^2\sin x+x\cos x+Cx}\]

Equivalent forms. \(y=x\left(x\sin x+\cos x+C\right)\).

O1.3Exact equations: find F(x, y)

Chapter note · exact equations · method in the cards

Frequency: 8 of 13 recent papers; 2 of 10 older papers. Every one so far has been exact as printed; no integrating factor needed. (Dec 2018 Q6(b) looks similar but is not exact: it is under O1.1.)

Ready answer · shortcut for checking Beyond the handouts

If \(M\,dx+N\,dy=0\) is exact, then \(F\) = (all terms of \(\int M\,dx\)) + (the terms of \(\int N\,dy\) that contain no \(x\)). Answer \(F(x,y)=C\).

Example: \((2xy+3)\,dx+(x^2-1)\,dy\): \(\int M\,dx=x^2y+3x\); \(\int N\,dy=x^2y-y\), new term \(-y\); so \(F=x^2y+3x-y\). Use it to check. In the exam show the \(g(y)\) steps: test, integrate, differentiate, compare.

Past-paper questions

Every solution below follows the same steps: test \(M_y=N_x\) → \(F=\int M\,dx+g(y)\) → set \(F_y=N\) to find \(g\) → answer \(F=C\).

May 2026 Q6(b)Q6: 10 pts; part marks not shown

Solve the differential equation: \[\begin{aligned}&\left(e^x+\ln y+\frac yx\right)dx\\&\quad+\left(\frac xy+\ln x+\sin y\right)dy=0.\end{aligned}\]

Other part of this question: (a) is under O1.2.

Hint

Type O1.3, exact equation. First step: with \(M=e^x+\ln y+\frac yx\) and \(N=\frac xy+\ln x+\sin y\), compare \(\frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\) and \(\frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\).

Solution

1. Exact test. \(M_y=\dfrac1y+\dfrac1x\) and \(N_x=\dfrac1y+\dfrac1x\). Equal, so the equation is exact.

2. Integrate \(M\) in \(x\) (treat \(y\) as a constant):

\[F=e^x+x\ln y+y\ln x+g(y).\]

3. Find \(g\). \(F_y=\dfrac xy+\ln x+g^{\prime}(y)\) must equal \(N\), so \(g^{\prime}(y)=\sin y\) and \(g(y)=-\cos y\).

\[\boxed{e^x+x\ln y+y\ln x-\cos y=C}\]

Marker: show the test \(M_y=N_x\) with both partial derivatives written out; the answer is the equation \(F=C\).

Equivalent forms. Any constant moved across, e.g. \(e^x+x\ln y+y\ln x=\cos y+C\). (Here \(x,y\gt 0\).)

Solve the differential equation: \[\begin{aligned}&e^x(x^4y^2+4x^3y^2+1)\,dx\\&\quad+(2x^4ye^x+2y)\,dy=0.\end{aligned}\]

Other part of this question: (a) is under O1.2.

Hint

Type O1.3, exact equation. First step: the exact test. Then integrate the simpler bracket, \(N=2x^4ye^x+2y\), with respect to \(y\).

Solution

1. Exact test. \(M_y=e^x(2x^4y+8x^3y)\) and \(N_x=2y(4x^3e^x+x^4e^x)\). Equal, so exact.

2. Integrate \(N\) in \(y\) (easier than \(M\) in \(x\)):

\[F=x^4y^2e^x+y^2+h(x).\]

3. Find \(h\). \(F_x=(4x^3+x^4)y^2e^x+h^{\prime}(x)\) must equal \(M=e^x(x^4y^2+4x^3y^2)+e^x\), so \(h^{\prime}=e^x\), \(h=e^x\).

\[\boxed{x^4y^2e^x+e^x+y^2=C}\]

Marker: either order of integration is fine; integrating \(M\) in \(x\) first needs \(\int x^4e^x\,dx\) by parts, which is slow.

Equivalent forms. \(e^x(x^4y^2+1)+y^2=C\).

May 2016 Q6(c)Q6: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find the general solution of the following 'exact' equation: \[\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{5x^4-y^2}{2xy+3y^2}.\]

Other parts: (a) is under O1.2; (b) is in the O2 guide.

Hint

Type O1.3, exact equation. First step: cross-multiply into \(M\,dx+N\,dy=0\) form: \((y^2-5x^4)\,dx+(2xy+3y^2)\,dy=0\). Watch the sign of \(M\).

Solution

1. Rewrite. \((2xy+3y^2)\,dy=(5x^4-y^2)\,dx\), so \(M=y^2-5x^4\), \(N=2xy+3y^2\).

2. Exact test. \(M_y=2y=N_x\). Exact.

3. Integrate \(M\) in \(x\):

\[F=xy^2-x^5+g(y).\]

4. Find \(g\). \(F_y=2xy+g^{\prime}(y)=2xy+3y^2\), so \(g=y^3\).

\[\boxed{xy^2-x^5+y^3=C}\]

Marker: with the wrong sign (\(M=5x^4-y^2\)) the test fails: that is the signal you moved a term wrongly.
