MATH1851 Exam Guide · Differentiation Rules

All 15 differentiation-rule question parts from the past papers (2013–2026), sorted into three types, each type with a fixed procedure and every question with a hint and a full worked solution.

The short version

What to expect

Differentiation rules appear as their own question parts in 5 of the 13 recent papers, but they are inside almost every calculus question: curve sketching, L'Hôpital, extreme values and integration all need a derivative first. The parts asked directly are short and routine: differentiate a formula (L6.1), find a slope or tangent line of a curve given by an equation in \(x\) and \(y\) (L6.2), or work out \(h'(a)\) from a table of values of an unknown \(f\) (L6.3, older papers only). Each type below has a standard procedure. The marks are lost on slips, not on ideas, so the procedures stress the order of the rules and the checks.

How to practise: use Start here to find the type, work the question with that type's procedure open, then check with the Hint and the Solution. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the rules themselves, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: one part of a Part I question, most often Q2 (May 2019, Dec 2019, Dec 2024; Dec 2024 Q2(b) is 5%). May 2026 has a whole question on it (Q2, "find the derivatives … using any method") and a long question on an astroid (Q5(a)–(c)); Dec 2025 asks a slope in multiple choice (MC5).
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Part I Q1–3 answers only. Q2 there is pure L6.1: only the final derivative scores, so one dropped chain-rule factor loses the whole part. Written papers ask you to "show your calculation that illustrates the use of differentiation rules" (Dec 2019), so name each rule as you use it.
  • Formula sheet: every paper prints trig identities. Most papers (May 2019, Dec 2020, Dec 2021, May 2024, May 2025, Dec 2025 and most older papers) also print the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\) and usually \(\tan^{-1}x\). No paper prints the product, quotient or chain rule, or the derivatives of \(e^x\), \(\ln x\), \(a^x\). May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2024 and May 2026 print no derivatives at all. Learn the table below.
  • Notation: \(y'\), \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) and "the slope at the point" all mean the same thing. "The tangent line" means its equation, not just its slope. \(\exp(x)\) means \(e^x\).

L6.1Differentiate a formula: product, quotient and chain rules

Chapter note · product and quotient rules · Chapter note · chain rule

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, Dec 2019, May 2026); 0 of 10 older papers. On older papers the same skill is asked inside other questions (curve sketching, L'Hôpital).

Recognise it

An explicit formula \(f(x)=\dots\) and the words "find \(f'(x)\)", "evaluate \(f'(a)\)" or "find the derivative".

Standard procedure
  1. Clean up the formula first.
    • 1a. Absolute value \(|u|\) and a value \(f'(a)\) asked: find the sign of \(u\) near \(x=a\) and replace \(|u|\) by \(u\) (if positive) or \(-u\) (if negative). Then differentiate an ordinary formula. Asked: May 2026.
    • 1b. Piecewise function: differentiate each formula only on its own open interval (\(x\gt0\), \(x\lt0\)). The join point itself is not done by the rules (L5 guide). Asked: Dec 2019.
    • 1c. Otherwise go to step 2.
  2. Name the last operation you would do if you calculated \(f(x)\) for a number \(x\) (outside-in). That operation decides the first rule.
    • 2a. Product \(uv\): \((uv)'=u'v+uv'\). Asked: May 2026 (b), Dec 2019 (ii).
    • 2b. Quotient \(\frac uv\): \(\left(\frac uv\right)'=\dfrac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}\). If the bottom is a constant, just take the constant out. Asked: May 2026 (c), May 2019.
    • 2c. Function of a function \(F(g(x))\): chain rule, \(F'(g(x))\cdot g'(x)\) ("derivative of the outside, inside kept, times derivative of the inside"). Asked: Dec 2019 (i).
  3. Differentiate each piece \(u\), \(v\) or \(g\), going back to step 2 for each piece that is itself a product, quotient or composition. Every inside function gives one extra chain-rule factor.
  4. If a value \(f'(a)\) is asked:
    • 4a. \(a\) is not in the domain of \(f\) (a zero denominator, \(\ln\) of a non-positive number): \(f'(a)\) does not exist. Say why in words. Asked: May 2019 (ii).
    • 4b. Otherwise substitute \(x=a\) before simplifying; terms with a factor \(\sin0\) or \(0\) vanish at once. Asked: May 2019 (i), May 2026 (a).
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2019 Q2(b), Q2: 10%. Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{\sin(x^2-1)}{x^3+1}\). (i) Evaluate \(f'(1)\). (ii) Explain (in a few words) whether \(f'(-1)\) exists.

Step 1c No \(|\cdot|\) and no pieces: go on.

Always look first: an \(|\cdot|\) or a piecewise formula changes the whole plan.

Step 2b The last operation is the division, so the quotient rule comes first, with \(u=\sin(x^2-1)\) and \(v=x^3+1\).

Outside-in: to compute \(f(2)\) you would find the top, find the bottom, and divide last.

Step 3 · 2c \(u\) is a function of a function (sine of \(x^2-1\)), so the chain rule gives \(u'=\cos(x^2-1)\cdot2x\). Also \(v'=3x^2\). Then

\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac{N(x)}{(x^3+1)^2},\ \text{where}\\N(x)&=2x\cos(x^2-1)\,(x^3+1)\\&\quad-3x^2\sin(x^2-1).\end{aligned}\]

The factor \(2x\) is the chain-rule factor. It is the most common slip in this type.

Step 4b At \(x=1\): \(\cos0=1\), \(\sin0=0\), \(x^3+1=2\):

\[f'(1)=\frac{2\cdot1\cdot2-0}{2^2}=1.\]
\[\boxed{f'(1)=1}\]

Substituting before simplifying kills the \(\sin0\) term at once and saves time.

Step 4a At \(x=-1\) the bottom \(x^3+1=0\), so \(f(-1)\) is not defined. A function must be defined at a point to have a derivative there.

\[\boxed{f'(-1)\text{ does not exist}}\]

"A few words" means exactly this reason. Do not try to compute a limit: even though the top is also 0 there, \(f(-1)\) itself is missing.

Traps
  • Dropping the inside factor. \(\frac{d}{dx}\cos5x=-5\sin5x\), not \(-\sin5x\) (May 2026 Q2(b)); \(\frac{d}{dx}\sin(x^2-1)\) needs the \(2x\) (May 2019 Q2(b)). On an answers-only paper this costs the whole part.
  • Differentiating \(|u|\) as if it were \(u\). In May 2026 Q2(a), \(|1-2x|=2x-1\) near \(x=1.5\); treating it as \(1-2x\) gives \(p'(1.5)=-5\) instead of \(-1\). Decide the signs first.
  • Quotient-rule order. The top is \(u'v-uv'\) (derivative of the top first). Swapping gives the negative of the answer.

Inverse trig: \(\frac{d}{dx}\sin^{-1}x=\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\cos^{-1}x=-\frac1{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}x=\frac1{1+x^2}\). Printed on most papers, but not on May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2024 or May 2026, so learn them too (L7 guide).

Beyond the handouts Hyperbolic functions (May 2026): \(\sinh x=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}2\), \(\cosh x=\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}2\), so \((\sinh x)'=\cosh x\) and \((\cosh x)'=\sinh x\) (no minus sign).

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q2(a)Q2: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the derivatives of the following functions using any method. (a) Find \(p'(1.5)\) if \(p(x)=|1-2x|-|x^2-1|\).

Hint

Type L6.1, step 1a. First step: find the signs of \(1-2x\) and \(x^2-1\) at \(x=1.5\), and rewrite \(p\) without absolute values near there.

Solution

Step 1a Near \(x=1.5\): \(1-2x\approx-2\lt0\), so \(|1-2x|=2x-1\); \(x^2-1\approx1.25\gt0\), so \(|x^2-1|=x^2-1\). Hence near 1.5,

\[p(x)=(2x-1)-(x^2-1)=2x-x^2.\]

Step 3–4b \(p'(x)=2-2x\), so \(p'(1.5)=2-3\).

\[\boxed{p'(1.5)=-1}\]

Marker: answers only, so only \(-1\) scores. The rewrite is valid because both signs stay fixed on an open interval around 1.5 (both hold for all \(x\gt1\)), which is what lets you differentiate.

May 2026 Q2(b)Q2: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the derivatives of the following functions using any method. (b) Find \(f'(x)\) if \(f(x)=x^3\cos5x\).

Hint

Type L6.1, product rule (step 2a). First step: \(u=x^3\), \(v=\cos5x\); \(v\) needs the chain rule.

Solution

Step 2a Product rule with \(u=x^3\), \(v=\cos5x\).

Step 3 \(u'=3x^2\); chain rule: \(v'=-\sin5x\cdot5\).

\[\boxed{f'(x)=3x^2\cos5x-5x^3\sin5x}\]

Equivalent forms. \(x^2(3\cos5x-5x\sin5x)\).

May 2026 Q2(c)Q2: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the derivatives of the following functions using any method. (c) Find \(g'(x)\) if \(g(x)=\dfrac{\sinh x}{3x+2}\).

Beyond the handouts \(\sinh\) is not in the calculus handouts. Parts (d) and (e) of this question are in the L7 guide.

Hint

Type L6.1, quotient rule (step 2b). First step: write \(\sinh x=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}\) to find its derivative, then use \(u=\sinh x\), \(v=3x+2\).

Solution

Step 3 Define \(\sinh\) and \(\cosh\). \(\sinh x=\dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}\) and \(\cosh x=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}\). Differentiating, \((\sinh x)'=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}=\cosh x\).

Step 2b Quotient rule with \(u=\sinh x\), \(u'=\cosh x\), \(v=3x+2\), \(v'=3\):

\[\boxed{g'(x)=\frac{(3x+2)\cosh x-3\sinh x}{(3x+2)^2}}\]

Marker: answers only; any correct equivalent form scores. Valid for \(x\ne-\frac23\).

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac{\cosh x}{3x+2}-\dfrac{3\sinh x}{(3x+2)^2}\); in exponentials, \(\dfrac{(3x+2)(e^x+e^{-x})-3(e^x-e^{-x})}{2(3x+2)^2}\).

Dec 2019 Q2(a)Q2: 10%; part marks not shown

Let

\[f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(x+b)^{3/2}-x^{3/2}}{b}, & x\ge0,\\[2mm] 2+x\sin\dfrac{b}{x}, & x\lt0,\end{cases}\]

where \(b\gt0\) is a constant. (a) Evaluate (i) \(f'(x)\) for \(x\gt0\), (ii) \(f'(x)\) for \(x\lt0\). [Show your calculation that illustrates the use of differentiation rules.]

Part (b) is in the L2 guide and part (c) (differentiability at 0) in the L5 guide.

Hint

Type L6.1, piecewise (step 1b). First step: on \(x\gt0\) use only the top formula, with \(b\) a constant; on \(x\lt0\) the bottom formula is a product, \(x\cdot\sin\frac bx\).

Solution

Step 1b On the open interval \(x\gt0\) only the first formula matters; on \(x\lt0\) only the second. \(b\) is a constant.

Step 2c–3 (i) For \(x\gt0\): take \(\frac1b\) out. Chain rule on \((x+b)^{3/2}\) (inside derivative 1):

\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\frac1b\left[\tfrac32(x+b)^{1/2}-\tfrac32x^{1/2}\right]\\&=\frac{3}{2b}\left(\sqrt{x+b}-\sqrt x\right).\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{f'(x)=\frac{3}{2b}\left(\sqrt{x+b}-\sqrt x\right)}\]

Step 2a–3 (ii) For \(x\lt0\): product rule on \(x\sin\frac bx\); chain rule on \(\sin\frac bx\), where \(\frac{d}{dx}\frac bx=-\frac{b}{x^2}\):

\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=1\cdot\sin\frac bx+x\cos\frac bx\cdot\left(-\frac b{x^2}\right)\\&=\sin\frac bx-\frac bx\cos\frac bx.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{f'(x)=\sin\frac bx-\frac bx\cos\frac bx}\]

Marker: the bracket asks you to show the rules: write "product rule" and "chain rule" where you use them. The constant 2 differentiates to 0.

Equivalent forms. (i) \(\dfrac{3(\sqrt{x+b}-\sqrt x)}{2b}\), or \(\dfrac{3}{2(\sqrt{x+b}+\sqrt x)}\) after rationalising. (ii) \(\dfrac{x\sin(b/x)-b\cos(b/x)}{x}\).

May 2019 Q2(b)Q2: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(f(x)=\dfrac{\sin(x^2-1)}{x^3+1}\). (b) (i) Evaluate \(f'(1)\). (ii) Explain (in a few words) whether \(f'(-1)\) exists.

L6.2Implicit differentiation, tangent lines and tangent-line approximation

Chapter note · implicit differentiation · Chapter note · tangent lines

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2024, Dec 2025, May 2026); 3 of 10 older papers (May 2015, May 2017, May 2018). The most common L6 type.

Recognise it

A curve given by an equation in \(x\) and \(y\) that you cannot easily solve for \(y\) (\(x^2+2y^3=\frac3{xy}\), \(y=2\sin(\pi x-y)\)), and "find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\)", "the slope at", "the tangent line at", or "approximate using the tangent line".

Standard procedure
  1. Fix the point \((x_0,y_0)\). Substitute it into the equation to check it is on the curve. If only \(x_0\) is given, solve the equation for \(y_0\). Asked: May 2015.
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\). Treat \(y\) as a function of \(x\): by the chain rule \(\frac{d}{dx}y^n=ny^{n-1}y'\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\sin y=\cos y\cdot y'\), \(\frac{d}{dx}e^{y}=e^{y}y'\); a mixed term like \(xy\) needs the product rule, \((xy)'=y+xy'\). If the equation has a fraction, multiply it out first (fewer quotient rules).
  3. Get \(y'\).
    • 3a. \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) asked "in terms of \(x\) and \(y\)": collect the \(y'\) terms on one side, factor out \(y'\), divide. Asked: May 2026 (a).
    • 3b. Only the slope at a point is needed: substitute \((x_0,y_0)\) now, before solving. The equation becomes a simple linear equation in the number \(y'\). Asked: Dec 2025 (ends here), Dec 2024, May 2018, May 2017, May 2015.
  4. Tangent line: \(y-y_0=m(x-x_0)\) with \(m=y'\) at the point. Give the line as an equation. Asked: May 2026 (b), Dec 2024, May 2018, May 2017, May 2015 (i).
  5. Follow-up questions on the tangent line:
    • 5a. Tangent-line approximation of a nearby point: for \(x\) close to \(x_0\), \(y\approx y_0+m(x-x_0)\), i.e. read \(y\) off the tangent line instead of the curve. Asked: May 2026 (c).
    • 5b. How many points does the line meet the curve? Substitute the line into the curve's equation to get one equation \(\varphi(x)=0\). Use the sign of \(\varphi'\) to split the line into pieces where \(\varphi\) only increases or only decreases; each piece has at most one root (L8 idea). Asked: May 2015 (ii).
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2024 Q2(b), 5%. Find the equation of the tangent line to \(x^2+2y^3=\dfrac{3}{xy}\) at the point \((1,1)\).

Step 1 At \((1,1)\): left side \(1+2=3\), right side \(\frac31=3\). The point is on the curve.

One line of checking. It also catches a misread question before you spend time on it.

Step 2 Near \((1,1)\), \(xy\ne0\), so multiply both sides by \(xy\):

\[x^3y+2xy^4=3.\]

Differentiate with respect to \(x\) (product rule on each term; chain rule on \(y^4\)):

\[3x^2y+x^3y'+2y^4+8xy^3y'=0.\]

Clearing the fraction first avoids differentiating \(\frac{3}{xy}\) with the quotient and product rules together. Say why it is allowed (\(xy\ne0\)).

Step 3b Substitute \(x=y=1\): \(3+y'+2+8y'=0\), so \(9y'=-5\) and

\[y'=-\frac59.\]

Substituting before solving turns the algebra into a one-line linear equation.

Step 4 Tangent line: \(y-1=-\frac59(x-1)\).

\[\boxed{y=-\frac59x+\frac{14}9}\]

Equivalent: \(5x+9y=14\). The question says "equation", so a slope alone scores only part.

Traps
  • Forgetting \(y'\) on a \(y\) term. \(\frac{d}{dx}\sin(\pi x-y)=\cos(\pi x-y)\cdot(\pi-y')\), not \(\cdot\pi\) (May 2018 Q4(a)).
  • Treating \(xy\) or \(e^{xy}\) as if \(y\) were a constant. \(\frac{d}{dx}e^{xy}=e^{xy}(y+xy')\) (Dec 2025 MC5).
  • Giving only the slope when the line is asked, or using the curve instead of the tangent line in an approximation (May 2026 Q5(c) wants \(0.407\), the line's value, not the true \(0.410\)).

May 2026 Q5(a)Q5: 10 pts, long question; part marks not shown

Provide detailed arguments. Consider the graph of the astroid \(x^{\frac23}+y^{\frac23}=1\), as shown in the figure below:

(a) Find \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\) in terms of \(x\) and \(y\).

Parts (d) and (e) of this question are in the L16 and L17 guides.

Hint

Type L6.2, step 3a. First step: differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\); \(\frac{d}{dx}y^{2/3}=\frac23y^{-1/3}\,y'\).

Solution

Step 2 Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\) (chain rule on \(y^{2/3}\)):

\[\tfrac23x^{-1/3}+\tfrac23y^{-1/3}\,y'=0.\]

Step 3a Solve for \(y'\) (for \(x\ne0\), \(y\ne0\)):

\[y'=-\frac{x^{-1/3}}{y^{-1/3}}.\]
\[\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{y^{1/3}}{x^{1/3}}=-\left(\frac yx\right)^{1/3}}\]

Marker: state the restriction \(x\ne0\), \(y\ne0\): at the four cusps \((\pm1,0)\), \((0,\pm1)\) the formula breaks down.

Equivalent forms. \(-\sqrt[3]{\dfrac yx}\).

May 2026 Q5(b)Q5: 10 pts, long question; part marks not shown

Provide detailed arguments. Consider the graph of the astroid \(x^{\frac23}+y^{\frac23}=1\), as shown in the figure below:

(b) Find the equation of the line tangent to the graph at the point \(A=\left(\frac1{\sqrt8},\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)=(0.35,0.35)\).

Hint

Type L6.2, step 4. First step: put \(x=y=\frac1{\sqrt8}\) into the \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) from part (a).

Solution

Step 1 \(A\) is on the curve: \(\left(\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)^{2/3}=\left(\frac18\right)^{1/3}=\frac12\), and \(\frac12+\frac12=1\).

Step 3 From (a), at \(A\) \(x=y\), so the slope is \(m=-1\).

Step 4 \(y-\frac1{\sqrt8}=-\left(x-\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)\), so \(y=-x+\frac2{\sqrt8}\).

\[\boxed{y=-x+\frac1{\sqrt2}}\]

Equivalent forms. \(x+y=\frac{\sqrt2}2\approx0.707\); \(y=-x+\frac2{\sqrt8}\).

May 2026 Q5(c)Q5: 10 pts, long question; part marks not shown

Provide detailed arguments. Consider the graph of the astroid \(x^{\frac23}+y^{\frac23}=1\), as shown in the figure below:

(b) Find the equation of the line tangent to the graph at the point \(A=\left(\frac1{\sqrt8},\frac1{\sqrt8}\right)=(0.35,0.35)\).
(c) Let \(B=\left(\frac3{10},y^*\right)=(0.3,y^*)\). Using the tangent line in part (b) to approximate the value of \(y^*\) in \(B\).

Hint

Type L6.2, step 5a. First step: \(x=0.3\) is close to \(x_0=\frac1{\sqrt8}\approx0.354\), so put \(x=0.3\) into the tangent line from (b).

Solution

Step 5a Near \(A\) the curve is close to its tangent line \(y=-x+\frac1{\sqrt2}\). Since \(B\) lies on the curve with \(x=0.3\) near \(\frac1{\sqrt8}\approx0.354\),

\[y^*\approx-0.3+\frac1{\sqrt2}\approx0.407.\]
\[\boxed{y^*\approx\frac1{\sqrt2}-0.3\approx0.407}\]

Marker: name the idea ("tangent-line approximation", also called linear approximation; the handouts use the idea without the name) and say why it is reasonable: \(0.3\) is close to \(x_0\).

Check. Solving the curve exactly, \(y^*=\left(1-0.3^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\approx0.410\), so the approximation is good to about \(0.003\).

Dec 2025 MC5Part I multiple choice: 2 pts

A curve is defined by the equation \(e^{xy}\cdot\sin(y)=x+2y\). What is the slope at the point \((0,0)\)?
(A) \(1\)   (B) \(0\)   (C) \(-1\)   (D) \(2\)   (E) Does not exist

Hint

Type L6.2, step 3b. First step: differentiate both sides (product rule on the left; \(\frac{d}{dx}e^{xy}=e^{xy}(y+xy')\)), then put \(x=y=0\) at once.

Solution

Step 1 \((0,0)\): \(e^0\sin0=0=0+0\). On the curve.

Step 2 Differentiate:

\[\begin{aligned}&e^{xy}(y+xy')\sin y+e^{xy}\cos y\cdot y'\\&\quad=1+2y'.\end{aligned}\]

Step 3b At \((0,0)\) the first term has \(\sin0=0\): \(y'=1+2y'\), so \(y'=-1\).

\[\boxed{\text{(C)}\ \ -1}\]

Marker: multiple choice, only the letter scores. Substituting before solving makes this a 30-second question.

May 2015 Q2(b)Q2: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Consider the curve \(C\) defined by the equation \(ye^y=e^x-1\).
i. Find the tangent line \(L\) to the curve \(C\) at \(x=0\).
ii. How many points does \(L\) intersect \(C\)? Explain your answer.

Part (ii) also uses the "monotonic, so at most one root" idea of L8.3; the L8 guide points here.

Hint

Type L6.2, steps 1 and 5b. First step: at \(x=0\), \(ye^y=0\) gives \(y_0\). For (ii), put the line into the equation of \(C\) and study the resulting function of \(x\) with its derivative.

Solution

Step 1 At \(x=0\): \(ye^y=0\). Since \(e^y\gt0\), \(y=0\). Point \((0,0)\).

Step 2 Product rule on the left: \((e^y+ye^y)\,y'=e^x\), i.e. \((1+y)e^y\,y'=e^x\).

Step 3b–4 At \((0,0)\): \(y'=1\). So \(L\): \(y=x\).

\[\boxed{\text{(i) } L:\ y=x}\]

Step 5b A point on both has \(y=x\) and \(xe^x=e^x-1\), i.e.

\[\varphi(x)=(x-1)e^x+1=0.\]

\(\varphi'(x)=e^x+(x-1)e^x=xe^x\): negative for \(x\lt0\), positive for \(x\gt0\). So \(\varphi\) is strictly decreasing on \((-\infty,0]\) and strictly increasing on \([0,\infty)\), with minimum \(\varphi(0)=0\). Hence \(\varphi(x)\gt0\) for every \(x\ne0\), and \(x=0\) is the only root.

\[\boxed{\text{(ii) exactly one point, }(0,0)}\]

Marker: "explain" needs the monotonicity argument (sign of \(\varphi'\)); finding \(x=0\) by inspection alone does not show there are no other points.

Equivalent forms. "One point": \(L\) meets \(C\) only at its point of tangency, the origin.

L6.3Derivative values from given data (f(2) = 3, f′(2) = 2, …)

Chapter note · chain rule · Chapter note · product and quotient rules

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 2 of 10 older papers (May 2017, May 2018). Recent papers ask the same skill with \(\ln\), \(e^x\) or inverse functions (Dec 2020 Q4(a), Dec 2025 MC4); those are in the L7 guide.

Recognise it

An unknown function \(f\) with only some numbers given (\(f(2)=3\), \(f'(2)=2\), or a rule like \(f(r)=r\)), and a new function \(h\) built from \(f\); "evaluate \(h'(2)\)".

Standard procedure
  1. Differentiate \(h\) with the rules (L6.1 steps 2–3), keeping \(f\) and \(f'\) as symbols. A composition inside \(f\) gives a chain-rule factor: \(\frac{d}{dx}f(x^2)=f'(x^2)\cdot2x\).
  2. Substitute \(x=a\) and write down exactly which values you need. Inside \(f\), the argument changes: at \(x=2\), \(f(x^2)\) needs \(f(4)\) and \(f'(4)\), not \(f(2)\).
  3. Read those values from the data and compute. Asked: May 2018, May 2017.
Traps
  • Wrong argument. In May 2018 Q5(b)(i), \(h(x)=\frac{f(x^2)}{x}\) at \(x=2\) needs \(f(4)=4\) and \(f'(4)=5\). Using \(f(2)\), \(f'(2)\) gives a wrong answer.
  • Substituting too early. Differentiate first, then put in numbers. \(f(2)=3\) is a number; its derivative is not \(f'(2)\).

Past-paper questions

No recent paper has asked this type with plain \(f\) and \(f'\) data; see the L7 guide for Dec 2020 Q4(a) and Dec 2025 MC4.

Let \(f\) be a twice differentiable function such that \(f(r)=r\), \(f'(r)=r+1\) for \(r=0,1,2,3,\cdots\).
(i) Evaluate \(h'(2)\) if \(h(x)=\dfrac{f(x^2)}{x}\).

Part (ii) is in the L13 guide.

Hint

Type L6.3. First step: quotient rule with \(u=f(x^2)\) (chain rule: \(u'=2xf'(x^2)\)) and \(v=x\); then note that \(x=2\) makes the argument \(x^2=4\).

Solution

Step 1 Quotient rule, chain rule on top:

\[\begin{aligned}h'(x)&=\frac{2xf'(x^2)\cdot x-f(x^2)\cdot1}{x^2}\\&=\frac{2x^2f'(x^2)-f(x^2)}{x^2}.\end{aligned}\]

Step 2 At \(x=2\): \(h'(2)=\dfrac{8f'(4)-f(4)}{4}\). Needed: \(f(4)\), \(f'(4)\).

Step 3 With \(r=4\): \(f(4)=4\), \(f'(4)=5\). So \(h'(2)=\dfrac{40-4}{4}\).

\[\boxed{h'(2)=9}\]

Marker: show the chain-rule factor \(2x\) and the values \(f(4)\), \(f'(4)\) you used.

Equivalent forms. Writing \(h(x)=x^{-1}f(x^2)\) and using the product rule gives the same \(2f'(4)-\frac{f(4)}4=10-1=9\).
