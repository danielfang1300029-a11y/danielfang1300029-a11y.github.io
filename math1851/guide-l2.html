MATH1851 Exam Guide · Sandwich Theorem and sin x / x

The short version

What to expect

These limits are in 5 of the 13 recent papers and 2 of the 10 older ones, almost always as one part of Q1 ("Evaluate the limit, or explain why it does not exist"). There are only two types. L2.1: a factor such as \(\sin\frac1x\) that keeps swinging between \(-1\) and \(1\) (a bounded wiggle) multiplied by something that shrinks to 0; the Sandwich Theorem (also called the Squeeze Theorem) gives 0. L2.2: \(\sin\), \(\tan\) or \(1-\cos\) of a small quantity (the box); you rewrite so that each \(\sin(\text{box})\) sits over the same box and use \(\frac{\sin u}{u}\to1\). The numbers change every time, but the moves do not, so each type below has a standard procedure to follow step by step.

How to practise: use Start here to name the type, work the question with the procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and the Solution at the end. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind the theorems, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Part I Q1 limit parts (Q1 is 10%; Dec 2024 shows 5% for its part) on May 2024, Dec 2024, May 2025, Dec 2021, May 2017 and Dec 2018; Dec 2019 asks it inside Q2, a piecewise function question. Not asked as its own part on Dec 2025 or May 2026, but the same moves appear inside many L1 and L10 limits.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Q1–3 answers only ("a number, \(\infty\), \(-\infty\), or non-existence"). There only the final value scores. On written papers the opposite holds: "Indicate clearly the theorems you have used" (Dec 2021), "Write down the names of all the theorems you have used" (Dec 2018), "with justification" (May 2024). Name the Sandwich Theorem and the limit laws each time.
  • Formula sheet: every paper prints the trig identities (\(\cos2A=1-2\sin^2A\) and so on); recent sheets such as May 2024 and May 2026 also print the sum-to-product forms, e.g. \(\sin A-\sin B=2\cos\frac{A+B}2\sin\frac{A-B}2\). No paper prints the Sandwich Theorem, the limit laws or the standard limits \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1\), \(\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}\to\frac12\). Learn them.
  • Notation: \(\csc x=\frac1{\sin x}\); \(\sin^nx=(\sin x)^n\); \(x^{-2}=\frac1{x^2}\); "the limit exists" means it is a finite number.

L2.1Bounded wiggle times something shrinking to 0: squeeze

Chapter note · the Sandwich Theorem · Chapter note · x sin(1/x) and other examples

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2019, Dec 2021, May 2024); 2 of 10 older papers (May 2017, Dec 2018).

Recognise it

A \(\sin\) or \(\cos\) of something that blows up as \(x\to0\): \(\sin\frac1x\), \(\cos\frac1x\), \(\sin\frac bx\), \(\sin(x^{-2})\). It swings between \(-1\) and \(1\) infinitely often and has no limit, so the limit laws cannot be used on it directly. Call it the wiggle \(W\); the only thing you know is \(|W|\le1\).

Standard procedure
  1. Find the wiggle and write \(|W|\le1\). Substitute everything else and note what each other piece tends to.
  2. Get the wiggle into one product \(W\cdot S\), where \(S\) is everything multiplying it.
    • 2a. Already a product such as \(x^2\cos\frac1x\) or \(\frac bx\cos\frac bx\): go to step 3. Asked: Dec 2019.
    • 2b. A difference \(\sin A-\sin B\) (or \(\cos A-\cos B\)): use the sum-to-product formula on the sheet, \(\sin A-\sin B=2\cos\frac{A+B}2\sin\frac{A-B}2\). The \(\frac1x\) parts land in the cosine; the rest is a small sine. Asked: May 2024.
    • 2c. Inside a bigger fraction: divide top and bottom by the lowest power of \(x\), or split off a factor with a known limit (such as \(\frac{x}{\ln(1+x)}\to1\) or \(\frac{\sin x}x\to1\)), so each wiggle term becomes \(x^n\cdot W\). Asked: Dec 2021, Dec 2018.
    • 2d. \(\frac{\sin u}{u}\) with the box \(u\to\infty\) (not 0): it is \(\sin u\cdot\frac1u\), and \(\frac1u\to0\). Asked: May 2017.
  3. Does \(S\) tend to 0?
    • 3a. Yes: squeeze. Write \(-|S|\le W\cdot S\le|S|\), note \(\pm|S|\to0\), and conclude \(W\cdot S\to0\) by the Sandwich Theorem. If nothing else is left, stop. Asked: May 2017.
    • 3b. No (\(S\) blows up or tends to a non-zero number): the squeeze fails and the limit does not exist. Prove it with two lists of points \(x_n\to0\), one where the wiggle is \(1\) and one where it is \(-1\) (or \(0\)), giving different values. Asked: Dec 2019.
  4. Put the pieces back with the Sum, Product and Quotient Laws (check the bottom's limit is not 0) and any standard limit. Name every theorem you use. Asked: May 2024, Dec 2021, Dec 2018.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2021 Q1(d) (Q1: 10%). Either evaluate the limit or explain carefully why the limit does not exist. Indicate clearly the theorems you have used in your argument.

\[\lim_{x\to0}\left(x+x^2\sin\frac1x\right)(x+\sin x)^{-1}\]

Step 1 The wiggle is \(W=\sin\frac1x\), with \(|W|\le1\). The top tends to 0 and the bottom \(x+\sin x\) tends to 0: form \(\frac00\).

\(\sin\frac1x\) has no limit, so "top limit over bottom limit" is not available yet. Saying so shows the marker why a theorem is needed.

Step 2c For \(x\ne0\), divide top and bottom by \(x\):

\[\frac{x+x^2\sin\frac1x}{x+\sin x}=\frac{1+x\sin\frac1x}{1+\dfrac{\sin x}{x}}.\]

Dividing by \(x\) is allowed because \(x\ne0\) near the limit. Now the wiggle appears only as \(x\cdot W\), and the bottom holds the standard limit \(\frac{\sin x}x\).

Step 3a Here \(S=x\to0\), so squeeze:

\[-|x|\le x\sin\frac1x\le|x|,\qquad \pm|x|\to0.\]

By the Sandwich Theorem, \(x\sin\frac1x\to0\).

Write the two bounds and their common limit; the theorem's name is part of the answer here ("indicate clearly the theorems").

Step 4 Standard limit \(\frac{\sin x}{x}\to1\) (the L2.2 fact). By the Sum Law the top tends to \(1+0=1\) and the bottom to \(1+1=2\). The bottom's limit is not 0, so by the Quotient Law

\[\boxed{\tfrac12}\]

Common slip: applying L'Hôpital to the original fraction. The new top, \(1+2x\sin\frac1x-\cos\frac1x\), has no limit, so the rule gives no answer.

Traps
  • "The box goes to 0" is not checked. May 2017: \(\frac{\sin(x^{-2})}{x^{-2}}\) is not 1, because \(x^{-2}\to\infty\). It squeezes to 0.
  • L'Hôpital on the whole fraction. Dec 2018 and Dec 2021 are \(\frac00\), but after differentiating, the top contains a bare \(\sin\frac1x\) or \(\cos\frac1x\) with no limit. That does not mean the original limit fails to exist: split first (step 2c).
  • Squeezing when \(S\) does not shrink. Dec 2019: \(x\sin\frac bx\to0\), but its derivative contains \(\frac bx\cos\frac bx\), where \(\frac bx\) blows up. And the reverse, May 2024: \(\sin\frac1x\) has no limit, yet the difference \(\sin\big(bx+\frac1x\big)-\sin\frac1x\) does.
Ready answer · power of \(x\) times a wiggle

If \(|W(x)|\le M\) near 0 and \(n\) is a positive whole number, then

\[-M|x|^n\le x^nW(x)\le M|x|^n,\]

both bounds tend to 0, so \(x^nW(x)\to0\) by the Sandwich Theorem.

It gives \(x\sin\frac1x\to0\) (Dec 2021), \(x\cos\frac1x\to0\) (Dec 2018), \(x^2\sin(x^{-2})\to0\) (May 2017). Copy the three lines, with the actual \(W\), into any written answer.

Past-paper questions

May 2024 Q1(b)Q1: 10%; part marks not shown

Determine, with justification, the value(s) of \(b\) so that \[\lim_{x\to0}\left(\sin\left(bx+\frac1x\right)-\sin\left(\frac1x\right)\right)\] exists.

Part (a)(i) is under L2.2; part (a)(ii) is in the L10 guide.

Hint

Type L2.1, bounded wiggle, step 2b. First step: apply \(\sin A-\sin B=2\cos\frac{A+B}2\sin\frac{A-B}2\) with \(A=bx+\frac1x\), \(B=\frac1x\).

Solution

Step 1 Each sine on its own has no limit (\(\frac1x\) blows up), so the limit laws cannot be used term by term.

Step 2b Sum-to-product, with \(\frac{A+B}2=\frac{bx}2+\frac1x\) and \(\frac{A-B}2=\frac{bx}2\):

\[\begin{aligned}&\sin\Big(bx+\frac1x\Big)-\sin\frac1x\\&=2\cos\Big(\frac{bx}2+\frac1x\Big)\sin\frac{bx}2.\end{aligned}\]

Step 3a Wiggle \(W=\cos\big(\frac{bx}2+\frac1x\big)\), \(|W|\le1\). The other factor \(S=2\sin\frac{bx}2\to2\sin0=0\) for every real \(b\) (sine is continuous). So

\[-2\Big|\sin\frac{bx}2\Big|\le W\cdot S\le2\Big|\sin\frac{bx}2\Big|,\]

both bounds tend to 0, and by the Sandwich Theorem the expression tends to 0.

Step 4 Nothing else is left: the limit exists and equals 0 whatever \(b\) is. (For \(b=0\) the expression is \(0\) for every \(x\).)

\[\boxed{\text{every real }b\text{; the limit is }0}\]

Marker: "with justification" means the identity, the bound \(|W|\le1\) and the Sandwich Theorem by name; answering "no \(b\)" because \(\sin\frac1x\) has no limit is the trap.

Equivalent forms. "\(b\in\mathbb R\)", "for all \(b\)". Check: sympy confirms the identity; at \(b=-3\), \(x=3\times10^{-5}\) the value is about \(-4.6\times10^{-5}\).

Dec 2019 Q2(b)Q2: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(f\) be the function defined in two pieces: for \(x\ge0\), \[f(x)=\frac{(x+b)^{3/2}-x^{3/2}}{b},\] and for \(x\lt0\), \[f(x)=2+x\sin\frac{b}{x},\] where \(b\gt0\) is a constant.

The paper writes \(f\) with one brace (cases); it is split into two lines here to fit a phone screen.

(b) Does \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}f'(x)\) exist? Justify your answer briefly.

Part (a), \(f'(x)\) for \(x\gt0\) and \(x\lt0\), is in the L6 guide; part (c), differentiability at 0, is in the L5 guide.

Hint

Type L2.1, but this is the step 3b case. First step: differentiate \(2+x\sin\frac bx\) (product and chain rules) and look at what multiplies the wiggle \(\cos\frac bx\).

Solution

Step 1–2a From part (a), for \(x\lt0\):

\[\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=\sin\frac bx\\&\quad+x\cos\frac bx\cdot\Big(-\frac b{x^2}\Big)\\&=\sin\frac bx-\frac bx\cos\frac bx.\end{aligned}\]

(For \(x\gt0\), \(f'(x)=\frac{3}{2b}\big(\sqrt{x+b}-\sqrt x\big)\to\frac3{2\sqrt b}\); the right side is fine.)

Step 3b The wiggle \(\cos\frac bx\) is multiplied by \(S=\frac bx\), which blows up: no squeeze. Two lists of points tending to \(0^-\):

\(x_n=-\frac{b}{2n\pi}\): \(\frac b{x_n}=-2n\pi\), so \(\sin\frac b{x_n}=0\), \(\cos\frac b{x_n}=1\) and

\[f'(x_n)=0-(-2n\pi)(1)=2n\pi\to\infty.\]

\(z_n=-\frac{b}{(2n+1)\pi}\): \(\sin\frac b{z_n}=0\), \(\cos\frac b{z_n}=-1\) and

\[\begin{aligned}f'(z_n)&=-\big(-(2n+1)\pi\big)(-1)\\&=-(2n+1)\pi\to-\infty.\end{aligned}\]

So \(\lim_{x\to0^-}f'(x)\) does not exist (not even as \(\infty\)), and therefore neither does the two-sided limit.

\[\boxed{\text{No: it does not exist.}}\]

Marker: "briefly": the formula for \(f'(x)\), \(x\lt0\), plus one sentence that \(\frac bx\cos\frac bx\) swings unboundedly (or the two lists of points) is enough. The right-hand limit alone decides nothing.

Equivalent forms. "The left-hand limit of \(f'\) does not exist." Check: sympy confirms \(f'\) and gives \(f'(x_n)=2n\pi\).

Find \[\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{x^2\cos\frac1x}{\ln(1+x)}.\] Write down the names of all the theorems you have used.

Part (b) of this question is in the L1 guide.

Hint

Type L2.1, bounded wiggle, step 2c. First step: write the fraction as \(\big[x\cos\frac1x\big]\cdot\dfrac{x}{\ln(1+x)}\) and find each factor's limit.

Solution

Step 1 Wiggle \(\cos\frac1x\), \(|\cos\frac1x|\le1\). Top and bottom both tend to 0, but L'Hôpital on the whole fraction gives a top \(2x\cos\frac1x+\sin\frac1x\) with no limit, so split instead.

Step 2c For \(x\gt0\):

\[\frac{x^2\cos\frac1x}{\ln(1+x)}=\Big[x\cos\frac1x\Big]\cdot\frac{x}{\ln(1+x)}.\]

Step 3a \(0\le\big|x\cos\frac1x\big|\le x\) and \(x\to0^+\), so \(x\cos\frac1x\to0\) by the Sandwich Theorem.

Step 4 \(\frac{x}{\ln(1+x)}\) is \(\frac00\); by L'Hôpital's Rule

\[\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac{1}{\frac1{1+x}}=\lim_{x\to0^+}(1+x)=1.\]

By the Product Law, the limit is \(0\cdot1\).

\[\boxed{0}\]

Marker: three theorems to name: Sandwich Theorem, L'Hôpital's Rule (or the standard limit \(\frac{\ln(1+x)}x\to1\)), Product Law.

L2.2sin of a box over the same box: make the boxes match

Chapter note · the limit sin x / x · Chapter note · one-sided limits

Frequency: 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2024, Dec 2024, May 2025); 0 of 10 older papers. Dec 2021 Q1(d) also uses it (counted under L2.1).

Recognise it

\(\sin\), \(\tan\), \(1-\cos\), \(\csc\) or \(\cot\) of a small quantity (the box, which tends to 0), mixed with powers or roots of \(x\). Substituting gives \(\frac00\) or \(0\cdot\infty\).

Standard procedure

The one fact: as the box \(u\to0\), \(\dfrac{\sin u}{u}\to1\). The box on top and the box underneath must be the same.

  1. Substitute and check the box tends to 0. If it tends to \(\infty\) instead, this is L2.1 (step 2d), and the answer is not built on \(\frac{\sin u}u\to1\).
  2. Rewrite in \(\sin\) and \(\cos\). \(\csc u=\frac1{\sin u}\), \(\tan u=\frac{\sin u}{\cos u}\), \(1-\cos u=2\sin^2\frac u2\) (from \(\cos2A=1-2\sin^2A\) on the sheet), and \(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\), not \(x\).
  3. Make the boxes match. Turn each \(\sin u\) into \(\dfrac{\sin u}{u}\cdot u\) with exactly the same \(u\), e.g. \(\sin2x=\frac{\sin2x}{2x}\cdot2x\). Every \(\frac{\sin u}u\) and every \(\cos(\text{box})\) tends to 1.
  4. Collect what is left over (powers of \(x\), numbers, \(\frac{x}{|x|}\)) and finish with the Product and Quotient Laws.
    • 4a. The leftover is a number: that is the answer. Asked: May 2024, Dec 2021 (inside L2.1).
    • 4b. The leftover contains \(\frac{x}{|x|}\): it is \(1\) for \(x\gt0\) and \(-1\) for \(x\lt0\). Find both one-sided limits; if they differ, the limit does not exist. Asked: May 2025.
    • 4c. The leftover is \(x^k\) with a parameter in \(k\): \(k\gt0\) gives 0, \(k=0\) gives the number in front, \(k\lt0\) blows up (no finite limit). Choose the parameter. Asked: Dec 2024.
Worked example · the procedure in action

May 2025 Q1(a) (Q1: 10%). Evaluate the limit, or explain carefully why the limit does not exist.

\[\lim_{x\to0}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{x^2}}\cdot\sin x\]

Step 1 \(\sqrt{1+\frac1{x^2}}\to\infty\) and \(\sin x\to0\): form \(\infty\cdot0\). The box is \(x\to0\).

\(\infty\cdot0\) has no fixed value, so the limit laws cannot finish it yet.

Step 2 For \(x\ne0\),

\[\sqrt{1+\frac1{x^2}}=\sqrt{\frac{x^2+1}{x^2}}=\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{|x|}.\]

\(\sqrt{x^2}=|x|\) because a square root is never negative. Writing \(x\) here is the slip that turns "does not exist" into a wrong answer of 1.

Step 3 Match the box: \(\sin x=\frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot x\), so

\[\begin{aligned}&\sqrt{1+\frac1{x^2}}\,\sin x\\&=\sqrt{x^2+1}\cdot\frac{\sin x}{x}\cdot\frac{x}{|x|}.\end{aligned}\]

Step 4b \(\sqrt{x^2+1}\to1\) and \(\frac{\sin x}x\to1\). The leftover \(\frac{x}{|x|}\) is \(1\) for \(x\gt0\) and \(-1\) for \(x\lt0\). By the Product Law,

\[\begin{aligned}x\to0^+&:\ 1\cdot1\cdot1=1,\\x\to0^-&:\ 1\cdot1\cdot(-1)=-1.\end{aligned}\]

The one-sided limits differ, so the two-sided limit does not exist.

\[\boxed{\text{Does not exist}}\]

(Right-hand limit \(1\), left-hand limit \(-1\).)

"Explain carefully" is answered by the two one-sided values; give both numbers.

Traps
  • \(\sqrt{x^2}=x\). May 2025: this hides the sign change and gives the wrong answer 1.
  • Boxes that do not match. May 2024: \(\frac{x}{\sin2x}\) is not \(\to1\); the box is \(2x\), so the limit is \(\frac12\).
  • Losing the power, or accepting \(\infty\). Dec 2024: \((1-\cos x)^3\) behaves like \(\frac{x^6}{8}\), not \(\frac{x^2}2\). For \(n=2\) and \(n=4\) the limit is \(+\infty\), which does not count as existing.
Ready answer · the box limits

As \(u\to0\):

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{\sin u}{u}&\to1,\\ \frac{\tan u}{u}&\to1,\\ \frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}&\to\frac12.\end{aligned}\]

The last two come from the first: \(\frac{\tan u}u=\frac{\sin u}u\cdot\frac1{\cos u}\), and \(\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}=\frac{2\sin^2(u/2)}{u^2}=\frac12\Big(\frac{\sin(u/2)}{u/2}\Big)^2\). So \(\frac{\sin ax}{bx}\to\frac ab\) and \(1-\cos x\approx\frac{x^2}2\) near 0. On written papers, show the rewrite in one line before using them.

Dec 2024 Q1(b)Q1: 10%; this part 5%

What is the smallest natural number \(n\) for which the limit \[\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin^nx}{\cos^2x\,(1-\cos x)^3}\] exist?

Part (a) of this question is in the L10 guide. "Exists" is read as "is a finite number", the course's meaning.

Hint

Type L2.2, sin of a box, step 4c. First step: replace \(1-\cos x\) by \(2\sin^2\frac x2\), then turn every sine into (sine over its box) × box and count the leftover power of \(x\).

Solution

Step 1–2 Every box is \(x\) or \(\frac x2\), tending to 0. Since \(1-\cos x=2\sin^2\frac x2\),

\[(1-\cos x)^3=8\sin^6\frac x2.\]

Step 3 Match the boxes:

\[\begin{aligned}\sin^nx&=\Big(\frac{\sin x}{x}\Big)^nx^n,\\ \sin^6\frac x2&=\Big(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\Big)^6\frac{x^6}{64}.\end{aligned}\]

So, for \(x\ne0\) near 0,

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{\sin^nx}{\cos^2x\,(1-\cos x)^3}\\&=\frac{8\big(\frac{\sin x}{x}\big)^n}{\cos^2x\big(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\big)^6}\cdot x^{n-6}.\end{aligned}\]

Step 4c The first factor tends to \(\frac{8\cdot1}{1\cdot1}=8\). The leftover \(x^{n-6}\):

\(n\lt6\): \(x^{n-6}\) blows up (to \(+\infty\) for even \(n\); to \(+\infty\) on one side and \(-\infty\) on the other for odd \(n\)), so there is no finite limit.
\(n=6\): \(x^0=1\), and the limit is \(8\).
\(n\gt6\): the limit is \(0\).

\[\boxed{n=6}\]

Marker: show why \(n=5\) fails, not only that \(n=6\) works; "smallest" needs both.

Equivalent forms. At \(n=6\) the limit is \(8\). Check: sympy gives \(\infty\) (or \(\pm\infty\)) for \(n=1,\dots,5\), \(8\) for \(n=6\), \(0\) for \(n=7,8\).

May 2024 Q1(a)(i)Q1: 10%; part marks not shown

Evaluate \[\lim_{x\to0}x\csc(2x).\]

Part (a)(ii), \(\lim_{x\to0^+}\sqrt[x]{\cos2\sqrt x}\), is in the L10 guide; part (b) is under L2.1.

Hint

Type L2.2, sin of a box, step 4a. First step: \(x\csc2x=\dfrac{x}{\sin2x}\); the box is \(2x\), so make a \(2x\) appear on top.

Solution

Step 1–2 \(x\to0\) and \(\csc2x\to\pm\infty\): form \(0\cdot\infty\). Rewrite: \(x\csc2x=\dfrac{x}{\sin2x}\), form \(\frac00\), box \(2x\to0\).

Step 3 Match the boxes:

\[\frac{x}{\sin2x}=\frac12\cdot\frac{2x}{\sin2x}=\frac12\cdot\frac{1}{\frac{\sin2x}{2x}}.\]

Step 4a \(\frac{\sin2x}{2x}\to1\), so by the Quotient Law the limit is \(\frac12\cdot\frac11\).

\[\boxed{\tfrac12}\]

Equivalent forms. \(0.5\). L'Hôpital also works: \(\frac{1}{2\cos2x}\to\frac12\).
