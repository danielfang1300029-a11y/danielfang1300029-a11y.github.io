The short version

What to expect

These integrals are in 8 of the 13 recent papers, usually as one or two parts of Q3 ("Evaluate each of the following integrals"). Partial fractions (L14.1) is by far the most common: 19 of the 28 parts, and on almost every older paper. The integrands change every time, but the method does not: every question is one of three types, and each type below has a standard procedure of numbered steps ("if you see this, do that"). Follow them in order and you never have to invent an approach in the exam.

How to practise: use Start here to name the type from what is under the integral sign. Work the question with the procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end. Always check an antiderivative by differentiating it back. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind each method, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Q3 "Evaluate each of the following integrals, showing all the reasoning" (10–12%, 3–5% per part) on most recent papers. Partial fractions also turns up as the last step of other questions (Dec 2021 Q3(b), Dec 2020 Q2(b), Dec 2019 Q4(b)), and trig substitution inside area, volume and arc-length questions (L16, L17 guides). Older papers (2013–2018) had a whole question on it.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Q1–3 answers only; its Q3 was four integrals "using any method". Only the final antiderivative scores there, so differentiate it back before you move on. On written papers the steps (the partial-fraction form, the substitution, the change of limits) carry the marks.
  • Formula sheet: recent papers print trig identities. Dec 2025 (and May 2014) also print \(\int\sec x\,dx\), \(\int\tan x\,dx\) and the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\), \(\tan^{-1}x\); May 2026 printed no integral formulas and then asked for \(\int\sec x\,dx\). No sheet gives the partial-fraction forms, \(\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}\), or which trig substitution to use. Learn those.
  • Wording to read carefully: "Resolve … into partial fractions" means decompose only, no integral; "Write down the form of the partial fractions" or "Write down the appropriate substitutions (no need to carry out the integration)" means the form alone scores. Older papers use \(u\) or \(y\) as the variable.

L14.1Rational function: divide first if needed, then partial fractions

Chapter note · partial fractions · Chapter note · repeated and quadratic factors

Frequency: 9 parts in 7 of 13 recent papers (May 2026, Dec 2023 twice, May 2023, May 2022, Dec 2021 twice, Dec 2020, Dec 2019); 10 parts in 9 of 10 older papers (every one except Dec 2015). The most common type on this page.

Recognise it

A rational function (one polynomial divided by another) with no quick substitution, or an integral that becomes one after a substitution such as \(u=\cos x\), \(u=x^2\) or \(u=x^{1/6}\).

Standard procedure

Write the integrand as \(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\): \(P\) is the top (numerator), \(Q\) the bottom (denominator).

  1. Make it a rational function.
    • 1a. Already \(\frac{P}{Q}\): go to step 2.
    • 1b. Not yet: substitute so it becomes one. A function next to its derivative: \(u=\cos x\) (from \(\tan x\,dx\)), \(u=x^2\) (from \(2x\,dx\)), \(u=m(t)\) (from \(m'(t)\,dt\)). Several fractional powers \(x^{1/p}\), \(x^{1/q}\): \(u=x^{1/n}\) with \(n\) the lowest common multiple of \(p,q\). Asked: Dec 2023, Dec 2020, Dec 2019, May 2016.
    • 1c. The question only gives an equation for \(F'(x)\) (or \(g'(x)\)): solve it for \(F'\) first. Asked: Dec 2021, Dec 2019.
  2. Degree check. If degree of \(P\) ≥ degree of \(Q\), long-divide: \(\frac PQ=\text{polynomial}+\frac RQ\) with degree \(R\) < degree \(Q\). Carry on with \(\frac RQ\).
  3. Factor \(Q\) completely into linear factors \((x-a)\) and irreducible quadratics (quadratics with \(b^2-4ac\lt0\), which cannot be factored further). Use any hint given (e.g. \(x^4+4=(x^2+2)^2-(2x)^2\)).
  4. Write the form, one group of terms per factor:
    • \((x-a)\) gives \(\frac{A}{x-a}\).
    • \((x-a)^k\) gives \(\frac{A_1}{x-a}+\frac{A_2}{(x-a)^2}+\dots+\frac{A_k}{(x-a)^k}\).
    • An irreducible \(q(x)=x^2+px+r\) gives \(\frac{Bx+D}{q(x)}\); \(q(x)^k\) gives one such term for each power up to \(k\).
    • "Write down the form" questions: stop here. Asked: Dec 2016.
  5. Find the constants. Multiply both sides by \(Q(x)\).
    • 5a. Cover-up: put \(x=a\) for each linear factor; all terms but one vanish. For \((x-a)^k\) this gives the top power \(A_k\).
    • 5b. The rest (lower powers of a repeated factor, \(B\) and \(D\) of a quadratic): compare coefficients of \(x^2\), \(x\), constant, or put in easy values such as \(x=0\).
    • "Resolve into partial fractions" questions: stop here. Asked: Dec 2018, Dec 2016.
  6. Integrate term by term.
    • 6a. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{A}{x-a}\,dx=A\ln|x-a|\). Asked: May 2023, May 2022, Dec 2020, Dec 2019, and 2 older papers.
    • 6b. \(\displaystyle\int\frac{A}{(x-a)^k}\,dx=\frac{-A}{(k-1)(x-a)^{k-1}}\) for \(k\ge2\). Asked: May 2026, Dec 2023, and 2 older papers.
    • 6c. \(\frac{Bx+D}{x^2+px+r}\): split into a multiple of \(\frac{2x+p}{x^2+px+r}\) (gives \(\ln\)) plus a constant over the completed square \((x+\frac p2)^2+c^2\) (gives \(\frac1c\tan^{-1}\frac{x+p/2}{c}\)). Asked: Dec 2021 (twice), and 3 older papers.
  7. Finish. Substitute back if you used step 1b, add \(+C\). For a definite integral, first check the integrand is defined on the whole interval; if it blows up at an end, it is an improper integral, so take a limit. Asked: Dec 2023.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2023 Q3(c), 4%. Evaluate the following integral, showing all the reasoning.

\[\int\frac{x^3+x+1}{(x-1)^2(x+1)}\,dx\]

Step 1 Already a rational function.

No \(\sin\), \(e^x\) or root to remove, so go straight on.

Step 2 Top and bottom both have degree 3, so divide. The bottom expands to \(x^3-x^2-x+1\), and

\[\begin{aligned}x^3+x+1&=1\cdot(x^3-x^2-x+1)\\&\quad+(x^2+2x).\end{aligned}\]

So the integrand is \(1+\dfrac{x^2+2x}{(x-1)^2(x+1)}\).

Skipping the division is the most common error: the partial-fraction form below only works when the top has lower degree than the bottom.

Step 3–4 The bottom is already factored; \((x-1)\) is repeated, so

\[\begin{aligned}&\frac{x^2+2x}{(x-1)^2(x+1)}\\&=\frac{A}{x-1}+\frac{B}{(x-1)^2}+\frac{D}{x+1}.\end{aligned}\]

Write the form down in full before finding numbers; the marker gives credit for the form.

Step 5a Multiply by the bottom:

\[\begin{aligned}x^2+2x&=A(x-1)(x+1)\\&\quad+B(x+1)+D(x-1)^2.\end{aligned}\]

\(x=1\): \(3=2B\), so \(B=\frac32\). \(x=-1\): \(-1=4D\), so \(D=-\frac14\).

Step 5b Compare the \(x^2\) coefficients: \(1=A+D\), so \(A=\frac54\).

Cover-up cannot reach \(A\) (the lower power of the repeated factor), so one comparison of coefficients finishes the job.

Step 6a–6b Integrate term by term:

\[\begin{aligned}&x+\tfrac54\ln|x-1|\\&-\frac{3}{2(x-1)}-\tfrac14\ln|x+1|+C.\end{aligned}\]

\(\int\frac{3/2}{(x-1)^2}dx=-\frac{3}{2(x-1)}\): a power, not a log. Keep the absolute values in the logs.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&x+\tfrac54\ln|x-1|\\&-\frac{3}{2(x-1)}-\tfrac14\ln|x+1|+C\end{aligned}}\]

Check by differentiating back: sympy confirms it.

Traps
  • Not dividing first. Eight of the 19 parts need it (May 2026 Q3(b), Dec 2023 Q3(c), May 2023 Q3(c), May 2022 Q3(d), Dec 2018 Q3(b), Dec 2017 Q1(a), Dec 2016 Q2(a), May 2016 Q3(a)). If you skip it, the form is wrong and the answer loses the polynomial part.
  • Wrong form. A repeated factor needs every power (\(\frac{A}{x-1}+\frac{B}{(x-1)^2}\), as in Dec 2023 Q3(c), May 2018 Q4(d)); an irreducible quadratic needs \(Bx+D\) on top, not just a constant (Dec 2021 Q3(a), May 2014 Q5(b)).
  • A definite integral through a point where the integrand blows up. Dec 2023 Q3(b) as transcribed runs up to \(x=\frac\pi2\), where \(\tan x\) is undefined: you must take a limit, and it diverges.
Ready answer · two forms that recur

May 2023 Q3(c) and May 2022 Q3(d) are both \(\int\frac{x^2+px+q}{x^2-4}\,dx\). Dividing and using cover-up once and for all:

\[\begin{aligned}&\int\frac{x^2+px+q}{x^2-4}\,dx\\&=x+\frac{4+2p+q}{4}\ln|x-2|\\&\quad-\frac{4-2p+q}{4}\ln|x+2|+C.\end{aligned}\]

And the piece behind Dec 2019 Q4(b) and May 2016 Q3(a): for \(a\gt0\),

\[\int\frac{dx}{x^2-a^2}=\frac1{2a}\ln\left|\frac{x-a}{x+a}\right|+C.\]

On written papers show the division and cover-up; use these to check.

Past-paper questions

May 2026 Q3(b)Q3: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Evaluate the following integral using any method. \[\int\frac{x^3}{x^2+2x+1}\,dx\]

Parts (a) and (c) are under L14.2; part (d) is in the L13 guide.

Hint

Type L14.1, rational function. First step: the top has degree 3 and the bottom degree 2, so long-divide; the bottom is \((x+1)^2\).

Solution

Step 2 Divide: \(x^3=(x-2)(x^2+2x+1)+(3x+2)\), so the integrand is \(x-2+\dfrac{3x+2}{(x+1)^2}\).

Step 3–4 Form: \(\dfrac{3x+2}{(x+1)^2}=\dfrac{A}{x+1}+\dfrac{B}{(x+1)^2}\).

Step 5 \(3x+2=A(x+1)+B\). \(x=-1\): \(B=-1\). Compare \(x\): \(A=3\).

Step 6a–6b Integrate:

\[\begin{aligned}&\int\Big(x-2+\frac{3}{x+1}-\frac{1}{(x+1)^2}\Big)dx\\&=\tfrac{x^2}{2}-2x+3\ln|x+1|+\frac{1}{x+1}+C.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\frac{x^2}{2}-2x+3\ln|x+1|+\frac{1}{x+1}+C}\]

Marker: answers only, so the sign of the \(\frac1{x+1}\) term decides the part; differentiate back to check it.

Equivalent forms. Any constant may be absorbed into \(C\), e.g. \(\frac{(x+1)^2}{2}-3(x+1)+3\ln|x+1|+\frac1{x+1}+C\) (from \(u=x+1\)).

Dec 2023 Q3(b)4% (Q3: 12%)

Evaluate each of the following integrals, showing all the reasoning. \[\int_{\frac\pi3}^{\frac\pi2}\frac{\tan x}{1+\cos x}\,dx\]

Part (c) is the worked example above. Checked against the paper image: the limits really are \(\frac\pi3\) to \(\frac\pi2\). Since \(\tan x\) is undefined at \(\frac\pi2\), this is an improper integral and it diverges, which is probably not what the setters intended (compare May 2023 Q3(b), which converges). Treat it as practice in spotting an improper integral.

Hint

Type L14.1 after a substitution (step 1b). First step: \(\tan x\,dx=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}dx\), so put \(u=\cos x\); then look at what happens at the upper limit.

Solution

Step 1b Substitute \(u=\cos x\), \(du=-\sin x\,dx\):

\[\frac{\tan x\,dx}{1+\cos x}=\frac{-du}{u(1+u)}.\]

Step 5a Cover-up: \(\dfrac1{u(1+u)}=\dfrac1u-\dfrac1{1+u}\).

Step 6a Antiderivative: \(-\ln|u|+\ln|1+u|\), i.e.

\[G(x)=\ln\frac{1+\cos x}{\cos x}=\ln(1+\sec x)\]

for \(\frac\pi3\le x\lt\frac\pi2\).

Step 7 Improper at \(\frac\pi2\). For \(\frac\pi3\lt b\lt\frac\pi2\),

\[\int_{\pi/3}^{b}\frac{\tan x}{1+\cos x}\,dx=\ln(1+\sec b)-\ln3.\]

As \(b\to\frac\pi2^-\), \(\sec b\to+\infty\), so this tends to \(+\infty\).

\[\boxed{\text{The integral diverges (to }+\infty)}\]

Marker: say why the integral is improper (\(\tan x\) undefined at \(\frac\pi2\)) and write the limit; just putting \(x=\frac\pi2\) into \(G\) is not valid.

Check. Numerically, up to \(b=\frac\pi2-10^{-6}\) the integral is already about \(12.7\) and still growing like \(\ln\frac{1}{\frac\pi2-b}\).

May 2023 Q3(c)4% (Q3: 12%)

Evaluate each of the following integrals, showing all the reasoning. \[\int\frac{x^2+2x+4}{x^2-4}\,dx\]

Hint

Type L14.1, rational function. First step: equal degrees, so divide: the quotient is 1. Then factor \(x^2-4\).

Solution

Step 2 Divide: \(\dfrac{x^2+2x+4}{x^2-4}=1+\dfrac{2x+8}{x^2-4}\).

Step 3–4 \(\dfrac{2x+8}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\dfrac{A}{x-2}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\).

Step 5a Cover-up: \(x=2\): \(A=\frac{12}{4}=3\). \(x=-2\): \(B=\frac{4}{-4}=-1\).

Step 6a Integrate: \(x+3\ln|x-2|-\ln|x+2|+C\).

\[\boxed{x+3\ln|x-2|-\ln|x+2|+C}\]

Equivalent forms. \(x+\ln\dfrac{|x-2|^3}{|x+2|}+C\). Matches the Ready answer with \(p=2\), \(q=4\).

May 2022 Q3(d)3% (Q3: 12%)

Evaluate each of the following integrals, showing all reasoning. \[\int\frac{x^2+3x+1}{x^2-4}\,dx\]

Part (a) is under L14.3 and part (c) under L14.2.

Hint

Type L14.1, rational function. First step: divide (quotient 1), then cover-up on \((x-2)(x+2)\).

Solution

Step 2 Divide: \(\dfrac{x^2+3x+1}{x^2-4}=1+\dfrac{3x+5}{x^2-4}\).

Step 3–5a \(\dfrac{3x+5}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\dfrac{A}{x-2}+\dfrac{B}{x+2}\). \(x=2\): \(A=\frac{11}{4}\). \(x=-2\): \(B=\frac{-1}{-4}=\frac14\).

Step 6a Integrate:

\[\boxed{x+\tfrac{11}{4}\ln|x-2|+\tfrac14\ln|x+2|+C}\]

Dec 2021 Q3(a)(i)Q3: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(A\gt0\) be a constant. Evaluate (i) \(\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{x^3+Ax}\,dx\), and (ii) \(\displaystyle\int\ln(x+A)\,dx\).

Part (ii) is in the L13 guide. Part (b) is the next card.

Hint

Type L14.1, with an irreducible quadratic. First step: \(x^3+Ax=x(x^2+A)\) and \(x^2+A\) cannot be factored because \(A\gt0\); use the form \(\frac{D}{x}+\frac{Bx+E}{x^2+A}\).

Solution

Step 3–4 \(\dfrac{1}{x(x^2+A)}=\dfrac{D}{x}+\dfrac{Bx+E}{x^2+A}\), so \(1=D(x^2+A)+(Bx+E)x\).

Step 5a \(x=0\): \(D=\frac1A\).

Step 5b \(x^2\): \(0=D+B\), so \(B=-\frac1A\). \(x\): \(E=0\).

Step 6a, 6c \(\displaystyle\int\frac{x}{x^2+A}\,dx=\tfrac12\ln(x^2+A)\), so

\[\boxed{\frac1A\ln|x|-\frac1{2A}\ln(x^2+A)+C}\]

Marker: state why \(x^2+A\) does not factor (\(A\gt0\)); that justifies the \(Bx+E\) term.

Equivalent forms. \(\dfrac1{2A}\ln\dfrac{x^2}{x^2+A}+C\).

Dec 2021 Q3(b)Q3: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(A\gt0\) be a constant. Let \(F\) be a function on \((0,\infty)\) such that its derivative \(F'(x)\) satisfies

\[\Big(x+\frac Ax\Big)F'(x)-\frac1{x^2}=0.\]

Show that \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)\) exists and the limit value does not depend on \(A\).

Reading used: the equation fixes \(F\) only up to an added constant \(C\), so \(\lim F=C\). The limit always exists, and the part that involves \(A\) tends to 0 for every \(A\). "Does not depend on \(A\)" holds when \(F\) is the antiderivative from (a)(i) (\(C=0\), limit 0). If \(F\) were instead pinned by a value such as \(F(1)=0\), the limit would be \(\frac{\ln(1+A)}{2A}\), which does depend on \(A\).

Hint

Type L14.1 via step 1c. First step: solve the equation for \(F'(x)\); you should get the integrand of part (a)(i).

Solution

Step 1c For \(x\gt0\), \(x+\frac Ax\gt0\), so

\[F'(x)=\frac{1}{x^2\big(x+\frac Ax\big)}=\frac{1}{x^3+Ax}.\]

Step 2–6 By (a)(i), for some constant \(C\) (two antiderivatives on an interval differ by a constant),

\[F(x)=\frac1{2A}\ln\frac{x^2}{x^2+A}+C.\]

Step 7 Limit. \(\dfrac{x^2}{x^2+A}=\dfrac{1}{1+A/x^2}\to1\), and \(\ln\) is continuous at 1, so

\[\frac1{2A}\ln\frac{x^2}{x^2+A}\to\frac1{2A}\cdot0=0.\]

Hence \(\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=C\) exists, and \(A\) has dropped out.

\[\boxed{\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=C}\]

For the \(F\) of (a)(i), \(C=0\) and the limit is 0, whatever \(A\) is.

Marker: write \(F=G+C\) explicitly and say the \(A\)-term tends to 0 for every \(A\gt0\) (continuity of \(\ln\)).

Check. With \(C=0\): \(F(1000)\approx-5\times10^{-7}\) for both \(A=1\) and \(A=5\) (for large \(x\), the \(A\)-term is about \(-\frac1{2x^2}\)). sympy: \(\lim G=0\).

Dec 2019 Q4(b)Q4: 10%; part marks not shown

Evaluate \(g(x)\) if its derivative \(g'(x)\) satisfies \((x^2-1)g'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{x^2+1}\). [Show your calculation.]

Hint

Type L14.1 via steps 1c and 1b. First step: \(g'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{(x^2-1)(x^2+1)}\) for \(x\ne\pm1\); the \(2x\) on top suggests \(u=x^2\).

Solution

Step 1c For \(x\ne\pm1\): \(g'(x)=\dfrac{2x}{(x^2-1)(x^2+1)}\).

Step 1b \(u=x^2\), \(du=2x\,dx\): \(\displaystyle g=\int\frac{du}{(u-1)(u+1)}\).

Step 5a Cover-up: \(\dfrac1{(u-1)(u+1)}=\dfrac{1/2}{u-1}-\dfrac{1/2}{u+1}\).

Step 6a–7 \(\frac12\ln|u-1|-\frac12\ln|u+1|+C\), and \(u+1=x^2+1\gt0\):

\[\boxed{g(x)=\tfrac12\ln\frac{|x^2-1|}{x^2+1}+C}\]

Marker: \(g\) is only defined for \(x\ne\pm1\); strictly the constant may differ on \((-\infty,-1)\), \((-1,1)\), \((1,\infty)\).

Equivalent forms. Working in \(x\) gives \(\frac12\ln|x-1|+\frac12\ln|x+1|-\frac12\ln(x^2+1)+C\), the same function.

L14.2Powers and products of trig functions

Chapter note · trig integrals

Frequency: 4 parts in 3 of 13 recent papers (May 2026 twice, Dec 2024, May 2022); 0 of 10 older papers as a part of its own. It is also a step inside Dec 2017 Q4(b) (L17 guide), Dec 2015 Q3(b) (L12 guide) and May 2016 Q7(a) (O4 guide).

Recognise it

Only trig functions under the integral sign, and none of the quick standard forms (\(\sin x\), \(\cos x\), \(\sec^2x\)) on its own: \(\sec x\), a power such as \(\sin^4x\), a product of different angles such as \(\sin x\sin2x\), or \(\frac1{1\pm\sin x}\).

Standard procedure
  1. Standard result? \(\int\sec x\,dx=\ln|\sec x+\tan x|+C\), \(\int\tan x\,dx=\ln|\sec x|+C\), \(\int\sec^2x\,dx=\tan x+C\), \(\int\sec x\tan x\,dx=\sec x+C\). If the sheet does not print \(\int\sec x\), derive it: multiply top and bottom by \(\sec x+\tan x\); the top becomes the derivative of the bottom. Asked: May 2026.
  2. Different angles multiplied? Use product-to-sum, then integrate each term:
    • \(2\sin A\sin B=\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)\)
    • \(2\sin A\cos B=\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\)
    • \(2\cos A\cos B=\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)\) Asked: Dec 2024.
  3. Powers \(\sin^mx\cos^nx\).
    • 3a. One power is odd: keep one factor of it, turn the rest into the other function with \(\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1\), then substitute \(u=\cos x\) (odd sine) or \(u=\sin x\) (odd cosine). Powers of \(\tan x\): use \(\tan^2x=\sec^2x-1\). Not asked on its own yet.
    • 3b. All powers even: lower them with \(\sin^2x=\frac{1-\cos2x}{2}\), \(\cos^2x=\frac{1+\cos2x}{2}\); repeat until every term is a single cosine. Asked: May 2022.
  4. \(\frac{1}{1\pm\sin x}\) or \(\frac{1}{1\pm\cos x}\). Multiply top and bottom by the "conjugate" (\(1\mp\sin x\), \(1\mp\cos x\)) so the bottom becomes \(\cos^2x\) or \(\sin^2x\), then split into standard results from step 1. Asked: May 2026.
  5. Finish. Add \(+C\), or put in the limits and use exact values (\(\sin\frac{3\pi}4=\frac{\sqrt2}2\), …).
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2024 Q3(a), 5%. Compute

\[\int_0^{\frac\pi4}(\cos x-2\sin x\sin2x)\,dx.\]

Step 1 \(\cos x\) is standard, but \(\sin x\sin2x\) is not.

Do not try to integrate the product by parts; a trig identity is much faster.

Step 2 Two different angles multiplied: product-to-sum with \(A=x\), \(B=2x\):

\[\begin{aligned}2\sin x\sin2x&=\cos(x-2x)-\cos(x+2x)\\&=\cos x-\cos3x,\end{aligned}\]

since \(\cos(-x)=\cos x\). So the integrand is

\[\cos x-(\cos x-\cos3x)=\cos3x.\]

The \(\cos x\) terms cancel; the question was built so that this happens. Write the identity you used, as the marker looks for it.

Step 5 Integrate and put in the limits:

\[\Big[\tfrac13\sin3x\Big]_0^{\pi/4}=\tfrac13\sin\tfrac{3\pi}4-0=\tfrac13\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt2}2.\]
\[\boxed{\frac{\sqrt2}{6}}\]

Check: \(\frac{\sqrt2}6\approx0.2357\), the same as sympy's value of the original integral.

  • Relying on the formula sheet for \(\int\sec x\). Dec 2025 printed it; May 2026 printed no integrals and then asked for it (Q3(a)). Know the derivation in step 1.
  • Sign errors in product-to-sum. \(2\sin A\sin B\) has a minus: \(\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)\) (Dec 2024 Q3(a)).
  • Stopping too early in step 3b. \(\sin^4x\) gives a \(\cos^22x\) term, which needs the half-angle formula again (May 2022 Q3(c)); the last term is \(\frac{\sin4x}{32}\), not \(\frac{\sin4x}{8}\).

L14.3√(a² ± x²) or √(x² − a²): trig substitution

Chapter note · trig substitution

Frequency: 1 part in 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2022); 4 parts in 4 of 10 older papers (Dec 2018, Dec 2016, May 2016, May 2014). In recent papers it mostly appears as a step inside area, volume and arc-length questions: Dec 2023 Q5(a), May 2023 Q5(a), May 2022 Q4 (L16 guide), Dec 2025 Q13(b) (L17 guide).

Recognise it

A square root (or a power \(\frac32\), \(-\frac32\)) of \(a^2-x^2\), \(a^2+x^2\) or \(x^2-a^2\), with no \(x\) outside that would allow a quick substitution \(u=a^2\pm x^2\).

Standard procedure
  1. Match the root and choose the substitution (so that \(1-\sin^2\theta=\cos^2\theta\) or \(1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta\) removes the root):
    • \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\): \(x=a\sin\theta\), \(-\frac\pi2\le\theta\le\frac\pi2\); root \(=a\cos\theta\).
    • \(\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\): \(x=a\tan\theta\), \(-\frac\pi2\lt\theta\lt\frac\pi2\); root \(=a\sec\theta\).
    • \(\sqrt{x^2-a^2}\): \(x=a\sec\theta\); root \(=a\tan\theta\) (for \(x\ge a\)).
    • A coefficient, \(\sqrt{a^2-b^2x^2}\): put \(bx=a\sin\theta\) (same idea for the others).
    • "Write down the appropriate substitution" questions: stop here. Asked: Dec 2018.
  2. Replace everything: \(x\), \(dx\) (e.g. \(dx=a\cos\theta\,d\theta\)) and the root. For a definite integral, change the limits to \(\theta\) now.
  3. Integrate in \(\theta\) with the L14.2 steps: half-angle formulas for even powers, or the Ready answer \(\int\sec^3\theta\,d\theta\) below.
  4. Finish.
    • 4a. Definite: put in the \(\theta\)-limits; no need to go back to \(x\). Asked: May 2022, Dec 2016.
    • 4b. Indefinite: go back to \(x\) with a right-angled triangle (for \(x=a\sin\theta\): \(\sin\theta=\frac xa\), \(\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt{a^2-x^2}}{a}\)), expanding \(\sin2\theta\), \(\sin4\theta\) by double-angle formulas first. Asked: May 2014.
  5. Formula given for \(\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}\)? Then for \(\int\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\,dx\) use parts instead: integrate 1, differentiate the root, write \(x^2=(a^2+x^2)-a^2\), and solve for the integral. Asked: May 2016.
Worked example · the procedure in action

\[\int_0^1\sqrt{1+x^2}\,dx\]

Step 1 \(\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\) with \(a=1\): put \(x=\tan\theta\).

Step 2 \(dx=\sec^2\theta\,d\theta\); for \(0\le\theta\le\frac\pi4\), \(\sec\theta\gt0\), so \(\sqrt{1+x^2}=\sec\theta\). Limits: \(x=0\to\theta=0\), \(x=1\to\theta=\frac\pi4\).

\[\int_0^1\sqrt{1+x^2}\,dx=\int_0^{\pi/4}\sec^3\theta\,d\theta.\]

Say why \(\sqrt{\sec^2\theta}=\sec\theta\) (it is positive on the range); the marker checks this.

Step 3 By parts, with \(\int\sec\theta\tan\theta=\sec\theta\) and \(\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta-1\):

\[\begin{aligned}\int\sec^3\theta\,d\theta&=\sec\theta\tan\theta-\int\sec\theta\tan^2\theta\,d\theta\\&=\sec\theta\tan\theta-\int\sec^3\theta\,d\theta\\&\quad+\int\sec\theta\,d\theta,\end{aligned}\]

The integral comes back on the right with a minus sign; move it to the left and halve. On an answers-only paper just quote the Ready answer below.

Step 4a At \(\frac\pi4\): \(\sec=\sqrt2\), \(\tan=1\). At 0: \(\sec=1\), \(\tan=0\), giving \(\frac12(0+\ln1)=0\).

\[\boxed{\tfrac12\big(\sqrt2+\ln(1+\sqrt2)\big)}\]

May 2016 Q3(b)Q3: 7%; part marks not shownolder format

Show that \(\displaystyle\int_0^2(1+u^2)^{1/2}\,du=\sqrt5+\frac12\ln(2+\sqrt5)\). [Hint: You may use the formula \(\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{\sqrt{a^2+x^2}}=\ln\big(x+\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\big)+C\).]

Part (a) is under L14.1.

Hint

Type L14.3, step 5 (formula given). First step: integrate by parts with \(\sqrt{1+u^2}\) differentiated and \(1\) integrated; then write \(u^2=(1+u^2)-1\).

Solution

Step 5 Let \(I=\int_0^2\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du\). By parts:

\[I=\Big[u\sqrt{1+u^2}\Big]_0^2-\int_0^2\frac{u^2}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\,du.\]

With \(u^2=(1+u^2)-1\), the last integral is \(I-\int_0^2\frac{du}{\sqrt{1+u^2}}\). Using the hint (\(a=1\)):

\[\begin{aligned}I&=2\sqrt5-I+\Big[\ln\big(u+\sqrt{1+u^2}\big)\Big]_0^2\\&=2\sqrt5-I+\ln(2+\sqrt5).\end{aligned}\]

So \(2I=2\sqrt5+\ln(2+\sqrt5)\).

\[\boxed{\int_0^2\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du=\sqrt5+\tfrac12\ln(2+\sqrt5)}\]

Marker: a "show that": every line must lead to the printed answer; state where the hint is used.

Check. Both sides \(\approx2.958\) (sympy). Also follows from the Ready answer with \(x=2\), or from \(u=\tan\theta\) up to \(\theta=\tan^{-1}2\).

May 2014 Q5(a)Q5: 10%; part marks not shownolder format

Find \(\displaystyle\int x^2\sqrt{4-x^2}\,dx\) by suitable trigonometric substitution.

Part (b) is under L14.1.

Hint

Type L14.3, \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\) with \(a=2\). First step: \(x=2\sin\theta\); the integrand becomes \(16\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\), which is \(4\sin^22\theta\).

Solution

Step 1–2 \(x=2\sin\theta\) (\(-\frac\pi2\le\theta\le\frac\pi2\)), \(dx=2\cos\theta\,d\theta\), \(\sqrt{4-x^2}=2\cos\theta\):

\[\begin{aligned}&\int4\sin^2\theta\cdot2\cos\theta\cdot2\cos\theta\,d\theta\\&=16\int\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta\,d\theta\\&=4\int\sin^22\theta\,d\theta.\end{aligned}\]

Step 3 Half-angle: \(4\sin^22\theta=2(1-\cos4\theta)\), so the integral is \(2\theta-\frac12\sin4\theta+C\).

Step 4b \(\sin4\theta=4\sin\theta\cos\theta(1-2\sin^2\theta)\) with \(\sin\theta=\frac x2\), \(\cos\theta=\frac{\sqrt{4-x^2}}2\):

\[\sin4\theta=\frac{x(2-x^2)\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2}.\]
\[\boxed{2\sin^{-1}\frac x2+\frac{x(x^2-2)\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4}+C}\]

Equivalent forms. \(2\sin^{-1}\frac x2+\frac{x^3\sqrt{4-x^2}}{4}-\frac{x\sqrt{4-x^2}}{2}+C\). sympy differentiates it back to \(x^2\sqrt{4-x^2}\).
