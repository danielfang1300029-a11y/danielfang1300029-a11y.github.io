The short version

What to expect

This chapter is in 12 of the 13 recent papers. Most of it is Q2 "Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\), using any method you choose": one or two parts need logarithmic differentiation (a variable power like \(x^{1/x}\), or a long product or quotient), and one or two are straight derivatives of \(e^{u}\), \(a^{u}\), \(\ln u\) or \(\sin^{-1}u\). These two types are 18 of the 21 recent parts. Inverse-function questions (slope of \(f^{-1}\), choosing a domain so that \(f^{-1}\) exists) are rare on recent papers but were common before 2019.

How to practise: use Start here to name the task and find the type. Then work the question with the type's procedure open, check with the Hint if stuck and with the Solution at the end. Newest papers come first; 2013–2018 papers are tagged older format. For the ideas behind each rule, use the Chapter note links. All answers checked with sympy.

Exam format

  • Where: Q2 "Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\), using any method you choose, and showing all the reasoning" (12%, 3–5% per part) on May 2020, May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023 and Dec 2024; also inside other Part I questions (May 2019 Q4, Dec 2020 Q4, May 2025 Q3, May 2024 Q2, Dec 2021 Q1). Dec 2025: multiple-choice MC4 and free-response Q12.
  • Speed: Dec 2025 moved part of Part I to multiple choice, and May 2026 made Q1–3 answers only (Q2 here: derivatives). Only the final answer scores there. On written papers "showing all the reasoning" means the log step and each rule you use carry the marks.
  • Formula sheet: the derivatives of \(\sin^{-1}x\), \(\cos^{-1}x\) (and \(\tan^{-1}x\) on most) are printed on May 2019, Dec 2020, Dec 2021, May 2024, May 2025, Dec 2025 and most 2014–2018 papers (May 2020: \(\sin^{-1}\), \(\cos^{-1}\) only). They are not printed on May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2024 and May 2026, the papers with the most L7 parts. No paper prints the derivatives of \(e^x\), \(\ln x\), \(a^x\) or the product, quotient and chain rules. Learn them all.
  • Notation: \(\sin^{-1}x=\arcsin x\) is the inverse sine, not \(\frac1{\sin x}\); \(\exp(x)=e^x\); \(\ln\) is base \(e\). \(\arcsin x^2\) means \(\arcsin(x^2)\). \(f^{-1}\) is the inverse function; \(\left(\frac1{f^{-1}}\right)\) is 1 over it.

L7.1Variable in both base and exponent, or a messy product: logarithmic differentiation

Chapter note · logarithmic differentiation · Chapter note · derivatives of ln and exp

Frequency: 6 of 13 recent papers (May 2020, May 2022, May 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2024, Dec 2025); 0 of 10 older papers. Usually one or two parts of Q2 on written papers; May 2022 and May 2023 had two each.

Recognise it

The variable is in the base and the exponent at once (\((\cos x)^x\), \(x^{1/x}\), \(f(x)^{g(x)}\)), or \(y\) is a long product or quotient of powers, roots and exponentials, where the quotient rule would be painful.

Standard procedure

\(u\) and \(v\) are functions of \(x\). Logarithmic differentiation means: take \(\ln\) of both sides, simplify with the log laws, then differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\) (the left side gives \(\frac{y'}{y}\) by the chain rule).

  1. Check that logs are needed. A constant power \(x^n\) or a constant base \(a^x\) on its own does not need logs: use the L7.2 rules. \(x\) in both base and exponent: go to step 2. A long product/quotient: go to step 3.
  2. Variable power \(u^v\) (needs \(u\gt0\)).
    • 2a. On its own, \(y=u^v\): \(\ln y=v\ln u\), so \(\dfrac{y'}{y}=v'\ln u+v\dfrac{u'}{u}\) and \(y'=u^v\Big(v'\ln u+\dfrac{v\,u'}{u}\Big)\). Asked: May 2020.
    • 2b. Inside an outer function, \(y=F(u^v)\) such as \(\sin(x^{1/x})\): call the inner part \(w=u^v\), find \(w'\) by 2a, then chain rule \(y'=F'(w)\,w'\). Asked: Dec 2023, May 2023, May 2022.
    • 2c. Only values given (\(f(2)\), \(f'(2)\), …): write the 2a formula with \(f,g\), then put the numbers in. Asked: Dec 2025.
  3. Long product or quotient. Take \(\ln|y|\) (absolute values, so that negative factors are allowed), split it with \(\ln(AB)=\ln A+\ln B\), \(\ln\frac AB=\ln A-\ln B\), \(\ln A^k=k\ln A\), then differentiate term by term: \(\frac{d}{dx}\ln|A|=\frac{A'}{A}\). Asked: Dec 2024, May 2023, May 2022.
  4. Multiply back by \(y\) and write \(y\) out in full, so the answer is in terms of \(x\) only.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2024 Q2(a), 5%. Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for

\[y=\frac{x^{\sqrt5}\,e^{x^3}\sqrt{1+x^3}}{\sin x}.\]

Step 1 Four factors, one on the bottom: a long quotient. Logs (step 3).

The quotient rule with a triple product on top works, but it gives a long expression that is easy to get wrong. Logs turn the product into a sum.

Step 3 \(x^{\sqrt5}\) needs \(x\gt0\). Take \(\ln|y|\) and split:

\[\begin{aligned}\ln|y|&=\sqrt5\ln x+x^3\\&\quad+\tfrac12\ln(1+x^3)-\ln|\sin x|.\end{aligned}\]

\(\ln e^{x^3}=x^3\) and \(\ln\sqrt{A}=\frac12\ln A\). The \(|\sin x|\) matters: \(\sin x\) is negative on parts of the domain, and \(\ln\) of a negative number is undefined. The marker wants the log laws written out, one per factor.

Step 3 Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\):

\[\frac{y'}{y}=\frac{\sqrt5}{x}+3x^2+\frac{3x^2}{2(1+x^3)}-\cot x.\]

Each term is \(\frac{A'}{A}\): \(\frac{d}{dx}\ln|\sin x|=\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}=\cot x\). The left side is \(\frac{y'}{y}\) by the chain rule.

Step 4 Multiply by \(y\) and write \(y\) out:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\frac{dy}{dx}&=\frac{x^{\sqrt5}e^{x^3}\sqrt{1+x^3}}{\sin x}\\&\quad\times\Big(\frac{\sqrt5}{x}+3x^2\\&\qquad+\frac{3x^2}{2(1+x^3)}-\cot x\Big)\end{aligned}}\]

Common slip: stopping at the bracket. The bracket is \(\frac{y'}{y}\), not \(y'\).

Traps
  • Using only half a rule on \(u^v\). Treating \(x^{1/x}\) as a power (\(\frac1x\cdot x^{\frac1x-1}\)) or as an exponential (\(x^{1/x}\ln x\cdot(\dots)\)) is wrong: both base and exponent vary. Dec 2025 MC4 made the two half-answers into options (A) and (C).
  • Forgetting step 4. Every L7.1 part needs the final multiplication by \(y\).
  • \(\ln\) of a negative. In May 2022 Q2(c) \((x^2-2)^3\) and in Dec 2024 Q2(a) \(\sin x\) can be negative: take \(\ln|y|\). The derivative \(\frac{A'}{A}\) is the same either way.
Ready answer · \(x^{1/x}\) and \(u^v\)

For \(x\gt0\),

\[\frac{d}{dx}\,x^{1/x}=x^{1/x}\cdot\frac{1-\ln x}{x^2}.\]

Asked three sittings in a row inside a different trig function: \(\cos(x^{1/x})\) (May 2022 Q2(d)), \(\tan(x^{1/x})\) (May 2023 Q2(c)), \(\sin(x^{1/x})\) (Dec 2023 Q2(c)). Then just multiply by \(-\sin(x^{1/x})\), \(\sec^2(x^{1/x})\) or \(\cos(x^{1/x})\).

In general, for \(f\gt0\): \(\dfrac{d}{dx}f^{g}=f^{g}\Big(g'\ln f+\dfrac{g\,f'}{f}\Big)\).

Why: \(\ln(x^{1/x})=\frac{\ln x}{x}\), whose derivative by the quotient rule is \(\frac{\frac1x\cdot x-\ln x}{x^2}=\frac{1-\ln x}{x^2}\). On written papers, show this log step; do not just quote the result.

Dec 2025 MC4Part I multiple choice, 2 pts

Let \(F(x)=f(x)^{g(x)}\) where \(f(x)\gt0\) for all \(x\), and \(f,g\) are differentiable functions. If \(f(2)=4\), \(f'(2)=-1\), \(g(2)=\frac12\), and \(g'(2)=3\), what is \(F'(2)\)?

(A) \(-\frac14\)   (B) \(12\ln(2)-\frac14\)   (C) \(6\ln(4)\)   (D) \(8\ln(2)+6\)   (E) \(0\)

Hint

Type L7.1, variable power with only values given (step 2c). First step: write \(\ln F=g\ln f\) and differentiate both sides, keeping \(f\) and \(g\) as letters.

Solution

Step 2c \(f\gt0\), so \(\ln F=g\ln f\). Differentiate:

\[\frac{F'}{F}=g'\ln f+g\,\frac{f'}{f}.\]

Step 2c At \(x=2\): \(F(2)=4^{1/2}=2\), and the bracket is

\[3\ln4+\frac12\cdot\frac{-1}{4}=6\ln2-\frac18.\]

Step 4 \(F'(2)=2\left(6\ln2-\frac18\right)=12\ln2-\frac14\).

\[\boxed{\text{(B)}\ \ 12\ln2-\tfrac14}\]

Marker: multiple choice, only the letter scores. (A) is the power-rule half only, (C) the exponential half only.

Equivalent forms. \(6\ln4-\frac14\approx8.068\).

Hint

Type L7.1, variable power inside \(\sin\) (step 2b). First step: put \(w=x^{1/x}\) and find \(w'\) from \(\ln w=\frac{\ln x}{x}\).

Step 2b Inner part. \(w=x^{1/x}\), \(x\gt0\). Then \(\ln w=\dfrac{\ln x}{x}\), and by the quotient rule

L7.2Differentiate exp, log and inverse-trig expressions

Chapter note · derivatives of exp, ln and aˣ · Chapter note · derivatives of inverse trig functions

Frequency: 7 of 13 recent papers (May 2019, May 2020, Dec 2020, May 2022, Dec 2023, Dec 2025, May 2026); 3 of 10 older papers (May 2016, May 2017, May 2018). The most common L7 type.

Recognise it

The expression contains \(e^{u}\), \(a^{u}\) with a constant base \(a\), \(\ln u\), \(\sin^{-1}u\) or \(\tan^{-1}u\), and the variable is not in a base and an exponent at once. The question asks for a derivative, or for something built from one (a horizontal tangent, \(h''\), a value at 0, a curvature).

Standard procedure

\(u\) is a function of \(x\); every rule below already includes the chain-rule factor \(u'\).

  1. Simplify first with \(e^{\ln A}=A\) (for \(A\gt0\)), \(\ln e^{A}=A\), \(\ln(AB)=\ln A+\ln B\), \(\ln A^k=k\ln A\). Write down why \(A\gt0\). Used in: May 2026 Q2(e), May 2016 Q2(a)(i).
  2. Find the outermost operation (product, quotient or "function of a function") and apply its rule; then work inwards one layer at a time.
  3. Use the standard derivatives: \[\begin{aligned}(e^{u})'&=e^{u}\,u'\\ (a^{u})'&=a^{u}\ln a\cdot u'\\ (\ln u)'&=\frac{u'}{u}\\ (\sin^{-1}u)'&=\frac{u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\\ (\cos^{-1}u)'&=\frac{-u'}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}\\ (\tan^{-1}u)'&=\frac{u'}{1+u^2}\end{aligned}\]
  4. Finish what was asked.
    • 4a. A derivative: tidy up (factor out \(e^{u}\) or \(a^{u}\)) and stop. Asked: Dec 2023, May 2022 (a), (b), May 2020, and 2 older papers.
    • 4b. A higher derivative (\(h''\), \(T^{(n)}\)): differentiate again; for the \(n\)-th one, find the repeating pattern after two or three steps. Asked: May 2026 (d), (e).
    • 4c. A value at a point with an unknown function (\(F(0)\), \(g(0)\) given): differentiate the whole identity first, then substitute the given values. Asked: Dec 2020, May 2016.
    • 4d. Use the derivative: set it to 0 (horizontal tangent), put it into a given formula (curvature), or read a rule backwards as an antiderivative (\(\int\frac{1}{1+x^2}dx=\tan^{-1}x\)). Asked: May 2019, Dec 2025, May 2020 Q3(c).
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2023 Q2(a), 4%. Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\), using any method you choose, and showing all the reasoning.

\[y=2^x\sqrt{x^2+1}\,\sin^{-1}(x)\]

Step 1 Nothing to simplify. \(\sin^{-1}x\) needs \(-1\le x\le1\); the derivative exists for \(-1\lt x\lt1\).

The domain line is short and shows the marker you know where \(\sin^{-1}\) and its derivative make sense.

Step 2 Outermost operation: a product of three factors. Product rule for three:

\[(PQR)'=P'QR+PQ'R+PQR'.\]

Each term differentiates one factor and leaves the other two alone.

Step 3 The three derivatives:

\[\begin{aligned}(2^x)'&=2^x\ln2,\\ \big(\sqrt{x^2+1}\big)'&=\frac{2x}{2\sqrt{x^2+1}}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}},\\ (\sin^{-1}x)'&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.\end{aligned}\]

Dec 2023 did not print the \(\sin^{-1}\) derivative on the formula sheet. \((2^x)'\) is not \(x\,2^{x-1}\): the base is constant, the exponent varies.

Step 4a Put the pieces in and factor out \(2^x\):

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\frac{dy}{dx}&=2^x\Big(\ln2\,\sqrt{x^2+1}\,\sin^{-1}x\\&\quad+\frac{x\sin^{-1}x}{\sqrt{x^2+1}}+\frac{\sqrt{x^2+1}}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\Big)\end{aligned}}\]

Logarithmic differentiation (L7.1) also works here, but it needs \(\ln\) of \(\sin^{-1}x\), which is negative for \(x\lt0\). The product rule is shorter.

Traps
  • Dropping the chain factor \(u'\). \(e^{\sec^2z}\) needs \(2\sec^2z\tan z\) (May 2020 Q2(b)); \(\sin^{-1}(e^x)\) needs \(e^x\) on top (May 2018 Q3(b)); \(\pi^{2x-7}\) needs the 2 (May 2022 Q2(a)).
  • Power rule on \(a^x\). \((2^x)'=2^x\ln2\) and \((\pi^{2x-7})'=2\ln\pi\cdot\pi^{2x-7}\), never \(x\,2^{x-1}\) (Dec 2023 Q2(a), May 2022 Q2(a)).
  • Not simplifying first. May 2026 Q2(e) asks for the 2026th derivative of \(e^{\ln(\cosh x)}\): it is just \(\cosh x\). Differentiating the composite as printed goes nowhere.

May 2026 Q2(e)Q2: 10 pts, answers only; part marks not shown

Find the derivatives of the following functions using any method. Find \(T^{(2026)}(x)\) if \[T(x)=e^{\ln(\cosh x)}.\]

Beyond the handouts \(\cosh\) (hyperbolic cosine) is not in the calculus handouts. \(\cosh x=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}\) and \(\sinh x=\dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}\).

Hint

L7.3Slope of an inverse function at a point

Chapter note · derivative of an inverse function

Frequency: 1 of 13 recent papers (May 2025); 1 of 10 older papers (May 2014). The same step also ends two older L7.4 questions (Dec 2018, Dec 2017), so it has been needed on 4 papers in all.

Recognise it

You are asked for \((f^{-1})'(b)\), "the slope of \(f^{-1}\) at \(x=b\)", or the derivative of something built from \(f^{-1}\), such as \(\left(\dfrac1{f^{-1}}\right)'(b)\). Usually there is no formula for \(f^{-1}\) itself.

Standard procedure

\(b\) is the number you are given. It is an output of \(f\), not an input.

  1. Find the input \(a\) with \(f(a)=b\). Read it off a table, or try small whole numbers in the formula. Then \(f^{-1}(b)=a\).
  2. Check the inverse makes sense near \(a\). Show \(f'\gt0\) (or \(f'\lt0\)) there, so \(f\) is one-to-one (no two inputs give the same output) and \(f'(a)\ne0\).
  3. Flip the slope: \[(f^{-1})'(b)=\frac{1}{f'(a)}.\] Asked: May 2014, and inside Dec 2018, Dec 2017 (under L7.4).
  4. If the question wraps \(f^{-1}\) in something else, finish with the ordinary rules, e.g. by the chain rule \[\left(\frac1{f^{-1}}\right)'(b)=-\frac{(f^{-1})'(b)}{\big(f^{-1}(b)\big)^2}.\] Asked: May 2025.
Ready answer · slope of an inverse

If \(f(a)=b\) and \(f'(a)\ne0\), then

\[\begin{aligned}(f^{-1})'(b)&=\frac{1}{f'(a)},\\ \left(\frac1{f^{-1}}\right)'(b)&=-\frac{1}{a^2\,f'(a)}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: slope of the inverse at an output = 1 ÷ slope of \(f\) at the matching input. The mirror image in \(y=x\) swaps rise and run, so the slope turns upside down.

Traps
  • Using \(f'(b)\) instead of \(f'(a)\). In May 2014 Q4(c), \(f'(19)\) or \(f'(-19)\) is wrong; the slope you need is at the input \(a=1\).
  • \(\dfrac1{f^{-1}}\) is not \(f\). In May 2025 Q3(b), \(\left(\frac1{f^{-1}}\right)'\) means "one over the inverse", so step 4 is needed after step 3.

L7.4Restrict the domain so an inverse exists; sketch the inverse

Chapter note · one-to-one functions and inverses · Chapter note · derivative of an inverse function

Frequency: 0 of 13 recent papers; 3 of 10 older papers (May 2014, Dec 2017, Dec 2018). Not seen since 2018, but the idea underlies every inverse-trig derivative in L7.2.

Recognise it

"State a suitable domain and range so that \(f^{-1}\) exists / is well-defined", "sketch \(y=f^{-1}(x)\)", often followed by \(f^{-1}(b)\) or \((f^{-1})'(b)\). The function goes up and down (periodic, or a cubic with two turning points), so it has no inverse until you cut it down.

Standard procedure

A function has an inverse on an interval exactly when it is one-to-one there: each output comes from only one input (any horizontal line meets the graph at most once). A function that only goes up, or only goes down, on the interval is one-to-one.

  1. Sketch \(f\) and find where it turns. Solve \(f'(x)=0\) and note the sign of \(f'\) between those points. For a periodic function, one period is enough.
  2. Choose the piece \(D\). An interval between two turning points, where \(f'\ge0\) throughout or \(f'\le0\) throughout. It must contain any point the question names. With no point named, take the standard piece (the one starting at \(x=0\)). Say why: "\(f'\lt0\) on the interior, so \(f\) is strictly decreasing, hence one-to-one."
  3. Range \(R\) = values at the two ends of \(D\). \(f\) is continuous and only goes one way, so it covers everything between \(f(\text{left end})\) and \(f(\text{right end})\). Then \(f^{-1}:R\to D\): the domain of \(f^{-1}\) is \(R\), its range is \(D\).
  4. Sketch \(f^{-1}\) by reflecting the chosen piece in \(y=x\). Swap the coordinates of the end points and of any marked point; flat tangents of \(f\) become vertical tangents of \(f^{-1}\). Asked: May 2014 (stops here).
  5. Values and slopes of \(f^{-1}\). \(f^{-1}(b)\) is the \(a\) in \(D\) with \(f(a)=b\); the slope is \(\dfrac1{f'(a)}\) (type L7.3). If an easy formula for \(f^{-1}\) exists (e.g. \(\tfrac12\cos^{-1}x\)), use it as a check. Asked: Dec 2018, Dec 2017.

Hint

Type L7.4, restrict the domain. First step: \(f'(x)=2\sin x\cos x=\sin2x\); find a piece starting at \(x=0\) where it keeps one sign.

Solution

Step 1 \(\sin^2x\) lies between \(0\) and \(1\) and has period \(\pi\): \(0\) at multiples of \(\pi\), \(1\) at \(\pm\frac\pi2\). \(f'(x)=\sin2x\).

Step 2 On \((0,\frac\pi2)\), \(f'(x)=\sin2x\gt0\), so \(f\) is strictly increasing, hence one-to-one: take \(D=[0,\frac\pi2]\).

Step 3 \(f(0)=0\), \(f(\frac\pi2)=1\), so the range is \(R=[0,1]\).

Step 5 \(\sin^2a=\frac12\) with \(a\in[0,\frac\pi2]\) gives \(\sin a=\frac1{\sqrt2}\), so \(a=\frac\pi4\) and \(f^{-1}(\frac12)=\frac\pi4\). Then \(f'(\frac\pi4)=\sin\frac\pi2=1\), so

\[(f^{-1})'\!\left(\tfrac12\right)=\frac1{f'(\frac\pi4)}=1.\]
\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&D=[0,\tfrac\pi2],\ R=[0,1],\\&f^{-1}(\tfrac12)=\tfrac\pi4,\ (f^{-1})'(\tfrac12)=1\end{aligned}}\]

Marker: give the reason the piece works (\(f'\gt0\), so increasing and one-to-one); \(\sin a=-\frac1{\sqrt2}\) is rejected because \(a\) must be in \(D\).

Equivalent forms. On this piece \(f^{-1}(x)=\sin^{-1}\sqrt x\); its derivative \(\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1-x}}\) is \(1\) at \(x=\frac12\). ✓ Other pieces such as \([\frac\pi2,\pi]\) are also acceptable; then the answers change to match.

L7.5Limits and continuity with exp and ln that don't need L'Hôpital

Chapter note · graphs and limits of eˣ and ln x · Chapter note · continuity of composite functions

Frequency: 2 of 13 recent papers (Dec 2021, May 2024); 2 of 10 older papers (May 2015, May 2018). Usually one part of a mixed limits question.

Recognise it

A limit or a continuity check where \(e^{(\dots)}\) or \(\ln(\dots)\) sits inside or outside another function, and the inside does not lead to \(\frac00\), \(\frac\infty\infty\), \(0\cdot\infty\) or \(1^\infty\). The work is knowing where \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\) go, and from which side.

Standard procedure

Facts you need (from the graphs): \(e^x\gt0\) and increasing, \(e^0=1\); \(e^u\to+\infty\) as \(u\to+\infty\), \(e^u\to0^+\) as \(u\to-\infty\). \(\ln x\) is defined for \(x\gt0\) and increasing, \(\ln1=0\); \(\ln u\to-\infty\) as \(u\to0^+\), \(\ln u\to+\infty\) as \(u\to+\infty\). Also \(e^{\ln x}=x\) for \(x\gt0\).

  1. Split into outer(inner). Find the limit of the inner part, and the side it comes from (\(1^-\), \(0^+\), …). Use "increasing" to get the side: e.g. \(x\gt0\Rightarrow e^x\gt1\).
  2. Check the form. If you meet \(\frac00\), \(\frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty}\), \(0\cdot\infty\), \(\infty-\infty\) or a variable power, it is not this type: go to the L10 guide. Otherwise carry on; no L'Hôpital.
  3. Finish the limit.
    • 3a. The inner limit is a number where the outer function is continuous: substitute (continuity of \(e^x\), \(\ln x\) on \(x\gt0\), \(\sin\), the Quotient Law with a non-zero bottom). Asked: May 2018.
    • 3b. The inner part runs off to \(\pm\infty\) or to \(0^+\): use the end behaviour above, one layer at a time from the inside out. Asked: May 2015.
    • 3c. The outer function is known only through one-sided limits: use the side found in step 1 and take that one-sided limit. Asked: Dec 2021.
  4. Continuity at \(a\) (piecewise or composite): compute the value at \(a\), the left limit and the right limit, each with steps 1–3. All three equal means continuous. Asked: May 2024.
Worked example · the procedure in action

Dec 2021 Q1(c), Q1: 10%. Either evaluate the limit or explain carefully why the limit does not exist. Indicate clearly the theorems you have used in your argument.

\[\lim_{x\to0^+}g(2-e^x)\ \text{ if }\ \begin{aligned}\lim_{x\to1^+}g(x)&=2,\\ \lim_{x\to1^-}g(x)&=1.\end{aligned}\]

Step 1 Inner part \(u=2-e^x\). As \(x\to0^+\), \(e^x\to e^0=1\) (continuity of \(e^x\)), so \(u\to1\). Side: \(e^x\) is increasing, so \(x\gt0\Rightarrow e^x\gt1\Rightarrow u=2-e^x\lt1\). Hence \(u\to1^-\).

The side is the whole question: \(g\) has different one-sided limits at 1, so "\(u\to1\)" alone is not enough. The marker wants the inequality \(2-e^x\lt1\) and the reason (\(e^x\) increasing).

Step 2 No quotient or power here, so nothing indeterminate: carry on.

Ten seconds' check; it also tells you L'Hôpital is not needed.

Step 3c Substitution for limits: \(u(x)\to1\) with \(u(x)\lt1\) for \(x\gt0\), and \(\displaystyle\lim_{u\to1^-}g(u)=1\). Therefore

\[\lim_{x\to0^+}g(2-e^x)=\lim_{u\to1^-}g(u)=1.\]

"Indicate the theorems": name continuity of \(e^x\), the fact that \(e^x\) is increasing, and the limit-of-a-composite (substitution) rule with the one-sided limit.

\[\boxed{1}\]

Common slip: answering \(2\) by thinking \(2-e^x\) comes from above 1.

Traps
  • L'Hôpital on a limit that is not \(\frac00\). May 2018 Q3(a): the bottom \(1-\ln x\to1\), so the limit is just \(0\). Applying L'Hôpital anyway gives \(\pi\), which is wrong.
  • Losing the side. Dec 2021 Q1(c) (\(2-e^x\to1^-\)) and May 2024 Q2(a) (\(\ln(1-x)\to0^+\) from the left, \(\ln x\to-\infty\) from the right) both depend on it.
  • Wrong branch of a piecewise function. In May 2024 Q2(a), choose the piece of \(f\) by the sign of \(g(x)\), not by the sign of \(x\).

May 2024 Q2(a)Q2: 10%; part marks not shown

Let \(f\) and \(g\) be two functions defined as follows:

\[\begin{aligned}f(x)&=\begin{cases}xe^x & \text{for } x\ge0,\\ -e^x & \text{for } x\lt0,\end{cases}\\ g(x)&=\begin{cases}\ln x & \text{for } x\gt0,\\ \ln(1-x) & \text{for } x\le0.\end{cases}\end{aligned}\]

(a) Show that the composite function \(f\circ g(x)\) is continuous at \(x=0\). [Suggestion: Sketch the graph of \(y=\ln x\) for the properties of \(\ln x\).]

Part (b) is in the L10 guide (L10.6).

Hint

Type L7.5, continuity of a composite (step 4). First step: for \(x\lt0\) and for \(x\gt0\), find the sign of \(g(x)\); that decides which formula of \(f\) to use.

Solution

Step 4 Value. \(g(0)=\ln(1-0)=0\), so \((f\circ g)(0)=f(0)=0\cdot e^0=0\).

Step 1 · left For \(x\lt0\): \(1-x\gt1\), so \(g(x)=\ln(1-x)\gt0\), and \(g(x)\to\ln1=0^+\). Use the top piece of \(f\):

\[f(g(x))=g(x)\,e^{g(x)}.\]

Step 3a \(ue^u\) is continuous at \(u=0\), so the left limit is \(0\cdot e^0=0\).

Step 1 · right For \(0\lt x\lt1\): \(g(x)=\ln x\lt0\), and \(\ln x\to-\infty\) as \(x\to0^+\). Use the bottom piece of \(f\):

\[f(g(x))=-e^{\ln x}=-x.\]

Step 3b So the right limit is \(\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0^+}(-x)=0\). (Or: \(e^u\to0\) as \(u\to-\infty\).)

Step 4 Left limit \(=\) right limit \(=\) value \(=0\).

\[\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}(f\circ g)(x)=0=(f\circ g)(0)}\]

Marker: state the sign of \(g(x)\) on each side before choosing the piece of \(f\); the conclusion needs all three numbers.

Equivalent forms. So \(f\circ g\) is continuous at \(0\). Note \(g\) itself is not continuous at 0 (\(\ln x\to-\infty\)), yet the composite is: \(f\) flattens \(-\infty\) to \(0\).
