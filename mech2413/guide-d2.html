Exam format

3 h · any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question.

Formula sheet: Dec 2021–Dec 2024 print, at the start of Section B, the relative velocity of a rigid body (\(\vb r_{B/O}=\vb r_{A/O}+\vb r_{B/A}\); \(\vb v_{B/O}=\vb v_{A/O}+\vb v_{B/A}\); \(\vb v_B=\vb v_A+\vb\omega\times\vb r_{B/A}\)) and \(I=\int r^2\,\mathrm dm\), plus orbit formulas and \(g=9.81\u{m/s^2}\). They do not give the slider-crank formula, any standard moment of inertia (\(\tfrac12mR^2\), \(\tfrac1{12}mL^2\)), the parallel axis theorem, \(\tfrac12I\omega^2\) or \(\sum M_G=I_G\alpha\). Dec 2017 and Dec 2018 printed the relative-velocity lines beside the question; 2014–2015 printed \(I_w=m_wr_w^2/2\) inside the question when needed.

2026/27 changes: orbital dynamics and re-entry are removed (D1 handout, slide 7), so Section B has less rocket/orbit material; control (C1) is added and has no past papers. How the 2026 paper will share its marks has not been announced.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

\(x\) right, \(y\) up, \(z\) out of the page. \(\vb\omega=\omega\hat k\) with \(\omega\gt0\) anticlockwise: a clockwise 10 rad/s is \(\omega=-10\u{rad/s}\); the same for \(\alpha\). \(\vb r_{B/A}\) points from \(A\) to \(B\). Put signed numbers into the vector formulas and say the direction in words at the end. In kinetics (D2.3) a different positive sense is sometimes handier (e.g. clockwise for a wheel rolling right); it is stated beside the equations when used.

D2.1Relative velocity in mechanisms

D2.1Velocities in a slider-crank, linkage or rolling body7 sittings · 59 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a figure of a crank, connecting rod and slider, two or three pinned bars ending on a wheel, a bar with sliders on both ends, or a wheel on a floor or between racks; one angular velocity given; "determine the velocity of … and the angular velocity of … at this instant".
Do
origin and axes → write every \(\vb r\) in components from the figure → start at the point whose velocity you know → \(\vb v_B=\vb v_A+\omega\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}\) link by link → use the constraint (slider moves only along its guide, pin on a wheel moves perpendicular to the radius, contact point of a gear = rack velocity) → one unknown per component equation.
Watch
clockwise is negative; \(\vb r_{B/A}\) is from \(A\) to \(B\); read the angle in the figure (from horizontal or vertical?) before writing components; give magnitude and direction.

Concept: Notes §4 · relative velocity, §5 · instantaneous centre, rolling, §6 · rod in the plane, §7 · two links, §8 · slider-crank, §9 · other mechanisms.

Ready answer · the cross product in 2D \[\omega\hat k\times(x\,\hat\imath+y\,\hat\jmath)=-\omega y\,\hat\imath+\omega x\,\hat\jmath\]

In words: swap the components, put a minus on the new \(x\), multiply by \(\omega\). The result is always perpendicular to \(\vb r\).

Ready answer · slider-crank (slider on the line through the crank pivot) \[\begin{aligned}v_C&=-r\omega\Big(\sin\theta\\&\quad+\frac{r\sin\theta\cos\theta}{\sqrt{l^2-r^2\sin^2\theta}}\Big)\end{aligned}\]

In words: slider speed = crank speed \(r\omega\) times \(\sin\theta\), plus a correction that vanishes when the rod is very long; zero at both dead centres (\(\theta=0^\circ,180^\circ\)). Here \(\theta\) is the crank angle from the slider's line (towards the slider), \(\omega\) its rate, \(v_C\) positive away from the pivot. Derive it once in the exam if you use it: it is not on the sheet.

Ready answer · three recurring constraints

Rolling without slip on a fixed floor: the contact point has zero velocity, \(v_O=\omega_{\rm cw}R\). Wheel between two racks (top \(v_t\), bottom \(v_b\), right +): \(v_{\rm centre}=\tfrac12(v_t+v_b)\), \(\omega=(v_b-v_t)/(2R)\) anticlockwise +. Bar on a vertical and a horizontal guide (bar at \(\theta\) to the horizontal, \(A\) on the vertical guide): \(|\omega|=v_A/(L\cos\theta)\), \(v_B=v_A\tan\theta\).

Dec 2024 B2(a)–(c)12 marks(d), (e) in D2.2

The figure below shows a crank and vertical slider mechanism. The crank is attached to a uniform disk with total mass of \(m=200\u{kg}\), which acts as a flywheel. It is driven by the crank \(AB\) with a clockwise angular velocity of 10 rad/s.

Figure: crank \(AB=0.25\u{m}\) from the fixed pivot \(A\) (centre of the flywheel), connecting rod \(BC=0.75\u{m}\), slider \(C\) in a vertical guide directly above \(A\). The paper also marks \(\omega_{BC}=2.43\u{rad/s}\) and the rod at \(13.6^\circ\) to the vertical.

(a) Determine the velocity of point \(B\) at the instant shown. [4]
(b) Determine the velocity of the slider at the instant shown. [4]
(c) Draw a graph showing how the velocity of the slider changes when the crank rotates one cycle (i.e. crank angle sweeps from 0 to 360 degrees). [4]
(d) Derive the moment of inertia for a uniform disk and determine the moment of inertia for the flywheel shown in the figure. [4]
(e) Explain the role of the flywheel in this mechanism, and explain why a rimmed flywheel is preferred over a uniform flywheel. [4] (d), (e) → D2.2

The 45° label sits at \(B\); it is read as the crank's angle to the vertical (the printed \(\omega_{BC}=2.43\u{rad/s}\) and the 13.6° rod angle both come out of this reading). Same reading as the notes.

Hint

Type D2.1. Origin at \(A\), \(\omega_{AB}=-10\u{rad/s}\). For (b) the slider moves only vertically, so the \(x\)-component of \(\vb v_C\) is zero: that fixes \(\omega_{BC}\). For (c) use the slider-crank formula with the angle measured from the vertical.

Solution
  1. (a) \(\vb r_{B/A}=0.25(-\sin45^\circ,\ \cos45^\circ)=(-0.1768,\ 0.1768)\u{m}\). \[\begin{aligned}\vb v_B&=-10\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}\\&=(1.768,\ 1.768)\u{m/s}\end{aligned}\]
  2. (b) \(y_C=0.1768+\sqrt{0.75^2-0.1768^2}=0.906\u{m}\), so \(\vb r_{C/B}=(0.1768,\ 0.7289)\u{m}\) (rod at 13.6° to the vertical). \[\begin{aligned}\vb v_C&=\vb v_B+\omega_{BC}\hat k\times\vb r_{C/B}\\&=(1.768-0.7289\,\omega_{BC},\\&\qquad1.768+0.1768\,\omega_{BC})\end{aligned}\]
  3. Slider in a vertical guide: \(x\)-component zero, \(\omega_{BC}=1.768/0.7289=2.43\u{rad/s}\) (positive: anticlockwise). Then \(v_C=1.768+0.1768(2.43)=2.20\u{m/s}\).
  4. (c) With \(\theta\) the crank angle clockwise from top dead centre (the instant shown is \(\theta=315^\circ\)), \(r=0.25\u{m}\), \(l=0.75\u{m}\), \(\omega=10\u{rad/s}\), upward positive, the ready formula gives the curve below. It checks: \(v_C(315^\circ)=+2.20\u{m/s}\).

Slider velocity over one crank revolution

Zero at the dead centres 0° and 180°; extremes ±2.64 m/s at 73.2° and 286.8° (not at 90°/270°: the finite rod skews the sine wave). The dot is the instant in the figure.

Marker note

For (c) the marks are for a labelled sketch: axes with units, zeros at 0° and 180°, peaks of about ±2.6 m/s, and the shape (a distorted sine). Say which way is positive.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\vb v_B=(1.768,\,1.768)\u{m/s}:\\&2.5\u{m/s}\text{ up-right at }45^\circ\ (\perp AB)\\&\omega_{BC}=2.43\u{rad/s}\text{ anticlockwise}\\&v_C=2.20\u{m/s}\text{ upward}\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2023 B2(a)–(b)8 marksalso May 2019 B1(a) 10 marks · (c)–(e) in D2.2

As shown in the schematic below, two bars \(AB\) and \(BC\) are connected to a flywheel at point \(C\). The flywheel rotates around point \(D\). At this instant (\(\theta=60^\circ\)), the bar \(AB\) of the linkage has a clockwise angular velocity \(\omega_{AB}=30\u{rad/s}\).

Figure: \(AB=r_B=0.2\u{m}\) at \(\theta=60^\circ\) above the horizontal; \(BC=0.2\u{m}\) horizontal; \(C\) on the rim of the flywheel, directly below its centre \(D\), \(r_C=0.1\u{m}\).

(a) Determine the velocity of point \(C\) and angular velocity of link \(BC\) at this instant. [4]
(b) Determine the angular velocity of the flywheel centred at \(D\) at this instant. [4]
(c) \(I=\tfrac12mr_C^2\), (d) kinetic energy, (e) mass towards the edge → D2.2

May 2019 B1(a) is the same linkage in one 10-mark part: "Determine the angular velocities of member \(BC\) and the wheel at this instant."

Hint

Type D2.1. \(C\) is on the wheel directly below \(D\), so \(\vb v_C\) is horizontal. Write \(\vb v_B\) from bar \(AB\), then \(\vb v_C=\vb v_B+\omega_{BC}\hat k\times\vb r_{C/B}\) and set the \(y\)-component to zero.

Solution
  1. \(\vb r_{B/A}=0.2(\cos60^\circ,\ \sin60^\circ)=(0.1,\ 0.1732)\u{m}\), \(\omega_{AB}=-30\u{rad/s}\): \[\begin{aligned}\vb v_B&=-30\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}\\&=(5.196,\ -3.0)\u{m/s}\end{aligned}\]
  2. \(\vb r_{C/B}=(0.2,\ 0)\u{m}\): \(\vb v_C=(5.196,\ -3.0+0.2\,\omega_{BC})\). \(\vb v_C\) is horizontal, so \(\omega_{BC}=+15\u{rad/s}\) and \(v_C=5.196\u{m/s}\) to the right.
  3. (b) \(\vb r_{C/D}=(0,\ -0.1)\u{m}\): \(\omega_D\hat k\times\vb r_{C/D}=(0.1\,\omega_D,\ 0)=(5.196,\ 0)\Rightarrow\omega_D=51.96\u{rad/s}\), positive: anticlockwise.
Marker note

State why \(\vb v_C\) is horizontal (\(C\) moves on a circle about \(D\), so \(\vb v_C\perp DC\)); that sentence is what lets you solve for \(\omega_{BC}\).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&v_C=5.20\u{m/s}\rightarrow\\&\omega_{BC}=15\u{rad/s}\text{ anticlockwise}\\&\omega_D=52.0\u{rad/s}\text{ anticlockwise}\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2022 B2(c)10 marks

The figure below shows a tilting device that maintains a sloshing water bath for washing vegetable produce. The crank \(OA\) oscillates about the vertical axis.

Figure: basket pinned at the fixed pivot \(D\); its edge \(DB=360\u{mm}\) at \(\theta\) above the horizontal. A rigid L-shaped link runs from \(B\) 240 mm horizontally right, then 100 mm down to pin \(A\). Crank \(OA=80\u{mm}\), vertical, \(O\) fixed directly below \(A\); \(O\) and \(D\) at the same height.

(i) The crank \(OA\) has a clockwise angular velocity of \(4\pi\) rad/s when it is exactly vertical. Determine the angular velocity of the basket in the position shown where \(\theta=30\) degrees. [6]
(ii) If the angle \(\theta\) is increased to 45 degrees, describe how the angular velocity of the basket in this position is affected. [4] (a) airplane → D1.2 · (b) bar \(I\) → D2.2

(ii) is read as "same crank and link positions, only \(\theta\) changed in the velocity equations". Strictly, with these lengths the basket cannot reach \(45^\circ\) (the link can only reach \(B\) up to \(\theta\approx36.7^\circ\)), so treat (ii) as a trend question.

Hint

Type D2.1. Three bodies: crank \(OA\), the L-link \(BA\) (one rigid body, one \(\omega_{AB}\)), basket \(DB\). Write \(\vb v_A\) from the crank and \(\vb v_B\) from the basket, then join them with \(\vb v_B=\vb v_A+\omega_{AB}\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}\): two components, two unknowns.

Solution
  1. Crank: \(\vb r_{A/O}=(0,\ 0.08)\u{m}\), \(\omega_{OA}=-4\pi\): \(\vb v_A=(0.32\pi,\ 0)=(1.005,\ 0)\u{m/s}\).
  2. Basket: \(\vb r_{B/D}=0.36(\cos30^\circ,\sin30^\circ)=(0.3118,\ 0.18)\u{m}\), so \(\vb v_B=\omega_b\hat k\times\vb r_{B/D}=(-0.18\,\omega_b,\ 0.3118\,\omega_b)\).
  3. Link: \(\vb r_{B/A}=(-0.24,\ 0.10)\u{m}\), so \(\omega_{AB}\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}=(-0.10\,\omega_{AB},\ -0.24\,\omega_{AB})\). \[\begin{aligned}x:&\ -0.18\,\omega_b=1.005-0.10\,\omega_{AB}\\ y:&\ 0.3118\,\omega_b=-0.24\,\omega_{AB}\end{aligned}\]
  4. \(y\) gives \(\omega_{AB}=-1.299\,\omega_b\); into \(x\): \(-0.3099\,\omega_b=1.005\), so \(\omega_b=-3.24\u{rad/s}\) and \(\omega_{AB}=+4.21\u{rad/s}\). Check by instantaneous centre: \(\vb v_A\) horizontal and \(\vb v_B\perp DB\) put the IC of the link at \((0.552,\ 0.319)\u{m}\), and \(1.005/(0.319-0.08)=4.21\u{rad/s}\). ✓
  5. (ii) In general \(\omega_b=-\dfrac{0.32\pi}{0.36\sin\theta+0.15\cos\theta}\). The denominator grows with \(\theta\) (up to \(\theta=67.4^\circ\)), so the basket turns more slowly: at \(45^\circ\), \(0.36\sin45^\circ+0.15\cos45^\circ=0.3606\) and \(\omega_b=2.79\u{rad/s}\) clockwise, about 14% less. Reason: the effective lever arm \(0.36\sin\theta+0.15\cos\theta\) (in m) through which the crank's horizontal speed turns the basket grows with \(\theta\), so the same \(v_A\) gives a smaller \(\omega_b\).
Marker note

The L-shaped link is one rigid body: use its straight-line vector \(\vb r_{B/A}=(-0.24,\ 0.10)\), not the two legs separately.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{(i) }\omega_{\rm basket}=3.24\u{rad/s}\text{ clockwise}\\&\quad(=0.32\pi/0.3099)\\&\text{(ii) smaller: }\approx2.79\u{rad/s}\text{ cw}\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2021 B1(d)7 marks

A portion of a machine consists of a rigid bar \(AB\), 750 mm long, with smooth sliders on both ends. The rigid bar moves in the vertical plane such that slider \(A\) follows a vertical guide and slider \(B\) follows a horizontal guide, as shown in the diagram below.

(i) At the instant when \(\theta=30\) degrees, determine the relative position vector of \(B\) with respect to \(A\) (\(\vb r_{B/A}\)). [2]
(ii) At the instant when \(\theta=30\) degrees, slider \(A\) is driven downward with a speed of 250 mm/s. Find the angular velocity \(\omega\) of the bar and the velocity of \(B\) at this instant. [5] (a) Newton's laws → D1.4 · (b) roller coaster → D1.2 · (c) bar \(I\) → D2.2

On the paper the \(\theta\) arc at \(B\) runs from the horizontal guide up to the bar, so \(\theta\) is read from the horizontal. (If it were from the vertical: \(\omega=0.667\u{rad/s}\), \(v_B=0.433\u{m/s}\).)

Hint

Type D2.1. Origin anywhere, \(x\) right, \(y\) up. \(B\) is to the right of and below \(A\). In (ii) \(\vb v_A=(0,\,-0.25)\) and \(\vb v_B=(v_B,\,0)\): write \(\vb v_B=\vb v_A+\omega\hat k\times\vb r_{B/A}\) and compare components.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\vb r_{B/A}=(0.650\,\hat\imath-0.375\,\hat\jmath)\u{m}\\&\omega=0.385\u{rad/s}\text{ anticlockwise}\\&\vb v_B=0.144\,\hat\imath\u{m/s}\ (144\u{mm/s}\rightarrow)\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2018 B1(b)4 marks

A bowling ball is cast on the "alley" with a backspin of \(\omega=10\u{rad/s}\) while its center \(O\) has a forward velocity of \(v_O=8\u{m/s}\). Determine the velocity of the contact point \(A\) in contact with the alley. (The relative-velocity formulas were printed beside the question.)

Ball radius 120 mm. (a) → D1.1 · (c) → D1.2

Pinion gear \(A\) rolls on the gear racks \(B\) and \(C\) as shown in Figure B1(b). D2.2Moment of inertia by integration; flywheel and rotational KE

D2.2Derive \(I\), then use \(\tfrac12I\omega^2\)6 sittings · 41 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"by starting from first principles and performing an integration, determine / show that \(I=\dots\)" for a uniform bar or disk (3–5 marks), then a number for a flywheel, its kinetic energy, and "explain" what happens if the mass moves to the rim or the axis moves to the end.
Do
sketch the body with one slice (bar: strip \(\mathrm dx\) at \(x\); disk: ring of radius \(r\), width \(\mathrm dr\)) → \(\mathrm dm\) = density × slice size → \(I=\int r^2\,\mathrm dm\) with limits → numbers → \(T=\tfrac12I\omega^2\).
Watch
the sheet only gives \(I=\int r^2\,\mathrm dm\): the marks are for the slice, \(\mathrm dm\) and the limits, not the final formula. For the bar about its centre the limits are \(-L/2\) to \(L/2\). Units kg·m², J.

Concept: Notes §11 · moment of inertia, §12 · by integration, §13 · parallel axis, §14 · flywheels and KE.

Ready answer · the four derivations (not on the sheet) \[\begin{aligned}\text{bar, centre: }&\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}x^2\tfrac mL\,\mathrm dx=\tfrac1{12}mL^2\\ \text{bar, end: }&\int_0^Lx^2\tfrac mL\,\mathrm dx=\tfrac13mL^2\\ \text{disk: }&\int_0^R r^2\tfrac{2m}{R^2}r\,\mathrm dr=\tfrac12mR^2\\ \text{ring / rim: }&I=mR^2\end{aligned}\]

In words: add up mass × (distance from the axis)² slice by slice. Annulus (inner radius \(R_1\)): \(\tfrac12m(R^2+R_1^2)\). Parallel axis: \(I=I_G+md^2\).

Ready answer · flywheel explanations

Role: stores energy \(T=\tfrac12I\omega^2\); absorbs it when the driving torque exceeds the load and returns it when it falls short, so the speed fluctuates less (\(\Delta E=\tfrac12I(\omega_{\max}^2-\omega_{\min}^2)\): for a given energy swing, larger \(I\) means a smaller speed swing). Rimmed / mass at the edge: same mass, larger \(I\) (up to \(mR^2\), twice the solid disk), so more energy at the same speed and less ripple per kilogram.

Dec 2024 B2(d)–(e)8 marksstem and figure: D2.1

(A uniform disk of mass \(m=200\u{kg}\) acts as a flywheel on the crank of a vertical slider mechanism.)

(d) Derive the moment of inertia for a uniform disk and determine the moment of inertia for the flywheel shown in the figure. [4]
(e) Explain the role of the flywheel in this mechanism, and explain why a rimmed flywheel is preferred over a uniform flywheel. [4]

The flywheel radius is not printed. As in the notes, \(I\) is left as \(100R^2\) and evaluated with \(R\approx0.22\u{m}\), scaled from the drawing against the 0.25 m crank (the disk radius is 0.88 of the crank; the figure is not to scale overall). Write your assumed radius in the exam.

Hint

Type D2.2. Slice the disk into thin rings of radius \(r\) and width \(\mathrm dr\); the ring's mass is (mass per area) × (circumference × width).

Solution
  1. (d) Mass per area \(m/(\pi R^2)\); ring area \(2\pi r\,\mathrm dr\), so \(\mathrm dm=\dfrac{2m}{R^2}r\,\mathrm dr\). Every point of the ring is \(r\) from the axis: \[\begin{aligned}I&=\int_0^Rr^2\frac{2m}{R^2}r\,\mathrm dr\\&=\frac{2m}{R^2}\cdot\frac{R^4}4=\tfrac12mR^2\end{aligned}\]
  2. Flywheel: \(I=\tfrac12(200)R^2=100R^2\u{kg\,m^2}\); with \(R\approx0.22\u{m}\), \(I\approx4.9\u{kg\,m^2}\).
  3. (e) Role: the slider's load varies through each revolution, so the torque needed at the crank fluctuates. The flywheel stores kinetic energy \(\tfrac12I\omega^2\), absorbing energy when the drive exceeds the demand and giving it back when it falls short, so \(\omega\) stays nearly constant.
  4. Rimmed: putting most of the mass at the rim raises \(I\) towards \(mR^2\) (twice the uniform disk's \(\tfrac12mR^2\)), so it stores more energy and smooths the speed better for the same mass (and the same, lighter, bearing load).
Marker note

In (d) write \(\mathrm dm\) and the limits explicitly; in (e) both halves (role, why rimmed) carry 2 marks each and need the word "energy" and the reason "larger \(I\) for the same mass".

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&I=\tfrac12mR^2=100R^2\u{kg\,m^2}\\&\approx4.9\u{kg\,m^2}\ (R\approx0.22\u{m})\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2023 B2(c)–(e)12 marksstem and figure: D2.1

(The flywheel centred at \(D\), radius \(r_C=0.1\u{m}\), was found in (b) to rotate at \(\omega_D=52.0\u{rad/s}\).)

(c) Assuming that the flywheel has uniform weight distribution and total mass \(m\), show that the moment of inertia of the flywheel about its symmetry axis is \(I=\tfrac12mr_C^2\). Calculate the moment of inertia if \(m=10\u{kg}\). [4]
(d) The flywheel is now detached from the bar \(BC\) and continues to rotate at the angular velocity determined in part (b); determine the rotational kinetic energy (\(T\)) of the flywheel. [4]
(e) Explain how the rotational kinetic energy changes if the weight distribution of the flywheel is shifted towards the edge. [4]

Hint

Type D2.2. (c) is the ring-slice integral with \(R=r_C\). (d) needs \(\omega_D\) from (b) in rad/s.

Solution
  1. (c) Rings: \(\mathrm dm=\dfrac{m}{\pi r_C^2}2\pi r\,\mathrm dr\); \(I=\displaystyle\int_0^{r_C}\frac{2m}{r_C^2}r^3\,\mathrm dr=\tfrac12mr_C^2\). With \(m=10\u{kg}\): \(I=\tfrac12(10)(0.1)^2=0.05\u{kg\,m^2}\).
  2. (d) \(T=\tfrac12I\omega_D^2=\tfrac12(0.05)(51.96)^2=67.5\u{J}\).
  3. (e) Moving mass outwards increases \(I\) (same \(m\), larger \(r\) in \(\int r^2\,\mathrm dm\)), up to \(mr_C^2=0.1\u{kg\,m^2}\) for a thin ring. At the same \(\omega\), \(T=\tfrac12I\omega^2\) rises in proportion, up to twice: 135 J.
Marker note

In (e) say "at the same angular velocity"; if instead the same energy is given, \(\omega\) would be lower. The examiner wants \(T\propto I\).

\(\boxed{I=0.05\u{kg\,m^2},\quad T=67.5\u{J},\quad\text{(e) }T\uparrow\text{ (up to }135\u{J})}\)

Dec 2022 B2(b)5 marksalso Dec 2021 B1(c) 5 marks

By starting from first principles and performing an integration, determine the mass moment of inertia of a uniform bar of mass \(m\) and length \(L\) about an axis through the center perpendicular to the length. Describe how the moment of inertia changes if the rotation axis is moved to the end of the bar.

Hint

Type D2.2. Origin at the centre; slice of length \(\mathrm dx\) at \(x\) has \(\mathrm dm=(m/L)\,\mathrm dx\). Mind the limits.

Solution
  1. \(\mathrm dm=\dfrac mL\,\mathrm dx\), distance from the axis \(|x|\): \[\begin{aligned}I_G&=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}x^2\frac mL\,\mathrm dx\\&=\frac mL\left[\frac{x^3}3\right]_{-L/2}^{L/2}=\tfrac1{12}mL^2\end{aligned}\]
  2. Axis at the end: integrate from 0 to \(L\), or use the parallel axis theorem with \(d=L/2\): \[I_{\rm end}=\tfrac1{12}mL^2+m\left(\tfrac L2\right)^2=\tfrac13mL^2\]
  3. \(I_{\rm end}=4I_G\): the same mass now lies on average further from the axis (up to \(L\) instead of \(L/2\)).
Marker note

"Describe" wants a sentence with the reason (mass further from the axis), not just \(\tfrac13mL^2\).

\(\boxed{I_G=\tfrac1{12}mL^2;\quad I_{\rm end}=\tfrac13mL^2=4I_G}\)

Dec 2015 B1(b), (d)8 marksstem and figure: D2.3

(A horizontal force \(P\) is applied at height \(y\) above the centre \(G\) of a homogeneous disk of weight \(W\), mass \(m\), radius \(r\), which rolls without sliding.)

(b) Show the moment of inertia \(I_G\) about point \(G\) of the disk as follows (where \(m\) is mass of the disk): \(I_G=\tfrac12mr^2\). [4]
(d) If the same disk is modified with a hollow, explain how the resultant angular acceleration would be affected. [4]

Hint

Type D2.2. (b): ring slices, as in Dec 2024 (d). (d): redo (c) of D2.3 with a general \(I_G\) and ask what a hollow does to \(I_G\).

Solution
  1. (b) \(\mathrm dm=\dfrac{m}{\pi r^2}2\pi s\,\mathrm ds\) for a ring of radius \(s\); \(I_G=\displaystyle\int_0^r s^2\frac{2m}{r^2}s\,\mathrm ds=\tfrac12mr^2\).
  2. (d) From (c) with a general \(I_G\): \(\alpha=\dfrac{P(r+y)}{I_G+mr^2}\).
  3. Same mass and radius, mass moved outwards (hollow centre): \(I_G\) larger, so \(\alpha\) smaller. Thin-ring limit \(I_G=mr^2\): \(\alpha=P(r+y)/(2mr^2)\), three quarters of the solid disk's.
  4. If instead the hollow is made by removing material (mass falls), \(I_G+mr^2\) falls and \(\alpha\) increases: state which case you assume.
Marker note

The examiner wants the formula \(\alpha=P(r+y)/(I_G+mr^2)\) and the link "mass further from \(G\) → larger \(I_G\) → smaller \(\alpha\)".

\(\boxed{I_G=\tfrac12mr^2;\quad\text{hollow, same }m:\ \alpha\downarrow\ (\text{ring: }\tfrac34\times)}\)

(The apparatus has a symmetrically mounted uniform bar of total mass \(M\) and length \(l\) on top of the rotating shaft.)

(a) Prove that the moment of inertia for the top straight bar is \(I=Ml^2/12\). [3]

D2.3Rigid-body kinetics: \(F=ma\), \(M=I\alpha\)

Decision procedure
  1. Split the system into bodies that move differently: each hanging or sliding mass (translation only), each wheel, drum or shaft (rotation, or rolling = both). Draw one FBD per body, with an acceleration arrow (\(a\) or \(\alpha\)) beside it.
  2. Write the equations: translating body \(\sum F=ma\) along its motion; wheel on a fixed axle \(\sum M_O=I_O\alpha\); rolling body \(\sum F_x=ma_G\), \(\sum F_y=0\), \(\sum M_G=I_G\alpha\).
  3. Add the kinematic links: cord on a drum of radius \(r\) without slip: \(a=\alpha r\); rolling without slip: \(a_G=\alpha r\). Count: unknowns = equations.
  4. If the question asks "will it move / minimum force to hold": set \(a=0\) and use static friction at its limit \(\mu_sN\). If it moves: kinetic friction \(\mu_kN\), opposing the motion.
  5. Check limits: massless wheel → both tensions equal; no friction, no inertia → \(a=g\); light hanging mass → the other body alone.

Cue: a wheel or bar with mass (or "\(I=\dots\)" given) plus a force or hanging mass → this type. Traps: one tension through a wheel with inertia (the two sides differ: \((T_2-T_1)r=I\alpha\)); friction direction on a rolling disk (assume one, let the sign decide); forgetting the second spring/contact force in a symmetric clamp.

Concept: Notes §2 · translation, \(\sum F=ma_G\), §3 · rotation about a fixed axis, §10 · torque and \(M=I\alpha\). Not set since 2015, but still on the handout (slide 68).

Ready answer · hanging mass on a drum with inertia \[a=\frac{mg-(\text{resisting force at rim})}{m+I/r^2}\]

In words: net driving force divided by the "effective mass"; a wheel of inertia \(I\) and radius \(r\) behaves like an extra mass \(I/r^2\) (a uniform disk adds \(m_w/2\)).

Dec 2015 B1(a), (c)12 marks(b), (d) in D2.2

As shown in Figure B1, a horizontally-directed force \(P\) is applied to a homogeneous disk weighing \(W\) with radius \(r\).

(a) Draw the free body diagram of the disk. [4]
(b) Show the moment of inertia \(I_G\) about point \(G\) of the disk as follows (where \(m\) is mass of the disk): \(I_G=\tfrac12mr^2\). [4]
(c) Considering force equilibrium in \(x\)- and \(y\)-directions and also the moment equilibrium about point \(G\), derive the expressions of i) angular acceleration \(\alpha\), ii) friction \(F\), and iii) normal force \(N\) between the plane and disk. Assume that the disk rolls without sliding on the ground. (Take acceleration of gravity as \(g\).) [8]
(d) If the same disk is modified with a hollow, explain how the resultant angular acceleration would be affected. [4] (b), (d) → D2.2

Hint

Type D2.3. Unknowns \(a_G\), \(\alpha\), \(F\), \(N\): three equations of motion plus rolling \(a_G=\alpha r\). Take \(a_G\) right and \(\alpha\) clockwise positive; draw \(F\) backwards and let the sign decide.

Solution
  1. (a) FBD: \(W=mg\) down at \(G\); \(N\) up at the contact point; friction \(F\) along the ground at the contact point (drawn left); \(P\) right at height \(y\) above \(G\). Add \(a_G\) and \(\alpha\) beside it.
  2. (c) Right and clockwise positive: \[\begin{aligned}\rightarrow:&\ P-F=ma_G\\ \uparrow:&\ N-W=0\\ \text{↻}G:&\ Py+Fr=\tfrac12mr^2\alpha\\ \text{rolling}:&\ a_G=\alpha r\end{aligned}\]
  3. Put \(F=P-m\alpha r\) into the moment equation: \(Py+Pr-m\alpha r^2=\tfrac12mr^2\alpha\), so \(\alpha=\dfrac{2P(r+y)}{3mr^2}\).
  4. Back-substitute: \(F=P-\tfrac23P\dfrac{r+y}{r}=\dfrac{P(r-2y)}{3r}\). Special cases: \(y=0\) gives \(\alpha=2P/(3mr)\); \(F=0\) at \(y=r/2\); \(F\) points forwards if \(y\gt r/2\).
Marker note

The FBD earns 4 marks only with all four forces at the right points (friction and \(N\) at the contact, not at \(G\)).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}\alpha&=\frac{2P(r+y)}{3mr^2}\ \text{(cw)}\\ F&=\frac{P(r-2y)}{3r}\ (\leftarrow),\quad N=W=mg\end{aligned}}\)

Dec 2014 B1(b)–(d)17 marks(a) in D2.2

As shown in Figure B1, a falling mass \(m\) is used to pull a cord which is wrapped around a horizontally mounted disc of radius \(r\), thereby rotating a symmetrically mounted, uniform bar of total mass \(M\) and length \(l\). The two rolling disks have negligible inertia but the vertical rotating shaft of the larger disk has friction with a control housing. The shaft has a radius of \(r_s=r/2\) and friction coefficients \(\mu_s\) (static) and \(\mu_k\) (dynamic). The housing consists of two springs which exert a pair of normal forces \(N\) (each) on two opposing sides.

(a) Prove that the moment of inertia for the top straight bar is \(I=Ml^2/12\). [3]
(b) Determine the minimum spring force \(N_{\min}\) that can hold mass \(m\) from falling. [5]
(c) Determine the falling acceleration \(a\) when \(N\lt N_{\min}\). [10]
(d) If the whole set of apparatus is installed in a lift which accelerates upward, will the acceleration \(a\) you observe from inside the lift be higher or lower than the value determined in step (c)? Why? [2] (a) → D2.2

In (d) the step letter is hand-corrected on the paper; read as "step (c)".

Hint

Type D2.3. Two bodies: the hanging mass (translation) and shaft + disc + bar (rotation about the vertical axis). Each spring force \(N\) gives a friction force \(\mu N\) at radius \(r_s\); there are two of them. Cord on the disc: \(a=\alpha r\).

Solution
  1. (b) Held at rest: cord tension \(T=mg\), torque \(mgr\) on the shaft. The largest static friction torque is \(2(\mu_sN)r_s=\mu_sNr\). Holds if \(\mu_sNr\ge mgr\), so \(N_{\min}=mg/\mu_s\).
  2. (c) Mass, down positive: \(mg-T=ma\). Shaft + disc + bar (disks massless, so \(I=Ml^2/12\)), kinetic friction: \[\begin{aligned}Tr-2\mu_kN\tfrac r2&=\frac{Ml^2}{12}\alpha\\ \alpha&=a/r\end{aligned}\]
  3. Eliminate \(T=m(g-a)\): \(m(g-a)r-\mu_kNr=\dfrac{Ml^2}{12}\dfrac ar\), so \[a=\frac{mg-\mu_kN}{m+Ml^2/(12r^2)}\] Check: \(N=0,\ M=0\Rightarrow a=g\). ✓
  4. (d) In a lift accelerating up at \(a_L\), the mass behaves as if gravity were \(g+a_L\); \(N\) comes from springs and does not change. So \(a\) (measured in the lift) is higher, by \(ma_L/(m+Ml^2/12r^2)\).
Marker note

Both sides of the shaft carry a friction force: the friction torque is \(2\mu N r_s=\mu Nr\), not \(\mu Nr_s\).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&N_{\min}=\frac{mg}{\mu_s}\\&a=\frac{mg-\mu_kN}{m+\dfrac{Ml^2}{12r^2}}\\&\text{(d) higher}\end{aligned}}\)

May 2014 B1(a), (c)14 marks

As shown in Figure B1, two crates are connected through a belt which runs over a pulley with grooved wheel surface such that the belt does not slip around the wheel. The wheel is made of a uniform circular disk of mass \(m_w\) and a certain radius \(r_w\). The masses of the crates are \(m_A\) and \(m_B\), respectively. The static coefficient of friction between crate A and the inclined surface is \(\mu_s=\tan(10^\circ)\), while the dynamic friction coefficient is less, \(\mu_k\lt\mu_s\). The second moment of inertia for the uniform wheel is calculated as \(I_w=\int r^2\,\mathrm dm=m_wr_w^2/2\).

(a) Draw free-body diagrams for crates A and B, separately, when the crates move. [5]
(b) When crate B is very light, \(m_B\to0\), determine the minimum slope \(\alpha\) (in degrees) when the crates do move. [6]
(c) Determine the acceleration of the crates, \(a\), if the crates do move with a finite \(m_B\) and a suitably given inclination \(\alpha\). Hint: there are different belt tensions at the two sides of the pulley wheel. [9] (b) → D1.3 (\(\tan\alpha\gt\mu_s\Rightarrow\alpha_{\min}=10^\circ\))

Hint

Type D2.3. Three bodies: crate A (slides down the slope towards the pulley), wheel (rotates, \(\alpha_w=a/r_w\)), crate B (falls). Belt tension \(T_A\) on A's side, \(T_B\) on B's side.

Solution
  1. (a) Crate A: weight \(m_Ag\) down, normal \(N_A=m_Ag\cos\alpha\) perpendicular to the slope, kinetic friction \(F_k=\mu_kN_A\) up the slope (opposing motion), belt tension \(T_A\) along the slope towards the pulley. Crate B: \(m_Bg\) down, \(T_B\) up.
  2. (c) Motion positive (A down the slope, B down): \[\begin{aligned}\text{B}:&\ m_Bg-T_B=m_Ba\\ \text{A}:&\ m_Ag\sin\alpha-\mu_km_Ag\cos\alpha\\&\quad+T_A=m_Aa\\ \text{wheel}:&\ (T_B-T_A)r_w=\tfrac12m_wr_w^2\frac a{r_w}\end{aligned}\]
  3. Add the three (the wheel line gives \(T_B-T_A=\tfrac12m_wa\)); the tensions cancel: \[\begin{aligned}&(m_A+m_B+\tfrac12m_w)\,a\\&\quad=g\big[m_B+m_A(\sin\alpha\\&\qquad\quad-\mu_k\cos\alpha)\big]\end{aligned}\] Checks: \(m_w=0\) gives equal tensions; \(m_A=m_w=0\) gives \(a=g\). ✓
Marker note

The wheel term \(\tfrac12m_w\) in the denominator is the point of the hint; a single tension through the pulley loses most of the 9 marks.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}a&=g\,\frac{m_B+m_A\sin\alpha-\mu_km_A\cos\alpha}{m_A+m_B+m_w/2}\end{aligned}}\)

May 2013 B1 · involute templates and epicyclic drive · 20 marks
  • B1(a)(i) · 4 marks — show \(\omega_2/\omega_1=r_1/r_2\) for involute templates on base circles \(r_1\), \(r_2\). Brief: the contact normal is the common tangent \(RP\) to both base circles; both contact points must have the same velocity component along it, and that component is \(\omega_1r_1\) for \(A\) and \(\omega_2r_2\) for \(B\), so \(\omega_1r_1=\omega_2r_2\).
  • B1(a)(ii) · 2 marks — why the ratio is constant for involute spur gears. Brief: it depends only on the fixed base radii, not on where along the profile contact occurs.
  • B1(a)(iii) · 4 marks — velocity diagram for \(P\), \(R\) and the sliding velocity of \(A\) relative to \(B\). Brief: \(v_R=\omega_1r_1\), \(v_P=\omega_2r_2\), equal and along \(RP\); the sliding velocity is along the common tangent of the profiles, of size \((\omega_1+\omega_2)\times\) (distance of the contact point from the pitch point \(Q\)).
  • B1(a)(iv) · 2 marks — angles when the relative velocity of \(A\) and \(B\) is zero. Brief: contact at the pitch point \(Q\) on the line of centres, where \(\phi_1=\phi_2\) (both equal the pressure angle).
  • B1(b)(i) · 2 marks — teeth on the sun wheels (annulus \(A\) 80 fixed, compound planets \(P_1\) 32, \(P_2\) 22; \(P_2\) meshes with \(S_2\) and \(A\), \(P_1\) with \(S_1\)). Brief: \(S_2=80-2(22)=36\); equal centre distances \(S_1+P_1=S_2+P_2=58\) give \(S_1=26\).
  • B1(b)(ii) · 6 marks — speed ratio propeller (\(S_2\)) / engine (\(S_1\)). Brief: with the carrier speed \(\omega_c\) and \(\omega_A=0\), the tabular (Willis) method gives \(\omega_{S2}=\omega_c(1+80/36)\) and \(\omega_{S1}=\omega_c\big(1+\tfrac{80\cdot32}{22\cdot26}\big)\), so \(\omega_{S2}/\omega_{S1}=143/243\approx0.589\), same sense of rotation.

✓Last-minute checklist

  • Clockwise is negative: \(\omega=-10\u{rad/s}\) for "10 rad/s clockwise"; \(\omega\hat k\times(x,y)=(-\omega y,\ \omega x)\).
  • Read every angle in the figure (from the horizontal or the vertical?) before writing \(\vb r\) in components; \(\vb r_{B/A}\) points from \(A\) to \(B\).
  • Use the constraint to kill one component: slider along its guide, pin on a wheel \(\perp\) radius, no-slip contact = rack/floor velocity.
  • Learn the slider-crank shape: zero at the dead centres, peaks a little before/after 90°/270°; it is not on the sheet.
  • For "derive \(I\)": slice, \(\mathrm dm\), limits, integrate. Bar \(\tfrac1{12}mL^2\) (centre), \(\tfrac13mL^2\) (end); disk \(\tfrac12mR^2\); ring \(mR^2\).
  • \(T=\tfrac12I\omega^2\) in joules with \(\omega\) in rad/s; mass towards the rim → larger \(I\) → more energy, steadier speed.
  • Kinetics: one FBD per body, \(\sum M=I\alpha\) for the wheel, different tensions on the two sides of a wheel with inertia, \(a=\alpha r\).
  • Finish every answer with magnitude and direction in words.
