S6 exam guide · Torsion of shafts

What the exam asks from this chapter, how to recognise each question type in seconds, and every past-paper part (2013–2024) solved once. Concepts are not re-taught here: each type links back to the notes. Torsion has no handout, so the whole chapter is beyond the handouts: it is built from the past papers and standard textbooks (Beer, Hibbeler, Gere).

0How this chapter is examined

Exam format

3 h · any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question. Formula sheet: there has never been one for Section A, so nothing in this chapter is given. Memorise \(J=\pi d^4/32\) (solid), \(J=\pi(d_o^4-d_i^4)/32\) (hollow), \(\tau=T\rho/J\), \(\varphi=TL/(GJ)\), the gear relations and \(T_{\text{pin}}=Vd\). 2026/27: orbits and re-entry were removed from Section B and control (C1) was added; how the 2026 paper is laid out has not been announced. Torsion is listed as statics topic 6 in the Ch01 contents but has no handout this year, so the past papers below are the best guide to what is asked.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

Torques are drawn as double-headed vectors (right-hand rule: curl the fingers in the turning sense, the thumb gives the arrow). An applied torque pointing \(+x\) is positive. The internal torque \(T\) on a cut is positive when its vector points away from the cut face. Angle of twist \(\varphi\) is positive turning right-handed about \(+x\). Unknown wall torques are always drawn \(+x\); a negative answer means they act in \(-x\).

Concepts: §3 torsion formula · §4 polar moment \(J\) · §6 angle of twist.

S6.1Stepped shaft fixed at one end

S6.1Stepped shaft clamped at one end2 sittings · 40 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
Shaft clamped at \(A\), two diameters, three torque vectors; (a) \(J\) of each section, (b) wall torque \(M_A\), (c) twist at \(B\) and \(C\), (d) \(\tau_{\max}\) and where.
Do
\(J=\pi d^4/32\) per section → \(\sum M_x=0\) for \(M_A\) → cut every segment, keep the free (right) piece: \(T=\) sum of torques beyond the cut → add \(TL/(GJ)\) from the wall → \(\tau=Tc/J\) per segment, pick the largest.
Watch
A torque in the middle of a section makes an extra segment. The thin section usually wins \(\tau_{\max}\) (\(\tau\propto T/d^3\)). Twists can cancel (Dec 2024: \(\varphi_B=0\)). Give the location: outer surface of which segment.

Concept: Notes §7 · torque diagrams and stepped shafts (worked example + live figure).

Ready answer · shaft fixed at one end \[\begin{aligned}M_A&=-\textstyle\sum T_{\text{applied}},\\ T_{\text{seg}}&=\textstyle\sum(\text{torques beyond cut}),\\ \varphi_P&=\textstyle\sum_{A\to P}\dfrac{T_iL_i}{GJ_i},\\ \tau_{\max,i}&=\dfrac{16|T_i|}{\pi d_i^3}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the wall cancels the net applied torque; each segment carries everything between it and the free end; twists pile up from the wall; the peak stress is on the outer surface.

The applied torques \(M_1\), \(M_2\) and \(M_3\) are 600, 500 and 200 N·m, respectively. The shear modulus of the shaft is \(G=80\u{GPa}\).

Figure A3 redrawn: \(M_1\) at the middle of \(AB\) and \(M_3\) at \(C\) point \(+x\); \(M_2\) at \(B\) points \(-x\).

(a) Determine the polar moments of inertia of sections AB and BC. [4]
(b) Determine the end reaction, \(M_A\), due to the applied torques \(M_1\), \(M_2\) and \(M_3\). [4]
(c) Determine the angle of twist at B and C. [6]
(d) Calculate the maximum shear stress and its location. [6]

Hint

Type S6.1. First step: draw the FBD with \(M_A\) pointing \(+x\) and notice that \(AB\) has two segments (\(M_1\) sits at its middle), so there are three segment torques to find.

Solution
  1. (a) \(J_{AB}=\pi(60)^4/32=1.272\times10^6\u{mm^4}=1.272\times10^{-6}\u{m^4}\); \(J_{BC}=\pi(30)^4/32=7.952\times10^4\u{mm^4}=7.952\times10^{-8}\u{m^4}\).
  2. (b) \(\sum M_x=0\): \(M_A+600-500+200=0\Rightarrow M_A=-300\u{N\,m}\), i.e. \(300\u{N\,m}\) in \(-x\).
  3. Segment torques (keep the right piece): \(A\)–\(M_1\): \(+300\); \(M_1\)–\(B\): \(-500+200=-300\); \(BC\): \(+200\u{N\,m}\).
  4. (c) \(GJ_{AB}=1.018\times10^5\u{N\,m^2}\), \(GJ_{BC}=6362\u{N\,m^2}\): \[\begin{aligned}\varphi_{M_1}&=\frac{300(0.45)}{1.018\times10^5}\\ &=1.33\times10^{-3}\u{rad},\\ \varphi_B&=\varphi_{M_1}+\frac{(-300)(0.45)}{1.018\times10^5}\\ &=0,\\ \varphi_C&=0+\frac{200(0.9)}{6362}\\ &=0.0283\u{rad}.\end{aligned}\]
  5. (d) \(AB\) (both halves): \(\tau=300(0.03)/J_{AB}=7.07\u{MPa}\). \(BC\): \(\tau=200(0.015)/J_{BC}=37.7\u{MPa}\) — the larger.

Torque diagram (N·m) and angle of twist (degrees) along \(x\) (m).

Marker note

(d) asks for the location too: "on the outer surface (\(\rho=15\u{mm}\)) of \(BC\), anywhere along \(BC\)". "In BC" alone does not answer the question.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&J_{AB}=1.272\times10^6,\ J_{BC}=7.952\times10^4\u{mm^4}\\ &M_A=-300\u{N\,m}\ (300\u{N\,m}\text{ in }-x)\\ &\varphi_B=0,\ \varphi_C=0.0283\u{rad}=1.62^\circ\\ &\tau_{\max}=37.7\u{MPa},\ \text{surface of }BC\end{aligned}}\)

S6.2Shaft fixed at both ends (statically indeterminate)

S6.2Both ends built in3 sittings · 42 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
Walls at both ends, one or more torques between them; asks \(J\), both wall torques, the internal torques or the torque diagram — with numbers (May 2019) or in symbols (Dec 2015, May 2014).
Do
Both wall torques \(+x\) → one equilibrium equation → every segment torque in terms of \(T_A\) (left piece: \(T=-T_A-\sum\)applied to the left) → compatibility \(\sum T_iL_i/(GJ_i)=0\) → solve, back-substitute, plot.
Watch
Each diameter has its own \(J\); \(G\) cancels only if one material. The torque diagram is steps (constant per segment), jumping by each applied torque. Check: the twists add to zero.

Concept: Notes §9 · shafts fixed at both ends (recipe, worked example, May 2014 A2(a) proof).

Ready answer · two segments, one torque \(T\) at \(C\) \[\begin{aligned}T_{AC}&=T\,\frac{l_2J_1}{l_1J_2+l_2J_1},\\ T_{CB}&=T\,\frac{l_1J_2}{l_1J_2+l_2J_1}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: each segment takes a share in proportion to its stiffness \(GJ/l\); the shorter, thicker side takes more. \(AC\) and \(CB\) twist in opposite senses through the same \(\varphi_C\).

Dec 2015 A3(b)10 markssame set-up as May 2014 A2(a)

A solid circular shaft AB is fixed to rigid walls at both ends and subjected to a torque \(T\) at section C, as shown in the following figure. The shaft diameters are \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) for segments AC and CB, respectively. The material used to make segments AC and CB has a shear modulus of \(G\). The length of AC and CB are \(l_1\) and \(l_2\), respectively.

(i) What are the torsion constants of segments AC and CB? [4]
(ii) Calculate the internal torques in segments AC and CB. [6]
(a)(i), (a)(ii) thin-walled vessel → S7.2

Hint

Type S6.2. First step: for a circular section the "torsion constant" is the polar moment \(J\). Then one equilibrium equation plus "both segments turn \(C\) by the same angle".

Solution
  1. (i) \(J_1=\pi d_1^4/32\) (\(AC\)), \(J_2=\pi d_2^4/32\) (\(CB\)).
  2. Equilibrium (magnitudes; the walls resist \(T\)): \(T_{AC}+T_{CB}=T\).
  3. Compatibility: \(C\) rotates by the same \(\varphi_C\) relative to either wall: \(\dfrac{T_{AC}l_1}{GJ_1}=\dfrac{T_{CB}l_2}{GJ_2}\).
  4. Solve the two equations (the \(\pi/32\) and \(G\) cancel).
Marker note

State the senses: with \(T\) in \(+x\), \(AC\) carries \(+T_{AC}\) and \(CB\) carries \(-T_{CB}\) (opposite twist). Check: \(d_1=d_2\), \(l_1=l_2\) gives \(T/2\) each.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}T_{AC}&=\frac{T\,l_2d_1^4}{l_1d_2^4+l_2d_1^4}\\ T_{CB}&=\frac{T\,l_1d_2^4}{l_1d_2^4+l_2d_1^4}\end{aligned}}\)

Equivalent: \(T_{AC}=T\,l_2J_1/(l_1J_2+l_2J_1)\), the ready answer above.

May 2014 A2(a)12 marks

A solid circular shaft AB is fixed to rigid walls at both ends and subjected to a torque \(T\) at section C, as shown in Figure A2(a).

The shaft diameters are \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) for segments AC and CB, respectively, which are made of the same material. If the maximum shearing stress in both shaft segments is the same, show that the relation between the lengths \(l_1\) and \(l_2\) for segments AC and CB, respectively, is given by

\[\frac{l_1}{l_2}+\alpha(\alpha^3-1)-\frac{l_2}{l_1}\alpha^5=0,\]

where \(\alpha=d_1/d_2\). [12]
(b) geared shafts → S6.3 below

the printed relation was checked by derivation: multiplied by \(r=l_1/l_2\) it factors as \((r-\alpha)(r+\alpha^4)=0\), so it says exactly \(l_1/l_2=d_1/d_2\).

Hint

Type S6.2 (symbolic). First step: write the torque ratio twice, once from compatibility and once from "equal maximum stress", and set them equal.

Solution
  1. FBD as in Dec 2015 A3(b) above. Equilibrium: \(T_{AC}+T_{CB}=T\).
  2. Compatibility: \(\dfrac{T_{AC}l_1}{GJ_1}=\dfrac{T_{CB}l_2}{GJ_2}\Rightarrow\dfrac{T_{AC}}{T_{CB}}=\dfrac{l_2}{l_1}\alpha^4\).
  3. Equal stresses: \(\dfrac{16T_{AC}}{\pi d_1^3}=\dfrac{16T_{CB}}{\pi d_2^3}\Rightarrow\dfrac{T_{AC}}{T_{CB}}=\alpha^3\).
  4. So \(\dfrac{l_2}{l_1}\alpha^4=\alpha^3\Rightarrow\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}=\alpha\).
  5. Substitute into the left side: \(\alpha+\alpha^4-\alpha-\dfrac{\alpha^5}{\alpha}=\alpha^4-\alpha^4=0\). ∎
Marker note

In a "show that" the working is the answer: write both ratio equations (steps 2 and 3) before simplifying; step 5 only confirms.

\(\boxed{\dfrac{l_1}{l_2}=\alpha=\dfrac{d_1}{d_2}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{relation holds}}\)

S6.3Geared shafts

S6.3Two shafts linked by a gear pair3 sittings · 23 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
Two parallel shafts, gears with given pitch diameters; torque at one end and the far end of the other shaft fixed → twist at the loaded end; or a stress limit → minimum diameters, maximum length.
Do
Tooth force \(F=T/r_B\) → other shaft \(T'=Fr_C\) → twist of the fixed shaft \(\varphi_C=T'L'/(GJ')\) → through the mesh \(\varphi_B=\varphi_C\,r_C/r_B\) → add the input shaft's own twist. Sizing: \(d=(16T/\pi\tau_{\text{allow}})^{1/3}\), \(L\le\varphi_{\text{allow}}GJ/T\).
Watch
Bigger gear → bigger torque, smaller rotation. Use pitch radii for the force (ratios can use diameters). The mesh multiplies the twist of the far shaft; the input shaft's own twist is added unscaled.

Concept: Notes §10 · geared shafts (gear relations, recipe, worked example).

Ready answer · input at \(A\) on shaft \(AB\), gear \(B\) drives \(C\), \(CD\) fixed at \(D\) \[\begin{aligned}\frac{T_C}{T_B}&=\frac{r_C}{r_B},\qquad \varphi_Br_B=\varphi_Cr_C,\\ \varphi_A&=\frac{TL_{AB}}{GJ_{AB}}+\Big(\frac{r_C}{r_B}\Big)^2\frac{TL_{CD}}{GJ_{CD}}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the gear ratio enters twice for the far shaft — once making its torque bigger, once making its rotation look bigger at the input.

S6.4Compound shaft (tube + bar)

Decision procedure · 1 sitting · 15 marks (2014–2024)
  1. If you see a tube and a bar joined along their whole length (bonded, shrink-fitted) do the parallel rule: same twist, torques add, \(T_i=T\,G_iJ_i/\sum GJ\).
  2. If they are joined only by a pin through one section do the pin couple: \(V=\tau_{\text{pin}}\pi d_{\text{pin}}^2/4\) on each shear plane, \(T_{\text{pin}}=Vd\) (\(d\) = bar diameter, the distance between the two shear planes).
  3. Draw two FBDs: bar (applied \(T\), pin couple \(T_p\) opposing, wall torque) and tube (pin couple \(T_p\), wall torque). Equilibrium for each.
  4. Compatibility: at the pin the bar and the tube turn by the same angle, both measured from the common wall: \(\dfrac{T_sL}{G_sJ_s}=\dfrac{T_pL}{G_aJ_a}\).
  5. If a limit is given (pin stress, allowable \(\tau\)) start from the limited quantity and work outwards to \(T\).

Cue: "contacting surfaces are smooth" = no torque passes through the contact, only through the pin. Traps: tube \(J=\pi(d_o^4-d_i^4)/32\); the pin couple uses the bar diameter; read carefully which ends are fixed (text and figure can differ).

Concept: Notes §11 · compound shafts and pin joints.

Ready answer · pinned collar, bar and tube both fixed at the wall \[\begin{aligned}T_p&=Vd,\quad T_s=T_p\,\frac{G_sJ_s}{G_aJ_a},\\ T_{\max}&=T_p+T_s,\quad \varphi=\frac{T_pL}{G_aJ_a}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the pin limit fixes the tube's torque; the bar's fixed length beside it twists by the same angle, so it carries torque in the ratio of the rigidities.

(iii) Calculate the maximum torque \(T\) that can be applied to the steel shaft. [5]
(a) geared shafts, same as Dec 2018 A3(a) → S6.3 above

dimensions read from the 3D figure: tube outer Ø65 mm, inner Ø45 mm (= steel bar Ø45 mm), pin Ø10 mm; \(D\) to pin \(C\) = 250 mm, \(C\) to tube end \(E\) = 125 mm, \(E\) to the loaded end = 250 mm. Following the text, both the tube and the bar are fixed at \(D\); if only the tube were fixed, \(T_{\max}=T_p=35.3\u{N\,m}\).

Hint

Type S6.4. First step: the largest shear force on one pin cross-section, \(V=\tau A_{\text{pin}}\), and the couple it makes across the bar diameter.

Solution

FBDs: bar (top) \(T=T_p+T_s\); tube (bottom), wall torque \(=T_p\). Pin couple \(T_p\) green, wall torques teal.

  1. (i) Bar: \(T\) at the end (\(+x\)), pin couple \(T_p\) at \(C\) (\(-x\)), wall torque \(T_s\) at \(D\) (\(-x\)): \(T=T_p+T_s\). Tube: \(T_p\) at \(C\) (\(+x\)), wall torque \(T_p\) at \(D\) (\(-x\)). Compatibility over \(DC\) (\(L=0.25\u{m}\)): \(\dfrac{T_sL}{G_sJ_s}=\dfrac{T_pL}{G_aJ_a}\).
  2. (ii) \(V=10\times10^6\times\pi(0.01)^2/4=785.4\u{N}\); \(T_p=785.4(0.045)=35.3\u{N\,m}\).
  3. \(J_a=\pi(0.065^4-0.045^4)/32=1.350\times10^{-6}\u{m^4}\); \(G_aJ_a=3.780\times10^4\u{N\,m^2}\): \[\begin{aligned}\varphi&=\frac{35.3(0.25)}{3.780\times10^4}\\ &=2.34\times10^{-4}\u{rad}.\end{aligned}\]
  4. (iii) \(J_s=\pi(0.045)^4/32=4.026\times10^{-7}\u{m^4}\), \(G_sJ_s=3.221\times10^4\u{N\,m^2}\): \(T_s=\varphi G_sJ_s/L=30.1\u{N\,m}\) (the length cancels).
  5. \(T_{\max}=T_p+T_s=35.3+30.1=65.5\u{N\,m}\).
Marker note

(i) is 5 marks for the two FBDs and two equations: show \(T_p\) on both bodies in opposite senses (action and reaction).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\varphi_{\text{collar}}=2.34\times10^{-4}\u{rad}=0.0134^\circ\\ &T_{\max}=65.5\u{N\,m}\end{aligned}}\)
