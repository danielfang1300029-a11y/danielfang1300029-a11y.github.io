S3 exam guide · Stress, strain, principal stress and Mohr's circle

0How this chapter is examined

Verdict: predictable. S3 turned up in Section A in 8 of the 11 sittings from 2014 to 2024 (83 marks in all), always as part of A1 or A2. Since 2022 it is a short 5–7 mark part of A1: "for the stress state shown, calculate the principal stresses and principal stress angles", with an element whose numbers change every year but whose shape does not. In 2015–2017 it was half a question (10–20 marks): a full Mohr's circle with a rotated element and sketches. The working is always the same five formulas; the marks are lost on signs and on matching each angle to the right principal stress.

Exam format

3 h · any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) since 2014 · about \(36\) min per question, so a 5-mark S3.1 part is worth about 9 minutes. Formula sheet: Section A has never had one. Nothing from this chapter is given: you must remember the transformation equations, the principal-stress formula and angle, the Mohr construction and the plane-stress Hooke's law (all in the Ready answers below). The Section B sheet (2021–2024) is dynamics only. Older format: May 2013 (MECH1013/MECH2413) was "any five" with no per-section rule. 2026/27: orbits and re-entry are removed from dynamics and control (C1) is added, with no past papers for it; how the 2026 paper will be split between the parts has not been announced.

Sign convention (same as the notes)
  • \(\sigma\) positive in tension (arrow out of the face).
  • \(\tau_{xy}\) positive when, on the \(+x\) face, it points \(+y\): the \(x\)-face pair turns the element anticlockwise (handout slides 37, 48; also the wording in Dec 2015 and Dec 2016).
  • Angles \(\theta\) anticlockwise from \(x\) to \(x'\).
  • Mohr: \(\sigma\) right, \(\tau\) up; \(P(\sigma_x,\tau_{xy})\), \(P'(\sigma_y,-\tau_{xy})\); element \(\theta\) anticlockwise ↔ circle \(2\theta\) clockwise.

Read \(\tau_{xy}\) from the right-hand face of the drawn element: arrow up → \(+\), arrow down → \(-\). Every element drawn in 2016–2024 had the right-face arrow pointing down, so \(\tau_{xy}\) was negative each time.

S3.1Principal stresses and angles from a drawn element

S3.1Principal stresses from a drawn element3 sittings · 17 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"For the stress state (in MPa) shown, calculate the principal stresses and principal stress angles", a square with arrows on all four faces; 5–7 marks in A1.
Do
Read \(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\tau_{xy}\) with signs → centre \(\sigma_M\), radius \(r\) → \(\sigma_{1,2}=\sigma_M\pm r\) → \(\theta_p\) from \(\tan2\theta_p\) → substitute \(\theta_p\) to see which stress it carries → sketch the turned element.
Watch
Arrows into a face are compression; right-face arrow down means \(\tau_{xy}\lt 0\). The angle from the tan formula is not automatically \(\sigma_1\)'s.

Concept: Notes §16 · Principal stresses, §17 · Max shear, §18 · Mohr's circle.

Ready answer · principal stresses \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_M&=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2},\\ r&=\sqrt{\left(\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\right)^2+\tau_{xy}^2},\\ \sigma_{1,2}&=\sigma_M\pm r,\quad \tau_{\max}=r,\\ \tan2\theta_p&=\frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: principal stresses = average normal stress ± circle radius; the radius is also the largest in-plane shear stress.

Which stress goes with \(\theta_p\)? Take \(\theta_p\) between \(-45^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\) (the calculator's \(\tan^{-1}\)). That direction carries \(\sigma_1\) if \(\sigma_x\gt\sigma_y\), and \(\sigma_2\) if \(\sigma_x\lt\sigma_y\); the other stress acts at \(\theta_p\pm90^\circ\). Confirm by substituting into \(\sigma_{x'}\) (one line).

Dec 2024 A1(c)5 marks

For the stress state (in MPa) shown, calculate the principal stresses and principal stress angles.

Figure: 10 MPa arrows into the vertical faces, 30 MPa out of the horizontal faces; 20 MPa shear, right-face arrow down. (a) → S2.2 · (b) → S1.4

Hint

Type S3.1. First step: write \(\sigma_x\), \(\sigma_y\), \(\tau_{xy}\) with signs from the arrows (into = compression; right-face arrow down = negative shear).

Solution
  1. \(\sigma_x=-10\u{MPa}\), \(\sigma_y=+30\u{MPa}\), \(\tau_{xy}=-20\u{MPa}\).
  2. \(\sigma_M=\tfrac{-10+30}{2}=10\u{MPa}\); \(r=\sqrt{(-20)^2+(-20)^2}=28.28\u{MPa}\).
  3. \(\sigma_1=10+28.28=38.28\u{MPa}\), \(\sigma_2=10-28.28=-18.28\u{MPa}\).
  4. \(\tan2\theta_p=\dfrac{2(-20)}{-10-30}=1\Rightarrow\theta_p=22.5^\circ\). Since \(\sigma_x\lt\sigma_y\) this carries \(\sigma_2\); check: \(\sigma_{x'}(22.5^\circ)=10-20\cos45^\circ-20\sin45^\circ=-18.28\u{MPa}\) ✓.
  5. \(\sigma_1\) acts at \(22.5^\circ+90^\circ=112.5^\circ\) anticlockwise, i.e. \(67.5^\circ\) clockwise.
Marker note

"Principal stress angles" means both stresses must be tied to a direction: two numbers and one angle without saying which stress it belongs to loses a mark.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}\sigma_1&=38.28\u{MPa}\ \text{at }67.5^\circ\text{ cw}\\ \sigma_2&=-18.28\u{MPa}\ \text{at }22.5^\circ\text{ acw}\end{aligned}}\)

Same as: \(\sigma_1\) at \(112.5^\circ\) acw; \(\tau_{\max}=28.28\u{MPa}\) (not asked). Agrees with the note's exam taster.

S3.2Full Mohr's circle: rotated element, principal and maximum shear, sketches

S3.2Full Mohr's circle question4 sittings · 37 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
\(\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\tau_{xy}\) given (in words or drawn) and "using Mohr's circle, determine … on an element rotated \(\theta\) anticlockwise … principal stresses … maximum shear … show on sketches of properly oriented elements".
Do
Plot \(P\), \(P'\) → centre, radius → principal \(\sigma_M\pm r\) → rotated element: turn diameter \(2\theta\) clockwise, read \(Q\), \(Q'\) (check with the equations) → max shear \(r\) at \(45^\circ\) from principal, \(\sigma=\sigma_M\) → one sketch per result.
Watch
Element anticlockwise = circle clockwise (τ-up convention). A "sketch" mark needs arrows on all four faces and the angle written on it.

Concept: Notes §18 · Mohr's circle (live explorer), §19 · Mohr example.

Ready answer · Mohr construction (slides 48–52)
  1. Axes: \(\sigma\) right, \(\tau\) up. Draw the sign sketch (slide 48).
  2. \(P(\sigma_x,\ \tau_{xy})\) is the \(x\)-face; \(P'(\sigma_y,\ -\tau_{xy})\) is the \(y\)-face. \(PP'\) is a diameter; centre \(M(\sigma_M,0)\), radius \(r\).
  3. Element turned \(\theta\) anticlockwise → \(Q\) is \(P\) turned \(2\theta\) clockwise: \(Q=(\sigma_{x'},\tau_{x'y'})\), \(Q'=(\sigma_{y'},-\tau_{x'y'})\).
  4. Ends of the horizontal diameter: \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\) (zero shear). Top and bottom: \(\pm\tau_{\max}=\pm r\), with \(\sigma=\sigma_M\) on all four faces, \(45^\circ\) on the element from the principal axes.
\[\begin{aligned}\sigma_{x'}&=\sigma_M+\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta\\&\quad+\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta,\\ \sigma_{y'}&=\sigma_x+\sigma_y-\sigma_{x'},\\ \tau_{x'y'}&=-\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\sin2\theta\\&\quad+\tau_{xy}\cos2\theta.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the rotated normal stress = average + (half-difference)·cos 2θ + shear·sin 2θ; the two normal stresses always add to the same total.

At \(\theta=45^\circ\) (asked in 2016 and 2017): \(\sigma_{x'}=\sigma_M+\tau_{xy}\), \(\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_M-\tau_{xy}\), \(\tau_{x'y'}=-\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\).

S3.3Transformation equations: meaning and derivation (written)

Answer skeleton
  1. Meaning of principal stress (about 2 marks): the planes through a point on which the shear stress is zero; the normal stresses on them are the largest and smallest normal stresses at that point, \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\).
  2. Principal coordinate system (about 2 marks): the pair of perpendicular axes along those plane normals, at \(\theta_p\) from \(\tan2\theta_p=2\tau_{xy}/(\sigma_x-\sigma_y)\); in it the element carries normal stress only. Draw the turned square with \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\) and no shear arrows.
  3. Deriving \(\sigma_{y'}\): the \(y'\)-face is the \(x'\)-face turned a further \(90^\circ\), so put \(\theta+90^\circ\) into \(\sigma_{x'}\); state \(\cos(2\theta+180^\circ)=-\cos2\theta\), \(\sin(2\theta+180^\circ)=-\sin2\theta\); write the result; check \(\sigma_{x'}+\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_x+\sigma_y\).
  4. Graphical rotation: plot \(P\), \(P'\); diameter, centre, radius; rotate the diameter \(2\theta\) (clockwise with \(\tau\) up); read \(Q\), \(Q'\); one sketch with \(2\theta\) marked.

Model answer (exam length), Dec 2015 A2(a): "At any point of a stressed body the normal and shear stresses depend on the orientation of the plane considered. There are always two perpendicular planes on which the shear stress is zero; these are the principal planes, and the normal stresses on them, \(\sigma_1\) (maximum) and \(\sigma_2\) (minimum), are the principal stresses. They are the extreme values of normal stress at the point, so they are what a designer compares with the strength of the material. The principal coordinate system is the set of axes \(x_p\), \(y_p\) normal to the principal planes, turned by \(\theta_p\) from \(x\), where \(\tan2\theta_p=2\tau_{xy}/(\sigma_x-\sigma_y)\). An element aligned with these axes has only normal stresses \(\sigma_1\), \(\sigma_2\) on its faces." (+ sketch of the turned element.)

Concept: Notes §15 · Transformation equations, §16 · Principal stresses, §18 · Mohr's circle.

Ready answer · \(\sigma_{y'}\) \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_{y'}&=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}-\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta\\&\quad-\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta.\end{aligned}\]

In words: the same as \(\sigma_{x'}\) with the two "difference" terms flipped in sign, because the \(y'\) face is \(90^\circ\) further round.

Dec 2015 A2(a), (b), (d)4 + 6 + 4 = 14 marks

Stem and figure as in Dec 2015 A2(c) above: \(\sigma_x\), \(\sigma_y\), \(\tau_{xy}\) on an element in \(x\)-\(y\); the same point in axes \(x'\)-\(y'\) rotated \(\theta\) anticlockwise; anticlockwise shear positive.

(a) Give the physical meaning of principle stress and principle coordinate system. [4]

(b) Assuming that the element is in equilibrium, and the following stress components in the rotated coordinate system \(x'\)-\(y'\) are given:

\[\begin{aligned}\sigma_{x'}&=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}+\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta\\&\quad+\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta,\\ \tau_{x'y'}&=-\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\sin2\theta+\tau_{xy}\cos2\theta.\end{aligned}\]

What is the corresponding expression for \(\sigma_{y'}\)? [6]

(d) Based on the Mohr's circle, explain how \((\sigma_{x'},\sigma_{y'},\tau_{x'y'})\) can be obtained graphically from \((\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\tau_{xy})\). [4]

(c) → S3.2 above.

Hint

Type S3.3. (a): "zero shear" is the key phrase. (b): which angle does the \(y'\) axis make with \(x\)? (d): two points, one diameter, one rotation.

Solution
  1. (a) Model answer in the skeleton above: principal planes have zero shear stress; principal stresses are the max/min normal stresses; principal axes are the normals of those planes, at \(\theta_p\) with \(\tan2\theta_p=2\tau_{xy}/(\sigma_x-\sigma_y)\).
  2. (b) \(y'\) is at \(\theta+90^\circ\) from \(x\), so \(\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_{x'}(\theta+90^\circ)\). With \(\cos(2\theta+180^\circ)=-\cos2\theta\) and \(\sin(2\theta+180^\circ)=-\sin2\theta\) the result follows (boxed). The same comes from force balance on a wedge whose sloping face has normal \(y'\).
  3. (b) check: \(\sigma_{x'}+\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_x+\sigma_y\) for every \(\theta\); at \(\theta=0\), \(\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_y\).
  4. (d) Axes \(\sigma\) right, \(\tau\) up. Plot \(P(\sigma_x,\tau_{xy})\) and \(P'(\sigma_y,-\tau_{xy})\); join them: the line is a diameter, crossing the \(\sigma\)-axis at the centre \(M\left(\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2},0\right)\); radius \(MP\). Rotate the diameter about \(M\) by \(2\theta\) clockwise (twice the element angle, opposite sense). The new end \(Q\) gives \((\sigma_{x'},\tau_{x'y'})\); the other end \(Q'\) gives \((\sigma_{y'},-\tau_{x'y'})\).
Marker note

(d) asks for an explanation "based on the Mohr's circle": a labelled sketch with \(P\), \(P'\), \(M\), \(Q\), \(Q'\) and the \(2\theta\) arc carries most of the 4 marks; say which way you rotate and why (τ plotted up).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}\text{(b) }\sigma_{y'}&=\tfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2}-\tfrac{\sigma_x-\sigma_y}{2}\cos2\theta\\&\quad-\tau_{xy}\sin2\theta\end{aligned}}\)

Equivalent form: \(\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_x+\sigma_y-\sigma_{x'}\). Agrees with the note's exam taster. If you plot \(\tau\) downward (Hibbeler), rotate \(2\theta\) anticlockwise instead; say which you use.

S3.4Stresses on an inclined plane from a uniaxial load (grain or weld)

S3.4Inclined plane in a bar under axial load1 sitting · 10 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
A bar or board pulled by \(F\), with grain lines or a weld seam at an angle; "sketch Mohr's circle … stresses on an element parallel/perpendicular to the grain".
Do
\(\sigma_0=F/A\) on the square cross-section → circle from \(0\) to \(\sigma_0\) (through the origin) → turn by twice the grain angle → read \(\sigma\) along, \(\sigma\) across, \(\tau\) → sketch the tilted element.
Watch
\(A\) is the cross-section perpendicular to the load (depth × thickness), not the length. Keep track of which way the grain slopes: it only flips the sign of \(\tau\).

Concept: Notes §2 · Stress on an inclined cut, §18 · Mohr's circle. Weld in a pressure vessel: S7.3.

Ready answer · uniaxial \(\sigma_0\), element turned \(\theta\) \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_{x'}&=\sigma_0\cos^2\theta,\\ \sigma_{y'}&=\sigma_0\sin^2\theta,\\ \tau_{x'y'}&=-\sigma_0\sin\theta\cos\theta.\end{aligned}\]

In words: along the tilted direction you keep \(\cos^2\) of the stress, across it \(\sin^2\); the largest shear is \(\sigma_0/2\), at \(45^\circ\).

Dec 2018 A1(c)5 + 5 = 10 marks

The grains of wood in the board, which is subjected to an axial load of \(250\u{N}\), make an angle of \(20^\circ\) with the horizontal edge, as shown in Figure A1(c).

(i) Sketch Mohr's circle indicating the principal stress \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\). [5]
(ii) Indicate on the Mohr's circle the stress on an element oriented with its edges either parallel or perpendicular to the grains of wood. Hence, determine these stress, and show them in a sketch of the properly oriented material element. [5]

(a) → S1.5 · (b) → S5.3

The grain is read as sloping down to the right (\(\theta=-20^\circ\)), as in the note; if it slopes up, only the sign of the shear stress changes (\(\tau=-53.6\u{kPa}\)).

Hint

Type S3.4. First step: cut the board square to its axis and find \(\sigma_0=F/A\) with \(A=60\times25\u{mm^2}\).

Solution
  1. \(\sigma_0=\dfrac{250\u{N}}{60\times25\u{mm^2}}=0.1667\u{MPa}=166.7\u{kPa}\); \(\sigma_y=\tau_{xy}=0\).
  2. (i) \(\sigma_1=166.7\u{kPa}\) (along the board), \(\sigma_2=0\) (across). Circle through the origin: centre \(83.3\u{kPa}\), radius \(83.3\u{kPa}\); \(\tau_{\max}=83.3\u{kPa}\).
  3. (ii) Element along the grain: \(\theta=-20^\circ\), so \(Q\) is \(P\) turned \(40^\circ\) anticlockwise on the circle.
  4. \(\sigma_{x'}=83.3+83.3\cos40^\circ=147.2\u{kPa}\) (along the grain); \(\sigma_{y'}=83.3-83.3\cos40^\circ=19.5\u{kPa}\) (across); \(\tau_{x'y'}=83.3\sin40^\circ=53.6\u{kPa}\).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}\text{(i) }&\sigma_1=166.7\u{kPa},\ \sigma_2=0\\ \text{(ii) }&\sigma_\parallel=147.2\u{kPa},\\ &\sigma_\perp=19.5\u{kPa},\ \tau=53.6\u{kPa}\end{aligned}}\)

S3.5Hooke's law in 2D: stresses from measured strains

S3.5Stresses from strain gauges1 sitting · 5 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Strain gauges indicate \(\varepsilon_x=\dots\), \(\varepsilon_y=\dots\). Determine \(\sigma_x\) and \(\sigma_y\)", with \(E\), \(\nu\) somewhere in the question.
Do
Plane stress (\(\sigma_z=0\)) → inverse Hooke's law (ready answer) → back-check one strain with the forward law.
Watch
\(\sigma_x\ne E\varepsilon_x\): the Poisson term matters (here \(57.6\) vs \(67.5\u{MPa}\)). Keep \(E\) in MPa to get MPa out.

Concept: Notes §9 · Multi-axial strain (Beyond box: inverse Hooke), §11 · Hooke's law in 3D.

Ready answer · plane stress, inverse Hooke's law \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_x&=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}\,(\varepsilon_x+\nu\,\varepsilon_y),\\ \sigma_y&=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}\,(\varepsilon_y+\nu\,\varepsilon_x).\end{aligned}\]

In words: stress in \(x\) = stiffness factor × (own strain + Poisson ratio × the other strain). Forward check: \(\varepsilon_x=(\sigma_x-\nu\sigma_y)/E\).

May 2019 A2(a)(i)5 marks

A brass plate is stretched by tensile forces acting in the \(x\)- and \(y\)-directions. Strain gauges indicate \(\varepsilon_x=0.00072\) and \(\varepsilon_y=0.00016\). (i) Determine \(\sigma_x\) and \(\sigma_y\); (ii) … Given \(E=80\u{GPa}\), \(\nu=0.3\).

(ii) → S3.2 · (b) → S7.2 · (c) → S7.4

\(E\) and \(\nu\) are printed in (ii) but are needed in (i); they are used in (i).

Hint

Type S3.5. First step: \(E/(1-\nu^2)\) with \(E=80\,000\u{MPa}\).

Solution
  1. Plate, no load on its faces: plane stress. \(\dfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}=\dfrac{80\,000}{0.91}=87\,912\u{MPa}\).
  2. \(\sigma_x=87\,912\,(0.00072+0.3\times0.00016)=87\,912\times0.000768=67.5\u{MPa}\).
  3. \(\sigma_y=87\,912\,(0.00016+0.3\times0.00072)=87\,912\times0.000376=33.1\u{MPa}\).
  4. Check: \((67.5-0.3\times33.1)/80\,000=0.00072\) ✓.

\(\boxed{\sigma_x=67.5\u{MPa},\quad\sigma_y=33.1\u{MPa}}\)

S3.6Mohr's circle at a free (unloaded) edge older format

Decision procedure
  1. If you see "unloaded edge" / "free surface" and only one stress number, do use the edge: no stress acts on it, so the edge normal is a principal direction with stress \(0\).
  2. Then the circle passes through the origin: the other principal stress is \(\pm2\tau_{\max}\), acting along the edge.
  3. Find the edge angle \(\theta_e\) from the drawn slope (e.g. 3 : 4 → \(\tan^{-1}(3/4)=36.87^\circ\)); with the sign from the picture.
  4. Transform back to \(x\)–\(y\) with the ready answer of S3.4 (uniaxial \(\sigma_0\) along the edge), or on the circle by \(2\theta_e\).
  5. If the sign (tension/compression) is not given, state your assumption; the other choice flips every sign.

Cue: one number only ("maximum shear stress is …") plus a drawn boundary. Traps: putting the zero stress along the edge instead of across it; using the edge slope angle with the wrong sign.

Concept: Notes §17 · Max shear, §18 · Mohr's circle.

✓Last-minute checklist

  • Read \(\tau_{xy}\) on the right face: arrow down = negative. Arrows into a face = compression.
  • \(\sigma_{1,2}=\sigma_M\pm r\), \(\tau_{\max}=r\), \(\tan2\theta_p=2\tau_{xy}/(\sigma_x-\sigma_y)\): no formula sheet in Section A.
  • Tie each angle to its stress: the \(\tan^{-1}\) angle carries \(\sigma_1\) only if \(\sigma_x\gt\sigma_y\). Substitute to confirm.
  • Mohr with \(\tau\) up: element \(\theta\) anticlockwise = circle \(2\theta\) clockwise. Draw the sign sketch.
  • Max-shear faces also carry \(\sigma_M\); show it on the sketch.
  • Check every rotation: \(\sigma_{x'}+\sigma_{y'}=\sigma_x+\sigma_y\).
  • Strain gauges: \(\sigma_x=\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\varepsilon_x+\nu\varepsilon_y)\), not \(E\varepsilon_x\).
  • Free edge: zero stress across it, \(\pm2\tau_{\max}\) along it; state tension/compression if not given.
