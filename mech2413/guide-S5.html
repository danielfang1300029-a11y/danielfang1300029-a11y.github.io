S5 exam guide · Deflection of beams

0How this chapter is examined

Verdict: predictable. Every deflection part is the same recipe: get \(M(x)\), integrate \(EIw''=M\) twice, fix the constants with the support conditions. What changes is only the beam (cantilever, simply supported, propped cantilever) and whether the deflection is the end goal or a tool to find an extra reaction. It appears in six of the eleven sittings from 2014 to 2024, normally as a 5–10 mark part of Question A1, A2 or A3 coming right after the shear-force and bending-moment diagrams (S2) of the same beam.

Exam format

3 h · attempt any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question, so a 10-mark deflection part is worth about 18 minutes. Formula sheet: Section A has never had one. You must remember \(EIw''=M\), the boundary conditions, and the standard results (ready box in S5.1). 2026/27: orbits and re-entry were removed from Section B and a control part (C1) was added; how the 2026 paper is laid out has not been announced, so assume the statics questions keep their usual style.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

\(x\) along the beam from the left end; deflection \(w\) positive up; bending moment \(M\) positive when it sags the beam; then \(EIw''=M\) and a downward deflection comes out negative (\(w_{\max}=-Fl^3/3EI\) as on the handout). Shear force \(V\) positive clockwise (as S2). The handout's figures draw \(w\) and \(z\) downward; their equations need \(w\) up, so the notes and this guide use \(w\) up. Say "(down)" next to every negative answer.

S5.1Cantilever deflection by integration

S5.1Cantilever deflection by integration3 sittings · 31 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a beam built into a wall at one end, an end force and/or a uniform load; "determine the deflection as a function of \(x\)", "the maximum deflection", "the force \(P\) for zero deflection at the free end", sometimes numbers to plug in.
Do
(1) FBD and cut at \(x\); keep the piece without the wall, so no reactions are needed; (2) write \(M(x)\), sagging positive; (3) \(EIw'=\int M+C_1\), \(EIw=\iint M+C_1x+C_2\); (4) at the wall \(w=0\) and \(w'=0\); (5) evaluate at the free end; numbers in SI (N, m, Pa, m⁴).
Watch
where the wall is: if it is at \(x=L\) (Dec 2021, Dec 2018) both conditions go at \(x=L\), not at 0; a load on the piece you keep that pushes the free end down gives negative (hogging) \(M\); \(I\) in m⁴, \(E\) in Pa.

Concept: Notes §4 · \(EIw''=M\), §5 · Boundary conditions, §6 · Cantilevers, §9 · Superposition and ready table.

Ready answers · standard cases (\(w\) up, loads down, length \(l\))

Cantilever, end force \(F\) (wall at \(x=0\)):

\[\begin{aligned}w&=\frac{F}{EI}\Big(\frac{x^3}{6}-\frac{lx^2}{2}\Big)\\ w_{\text{tip}}&=-\frac{Fl^3}{3EI},\quad \theta_{\text{tip}}=-\frac{Fl^2}{2EI}\end{aligned}\]

Cantilever, uniform load \(q\): \(w_{\text{tip}}=-\dfrac{ql^4}{8EI}\), \(\theta_{\text{tip}}=\mp\dfrac{ql^3}{6EI}\).

Simply supported, uniform \(q\): \(w_{\text{mid}}=-\dfrac{5ql^4}{384EI}\); central force \(F\): \(w_{\text{mid}}=-\dfrac{Fl^3}{48EI}\).

Propped cantilever, uniform \(q\): prop \(\tfrac38ql\), wall \(\tfrac58ql\) and \(\tfrac18ql^2\) (hogging).

In words: the tip of a cantilever drops by load × length³ ÷ (3 × stiffness) for an end force, and by load per metre × length⁴ ÷ (8 × stiffness) for a uniform load.

Dec 2024 A2(c), (e)7 + 2 marks

The cantilever is clamped on the wall at the left end with the free end on the right-hand side, which is subjected to a concentrated force \(F_1\) as shown in Figure A2.

(a) Sketch the reactions provided by the left walls. → S1.1 · (b) Shear force and bending moment diagrams in terms of \(F_1\), reactions and \(L\). → S2.1 · (d) Moment of inertia in terms of \(w\) and \(t\). → S4.1 (from (d): \(I=wt^3/12\))

(c) Determine the deflection of the cantilever as a function of \(x\), in terms of \(F_1\), \(L\) and any other quantities that are relevant. [7]

(e) Calculate the maximum deflection of cantilever. A 150 cm long, 1 cm width, 1 cm thickness cantilever with a uniform cross-section is subjected to downward force of \(F_1=100\u{N}\) at right end. The Young's modulus is \(E=190\u{GPa}\). [2]

Hint

Type S5.1 (end force, wall at \(x=0\)). Cut at \(x\) and keep the right-hand piece: only \(F_1\) acts on it. Both constants come from the wall at \(x=0\).

Solution
  1. FBD: wall gives \(F_1\) up and a couple \(F_1L\) anticlockwise. Right piece of a cut at \(x\): \(F_1\) down at distance \(L-x\) bends it hogging, so \(M=-F_1(L-x)\).
  2. \(EIw''=-F_1(L-x)\Rightarrow EIw'=-F_1\big(Lx-\tfrac{x^2}{2}\big)+C_1\), \(EIw=-F_1\big(\tfrac{Lx^2}{2}-\tfrac{x^3}{6}\big)+C_1x+C_2\).
  3. Wall: \(w'(0)=0\Rightarrow C_1=0\); \(w(0)=0\Rightarrow C_2=0\).
  4. (c) \(w(x)=\dfrac{F_1}{EI}\Big(\dfrac{x^3}{6}-\dfrac{Lx^2}{2}\Big)\); largest at the tip, \(w(L)=-\dfrac{F_1L^3}{3EI}\).
  5. (e) \(I=\dfrac{wt^3}{12}=\dfrac{0.01(0.01)^3}{12}=8.33\times10^{-10}\u{m^4}\), \(EI=190\times10^9\times8.33\times10^{-10}=158.3\u{N\,m^2}\). \[\begin{aligned}|w|_{\max}&=\frac{F_1L^3}{3EI}=\frac{100(1.5)^3}{3(158.3)}\\&=0.711\u{m}\end{aligned}\]

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}w(x)&=\frac{F_1}{EI}\Big(\frac{x^3}{6}-\frac{Lx^2}{2}\Big)\\ w_{\max}&=-\frac{F_1L^3}{3EI}=-0.711\u{m}\end{aligned}}\) (down, at the free end)

0.71 m on a 1.5 m bar is far outside small-deflection theory (the stress at the wall would be \(\sigma=Mz/I=900\u{MPa}\)), so the real bar would yield first. The paper wants the formula value; one line noting the limitation is a good extra.

Marker note

Using \(I=tw^3/12\) or forgetting to convert cm to m are the two ways to lose (e); here \(w=t\), so only the unit slip can hurt you.

Dec 2021 A3(b)10 marks

(a)(i)–(iii) Thin-walled spherical vessel: membrane stresses, in-plane and out-of-plane \(\tau_{\max}\). → S7.2

(b) The cantilever beam \(AB\) of length \(L\) shown in figure below carries a uniformly distributed load of intensity \(w_0\), which includes the weight of the beam. Calculate the deflection of the beam.

Hint

Type S5.1 with the wall on the right (\(x=L\)). Keep the left piece of a cut (no reactions on it). Both conditions go at \(x=L\). Here \(w_0\) is a load per length, not a deflection.

Solution
  1. Left piece of length \(x\): load \(w_0x\) acting at \(x/2\) from the cut, hogging: \(M=-\dfrac{w_0x^2}{2}\).
  2. \(EIw'=-\dfrac{w_0x^3}{6}+C_1\), \(EIw=-\dfrac{w_0x^4}{24}+C_1x+C_2\).
  3. Wall at \(x=L\): \(w'(L)=0\Rightarrow C_1=\dfrac{w_0L^3}{6}\); \(w(L)=0\Rightarrow C_2=\dfrac{w_0L^4}{24}-\dfrac{w_0L^4}{6}=-\dfrac{w_0L^4}{8}\).
  4. Check: \(w(L)=0\) ✓; free end \(w(0)=-\dfrac{w_0L^4}{8EI}\), the ready result ✓.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}w(x)&=-\frac{w_0}{24EI}\big(x^4-4L^3x+3L^4\big)\\ w_A&=-\frac{w_0L^4}{8EI}\ \text{(down)}\end{aligned}}\)

Equivalent form, distance \(s=L-x\) measured from the wall: \(w=-\dfrac{w_0s^2}{24EI}(6L^2-4Ls+s^2)\).

Marker note

Putting \(w(0)=w'(0)=0\) (as if the wall were on the left) gives a beam clamped at the free end and loses most of the method marks.

Dec 2018 A2(c), (d), (e)6 + 3 + 3 marks

The cantilever in Figure A2 is subjected to a distributed load of constant force \(q\) per unit length. At the free end, it is supported by a point force \(P\).

(a) Bending moment and shear force diagrams in terms of \(q\), \(P\), \(L\). → S2.2 · (b) Reactions at the wall. → S1.3 (from (a): \(M=Px-\tfrac12qx^2\))

(c) Determine the deflection of the cantilever as a function of \(x\), in terms of \(q\), \(P\) and \(L\), and any other quantities that are relevant. [6]

(d) Find the force \(P\) required to produce zero deflection at the free end. In this situation, what would be the magnitude and direction of the force and moment provided by the wall at the fixed end? [3]

(e) A 5 m long, heavy cantilever (density = 7870 kg m⁻³, Young's modulus = 200 GPa) with a uniform cross-section (moment of inertia = 8.96×10⁶ mm⁴) is supported at its free end by an upward force of 1 kN, without carrying additional loading. Calculate the deflection at the free end based on the results obtained for previous questions. [3]

(e) cannot be solved numerically as printed: the self-weight per length is \(q=\rho gA\) and the cross-sectional area \(A\) is not given (it cannot be found from \(I\) alone). The answer below is in terms of \(A\). Reading of the figure (as in the notes): free end at \(x=0\) on the left, wall at \(x=L\) on the right, \(P\) upward at the free end.

Hint

Type S5.1, wall on the right. Left piece: \(P\) up at the free end, load \(qx\) at \(x/2\). Both constants at \(x=L\). For (d) set \(w(0)=0\); for (e) put \(q=\rho gA\) into your \(w(0)\).

Solution
  1. Left piece: \(M=Px-\tfrac12qx^2\) (P sagging, load hogging).
  2. \(EIw'=\tfrac P2x^2-\tfrac q6x^3+C_1\), \(EIw=\tfrac P6x^3-\tfrac q{24}x^4+C_1x+C_2\).
  3. Wall: \(w'(L)=0\Rightarrow C_1=\tfrac q6L^3-\tfrac P2L^2\); \(w(L)=0\Rightarrow C_2=\tfrac P3L^3-\tfrac q8L^4\).
  4. (c) result below. Free end: \(EIw(0)=C_2=\tfrac{PL^3}{3}-\tfrac{qL^4}{8}\).
  5. (d) \(w(0)=0\Rightarrow P=\tfrac38qL\). Wall force \(qL-P=\tfrac58qL\) upward; \(M(L)=\tfrac38qL^2-\tfrac12qL^2=-\tfrac18qL^2\) (hogging), so the wall applies \(\tfrac18qL^2\) clockwise on the beam.
  6. (e) \(EI=200\times10^9\times8.96\times10^{-6}=1.792\times10^6\u{N\,m^2}\); \(P=1000\u{N}\), \(L=5\u{m}\), \(q=\rho gA=7870(9.81)A=7.72\times10^4A\u{N/m}\) (\(A\) in m²): \[\begin{aligned}w(0)&=\frac{1}{EI}\Big(\frac{PL^3}{3}-\frac{\rho gAL^4}{8}\Big)\\&=0.0233-3.37A\ \ \text{m}\end{aligned}\] Zero tip deflection would need \(A=6.91\times10^{-3}\u{m^2}\) (\(=8P/3\rho gL\)); a smaller section lifts, a larger one sags.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}EIw&=\tfrac P6x^3-\tfrac q{24}x^4\\&\quad+\Big(\tfrac{qL^3}{6}-\tfrac{PL^2}{2}\Big)x\\&\quad+\tfrac{PL^3}{3}-\tfrac{qL^4}{8}\end{aligned}}\)

\(\boxed{P=\tfrac38qL;\ \text{wall: }\tfrac58qL\uparrow,\ \tfrac18qL^2\ \text{clockwise}}\)

\(\boxed{w_{\text{tip}}=(0.0233-3.37A)\u{m},\ A\text{ in m}^2}\) (positive = up; not computable without \(A\))

Marker note

For (e) state the missing data, write \(q=\rho gA\), and give \(w_{\text{tip}}\) as a formula: that is what can be marked.

S5.2Simply supported beam deflection by integration

S5.2Simply supported beam deflection1 sitting · 10 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
pin at one end, roller at the other, uniform load (or a point load); "solve the deflection … determine the deflection in the middle".
Do
(1) reactions by symmetry or moments; (2) \(M(x)\) from the left piece; (3) integrate twice; (4) \(w(0)=0\) and \(w(l)=0\); (5) put \(x=l/2\). A point load away from the middle means two regions (or Macaulay, S5.4) plus matching of \(w\) and \(w'\).
Watch
both conditions are on \(w\) (never \(w'=0\) at a pin); check \(w'(l/2)=0\) for a symmetric load; the answer is negative (down).

Concept: Notes §5 · Boundary conditions, §7 · Multiple regions, §9 · ready table.

Dec 2017 A3(a)10 marks

(b) Aluminium tube shrunk onto a steel tube, extra hoop stress. → S7.6

(a) Provided the flexural rigidity \(EI\) is known, solve the deflection for a simply supported beam that carries a uniformly distributed load. Determine the deflection in the middle of the beam when \(x=l/2\).

Hint

Type S5.2. By symmetry each support carries half the load. Cut at \(x\), keep the left piece, and use \(w(0)=w(l)=0\).

Solution
  1. Reactions \(\tfrac12ql\) up at each end. Left piece: \(M=\tfrac12qlx-\tfrac12qx^2=\tfrac q2(lx-x^2)\).
  2. \(EIw'=\tfrac q2\big(\tfrac{lx^2}{2}-\tfrac{x^3}{3}\big)+C_1\), \(EIw=\tfrac q2\big(\tfrac{lx^3}{6}-\tfrac{x^4}{12}\big)+C_1x+C_2\).
  3. \(w(0)=0\Rightarrow C_2=0\); \(w(l)=0\Rightarrow \tfrac q2\cdot\tfrac{l^4}{12}+C_1l=0\Rightarrow C_1=-\tfrac{ql^3}{24}\).
  4. Collect: \(w=-\dfrac{qx}{24EI}(l^3-2lx^2+x^3)\). At \(x=l/2\): \(l^3-\tfrac{l^3}{2}+\tfrac{l^3}{8}=\tfrac58l^3\).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}w(x)&=-\frac{qx}{24EI}\big(l^3-2lx^2+x^3\big)\\ w\big(\tfrac l2\big)&=-\frac{5ql^4}{384EI}\ \text{(down)}\end{aligned}}\)

End slopes \(\mp ql^3/24EI\); symmetry check \(w'(l/2)=0\) ✓.

Marker note

Write the two boundary conditions explicitly before solving for \(C_1\), \(C_2\): they carry marks of their own.

S5.3Statically indeterminate beams

Decision procedure · one support too many
  1. If you see a wall at one end and a roller (or spring) at the other, or three supports on one beam: count unknown reactions vs the two useful equilibrium equations. One extra unknown = degree 1; say so in one line.
  2. If the question says "using the integral method" (Dec 2018 A1(b)): start from \(q\): \(V=-\int q+C_1\), \(M=\int V+C_2\), \(EIw'\), \(EIw\) with \(C_3\), \(C_4\). Use four conditions: \(w=w'=0\) at the wall, \(w=0\) and \(M=0\) at the roller.
  3. If it says "based on the equilibrium conditions" (Dec 2015 A1(c)): keep the redundant reaction (say \(B\)) as a symbol; write \(V\), \(M\) and the diagrams in terms of \(B\); then say a deflection condition is needed and quote its value (\(\tfrac38q_0l\)).
  4. If a support moves or is a spring (May 2013): remove it, call its force \(R\), and use compatibility by superposition: deflection under the load − deflection removed by \(R\) = \(R/k\) (or the given settlement). Take each piece from the ready table.
  5. Then finish with statics: other reactions, \(V\), \(M\), max moment where \(V=0\), and compare with the support moment.

Cue: "propped", "spring support", "deflects … per kgf", or a roller drawn under the free end of a cantilever. Traps: solving with equilibrium alone and inventing a value; forgetting \(M=0\) at the roller; units of a spring given per kgf (1 kgf = 9.807 N); the largest \(|M|\) may be the hogging value at the support, not the sagging peak.

2 sittings · 15 marks (2014–2024), plus May 2013 A3 (20 marks, older format).

Concept: Notes §10 · Statically indeterminate beams, S1 §11 · determinacy, S5 §9 · superposition.

Ready answer · propped cantilever, uniform \(q_0\) (wall at \(x=0\), roller at \(x=l\)) \[\begin{aligned}B&=\tfrac38q_0l,\quad A_V=\tfrac58q_0l\\ M_A&=-\tfrac18q_0l^2\ \text{(hogging)}\\ V&=q_0\big(\tfrac58l-x\big)\\ M_{\max}^{+}&=\tfrac{9}{128}q_0l^2\ \text{at }x=\tfrac58l\end{aligned}\]

In words: the prop takes three-eighths of the load, the wall five-eighths plus a hogging moment of one-eighth of load × span.

Dec 2018 A1(b)5 marksalso Dec 2015 A1(d)

(a) Resultant of a triangular load. → S1.5 · (c)(i)–(ii) Wood grain at 20°, Mohr's circle. → S3.4

(b) A constant distributed load \(q_0\) is applied to the beam shown in Figure A1(b). Calculate the shear force and bending moment using the integral method.

Hint

Type S5.3 (integral method). Integrate \(q\) four times with four constants; two conditions at the wall \(A\), two at the roller \(B\).

Solution
  1. Degree of indeterminacy 1 (\(A_V\), \(M_A\), \(B\); two useful equations), so deflection is needed. \(q=q_0\) down: \(V=-q_0x+C_1\), \(M=-\tfrac12q_0x^2+C_1x+C_2\).
  2. \(EIw=-\tfrac{q_0}{24}x^4+\tfrac{C_1}{6}x^3+\tfrac{C_2}{2}x^2+C_3x+C_4\).
  3. Wall: \(w(0)=0\Rightarrow C_4=0\); \(w'(0)=0\Rightarrow C_3=0\). Roller: \(M(l)=0\Rightarrow C_2=\tfrac12q_0l^2-C_1l\).
  4. \(w(l)=0\): \(-\tfrac{q_0l^2}{24}+\tfrac{C_1l}{6}+\tfrac{C_2}{2}=0\Rightarrow -\tfrac{C_1l}{3}+\tfrac{5q_0l^2}{24}=0\Rightarrow C_1=\tfrac58q_0l\), \(C_2=-\tfrac18q_0l^2\).

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}V&=q_0\big(\tfrac58l-x\big)\\ M&=-\tfrac12q_0x^2+\tfrac58q_0lx-\tfrac18q_0l^2\end{aligned}}\)

Reactions: \(A_V=\tfrac58q_0l\), \(B=\tfrac38q_0l\), wall moment \(\tfrac18q_0l^2\) (hogging). Diagrams: next question.

Marker note

State the four conditions in a list before using them; \(M(l)=0\) at the roller is the one most often missed.

Dec 2015 A1(c)6 marks

(a) Centroid of a C-beam; centre of force of \(q_0x/l\) (same as May 2019 A1(c)). → S1.5 · (b) Number of support reactions; degree of indeterminacy of system (C). → S1.2 · (d) Integral method with boundary conditions: same as Dec 2018 A1(b), solved above · (e) Rewrite Macaulay expressions (same as May 2019 A1(b)). → S2.4

(c) Draw the free body diagram for the following beam under a continuously distributed load. Calculate the shear force and bending moment based on the equilibrium conditions and plot the corresponding shear force and bending moment diagrams. \(q_0\) represents the force per unit length.

The beam is statically indeterminate, so equilibrium alone cannot finish it. Reading used (as in the notes): give \(V\), \(M\) in terms of the unknown roller force \(B\), then use \(B=\tfrac38q_0l\) from part (d) to draw the diagrams.

Hint

Type S5.3 (equilibrium form). Three unknowns, two equations: keep \(B\) as a symbol. Use the right-hand piece of a cut: it carries only \(B\) and the load.

Solution
  1. FBD: \(A_V\) up, \(M_A\) at the wall, \(B\) up at the roller, load \(q_0l\) at \(l/2\). \(\sum F_y=0\Rightarrow A_V=q_0l-B\); \(\sum M_A=0\) (↺ +): \(M_A+Bl-\tfrac12q_0l^2=0\Rightarrow M_A=\tfrac12q_0l^2-Bl\) (anticlockwise on the beam if positive).
  2. Indeterminate to degree 1: \(B\) needs a deflection condition (part (d)).
  3. Right piece of a cut at \(x\) (only \(B\) and the load \(q_0(l-x)\) act on it; \(V\) clockwise positive): \(V=q_0(l-x)-B\), \(M=B(l-x)-\tfrac12q_0(l-x)^2\). Check at \(x=0\): \(V=A_V\), \(M=-M_A\) ✓.
  4. With \(B=\tfrac38q_0l\): \(V=q_0(\tfrac58l-x)\), \(M(0)=-\tfrac18q_0l^2\), \(V=0\) and \(M_{\max}=\tfrac9{128}q_0l^2\) at \(x=\tfrac58l\), \(M(l)=0\).

\(V/(q_0l)\) (top) and \(M/(q_0l^2)\) (bottom) with \(B=\tfrac38q_0l\): \(V\) from \(+\tfrac58\) to \(-\tfrac38\); \(M\) from \(-\tfrac18\) (hogging at the wall) to \(+\tfrac9{128}\) at \(x=\tfrac58l\), 0 at the roller.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}V&=q_0(l-x)-B\\ M&=B(l-x)-\tfrac12q_0(l-x)^2\\ B&=\tfrac38q_0l\ \text{(from (d))}\end{aligned}}\)

Marker note

Saying "statically indeterminate, one redundant" earns credit; a diagram drawn with a guessed \(B\) (e.g. \(\tfrac12q_0l\), as for a simply supported beam) does not.

May 2013 A3(a), (b)14 + 6 marksolder format

Figure A3 shows a uniformly distributed simply supported beam with an additional spring support at \(B\), which deflects 0.0005 mm per kgf. Assume that the elastic modulus and the second moment of area of the beam are 204 GPa and 9350 cm⁴, respectively. Determine

(a) the deflection at \(B\); [14] and (b) the maximum bending moment and its location. [6]

"Deflects 0.0005 mm per kgf" is read as the spring's flexibility: \(k=1\u{kgf}/0.0005\u{mm}\) with 1 kgf = 9.807 N (as in the notes).

Hint

Type S5.3 (spring support). Remove the spring, put its force \(R\) up at mid-span, and make the beam's mid-span deflection equal the spring's compression \(R/k\). Both beam terms are in the ready box.

Solution
  1. SI data: \(EI=204\times10^9\times9350\times10^{-8}=1.907\times10^7\u{N\,m^2}\); \(k=\dfrac{9.807}{0.0005\times10^{-3}}=1.961\times10^7\u{N/m}\); \(q=16\u{kN/m}\), \(L=9\u{m}\).
  2. Compatibility (downward deflection at \(B\)): \[\frac{5qL^4}{384EI}-\frac{RL^3}{48EI}=\frac{R}{k}\]
  3. \(\dfrac{5qL^4}{384EI}=0.07166\u{m}\); \(\dfrac{L^3}{48EI}=7.96\times10^{-7}\u{m/N}\); \(\dfrac1k=5.10\times10^{-8}\u{m/N}\). \[\begin{aligned}R&=\frac{0.07166}{7.96\times10^{-7}+5.10\times10^{-8}}\\&=84.6\u{kN}\end{aligned}\]
  4. (a) \(\delta_B=R/k=84.6\times10^3/1.961\times10^7=4.31\times10^{-3}\u{m}\).
  5. (b) \(A=C=\tfrac12(16\times9-84.6)=29.7\u{kN}\). On \(AB\): \(M=29.7x-8x^2\) kN·m. \(V=0\) at \(x=1.86\u{m}\): \(M=+27.6\u{kN\,m}\). At \(B\): \(M=29.7(4.5)-8(4.5)^2=-28.3\u{kN\,m}\).

\(M\) (kN·m) along the beam: +27.6 at 1.86 m and 7.14 m, −28.3 (hogging) over the spring at 4.5 m.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}\delta_B&=4.31\u{mm}\ \text{(down)}\\ |M|_{\max}&=28.3\u{kN\,m}\ \text{(hogging) at }B\end{aligned}}\)

Sense check: a rigid prop (\(k\to\infty\)) takes \(\tfrac58qL=90\u{kN}\); the spring takes a little less, 84.6 kN ✓. Without the spring, mid-span would sag 71.7 mm.

Marker note

Part (b) asks for the maximum moment: compare the sagging peak (27.6) with the hogging value over the spring (28.3) and say which wins.

S5.4Deflection at a point with Macaulay brackets

S5.4Deflection at a point with Macaulay brackets1 sitting · 5 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a beam with several loads (point loads, a couple, a part-length distributed load) whose \(M\) was already written with brackets in an earlier part; "given \(EI\), determine the deflection at …".
Do
(1) take \(M(x)\) with brackets from the earlier part; (2) integrate bracket by bracket: \([x-a]^n\to[x-a]^{n+1}/(n+1)\), a couple term \([x-a]^0\to[x-a]\); (3) only two constants for the whole beam; (4) \(w=0\) at the two supports; (5) evaluate, dropping brackets that are negative at that \(x\).
Watch
never expand a bracket before integrating; the conditions are at the supports, not at the ends of the beam; a term whose bracket is negative at the point still affects \(C_1\) if it acts before the second support.

Concept: Notes §8 · Macaulay brackets for deflection, S2 §7 · Macaulay brackets.

Ready answer · bracket integration table \[\begin{aligned}\int[x-a]^n\mathrm dx&=\frac{[x-a]^{n+1}}{n+1}\\ [x-a]^n&=0\ \ \text{for }x\lt a\end{aligned}\]

In words: integrate a bracket as if \(x-a\) were the variable, and switch it off before \(a\).

Dec 2014 A1(c)5 marks

Figure A1 shows a 5.5 m long cantilever beam, which is subjected to a triangular distributed load of \(q(x)\) with \(q_0=10\u{kN/m}\) over 1.5 m of the beam between \(CD\) and a point load of 40 kN at \(E\).

(a) Free-body diagram and reactions at \(A\) and \(C\). → S1.3 · (b) Internal forces and moments with Macaulay brackets. → S2.4 (from (a): \(A=13.125\u{kN}\), \(C=34.375\u{kN}\), both up)

(c) Given the bending stiffness of beam \(EI\), determine the deflection at middle point between \(B\) and \(C\).

Reading used (as in the notes): the figure shows a pin at \(A\), a roller at \(C\) and an overhang \(CD\), not a cantilever. The 40 kN on the 0.5 m bracket acts on the beam at \(B\) as 40 kN down plus a 20 kN·m clockwise couple. "Middle point between \(B\) and \(C\)" is \(x=3\u{m}\).

Hint

Type S5.4. Write \(M\) from \(A\) with brackets, integrate twice, use \(w(0)=0\) and \(w(4)=0\), then put \(x=3\).

Solution
  1. Triangle on \(CD\): \(q=k[x-4]\) with \(k=q_0/1.5=\tfrac{20}{3}\u{kN/m^2}\). Bracket moment (kN, m): \[\begin{aligned}M&=13.125x-40[x-2]\\&\quad+20[x-2]^0+34.375[x-4]\\&\quad-\tfrac{k}{6}[x-4]^3\end{aligned}\]
  2. Integrate twice: \[\begin{aligned}EIw&=\tfrac{13.125}{6}x^3-\tfrac{20}{3}[x-2]^3\\&\quad+10[x-2]^2+\tfrac{34.375}{6}[x-4]^3\\&\quad-\tfrac{k}{120}[x-4]^5+C_1x+C_2\end{aligned}\]
  3. \(w(0)=0\Rightarrow C_2=0\). \(w(4)=0\): \(140-53.33+40+4C_1=0\Rightarrow C_1=-31.67\u{kN\,m^2}\).
  4. \(x=3\): \(EIw=59.06-6.67+10-95.0=-32.6\u{kN\,m^3}\).

\(\boxed{w(3\u{m})=-\dfrac{32.6\u{kN\,m^3}}{EI}\ \text{(down)}}\)

Marker note

The couple term \(+20[x-2]^0\) is sagging (a clockwise couple applied at \(B\) raises \(M\) to the right of \(B\)); dropping it (with the same reactions) changes the answer to \(-12.6\u{kN\,m^3}/EI\).

✓Last-minute checklist

  • \(EIw''=M\), \(w\) up, \(M\) sagging positive: downward answers come out negative; write "(down)".
  • Wall: \(w=0\) and \(w'=0\) where the wall is (often \(x=L\)). Pin/roller: \(w=0\) (and \(M=0\) if you integrate from \(q\)).
  • Cut so the piece you keep has no unknown reactions (the free-end side of a cantilever).
  • Know: \(Fl^3/3EI\), \(ql^4/8EI\), \(5ql^4/384EI\), \(Fl^3/48EI\), and the propped-cantilever \(\tfrac38,\tfrac58,\tfrac18\).
  • One support too many: say "indeterminate, degree 1", keep the redundant as a symbol, find it from a deflection condition.
  • Springs: \(k\) = force ÷ deflection; 1 kgf = 9.807 N; compatibility \(w_{\text{beam}}=R/k\).
  • Numbers: \(E\) in Pa, \(I\) in m⁴ (\(\mathrm{cm^4}\times10^{-8}\), \(\mathrm{mm^4}\times10^{-12}\)); check the answer is small compared with the span.
  • If data are missing (Dec 2018 A2(e): no area), say so and answer in symbols.
