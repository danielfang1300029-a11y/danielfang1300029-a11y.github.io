S2 exam guide · Force and moment resultants

0How this chapter is examined

Verdict: predictable. Every sitting from 2014 to 2024 (11 of 11) has a shear-force / bending-moment part, almost always in Question A1 or A2 and worth 5–20 marks. The same few beams come back with new numbers: a cantilever with a triangular load and an end couple (Dec 2022, Dec 2024), a cantilever with a uniform load on its outer part (Dec 2021, Dec 2023), a cantilever with a couple at mid-span (Dec 2016, 2017, 2021), a load hung on a bracket (Dec 2014, Dec 2017). All four types below are fixed recipes, so each gets a Looks like / Do / Watch card.

Exam format

3 h · any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question. May 2013 was "any five" with no section rule. Formula sheet: Section A has never had one, so nothing from this chapter is given: you must know \(\dfrac{\mathrm dV}{\mathrm dx}=-q\), \(\dfrac{\mathrm dM}{\mathrm dx}=V\), the area and centroid of a triangular load, the end-condition table and the Macaulay rules. (The sheet printed in Section B from 2021 is for dynamics only.) 2026/27: orbits and re-entry were removed from Section B and a control part (C1) was added; no past papers exist for control, and the 2026 paper's layout has not been announced, so use the 2014–2024 format as the best guide. Statics content is unchanged.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

\(x\) along the beam from the left end. \(N\) positive in tension. \(V\) positive when it turns a slice clockwise: on the cut face of the left piece it points down. \(M\) positive when sagging: on the cut face of the left piece it is anticlockwise. Distributed load \(q\) positive down. Reaction moment equations say each time which sense is positive (↺ or ↻). With these, \(\mathrm dV/\mathrm dx=-q\), \(\mathrm dM/\mathrm dx=V\), and a clockwise applied couple makes \(M\) jump up.

S2.1SFD/BMD by sections: point loads and couples

S2.1Point loads and couples, by cutting6 sittings · 48 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a beam with point forces and/or couples (sometimes an inclined force); "calculate the shear force and bending moment from equilibrium", "draw the FBD", "plot the SFD and BMD".
Do
FBD and reactions (\(\sum F\), \(\sum M\) about a support) → split the beam at every load and couple → cut each region and keep the piece with fewer unknowns → \(\sum F\) gives \(V\) (and \(N\)), \(\sum M\) about the cut gives \(M\) → plot: \(V\) in steps, \(M\) in straight lines.
Watch
at a couple \(V\) does not change but \(M\) jumps (clockwise couple: up). An inclined force also gives \(N\). Read each couple's arrow and say which way you read it.

Concept: Notes §3 · procedure + Example 1 · §4 · couples, dM/dx = V · §5 · Example 2, applied couple · §2 · inclined force.

Ready answer · recurring forms
  • Cantilever, wall at \(x=0\), end load \(F\) down at \(x=L\): \(V=F\), \(M=-F(L-x)\); at the wall \(M=-FL\) (hogging), wall couple \(FL\) anticlockwise.
  • Applied couple \(C\) at \(x=c\): \(V\) unchanged; \(M\) jumps by \(+C\) if clockwise, \(-C\) if anticlockwise.
  • Simply supported, two equal loads \(P\) at distance \(a\) from each support: reactions \(P\), \(V=0\) and \(M=Pa\) constant between the loads.
  • Load \(F\) on a bracket with offset \(e\): same as \(F\) at the attachment point plus a couple \(Fe\) (clockwise if the load hangs to the right).
Dec 2024 A2(b)5 marks

The cantilever is clamped on the wall at the left end with the free end on the right-hand side, which is subjected to a concentrated force \(F_1\) as shown in Figure A2.

(b) Determine and sketch the shear force and bending moment diagrams of the cantilever, in terms of \(F_1\), reactions and \(L\). (a) → S1.1 · (c), (e) → S5.1 · (d) → S4.1

Hint

Type S2.1. First step: wall reactions from \(\sum F_y\) and \(\sum M_A\); then one cut at \(x\), keeping the right piece (it has no reactions on it).

Solution
  1. Reactions: \(\sum F_y:\ R_A=F_1\) (up). \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A-F_1L=0\Rightarrow M_A=F_1L\) anticlockwise.
  2. Cut at \(x\), right piece (length \(L-x\), only \(F_1\) on it): \(\sum F_y\): \(V=F_1\).
  3. \(\sum M\) about the cut: \(M=-F_1(L-x)\). In terms of reactions: \(V=R_A\), \(M=R_Ax-M_A\).
  4. Sketch: \(V\) is a constant \(F_1\); \(M\) is a straight line from \(-F_1L\) at the wall to 0 at \(B\) (hogging all along).
Marker note

The question asks for "reactions": write \(V=R_A\), \(M=R_Ax-M_A\) first, then substitute.

\(\boxed{V=F_1,\quad M=-F_1(L-x)}\)

Dec 2021 A1(c)10 marksalso Dec 2017 A1(a) · 5 marks, Dec 2016 A1(b) · 9 marks

A beam is under a concentrated force and a concentrated moment as shown below.

(i) Draw the free body diagram. [2] (ii) Calculate the shear force and bending moment based on the equilibrium conditions. [6] (iii) Plot the shear force and bending moment diagrams. [2] (a) → S1.4 · (b) → S4.1

Dec 2017 A1(a) asks only for the FBD and the reaction force and moment at \(A\); Dec 2016 A1(b) asks for all three parts in one.

the couple's curved arrow is read as anticlockwise (as in the notes). Dec 2017's figure also draws a curved arrow "\(M_I=2lF\)" (anticlockwise) at the wall. It is drawn like \(M_0\), as a load, with the wall still in place, so read it as a second applied couple: the wall must hold it too, \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A+2lF+2lF-Fl=0\Rightarrow M_A=3lF\) clockwise, \(A_V=F\) up. If it were meant as the answer it would be wrong (the reaction without it is \(lF\) clockwise), so never copy it.

Hint

Type S2.1. First step: wall reactions \(A_V\) and \(M_A\); then two regions, split at the couple.

Solution
  1. (i) \(A_H=0\), \(A_V=F\) up. \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A+2lF-Fl=0\Rightarrow M_A=-lF\): the wall couple is \(lF\) clockwise.
  2. (ii) \(0\lt x\lt l/2\), left piece: \(V=F\); \(\sum M\) about the cut (↺ +): \(M-lF-Fx=0\Rightarrow M=F(l+x)\).
  3. \(l/2\lt x\lt l\), right piece (only \(F\)): \(V=F\), \(M=-F(l-x)=F(x-l)\).
  4. (iii) \(V=F\) everywhere. \(M\) rises from \(lF\) to \(\tfrac32lF\), drops by \(2lF\) to \(-\tfrac12lF\) at the couple, then rises to 0 at the free end.
Marker note

The couple changes \(M\) only: a jump in the SFD at \(x=l/2\) loses marks.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}V&=F,\\ M&=F(l+x),\ \ 0\lt x\lt \tfrac l2,\\ M&=F(x-l),\ \ \tfrac l2\lt x\lt l\end{aligned}}\]

Reaction: \(A_V=F\) up, wall couple \(lF\) clockwise.

May 2014 A1(c), (d)9 + 5 marks

Figure A1 shows a 6 m long cantilever beam, which is subjected to a distributed load of 10 kN/m over 2 m of the beam and a point load of 10 kN at B. A moment of 20 kNm is applied to C.

(c) Determine the internal forces and moments in section AB, BC and CD of the beam. [9] (d) Hence, draw the shear force, normal force and bending moment diagrams for the beam. [5] (a) → S1.1 · (b) → S1.3

the 10 kN force at \(B\) acts down and to the left at 30° to the vertical; the 20 kN·m couple at \(C\) is read as clockwise from its arrow.

Hint

Type S2.1 (with a distributed load on the last part). First step: split the 10 kN into vertical and horizontal parts, then the wall reactions; on \(CD\) keep the right piece.

Solution
  1. At \(B\): \(10\cos30^\circ=8.66\u{kN}\) down, \(10\sin30^\circ=5\u{kN}\) to the left. From (b): \(A_H=5\u{kN}\) (→), \(A_V=8.66+20=28.66\u{kN}\) (up); \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A-8.66(2)-20-20(5)=0\Rightarrow M_A=137.3\u{kN\,m}\) ↺.
  2. \(AB\), \(0\lt x\lt2\): \(N=-5\u{kN}\) (compression), \(V=28.66\u{kN}\), \(M=28.66x-137.3\u{kN\,m}\).
  3. \(BC\), \(2\lt x\lt4\): \(N=0\), \(V=20\u{kN}\), \(M=20x-120\u{kN\,m}\) (−80 to −40).
  4. \(CD\), \(4\lt x\lt6\), right piece: \(N=0\), \(V=10(6-x)\u{kN}\), \(M=-5(6-x)^2\u{kN\,m}\) (−20 at \(C\), 0 at \(D\)).
  5. (d) \(N\): −5 kN on \(AB\) only. \(V\): 28.66 → 20 at \(B\), straight down to 0 on \(CD\). \(M\): straight lines on \(AB\), \(BC\); jump +20 at \(C\) (−40 → −20, clockwise couple); parabola to 0 at \(D\).
Marker note

(d) asks for the normal-force diagram too: the 5 kN horizontal part compresses \(AB\) only.

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}AB&:\ V=28.66,\\ &\quad M=28.66x-137.3\\ BC&:\ V=20,\ M=20x-120\\ CD&:\ V=10(6-x),\\ &\quad M=-5(6-x)^2\end{aligned}}\]

(kN, kN·m, \(x\) in m from \(A\); \(N=-5\u{kN}\) on \(AB\).)

May 2013 A2(b)–(d)8 + 8 + 2 marksolder format

Figure A2 shows a beam AE loaded by three 100 kN downward forces at A, C and D.

Pin at \(B\), roller at \(D\); \(x\) from \(A\). (a) reactions → S1.3; from (a): \(B=175\u{kN}\), \(D=125\u{kN}\), both up.

(b) Determine the shear forces at the midpoints of AB, BC, CD and DE. Hence, sketch the shear force diagram. [8] (c) Calculate the bending moments at A, B, C, D and E. Hence, sketch the bending moment diagram. [8] (d) Without further calculation, state whether the shear force and bending moment diagrams will be affected if the 100 kN force acting at D is doubled. Explain. [2]

Hint

Type S2.1. First step: \(V\) at a midpoint is the sum of the vertical forces to its left (up +); \(M\) at a point is the moment of the forces to its left.

Solution
  1. Check (a): \(\sum M_B\) (↺ +): \(0.8D-100(0.3)-100(0.8)+100(0.1)=0\Rightarrow D=125\u{kN}\), \(B=300-125=175\u{kN}\).
  2. (b) \(V_{AB}=-100\), \(V_{BC}=-100+175=75\), \(V_{CD}=75-100=-25\), \(V_{DE}=-25-100+125=0\) (kN).
  3. (c) \(M_A=0\); \(M_B=-100(0.1)=-10\); \(M_C=-10+75(0.3)=12.5\); \(M_D=12.5-25(0.5)=0\); \(M_E=0\) (kN·m).
  4. (d) No. The force at \(D\) sits on the roller: it has no moment arm about \(D\), so doubling it only raises the reaction \(D\) by 100 kN (to 225 kN); \(B\) is unchanged and so are \(V\) and \(M\) everywhere.
Marker note

(d): "No" plus the reason (the load goes straight into the support) earns both marks.

\(\boxed{V:\,-100,\ 75,\ -25,\ 0\u{kN};\ \ M_B=-10,\ M_C=12.5\u{kN\,m}}\)

\(M_A=M_D=M_E=0\).

S2.2SFD/BMD with distributed loads; values at a point

S2.2Uniform and triangular loads5 sittings · 65 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a cantilever (sometimes simply supported) with a uniform or triangular load plus point loads or couples; "equations for shear force and moment in each section", "construct the diagrams", "values at B".
Do
replace each distributed load by its resultant (area, at the centroid) for the reactions only → cut in each region and keep the real load up to the cut → \(V\), \(M\) equations → check \(\mathrm dM/\mathrm dx=V\), jumps, free-end values → plot (uniform: \(V\) straight, \(M\) parabola; triangle: \(V\) parabola, \(M\) cubic).
Watch
inside a triangle, only the part of it before the cut counts. For a value at one point, keep the piece with fewer loads (often the right one). A bracket load is a force plus a couple at the attachment point.

Concept: Notes §8 · distributed loads by geometry · §12 · effect of loads · §13 · force + triangle example · §14 · dual loading (bracket).

Ready answer · recurring forms
  • Triangle from 0 at \(x=c\) to \(q_0\) at \(x=c+b\): \(q=\dfrac{q_0}{b}(x-c)\); resultant \(\tfrac12q_0b\) at \(\tfrac23b\) from the zero end. For a cut inside it (left piece): \(V\) drops by \(\dfrac{q_0(x-c)^2}{2b}\), \(M\) drops by \(\dfrac{q_0(x-c)^3}{6b}\).
  • Cantilever (wall at 0, free at \(l\)) with \(q_0\) on \(a\lt x\lt l\): \(V=q_0(l-a)\), \(M=q_0(l-a)x-\tfrac12q_0(l^2-a^2)\) for \(x\lt a\); \(V=q_0(l-x)\), \(M=-\tfrac12q_0(l-x)^2\) for \(x\gt a\).
  • Free end at the left (no reactions needed): \(V=P-qx\), \(M=Px-\tfrac12qx^2\) for an upward end force \(P\) and uniform \(q\).

Construct the shear and moment diagrams for the beam subjected to the concentrated force and torques and the triangular load. What are the values of the shear force and bending moment at B?

Hint

Type S2.2. First step: the triangle's resultant (area, and where it acts) for the wall reactions; for the values at \(B\) keep the piece to the right of \(B\).

Solution
  1. Triangle: \(q=\tfrac47(x-5)\u{kN/m}\) on \(5\lt x\lt12\); resultant \(\tfrac12(4)(7)=14\u{kN}\) at \(x=5+\tfrac23(7)=\tfrac{29}3\u{m}\).
  2. \(A_V=7+14=21\u{kN}\) up. \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A-7(2)-14(\tfrac{29}3)+30=0\Rightarrow M_A=119.3\u{kN\,m}\) ↺.
  3. Regions (left piece, kN and kN·m): \(0\)–\(2\): \(V=21\), \(M=21x-119.3\). \(2\)–\(5\): \(V=14\), \(M=14x-105.3\). \(5\)–\(12\): \(V=14-\tfrac27(x-5)^2\), \(M=14x-105.3-\tfrac2{21}(x-5)^3\) (passes 0 at \(x\approx7.6\)). \(12\)–\(15\): \(V=0\), \(M=+30\); the anticlockwise couple at the end brings \(M\) back to 0.

The whole triangle's resultant is fine for the reactions, never for \(V\), \(M\) at a cut inside the triangle.

\(\boxed{V_B=6.86\u{kN},\quad M_B=22.8\u{kN\,m}}\)

Dec 2023 A2(a)–(c)10 + 4 + 6 marks

A cantilever beam is subjected to an uniformly distributed load of \(q_0\) over some part of the beam as shown in Figure A2:

\[q(x)=\begin{cases}0, & 0\le x\lt a,\\ q_0, & a\lt x\lt l.\end{cases}\]

(a) Determine the equations for shear force and moment. [10] (b) Suppose \(l=3\u{m}\), \(a=2\u{m}\), \(q_0=10\u{N/m}\), determine the equations for shear force and moment. [4] (c) Construct the shear and moment diagrams for the beam. [6]

The same beam and numbers are asked by the integral method in Dec 2021 A2(b), S2.3.

Hint

Type S2.2. First step: wall reactions from the load's resultant \(q_0(l-a)\) at \(x=\tfrac{l+a}2\); then region I with the left piece and region II with the right piece.

Solution
  1. Reactions: \(A_V=q_0(l-a)\) up; \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A-q_0(l-a)\tfrac{l+a}2=0\Rightarrow M_A=\tfrac12q_0(l^2-a^2)\) ↺.
  2. (a) Region I, \(0\lt x\lt a\), left piece: \(V=q_0(l-a)\), \(M=q_0(l-a)x-\tfrac12q_0(l^2-a^2)\).
  3. Region II, \(a\lt x\lt l\), right piece (load \(q_0(l-x)\) at \(\tfrac{l-x}2\)): \(V=q_0(l-x)\), \(M=-\tfrac12q_0(l-x)^2\).
  4. (b) With the numbers: region I \(V=10\u{N}\), \(M=10x-25\u{N\,m}\); region II \(V=30-10x\u{N}\), \(M=-5x^2+30x-45\u{N\,m}\).
  5. (c) \(V\): 10 N flat, then a straight line to 0 at the free end. \(M\): straight from −25 N·m at the wall to −5 N·m at \(x=2\u{m}\), then a parabola to 0 with zero slope at \(x=3\u{m}\).
Marker note

Check \(M\) is continuous at \(x=a\): both regions give \(-\tfrac12q_0(l-a)^2\) (−5 N·m).

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}\text{I}&:\ V=10\u{N},\\ &\quad M=10x-25\u{N\,m}\\ \text{II}&:\ V=30-10x\u{N},\\ &\quad M=-5x^2+30x-45\u{N\,m}\end{aligned}}\]

Equivalent form for II: \(M=-5(3-x)^2\u{N\,m}\).

Dec 2022 A2(a)–(c)9 + 8 + 3 marks

A cantilever beam is subjected to a point load of 10 kN at 2 m and a triangular distributed load over 7 m of the beam and a point moment of 20 kN m at 15 m.

(a) Determine the shear force and moment in Section AB. [9] (b) Construct the shear and moment diagrams for the beam. [8] (c) What are the values of the shear force and bending moment at B? [3]

the 20 kN·m couple is anticlockwise (arrowhead at the lower-left end of the arc, as in Dec 2024 A1; corrected 17 Nov 2026).

Hint

Type S2.2. First step: triangle resultant \(\tfrac12(10)(7)\) at \(x=\tfrac{29}3\u{m}\) for the reactions; "Section AB" runs from the wall to \(B\) and crosses three load regions.

Solution
  1. Triangle: \(q=\tfrac{10}7(x-5)\u{kN/m}\), resultant \(35\u{kN}\) at \(x=\tfrac{29}3\u{m}\). \(A_V=10+35=45\u{kN}\) up. \(\sum M_A\) (↺ +): \(M_A-10(2)-35(\tfrac{29}3)+20=0\Rightarrow M_A=338.3\u{kN\,m}\) ↺.
  2. (a) \(0\lt x\lt2\): \(V=45\), \(M=45x-338.3\).
  3. \(2\lt x\lt5\): \(V=35\), \(M=35x-318.3\).
  4. \(5\lt x\lt10\): \(V=35-\tfrac57(x-5)^2\), \(M=35x-318.3-\tfrac5{21}(x-5)^3\) (kN, kN·m).
  5. (b) The cubic carries on to \(x=12\) (\(V=0\), \(M=+20\)); then \(V=0\), \(M=+20\u{kN\,m}\) to the free end, where the anticlockwise couple returns \(M\) to 0. \(M\) rises all the way from −338.3 at the wall.
  6. (c) Put \(x=10\) (check with the right piece: load \(\tfrac{120}7\u{kN}\), moment \(-18.1+20\)).
Marker note

"Section AB" needs all three expressions with their ranges, not one formula.

\(\boxed{V_B=\tfrac{120}7=17.1\u{kN},\quad M_B=1.90\u{kN\,m}}\)

Dec 2018 A2(a)5 marks

The cantilever in Figure A2 is subjected to a distributed load of constant force \(q\) per unit length. At the free end, it is supported by a point force \(P\).

(a) Determine and sketch the bending moment diagram and shear force diagram of the cantilever, in terms of \(q\), \(P\) and \(L\). (b) → S1.3 · (c)–(e) → S5.1

Type S2.2. First step: \(x\) starts at the free end, so the left piece has only \(P\) and the load on it; no wall reactions are needed.

Dec 2017 A2(a)10 marks

A cantilever beam AB is subjected to a distributed load of 48 kN/m over 2 m of the beam and a point load of 40 kN at E. Determine the internal forces and moments in section AC, CD and BD of the beam. Hence, draw the shear force and bending moment diagrams.

S2.3Integral method with boundary and matching conditions

S2.3Integrate q, then fix the constants3 sittings · 26 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"use the integral method", "give the boundary and matching conditions", "give the shear force and bending moment boundary conditions for these supports", or a load given as a function \(q(x)\) (cosine, linear).
Do
\(V=-\int q\,\mathrm dx+C_1\), \(M=\int V\,\mathrm dx+C_2\) in each region → end conditions from the table below → matching at each region boundary (\(V\), \(M\) continuous unless a point load or couple sits there) → solve for the constants → plot.
Watch

✓Last-minute checklist

  • State the sign convention once: \(V\) clockwise +, \(M\) sagging +, \(q\) down +; say ↺ or ↻ in every moment equation.
  • Reactions first, using each distributed load's resultant (area, at the centroid) — but never the whole resultant for a cut inside the load.
  • New region at every point load, couple, support, and start or end of a distributed load; give the \(x\)-range with every expression.
  • Couple: no step in \(V\), a jump in \(M\) (clockwise → up). Bracket load: force plus couple \(Fe\) at the attachment point.
  • Checks: \(\mathrm dM/\mathrm dx=V\), \(\mathrm dV/\mathrm dx=-q\); \(M=0\) at a free end, pin or roller with no couple; \(M\) peaks where \(V=0\).
  • Integral method: one constant per integration per region; conditions from free ends and pins plus matching at region boundaries; a fixed end gives none.
  • Macaulay: a bracket never switches off (cancel a part load); \([x-a]^0\) is a switch; a couple appears in \(M\) only.
  • Plot both diagrams under the beam with values at every change point and the peak, and the shape (flat, straight, parabola, cubic).
