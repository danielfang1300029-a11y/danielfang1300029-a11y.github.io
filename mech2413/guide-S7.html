S7 exam guide · Thin-walled pressure vessels

0How this chapter is examined

Beyond the handouts

No handout was issued for pressure vessels (it is topic 7 in the Ch01 contents list). The notes and this guide are written from the past papers and standard textbooks (Beer, Hibbeler, Gere, Pytel–Singer).

Verdict: predictable. Pressure vessels appeared in 11 of the 12 sittings (every paper from May 2013 to Dec 2023; none in Dec 2024), usually as a whole 20-mark Section A question or a 9–10 mark part. The same closed thin cylinder comes back every time, and the questions take only a few shapes: stresses → strains → change in diameter; "derive the formulas"; a weld or rotated direction on Mohr's circle; and the number of bolts or rivets. 24 parts, 138 marks in total.

Exam format

3 h · any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question (May 2013: older format, any five, no section rule). Formula sheet: Section A has never had one, so nothing in this chapter is given: you must know \(\sigma_h=pr/t\), \(\sigma_l=pr/2t\), \(\sigma_{\rm sphere}=pr/2t\), the 2D Hooke's law and the stress-transformation (Mohr) results by heart. The 2021–2024 sheet at the start of Section B is dynamics only. 2026/27: orbits and re-entry were removed from the dynamics part and an introduction to PID control (C1) was added; there are no past papers for control, and the 2026 exam format has not been announced. No handout covers this chapter, so whether it is examined in 2026 is not known; it was in every sitting except Dec 2024.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

Tension positive. \(\sigma_h\) hoop (round the vessel), \(\sigma_l\) longitudinal (along the axis), \(\sigma_r\) radial (\(\approx0\) in a thin wall). \(p\) is gauge pressure (above atmospheric). A weld angle \(\beta\) is measured from a transverse plane, so it is also the angle of the weld's normal from the axis. Mohr's circle as in S3: \(\sigma\) to the right, \(\tau\) up positive, face \(x\) plotted at \((\sigma_x,\tau_{xy})\); \(\tau_{x'y'}\) is positive when it acts in \(+y'\) on the \(+x'\) face; turning the element by \(\theta\) anticlockwise moves the point \(2\theta\) clockwise on the circle. Units: MPa (= N/mm²) and mm throughout, \(E\) in MPa.

S7.1Thin cylinder: stresses, strains, change in diameter

S7.1Thin cylinder: σ, ε, Δd5 sittings · 43 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Closed cylindrical vessel, radius …, wall …, E, ν, internal pressure …: find the hoop and longitudinal stresses and the change in diameter."
Do
check \(r/t\ge10\) → \(\sigma_h=pr/t\), \(\sigma_l=pr/2t\) → \(\varepsilon_h=(\sigma_h-\nu\sigma_l)/E\) → \(\Delta d=\varepsilon_h d\) (and \(\Delta r=\varepsilon_h r\), \(\Delta L=\varepsilon_l L\)).
Watch
radius, not diameter, in \(pr/t\); \(E=200\u{GPa}=200\,000\u{MPa}\); keep the Poisson term; with an outside diameter, say which radius you use.

Concept: Notes §2 · hoop and longitudinal stress · §3 · which radius · §6 · strains and change in size.

Ready answer · closed thin cylinder \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_h&=\frac{pr}{t},\qquad\sigma_l=\frac{pr}{2t},\\ \varepsilon_h&=\frac{pr}{2tE}(2-\nu),\\ \varepsilon_l&=\frac{pr}{2tE}(1-2\nu),\\ \Delta d&=\varepsilon_h d=\frac{pd^2(2-\nu)}{4tE}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: hoop stress is pressure × radius ÷ thickness, longitudinal is half of that; the diameter grows by the hoop strain times the diameter.

Dec 2023 A1(c)(i) 3 + (ii) 6 = 9 marks

Consider a closed cylindrical steel pressure vessel. The radius of the cylinder is 3 m and its wall thickness is 10 mm. Young's modulus of steel is 200 GPa and Poisson's ratio is 0.25. Assume that it is a thin wall vessel.

(i) Determine the hoop and longitudinal stresses in the cylindrical wall caused by an internal pressure 0.9 MPa. (ii) Calculate the change in diameter of the cylinder caused by pressurization.

Other parts of A1: (a) → S1.2 · (b) → S3.1

Hint

Type S7.1. First step: put everything in mm and MPa (\(r=3000\u{mm}\), \(E=200\,000\u{MPa}\)) and check the wall is thin.

Solution
  1. Thin? \(r/t=3000/10=300\ge10\) ✓.
  2. Hoop (FBD left): \(\sigma_h=\dfrac{pr}{t}=\dfrac{0.9(3000)}{10}=270\u{MPa}\).
  3. Longitudinal (FBD right): \(\sigma_l=\dfrac{pr}{2t}=135\u{MPa}\).
  4. Hoop strain: \(\varepsilon_h=\dfrac{270-0.25(135)}{200\,000}=1.181\times10^{-3}\).
  5. \(d=6000\u{mm}\): \(\Delta d=1.181\times10^{-3}\times6000=7.09\u{mm}\) (mm/mm × mm = mm ✓).
Marker note

(ii) carries 6 marks for the strain: \(\Delta d=\sigma_hd/E=8.10\u{mm}\) (no Poisson term) loses most of them.

\(\boxed{\sigma_h=270\u{MPa},\ \sigma_l=135\u{MPa},\ \Delta d=7.09\u{mm}}\)

S7.2Derive the vessel formulas and the maximum shear

Answer skeleton · "derive σ_h, σ_l (or the sphere) and τ_max"
  1. Diagram 1 (1 mark): half of the vessel plus its gas, cut along a diameter, length \(L\); pressure on the flat gas face, wall forces \(\sigma_h tL\) on the two cut edges.
  2. Hoop equilibrium (1–2 marks): \(p(2r)L=2\sigma_h tL\Rightarrow\sigma_h=pr/t\). (Pressure on a curved surface has a net force equal to \(p\) × projected area.)
  3. Diagram 2 (1 mark): one closed end plus gas, cut across; wall ring of area \(2\pi rt\).
  4. Longitudinal equilibrium (1 mark): \(p\pi r^2=\sigma_l(2\pi rt)\Rightarrow\sigma_l=pr/2t\). Sphere: the same cut in any direction gives \(\sigma=pr/2t\).
  5. Max shear (1–2 marks): principal stresses \(\sigma_h,\ \sigma_l,\ \sigma_r\approx0\); in-plane \(\tau_{\max}=(\sigma_h-\sigma_l)/2\) on planes at 45° in the wall; absolute \(\tau_{\max}=\sigma_{\max}/2\) on planes at 45° through the thickness. Sketch the three Mohr's circles.
  6. Assumptions (1 mark): \(r/t\ge10\), stress uniform through the wall, \(\sigma_r\) neglected, weight of gas and wall neglected, section far from the ends.

Model answer (exam length, cylinder): "Cut the cylinder and its gas along a diameter over a length \(L\). The gas pressure acts on the projected area \(2rL\); the two cut wall strips each carry \(\sigma_htL\). Vertical equilibrium: \(2\sigma_htL=p(2r)L\), so \(\sigma_h=pr/t\). Cut across the cylinder: the pressure on the end \(p\pi r^2\) is carried by the wall ring \(2\pi rt\), so \(\sigma_l=pr/2t\). The wall element has principal stresses \(\sigma_h=pr/t\), \(\sigma_l=pr/2t\) and \(\sigma_r\approx0\). In-plane maximum shear \(=(\sigma_h-\sigma_l)/2=pr/4t\) at 45° to the axis; absolute maximum \(=(\sigma_h-0)/2=pr/2t\) at 45° through the thickness. Valid for \(r/t\ge10\), away from the ends."

Concept: Notes §2 · derivations · §4 · sphere · §5 · in-plane and absolute shear.

Ready answer · maximum shear (σ_r = 0)
in-plane \(\tau_{\max}\)absolute \(\tau_{\max}\)
cylinder\(\dfrac{pr}{4t}\)\(\dfrac{pr}{2t}\)
sphere\(0\)\(\dfrac{pr}{4t}\)

In words: the in-plane value is half the difference of the two wall stresses; the absolute value is half the biggest stress, because the third principal stress (radial) is zero.

Consider a thin-walled spherical pressure vessel with inner radius \(r\) and wall thickness \(t\). It is subjected to an internal gage pressure \(p\).

(i) Derive vessel formulas for membrane stresses \(\sigma_\varphi\) and \(\sigma_t\). (ii) Determine the maximum shear stresses \(\tau_{\max}\) for in-plane rotation of the stress element. (iii) Neglecting stress in radial direction, determine the maximum shear stresses \(\tau_{\max}\) for out-of-plane rotation of the stress element.

(b) cantilever deflection → S5.1

Hint

Type S7.2 (sphere). First step: free body of a hemisphere and the gas inside it, cut through the centre.

Solution
  1. (i) Pressure on the flat gas face: \(p\pi r^2\). Wall ring force: \(\sigma(2\pi rt)\).
  2. Equilibrium: \(\sigma(2\pi rt)=p\pi r^2\Rightarrow\sigma=pr/2t\). Any plane through the centre gives the same, so \(\sigma_\varphi=\sigma_t=pr/2t\).
  3. (ii) In-plane: \(\tau_{\max}=(\sigma_\varphi-\sigma_t)/2=0\); the in-plane Mohr's circle is a point.

Marker note

(ii) is not a trick: "zero" with the reason (equal principal stresses, circle shrinks to a point) gets both marks.

\(\boxed{\sigma_\varphi=\sigma_t=\dfrac{pr}{2t};\ \tau_{\rm in}=0;\ \tau_{\rm out}=\dfrac{pr}{4t}}\)

Consider a thin walled cylindrical vessel with inner diameter \(2r\) and wall thickness \(t\). It is subjected to an internal gage pressure \(p\).

S7.3Vessel + Mohr's circle: weld plane, rotated element, maximum shear

S7.3Vessel + Mohr's circle4 sittings · 33 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Sketch Mohr's circle …": a helical weld at β to a transverse plane, a direction θ from the hoop direction, or "maximum shear stress and angle".
Do
\(\sigma_h,\sigma_l\) (S7.1) are principal → centre \(C=\tfrac12(\sigma_h+\sigma_l)\), radius \(R=\tfrac12(\sigma_h-\sigma_l)\) → start at the point of the reference face, turn \(2\theta\) → read \(\sigma=C\mp R\cos2\theta\), \(\tau=R\sin2\theta\); the perpendicular face is the opposite point.
Watch
β from a transverse plane ⇒ start at the axial point L; turn \(2\beta\), not β; "maximum shear" ⇒ give the absolute \(\sigma_h/2\) as well.

Concept: Notes §7 · stress on a weld · §5 · maximum shear · Mohr's circle: S3 §18.

Ready answer · weld at β to a transverse plane \[\begin{aligned}\sigma_w&=\sigma_l\cos^2\beta+\sigma_h\sin^2\beta,\\ \tau_w&=\tfrac12(\sigma_h-\sigma_l)\sin2\beta,\\ \sigma_\parallel&=\sigma_h+\sigma_l-\sigma_w.\end{aligned}\]

In words: normal stress across the weld blends the axial and hoop stresses (all axial at β = 0, all hoop at 90°); shear along the weld is the circle's radius times sin 2β; the two normal stresses on perpendicular faces always add to \(\sigma_h+\sigma_l\).

  2. (d) \(\sigma_x=36-12\cos50^\circ=28.3\u{MPa}\) (across the weld).
  3. \(\tau_{xy}=12\sin50^\circ=9.19\u{MPa}\) (along the weld).
  4. \(\sigma_y=72-28.3=43.7\u{MPa}\) (along the weld direction).
Marker note

"Properly orientated" means the element is drawn with one edge along the weld at 25° to the transverse plane, as on the right.

\(\boxed{\sigma_x=28.3,\ \sigma_y=43.7,\ \tau_{xy}=9.19\u{MPa}}\)

S7.4Bolts and rivets on vessel ends

S7.4How many bolts / what pressure for the rivets3 sittings · 23 marks (2013–2024)
Looks like
"How many bolts of Ø… to hold the cap / chamber, allowable stress …" or "rivets at … pitch, maximum shear …, find the maximum pressure".
Do
\(F=p\times\tfrac\pi4D^2\) (D inside the seal) → one bolt carries \(\sigma_{\rm avail}A_b\) → \(n=F/(\sigma_{\rm avail}A_b)\), round up. Rivets: one pitch carries \(prs\) (longitudinal seam) or \(\tfrac12prs\) (circumferential seam) = \(\tau\tfrac\pi4d^2\); smaller \(p\) governs.
Watch
inner diameter for the area; subtract any initial (pretension) stress; never round down; check both seams.
Answer skeleton · "why tighten the bolts initially?"
  1. Diagram: flange, gasket, cap, bolt (1 mark).
  2. Point 1: pretension squeezes the gasket so the joint stays sealed (no leak) when pressure pushes the cap off.
  3. Point 2: the flanges must not separate; if they did, the full pressure load would cycle in the bolts (fatigue) and the cap would knock.
  4. Point 3: with pretension, pressure mostly reduces the gasket compression instead of adding its whole load to the bolts.

Model answer (exam length): "The bolts are tightened first so the gasket is compressed. When the tank is pressurised the pressure force first relieves this compression, so the joint stays tight and does not leak, the flanges do not separate, and the bolts do not see the full fluctuating load (less fatigue)."

Concept: Notes §9 · bolts, rivets and joints · burst mode: §3.

Ready answer · fasteners \[\begin{aligned}n&\ge\frac{p\,\tfrac\pi4D^2}{(\sigma_{\rm allow}-\sigma_{\rm init})A_b},\\ \text{long. seam: }&p\,r\,s=\tau\tfrac\pi4d^2,\\ \text{circ. seam: }&\tfrac12p\,r\,s=\tau\tfrac\pi4d^2.\end{aligned}\]

In words: total pressure force on the cap ÷ what one bolt can still carry, rounded up; for rivets, the wall force arriving in one pitch must be carried by the one rivet in that pitch.

Dec 2014 A2(c)4 marks

Same tank as Dec 2014 A2 in S7.1.

(c) Why is it necessary to tighten the bolt initially? If the internal pressure is increased until the vessel burst, sketch the type of fracture that would occur.

Hint

Written part (skeleton above) + S7.1 fact: which stress is largest, and a crack opens perpendicular to it.

Solution
  1. Pretension: keeps the gasket compressed so the joint stays tight and leak-free; stops the flanges separating; the pressure then mainly relieves the gasket instead of loading the bolts fully (less fatigue).
  2. Fracture: \(\sigma_h=72\u{MPa}=2\sigma_l\), so the hoop stress reaches the strength first; the crack opens perpendicular to it and runs along the length (a longitudinal split).

S7.5Spherical vessel and end caps

S7.5Sphere / hemispherical cap1 sitting · 4 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Spherical end caps of thickness …: normal stress, maximum shear, increase in radius" (often beside an S7.1 cylinder for comparison).
Do
\(\sigma=pr/2t_{\rm cap}\) in every direction → \(\tau_{\rm abs}=\sigma/2\) (in-plane 0) → \(\varepsilon=\sigma(1-\nu)/E\) → \(\Delta r=\varepsilon r\).
Watch
use the cap's own thickness; asked for Δr, not Δd; the Poisson term is \((1-\nu)\) because both stresses are equal.

Concept: Notes §4 · spheres and end caps · §8 · where the cap meets the cylinder.

Ready answer · sphere \[\begin{aligned}\sigma&=\frac{pr}{2t},\qquad\tau_{\rm abs}=\frac{pr}{4t},\\ \Delta r&=\frac{pr^2(1-\nu)}{2tE}.\end{aligned}\]

In words: a sphere carries half the cylinder's hoop stress, the same in all directions, so it grows less.

S7.6Compound (shrink-fit) cylinder

Decision procedure
  1. If you see two tubes, "shrunk onto", "liner", "composite", "interface pressure" do treat it as statically indeterminate: two unknown hoop stresses, one equilibrium equation.
  2. Equilibrium: cut both tubes along a diameter: \(\sigma_1t_1+\sigma_2t_2=pr\) (per unit length).
  3. Compatibility: the tubes stay in contact, so equal hoop strains. Ends free (\(\sigma_z=0\)): \(\sigma_1/E_1=\sigma_2/E_2\). If the ends are closed, include the Poisson term of each tube's \(\sigma_l\).
  4. Solve; interface pressure from the outer tube: \(\bar p=\sigma_{\rm out}t_{\rm out}/r\). Check the inner tube with \((p-\bar p)r/t_{\rm in}\).
  5. If an interference \(\delta\) is given, add the fit stresses from \(\delta=\bar p r^2\left(\tfrac1{E_1t_1}+\tfrac1{E_2t_2}\right)\); "additional" stress means the pressure part only.

Cue: two materials, one radius. Traps: using \(pr/t\) for each tube separately; forgetting that the stiffer tube takes more stress; using \(\sigma_l=pr/2t\) when the ends slide.

S7.6Composite cylinder1 sitting · 10 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
Al tube shrunk on a steel tube, pressurised, ends free: "additional hoop stress in the aluminium?"
Do
equilibrium + equal strain (procedure above)
Watch
share is by \(E\times t\), not by thickness alone

Concept: Notes §10 · composite and shrink-fit cylinders.

Ready answer · two thin tubes, ends free \[\sigma_i=\frac{E_i\,pr}{E_1t_1+E_2t_2}\]

In words: each tube's hoop stress is its modulus times \(pr\), divided by the sum of "modulus × thickness".

Dec 2017 A3(b)10 marks

An aluminum-alloy tube is shrunk onto a steel tube to form the pressure vessel as shown in the following figure. The wall thickness of each tube is 4 mm. The average diameter of the assembly to be used in calculations is 400 mm. If the composite tube is pressurized at 2 MPa, what additional hoop stress develops in the aluminum tube? Assume that the ends of the tube can freely expand, preventing the development of longitudinal stresses, i.e., \(\sigma_z=0\). Let \(E_{al}=70\times10^3\u{MPa}\) and \(E_{st}=200\times10^3\u{MPa}\). Hint: the interface pressure, say \(\bar p\), between the two materials acts to cause hoop tension in the outer tube and hoop compression in the inner tube.

(a) simply supported beam deflection → S5.2

Hint

Type S7.6. First step: \(r=200\u{mm}\); write the equilibrium of a half-ring of both tubes.

Solution
  1. Equilibrium: \(4\sigma_{al}+4\sigma_{st}=2(200)=400\u{N/mm}\).
  2. Compatibility (\(\sigma_z=0\)): \(\dfrac{\sigma_{al}}{70\,000}=\dfrac{\sigma_{st}}{200\,000}\Rightarrow\sigma_{st}=\tfrac{20}{7}\sigma_{al}\).
  3. \(4\sigma_{al}\left(1+\tfrac{20}{7}\right)=400\Rightarrow\sigma_{al}=25.9\u{MPa}\); \(\sigma_{st}=74.1\u{MPa}\).
  4. \(\bar p=25.9(4)/200=0.519\u{MPa}\); check steel: \((2-0.519)(200)/4=74.1\u{MPa}\) ✓.
Marker note

Half the marks are for the compatibility equation; equal moduli would give 50 MPa each, a quick sense check.

\(\boxed{\sigma_{al}=25.9\u{MPa}\ (\text{steel }74.1,\ \bar p=0.519\u{MPa})}\)

✓Last-minute checklist

  • Radius in \(pr/t\), never the diameter; with an outside diameter use \(r_i=D_o/2-t\) and say so.
  • \(E\) in MPa (200 GPa = 200 000 MPa); stresses in MPa, lengths in mm.
  • \(\varepsilon_h=(\sigma_h-\nu\sigma_l)/E\) and \(\Delta d=\varepsilon_hd\): the Poisson term carries most of the marks.
  • "Maximum shear": give in-plane \(pr/4t\) and absolute \(pr/2t\) (sphere: 0 and \(pr/4t\)), labelled.
  • Weld at β to a transverse plane: start at the axial point, turn \(2\beta\); \(\sigma_w=\sigma_l\cos^2\beta+\sigma_h\sin^2\beta\).
  • Bolts: pressure × inside area ÷ (available stress × bolt area), round up; subtract pretension if stated.
  • Rivets: check both seams (\(prs\) and \(\tfrac12prs\) per pitch); the smaller pressure wins.
  • Composite tubes: equilibrium \(\sum\sigma t=pr\) + equal strains; the stiffer tube takes more.
