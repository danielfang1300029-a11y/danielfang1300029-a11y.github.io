S1 exam guide · Basic concepts

What the exam asks from this chapter, how to recognise each question type in seconds, and every past-paper part (2013–2024) solved once. Concepts are not re-taught here: each type links back to the notes.

0How this chapter is examined

Verdict: predictable. S1 never gets a whole question. It supplies the short opening parts (2–6 marks each) of Section A, almost always in A1, sometimes as the "reactions" line of A2. Every sitting from 2014 to 2024 had 5–9 marks of S1, and the same parts are reused word for word: Dec 2023 A1(a) was also set in Dec 2022 and Dec 2017. These are the cheapest marks on the paper.

Exam format

3 h · attempt any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics), the rule used from 2014 to 2024 · about 36 min per question, so about 1.8 min per mark. May 2013 (MECH1013/MECH2413) was "any five" with no section rule.

Formula sheet: no sitting has ever printed a formula sheet for Section A. Nothing in this chapter is given, so learn the reaction counts, \(x=r+v-3n\), the triangle resultant and centroid (one third of the base from the tall end) and \(x_c=\sum A_ix_i/\sum A_i\).

2026/27: orbital dynamics and re-entry have been removed from the dynamics part, and a control part (C1) has been added that has no past papers. How the 2026 paper will divide its questions between statics, dynamics and control has not been announced.

Sign convention (same as the notes)
  • \(x\) right, \(y\) up; a force component is positive along \(+x\) or \(+y\).
  • Moments in the plane are positive anticlockwise (↺).
  • Unknown reactions are drawn in an assumed direction; a negative answer means the real reaction points the other way. Say so in words in the final answer.

Colours in every diagram: applied loads red, support reactions teal, distributed loads and their resultants orange, resultants found in a solution green.

S1.1Support types and the reactions they provide

S1.1Sketch the support reactions4 sittings · 11 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Sketch the different types of support reactions" or "describe the constraints the wall imposes", with 1–3 small support drawings. 2–3 marks.
Do
For each support, ask which movements it stops (slide sideways, move up and down, rotate). Draw one teal arrow per stopped movement, labelled \(A_H\), \(A_V\), \(M_A\), and write the count.
Watch
A welded or built-in end is fixed: it gives a moment as well. A pin gives no moment. A roller gives one force only, perpendicular to its surface.

Concept: Notes §10 · Supports and reactions.

Ready answer · reaction count
SupportStopsReactions
roller, ball, rockermotion into the surface1 force \(\perp\) surface
strut / link (pinned both ends)motion along the link1 force along the link
pin (hinge)both translations2 forces \(A_H, A_V\)
sliding sleeve / guideone translation + rotation1 force + \(M_A\)
fixed (built-in, clamped, welded)both translations + rotation\(A_H, A_V, M_A\)
Dec 2024 A2(a)3 marks

The cantilever is clamped on the wall at the left end with the free end on the right-hand side, which is subjected to a concentrated force \(F_1\) as shown in Figure A2. The cross-section is a rectangle of thickness \(t\) and width \(w\).

(a) Sketch the reactions provided by the left walls.

(b) → S2.1 · (c) → S5.1 · (d) → S4.1 · (e) → S5.1

Hint

Type S1.1. First step: name the support (clamped = fixed) and list the movements it prevents.

Solution
  1. Clamped wall = fixed support: it stops horizontal motion, vertical motion and rotation, so it gives three reactions \(A_H\), \(A_V\), \(M_A\) (drawn teal above).
  2. Values (not asked, but they feed (b)): \(\sum F_x=0\Rightarrow A_H=0\); \(\sum F_y=0\Rightarrow A_V=F_1\) ↑; \(\sum M^{(A)}=0\Rightarrow M_A-F_1L=0\).
Marker note

The sketch must show the moment \(M_A\). Two forces only is the "pin" answer and loses the mark.

\(\boxed{A_H=0,\ \ A_V=F_1\uparrow,\ \ M_A=F_1L\ \circlearrowleft}\)

Dec 2022 A1(a)3 marksalso Dec 2016 A1(a)

Sketch the different types of support reactions in the following systems.

(a) beam end on a pin support; (b) beam end built into a wall; (c) beam end welded to a wall plate with fillet welds.

(b) → S3.1 · (c) → S4.1 · (d) → S1.2 (solved under Dec 2023 A1(a))

Hint

Type S1.1. First step: decide for each one whether the beam end can rotate.

Solution
  1. (a) Pin: the end cannot move but can rotate → two forces \(A_H\), \(A_V\), no moment.
  2. (b) Built into the wall: no movement, no rotation → \(A_H\), \(A_V\) and a couple \(M_A\).
  3. (c) Welded to a wall plate: the welds stop rotation too, so this is also a fixed support → \(A_H\), \(A_V\), \(M_A\).

\(\boxed{\text{(a) }2\text{ forces;}\ \ \text{(b), (c) }2\text{ forces}+1\text{ moment}}\)

May 2014 A1(a)2 marks

Figure A1 shows a 6 m long cantilever beam, which is subjected to a distributed load of 10 kN/m over 2 m of the beam and a point load of 10 kN at \(B\). A moment of 20 kN·m is applied to \(C\).

The 10 kN force is read as acting down and to the left at 30° to the vertical, and the 20 kN·m couple at \(C\) as clockwise (from the drawn arrows).

(a) How would you describe the mechanical constraints in terms of forces and moments imposed by the wall on the cantilever beam?

(b) → S1.3 · (c), (d) → S2.1

Hint

Type S1.1. First step: what kind of support is the wall, and how many independent movements does a body in a plane have?

Solution
  1. The wall is a fixed (built-in) support: the beam end cannot translate in \(x\) or \(y\) and cannot rotate.
  2. So the wall applies a horizontal force \(A_H\), a vertical force \(A_V\) and a couple \(M_A\): three reactions, which is exactly the three equilibrium equations, so the beam is statically determinate.

\(\boxed{\text{Fixed support: } A_H,\ A_V \text{ and a moment } M_A}\)

S1.2FBD and static determinacy

S1.2Draw the FBD, then classify4 sittings · 21 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
Three or four small systems (beam on several supports, block on links, propped cantilever). "Draw the FBD … determine if statically determinate; if not, give the degree." 3–6 marks.
Do
Remove every support and draw its reactions (S1.1 table). Count \(r\); for several connected bodies count \(v\) and \(n\); \(x=r+v-3n\). Then check the arrangement: not all parallel, not all through one point.
Watch
Count \(x=0\) is necessary but not enough: three parallel or three concurrent reactions still move. A link pinned at both ends is a strut: one force, not two.

Concept: Notes §2 · Free-body diagrams · §11 · Determinate or not · §12 · Multi-part structures.

Ready answer · classification

\[x=r+v-3n\]

In words: unknown reactions (support \(r\) + connection \(v\)) minus three equations per rigid body.

\(x\lt0\) or badly arranged: movable (a mechanism) · \(x=0\) and well arranged: statically determinate · \(x\gt0\): indeterminate of degree \(x\). One beam: \(x=r-3\).

Dec 2023 A1(a)6 marksalso Dec 2022 A1(d), Dec 2017 A1(c)

Draw the free body diagram for the following systems. Determine if they are statically determinate; if not, give the degree of static indeterminacy.

(a) beam on a pin at the left end and three rollers, loads \(F_1\), \(F_2\); (b) rigid block on two vertical legs \(A\), \(B\) pinned at both ends, with an inclined link \(C\) to the ceiling, loads \(F_1\) (inclined) and \(F_2\); (c) beam fixed at \(A\), roller at \(B\), triangular load rising to \(q_0\), couple \(M_0\) at the free end.

Dec 2022: (a) → S1.1 · (b) → S3.1 · (c) → S4.1. Dec 2023: (b) → S3.1 · (c) → S7.1. Dec 2017: (a) → S2.1 · (b) → S2.3 · (d) → S4.1.

The three inner and right supports in (a) are read as rollers (triangle on a double line), as in the notes.

Hint

Type S1.2. First step: replace each support by its reactions (pin 2, roller 1, fixed 3, pinned link 1) and count.

Solution
  1. (a) Pin 2 + three rollers 1 each: \(r=5\). One body: \(x=5-3=2\).
  2. (b) Each leg and the link are pinned at both ends, so each is a strut carrying one force along itself: \(r=3\). The lines of action of \(A\) and \(B\) are parallel and \(C\) is inclined, so they are neither all parallel nor all through one point: \(x=0\), well arranged.
  3. (c) Fixed support 3 + roller 1: \(r=4\), \(x=4-3=1\).
Marker note

Each FBD must show the loads too, with the supports removed. A diagram that still shows the support symbols is not a free-body diagram.

\(\boxed{\text{(a) indeterminate, degree }2;\ \text{(b) determinate;}\ \text{(c) indeterminate, degree }1}\)

Dec 2015 A1(b)3 marks

Give the number of support reactions in all of the following systems. Give the degree of static indeterminacy for system (C).

(A) beam built into a wall; (B) beam end on a roller; (C) beam fixed at the left wall and on a roller near the right end; (D) a short bar on the apex of an A-frame of two inclined bars standing on the ground.

(a) → S1.5 (solved under May 2019 A1(c)) · (c), (d) → S5.3 · (e) → S2.4

(D): the connections are not drawn clearly. Read as two bars pinned together at the apex and pinned to the ground at both feet (as RESOLVED.md). If the feet were on rollers the count would change.

Hint

Type S1.2. First step: use the S1.1 table for each support; for (C) compare the count with 3.

Solution
  1. (A) fixed: 3. (B) roller: 1. (C) fixed + roller: \(3+1=4\). (D) two pinned feet: \(2+2=4\).
  2. (C) is one body: \(x=r-3=4-3=1\).
  3. (D), for completeness: two bars \(n=2\), apex hinge \(v=2\), \(x=4+2-6=0\), determinate (a three-hinged frame).

\(\boxed{(A)\ 3,\ (B)\ 1,\ (C)\ 4,\ (D)\ 4;\ \ (C)\text{ indeterminate, degree }1}\)

S1.3Support reactions from equilibrium

S1.3Find the reactions3 sittings · 12 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
The first part of a beam question: "draw the FBD and determine the reactions at A (and C)" or "describe and determine the reactions at the wall". 2–5 marks, always followed by SFD/BMD or deflection parts.
Do
FBD with assumed reaction directions → replace each distributed load by its resultant (S1.5) → \(\sum M=0\) about the point with the most unknowns → \(\sum F_y=0\), \(\sum F_x=0\) → check with moments about a second point.
Watch
Inclined loads have a horizontal component (so \(A_x\ne0\)). Applied couples add straight into \(\sum M\) with no lever arm. A load on a bracket acts at the bracket tip, not on the beam axis.

Concept: Notes §6 · Equilibrium in a plane (worked examples 1, 2).

Ready answer · cantilever wall

For any cantilever with only vertical loads and couples:

\[\begin{aligned}A_V&=\textstyle\sum(\text{loads, down }+)\\ M_A&=\textstyle\sum(\text{load}\times\text{distance from }A)\\&\quad+\textstyle\sum(\text{cw couples})\\&\quad-\textstyle\sum(\text{acw couples})\end{aligned}\]

\(M_A\) here is anticlockwise on the beam for a wall at the left end.

In words: the wall carries all the load, and its moment cancels the turning effect of every load about the wall.

Dec 2018 A2(b)3 marks

The cantilever in Figure A2 is subjected to a distributed load of constant force \(q\) per unit length. At the free end, it is supported by a point force \(P\).

(b) Describe and determine the reactions provided by the wall at the fixed end of the cantilever.

(a) → S2.2 · (c), (d), (e) → S5.1

Hint

Type S1.3. First step: replace \(q\) by its resultant \(qL\) at mid-length, then take moments about the wall.

Solution
  1. The wall is fixed: horizontal force \(H\), vertical force \(R\) (↑ assumed), couple \(M_w\) (↺ assumed).
  2. \(\sum F_x=0\Rightarrow H=0\). \(\sum F_y=0\Rightarrow R+P-qL=0\Rightarrow R=qL-P\).
  3. \(\sum M^{(\text{wall})}=0\) (↺ +): \(qL\) acts \(L/2\) to the left (↺ \(qL^2/2\)), \(P\) acts \(L\) to the left (↻ \(PL\)): \(M_w+\tfrac12qL^2-PL=0\).
  4. Check about the free end: \(M_w+RL-qL\cdot\tfrac L2=PL-\tfrac12qL^2+qL^2-PL-\tfrac12qL^2=0\). ✓
Marker note

"Describe" earns its mark separately: say the wall gives a force and a moment, then give their senses. With \(P=0\) the moment must reduce to the familiar \(\tfrac12qL^2\) clockwise.

\(\boxed{\begin{aligned}H&=0,\quad R=qL-P\ \uparrow\\ M_w&=PL-\tfrac12qL^2\ \circlearrowleft\end{aligned}}\)

Equivalent: \(M_w=\tfrac12qL^2-PL\) clockwise. With \(P=\tfrac38qL\) from part (d): \(R=\tfrac58qL\) ↑ and \(M_w=\tfrac18qL^2\) clockwise, as in the S5 note.

Dec 2014 A1(a)5 marks

Figure A1 shows a 5.5 m long cantilever beam, which is subjected to a triangular distributed load of \(q(x)\) with \(q_0=10\u{kN/m}\) over 1.5 m of the beam between \(CD\) and a point load of \(40\u{kN}\) at \(E\).

(a) Draw the free-body diagram for the system and determine the reaction at \(A\) and \(C\).

(b) → S2.4 · (c) → S5.4

The paper calls it a "cantilever", but the figure shows a pin at \(A\) and a roller at \(C\) with an overhang \(CD\); the figure is used. \(E\) is read as 0.5 m to the right of \(B\) on a rigid bracket (as in the notes).

Hint

Type S1.3. First step: replace the triangle by its resultant, then take moments about \(A\).

Solution
  1. Triangle: \(R=\tfrac12(10\u{kN/m})(1.5\u{m})=7.5\u{kN}\), one third of the base from the tall end \(D\): \(x=4+\tfrac23(1.5)=5.0\u{m}\).
  2. The 40 kN acts at \(E\), \(x=2.5\u{m}\) (equivalently 40 kN at \(B\) plus a 20 kN·m clockwise couple).
  3. \(\sum M^{(A)}=0\) (↺ +): \(4C_y-40(2.5)-7.5(5.0)=0\Rightarrow C_y=137.5/4=34.375\u{kN}\).
  4. \(\sum F_y=0\): \(A_y=40+7.5-34.375=13.125\u{kN}\). \(\sum F_x=0\): \(A_x=0\).
  5. Check about \(C\): \(-4(13.125)+40(1.5)-7.5(1.0)=0\). ✓

\(\boxed{A_x=0,\ \ A_y=13.1\u{kN}\uparrow,\ \ C_y=34.4\u{kN}\uparrow}\)

May 2014 A1(b)4 marks

Same beam as May 2014 A1(a) above (6 m cantilever, wall at \(A\); 10 kN at \(B\), 30° to the vertical; 20 kN·m at \(C\); 10 kN/m on \(CD\)).

(b) Draw the free body diagram for the system and determine the reaction at \(A\).

(a) → S1.1 · (c), (d) → S2.1

Hint

Type S1.3. First step: split the 10 kN into components and replace the 10 kN/m by one force.

Solution
  1. Force at \(B\): \(10\cos30^\circ=8.66\u{kN}\) down, \(10\sin30^\circ=5.00\u{kN}\) to the left. UDL: \(10\times2=20\u{kN}\) down at \(x=5\u{m}\).
  2. \(\sum F_x=0\): \(A_x-5.00=0\Rightarrow A_x=5.00\u{kN}\) →.
  3. \(\sum F_y=0\): \(A_y=8.66+20=28.66\u{kN}\) ↑.
  4. \(\sum M^{(A)}=0\) (↺ +): \(M_A-8.66(2)-20(5)-20=0\Rightarrow M_A=137.3\u{kN\,m}\). The horizontal component acts along the beam axis through \(A\): no moment.
  5. Check about \(D\): \(137.3-6(28.66)+4(8.66)+1(20)-20=0\). ✓

\(\boxed{A_x=5.00\u{kN}\to,\ A_y=28.7\u{kN}\uparrow,\ M_A=137\u{kN\,m}\ \circlearrowleft}\)

S1.4Centre of gravity of a composite area

S1.4Centroid of a C, U or L section3 sittings · 14 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Calculate the center of gravity (center of mass) of the following C / L beam" with a section made of 2–3 rectangles and bare numbers. 4–5 marks.
Do
Put the origin at the bottom-left corner. Split into non-overlapping rectangles; tabulate \(A_i\), \(x_i\), \(y_i\); \(x_c=\sum A_ix_i/\sum A_i\), \(y_c=\sum A_iy_i/\sum A_i\). Use symmetry for one coordinate.
Watch
Overlapping rectangles count the corner twice. State the origin. If no units are printed, say which you assume.

Concept: Notes §8 · Centre of gravity, centroid, composite areas.

Ready answer · composite centroid

\[x_c=\frac{\sum A_ix_i}{\sum A_i},\qquad y_c=\frac{\sum A_iy_i}{\sum A_i}\]

In words: the centroid is the area-weighted average of the centroids of the pieces.

A cut-out may be entered as a negative area. Symmetric section: the centroid lies on the axis of symmetry.

Dec 2024 A1(b)5 marks

Determine the center of gravity of a C beam.

(a) → S2.2 · (c) → S3.1

No units are printed; dimensions taken as mm. The shape is drawn as a U (channel opening upward), as in the notes.

Hint

Type S1.4. First step: symmetry gives \(x_c\); split into the bottom bar and two uprights standing on it.

Solution
  1. Symmetric about \(x=12.5\): \(x_c=12.5\u{mm}\).
  2. ① bottom bar \(25\times3=75\u{mm^2}\) at \(y=1.5\u{mm}\); ②, ③ uprights \(5\times15=75\u{mm^2}\) each at \(y=3+7.5=10.5\u{mm}\).
  3. \(y_c=\dfrac{75(1.5)+2(75)(10.5)}{225}=\dfrac{1687.5}{225}=7.5\u{mm}\).
  4. Check with negative area: \(\dfrac{450(9)-225(10.5)}{450-225}=7.5\u{mm}\). ✓
Marker note

Uprights of full height 18 overlap the bar: that counts the corners twice and gives the wrong \(y_c\).

\(\boxed{(x_c,\ y_c)=(12.5\u{mm},\ 7.5\u{mm})}\)

Dec 2021 A1(a)5 marksalso in May 2019 A1(c), Dec 2015 A1(a)

Calculate the center of mass of the following beam using the dimensions given in the figure.

(b) → S4.1 · (c) → S2.1

No units are printed; answer in the drawing's units. Origin at the bottom-left corner of the web.

Type S1.4. First step: horizontal symmetry axis gives \(y_c\); split into the web and two flanges.

Solution

  2. ① web \(3\times11=33\) at \(x=1.5\); ②, ③ flanges \(8\times3=24\) each at \(x=3+4=7\). Total 81.

  4. Check (\(11\times11\) square minus the \(8\times5\) gap at \(x=7\)): \(\dfrac{121(5.5)-40(7)}{81}=4.76\). ✓

Given that gravity points downward, calculate the center of gravity of the following "L" beam using the dimensions given in the figure.

(a) → S1.1 (solved under Dec 2022 A1(a)) · (b) → S2.1 · (d) → S4.1

S1.5Resultant and centre of force of a distributed load

S1.5Replace a distributed load by one force3 sittings · 15 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
"Calculate the magnitude and location of the resultant force" or "the center of force" of a triangular load \(q(x)\), given as numbers or as \(q_0x/l\). 4–6 marks.
Do
Magnitude = area under \(q(x)\); position = centroid of that area. For a general \(q(x)\): \(R=\int q\,dx\), \(x_c=\int xq\,dx/R\).
Watch
The resultant of a triangle sits one third of the base from the tall end, two thirds from the zero end. Say which end you measure from.

Concept: Notes §7 · Centre of force, distributed loads.

Ready answer · common load shapes (length \(l\))
Shape\(R\)acts at
uniform \(q\)\(ql\)\(l/2\)
triangle, 0 → \(q_0\)\(\tfrac12q_0l\)\(\tfrac23l\) from the zero end
trapezoid \(q_1\) → \(q_2\)\(\tfrac12(q_1+q_2)l\)\(\dfrac{l(q_1+2q_2)}{3(q_1+q_2)}\) from the \(q_1\) end

In words: the resultant equals the area of the load diagram and passes through that area's centroid.

May 2019 A1(c)6 marksalso Dec 2015 A1(a) · 4 marks

Calculate the center of gravity of the following "C" beam using the dimensions given in the figure. Calculate the center of force of the continuously distributed load \(q(x)=q_0\,x/l\) as shown below.

May 2019: (a) → S2.1 · (b) → S2.4 · (d) → S2.3. The "C" beam half is the same section as Dec 2021 A1(a): S1.4.

Hint

Type S1.5 (plus S1.4 for the C beam). First step: write \(R=\int_0^l q(x)\,dx\).

Solution
  1. C beam (from Dec 2021 A1(a), S1.4): \((x_c,y_c)=(4.76,\ 5.5)\) from the bottom-left corner.
  2. \(R=\displaystyle\int_0^l\frac{q_0x}{l}\,dx=\frac{q_0l}{2}\).
  3. \(\displaystyle\int_0^l x\,\frac{q_0x}{l}\,dx=\frac{q_0l^2}{3}\Rightarrow x_c=\frac{q_0l^2/3}{q_0l/2}=\frac{2l}{3}\).
  4. Check: the triangle rule (one third of the base from the tall end) gives \(l-\tfrac l3=\tfrac{2l}{3}\). ✓

\(\boxed{R=\tfrac12q_0l\ \downarrow\ \text{at}\ x_c=\tfrac23l\ \text{from the left support}}\)

Dec 2018 A1(a)5 marks

Calculate the magnitude and location of the resultant force of the triangular distributed load shown in Figure A1(a).

(b) → S5.3 · (c) → S3.4

Hint

Type S1.5. First step: area of the triangle; where is its tall end?

Solution
  1. \(R=\tfrac12(5\u{kN/m})(4\u{m})=10\u{kN}\), downward.
  2. The tall end is the left end, so \(R\) acts one third of 4 m from it: \(x=\tfrac43=1.33\u{m}\).

✓Last-minute checklist

  • Reactions: roller 1, link 1, pin 2, fixed (built-in, welded) 3 including a moment.
  • \(x=r+v-3n\); then check the arrangement (all parallel or all through one point = movable).
  • FBD = supports removed, every load and reaction drawn, assumed directions labelled.
  • Take moments about the support with the most unknowns; check about a second point.
  • Inclined loads: resolve into components; the horizontal one usually makes \(A_x\ne0\).
  • Triangle load: \(R=\tfrac12q_0l\), at one third of the base from the tall end.
  • Composite centroid: non-overlapping pieces, state the origin, use symmetry, state units.
  • No formula sheet for Section A: all of the above must be memorised.
