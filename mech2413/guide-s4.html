S4 exam guide · Bending of beams

0How this chapter is examined

Verdict: predictable, and cheap marks. S4 never gets a whole question. It turns up as one short part (3–5 marks) inside Question A1 or A2, in five of the eleven sittings from 2014 to 2024. Two shapes only: compute a second moment or product of inertia of a simple section (rectangle, circle, I-profile, L), or explain/derive the neutral surface and the bending-moment integral. Total over all sittings: 7 parts, 28 marks. The same results (\(bh^3/12\), \(\pi R^4/4\), \(\sigma=Mz/I\)) are then reused in S5 deflection questions.

Exam format

3 h · attempt any five of six 20-mark questions · at least two from each section (A statics, B dynamics) · about 36 min per question, so a 4-mark S4 part is worth about 7 minutes. Formula sheet: Section A has never had one. Nothing in this chapter is given: memorise \(bh^3/12\), \(\pi R^4/4\), the parallel-axis theorem \(I=\bar I+Ad^2\), the product rule \(I_{yz}=\sum(\bar I_{yz}+A\bar y\bar z)\) and \(\sigma=Mz/I\). 2026/27: orbits and re-entry were removed from Section B and a control part (C1) was added; how the 2026 paper is laid out has not been announced, so assume the statics questions keep their usual style.

Sign convention (same as the notes)

Side view: \(x\) along the beam, \(z\) down, \(y\) out of the page. Section view (looking along \(x\)): \(y\) to the left, \(z\) down, origin at the centroid \(C\). Normal stress \(\sigma\) positive in tension; bending moment \(M\) positive when it sags the beam (bottom stretched). Then \(\sigma=Mz/I_y\): positive \(z\) (below the neutral axis) is in tension when \(M\gt 0\). The exam calls the second moment of area "rectangular moment of inertia"; it is a property of the area, units \(\mathrm{mm^4}\) or \(\mathrm{m^4}\).

S4.1Second moment / product of inertia of a section

S4.1Second moment or product of inertia of a cross-section5 sittings · 20 marks (2014–2024)
Looks like
a sketch of a section (rectangle, circle, I-profile, L) with \(y\)–\(z\) or \(x\)–\(y\) axes and "calculate the (rectangular) moment of inertia about …" or "the product of inertia"; 3–5 marks.
Do
(1) name the axis and the strip: for \(I_y\) use strips parallel to \(y\), \(\mathrm dA=b\,\mathrm dz\), and integrate \(z^2\,\mathrm dA\) (show this once if the part is worth 5 marks); (2) split built-up shapes into rectangles; (3) each piece: \(\bar I\) about its own centroid, plus \(A d^2\) to shift it; (4) add. Product: \(I_{yz}=\sum(\bar I_{yz}+A\,\bar y\,\bar z)\) with \(\bar I_{yz}=0\) for every rectangle.
Watch
the dimension across the axis is the one cubed (\(I_y=bh^3/12\) with \(h\) vertical when \(y\) is horizontal); "about z" in a y-left/z-down figure means the vertical axis; any symmetry axis makes the product of inertia zero; the sign of \(I_{xy}\) comes from the quadrant of each piece's centroid.

Concept: Notes §8 · Second moments, §11 · rectangle, §12 · circle, §13 · parallel axes and built-up sections.

Ready answers · recurring forms

Rectangle \(b\) wide, \(h\) high, axes through \(C\):

\[I_y=\frac{bh^3}{12},\qquad I_z=\frac{hb^3}{12},\qquad I_{yz}=0\]

Solid circle, radius \(R\) (diameter \(d\)), any centroidal axis:

\[I_y=I_z=\frac{\pi R^4}{4}=\frac{\pi d^4}{64},\qquad J=\frac{\pi R^4}{2}\]

Parallel-axis shift by a distance \(d\) (axis parallel to the centroidal one):

\[I=\bar I+Ad^2,\qquad I_{yz}=\bar I_{yz}+A\,\bar y\,\bar z\]

In words: the second moment about any axis is the value about the parallel centroidal axis plus area times the square of the gap.

Rectangle with one corner at the origin, sides along the axes (\(b\) along \(x\), \(h\) along \(y\)): \(I_{xy}=b^2h^2/4\).

Dec 2024 A2(d)3 marks

The cantilever is clamped on the wall at the left end with the free end on the right-hand side, which is subjected to a concentrated force \(F_1\) as shown in Figure A2.

(a) Sketch the reactions provided by the left walls. → S1.1 · (b) Shear force and bending moment diagrams. → S2.1 · (c) Deflection as a function of \(x\). → S5.1 · (e) Maximum deflection, 150 cm × 1 cm × 1 cm, \(F_1=100\u{N}\), \(E=190\u{GPa}\). → S5.1

(d) Determine the moment of inertia, in terms of \(w\) and \(t\).

Hint

Type S4.1. The beam bends about the horizontal axis through the centroid of the \(w\times t\) section; decide which of \(w\), \(t\) runs across that axis.

Solution
  1. The load \(F_1\) is vertical, so the beam bends about the horizontal centroidal axis \(y\) (width \(w\) along it, thickness \(t\) across it).
  2. Horizontal strip at depth \(z\): \(\mathrm dA=w\,\mathrm dz\), \(-t/2\le z\le t/2\).
  3. \(I=\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}z^2\,w\,\mathrm dz=\dfrac{w z^3}{3}\Big|_{-t/2}^{t/2}=\dfrac{wt^3}{12}\).

\(\boxed{I=\dfrac{wt^3}{12}}\) about the horizontal neutral axis. For part (e), \(w=t=1\u{cm}\): \(I=\dfrac{(0.01)^4}{12}=8.33\times10^{-10}\u{m^4}\).

Marker note

\(tw^3/12\) (the wrong dimension cubed) scores nothing and spoils (e). The thickness \(t\) is the dimension in the plane of bending.

Dec 2022 A1(c)4 marks

(a) Support reactions. → S1.1 · (b) Principal stresses. → S3.1 · (d) FBD and determinacy (same as Dec 2023 A1(a)). → S1.2

(c) Determine the product of inertia of the shaded area about the x-y axes.

Read from Figure A1(c) (as in the notes): top bar 120 mm × 30 mm (90 + 30) at \(60\le y\le 90\) mm; right leg 30 mm × 60 mm at \(90\le x\le 120\) mm, \(0\le y\le 60\) mm; origin at the bottom-left corner of the 120 × 90 box.

Hint

Type S4.1. Split into two rectangles that do not overlap. Each has zero product of inertia about its own centroid, so only the \(A\,\bar x\,\bar y\) terms survive. First: list \(A\), \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\) for each piece.

Solution
  1. Top bar: \(A_1=120\times30=3600\u{mm^2}\), centroid \(\bar x_1=60\u{mm}\), \(\bar y_1=75\u{mm}\).
  2. Right leg: \(A_2=30\times60=1800\u{mm^2}\), centroid \(\bar x_2=105\u{mm}\), \(\bar y_2=30\u{mm}\).
  3. \(I_{xy}=\sum\big(0+A_i\bar x_i\bar y_i\big)\): \[\begin{aligned}I_{xy}&=3600(60)(75)\\&\quad+1800(105)(30)\\&=16.20\times10^6+5.67\times10^6\end{aligned}\]

\(\boxed{I_{xy}=21.87\times10^6\u{mm^4}=2.19\times10^{-5}\u{m^4}}\) (positive: every piece lies in the first quadrant).

Check by integration over each rectangle, \(\int x\,\mathrm dA\int y\,\mathrm dA/(\text{width}\cdot\text{height})\): \(\tfrac{120^2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{90^2-60^2}{2}+\tfrac{120^2-90^2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{60^2}{2}=16.20\times10^6+5.67\times10^6\) ✓.

Dec 2021 A1(b)5 marks

(a) Centre of mass of a C-section. → S1.4 · (c)(i)–(iii) Cantilever with \(M_0=2lF\) and \(F\): FBD, \(V\), \(M\), diagrams. → S2.1

(b) Calculate the rectangular moment of inertia about y-axis using the dimensions shown in the following figure. Give the value of product of inertia \(I_{yz}\).

Hint

Type S4.1. Five marks: show the integral. Take a horizontal strip of width \(b\) and height \(\mathrm dz\) at depth \(z\). For \(I_{yz}\), look for a symmetry axis.

Solution
  1. Strip at depth \(z\): \(\mathrm dA=b\,\mathrm dz\), \(z\) from \(-h/2\) to \(h/2\).
  2. \(I_y=\displaystyle\int_A z^2\,\mathrm dA=\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}bz^2\,\mathrm dz=\frac{bz^3}{3}\Big|_{-h/2}^{h/2}=\frac{bh^3}{12}\).
  3. \(I_{yz}=\int_A yz\,\mathrm dA\). The \(z\)-axis is an axis of symmetry: every patch at \((y,z)\) has a twin at \((-y,z)\) and the two cancel.

\(\boxed{I_y=\dfrac{bh^3}{12},\quad I_{yz}=0}\)

Marker note

"By symmetry" earns the \(I_{yz}\) mark only if you say which axis is the symmetry axis (either one is enough).

Dec 2017 A1(d)4 marks

(a) FBD and reactions, cantilever with \(M_0=2lF\) (subset of Dec 2021 A1(c)). → S2.1 · (b) Integral method, \(q=\cos(\pi x/l)\). → S2.3 · (c) FBD and determinacy (same as Dec 2023 A1(a)). → S1.2

(d) Given the dimensions of the following I-profile, calculate the rectangular moment of inertia about z-axis.

Read as in the notes: each \(h/2\) runs from the centroid to the inner face of a flange (so \(h\) is the clear web height); flanges \(b\times t\), web thickness \(d\). "About the z-axis" (vertical) is answered as asked; \(I_y\), the usual bending value, is given too in case the setter meant that.

Hint

Type S4.1. Three rectangles. About the vertical \(z\)-axis every piece is centred on the axis, so there are no \(Ad^2\) terms; for each piece cube the dimension that runs across the \(z\)-axis.

Solution
  1. Each flange: \(t\) high, \(b\) wide across the \(z\)-axis, centroid on the axis: \(tb^3/12\).
  2. Web: \(h\) high, \(d\) wide across the axis, centroid on the axis: \(hd^3/12\).
  3. Add: \(I_z=2\cdot\dfrac{tb^3}{12}+\dfrac{hd^3}{12}\).
  4. (If \(I_y\) was meant.) Flange centroids are \((h+t)/2\) from \(y\): \[\begin{aligned}I_y&=2\Big[\frac{bt^3}{12}+bt\Big(\frac{h+t}{2}\Big)^2\Big]+\frac{dh^3}{12}\\&=\frac{b(h+2t)^3-(b-d)h^3}{12}\end{aligned}\]

\(\boxed{I_z=\dfrac{tb^3}{6}+\dfrac{hd^3}{12}}\)

Marker note

For \(I_y\) the flange \(Ad^2\) terms carry almost all of the answer; forgetting them is the usual way to lose the marks. The "big box minus two side gaps" form is a quick check.

Dec 2016 A1(d)4 marks

(a) Support reactions (same as Dec 2022 A1(a)). → S1.1 · (b) FBD, \(V\), \(M\) for the cantilever with \(M_0=2lF\) (same as Dec 2021 A1(c)). → S2.1 · (c) Centre of gravity of an L beam. → S1.4

(d) Given the radius (\(R\)) of the following circular area, calculate the rectangular moment of inertia about y-axis.

Hint

Type S4.1. Either integrate horizontal strips, or (faster) find the polar moment \(J=\int r^2\,\mathrm dA\) with thin rings \(\mathrm dA=2\pi r\,\mathrm dr\) and use \(J=I_y+I_z\) with \(I_y=I_z\) by symmetry.

Solution
  1. Thin ring of radius \(r\), width \(\mathrm dr\): \(\mathrm dA=2\pi r\,\mathrm dr\).
  2. \(J=\displaystyle\int_0^R r^2\,2\pi r\,\mathrm dr=\frac{\pi R^4}{2}\).
  3. \(r^2=y^2+z^2\), so \(J=I_y+I_z\); the circle looks the same about both axes, so \(I_y=I_z=J/2\).

\(\boxed{I_y=\dfrac{\pi R^4}{4}}\quad\big(=\pi d^4/64\big)\)

Strip route gives the same: \(I_y=\int_{-R}^{R}z^2\cdot2\sqrt{R^2-z^2}\,\mathrm dz=\pi R^4/4\).

S4.2Neutral axis and the statically equivalent moment

Answer skeleton · 4 marks · written

Two forms only: (A) "how to calculate a statically equivalent bending moment from \(\sigma=cz\)" (a derivation), (B) "explain the neutral plane before and after bending" (a description with sketches). 2 sittings · 8 marks (2014–2024)

  1. Sketch first (1 mark): (A) beam piece with the cut face, axes \(x,y,z\), the linear stress arrows (compression one side, tension the other) and \(M\); (B) a block before (straight, dashed neutral plane) and after (curved, top/bottom labelled tension/compression).
  2. (A) Force on a patch: \(\mathrm dF=\sigma\,\mathrm dA\); its moment about \(y\): \(\mathrm dM=z\,\sigma\,\mathrm dA\) (1 mark).
  3. (A) Integrate: \(M=\int_A z\sigma\,\mathrm dA=c\int_A z^2\,\mathrm dA=cI_y\) (1 mark).
  4. (A) Conclude: \(c=M/I_y\), \(\sigma=Mz/I_y\); and \(N=\int\sigma\,\mathrm dA=cQ_y=0\), so the axis passes through the centroid (1 mark).
  5. (B) Points to name: one side lengthens, the other shortens; a surface between keeps its length → zero strain and stress = neutral surface; flat plane before, arc of radius \(\rho\) after; passes through the centroid of each section; its trace on a section is the neutral axis, where \(\sigma=0\).

Model answers of exam length: in the two solutions below.

Concept: Notes §3 · Neutral surface, §5 · σ = cz and the equivalent moment, §6 · Bending formula.

Ready answer · recurring form \[\begin{aligned}N&=\int_A\sigma\,\mathrm dA=0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{N.A. through }C\\ M&=\int_A z\,\sigma\,\mathrm dA=cI_y\\ \sigma&=\frac{Mz}{I_y}\end{aligned}\]

In words: bending stress equals bending moment times distance from the neutral axis, divided by the second moment of area.

Dec 2021 A2(c)4 marks

(a) Shear-force and bending-moment boundary conditions for four supports. → S2.3 · (b)(i)–(iii) Cantilever with \(q_0\) on \(a\lt x\lt l\), integral method. → S2.3

(c) Given the normal stress distribution in the cross-sectional area, \(\sigma(z)=cz\), how to calculate a statically equivalent bending moment?

Hint

Type S4.2 (form A). Take a small patch \(\mathrm dA\) at depth \(z\) on the cut face: what force acts on it, and what is that force's moment about the \(y\)-axis?

Solution
  1. Free body: the beam piece to the left of the cut. On the cut face, a patch \(\mathrm dA\) at depth \(z\) carries the normal force \(\mathrm dF=\sigma\,\mathrm dA=cz\,\mathrm dA\) (along \(x\)).
  2. Its moment about the \(y\)-axis (lever arm \(z\)): \(\mathrm dM=z\,\mathrm dF=cz^2\,\mathrm dA\).
  3. "Statically equivalent" means the single moment \(M\) must have the same moment as all the patch forces together: \[M=\int_A z\,\sigma\,\mathrm dA=c\int_A z^2\,\mathrm dA=cI_y\]
  4. The same distribution must give no net axial force: \(N=\int_A cz\,\mathrm dA=0\Rightarrow\int_A z\,\mathrm dA=0\), so the \(y\)-axis (neutral axis) passes through the centroid.

\(\boxed{M=\int_A z\,\sigma\,\mathrm dA=cI_y\ \Rightarrow\ c=\dfrac{M}{I_y},\ \ \sigma=\dfrac{Mz}{I_y}}\)

Units: \(c\) in \(\mathrm{N/m^3}\), \(I_y\) in \(\mathrm{m^4}\), so \(cI_y\) in N·m ✓.

Dec 2016 A2(a)4 marks

(b)–(e) Stress element, Mohr's circle, principal stresses, rotated element. → S3.2

(a) Explain the neutral plane or surface in a beam before and after bending. Use sketches if necessary.

Hint

Type S4.2 (form B). Bend a rubber eraser in your mind: which face stretches, which squashes, and what lies in between? Then say where that in-between surface is.

Solution
  1. When a beam bends, the fibres on the convex side lengthen (tension) and those on the concave side shorten (compression).
  2. Between them is a surface whose fibres keep their original length: zero strain, so zero bending stress. This is the neutral surface.
  3. Before bending it is a flat plane through the beam (the neutral plane). After bending it is curved into an arc of radius \(\rho\); cross-sections stay plane and perpendicular to it, and strain grows linearly with distance \(z\) from it, \(\varepsilon=z/\rho\).
  4. Because the net axial force is zero (\(\int\sigma\,\mathrm dA=0\)), it passes through the centroid of every cross-section. Its line on a cross-section is the neutral axis, where \(\sigma=Mz/I=0\).

\(\boxed{\text{neutral surface: unstretched, zero stress, through the centroids; plane before, curved after}}\)

Marker note

Name both states ("before" and "after") and the centroid; a sketch with tension/compression labelled is the quickest way to collect the remaining mark.

✓Last-minute checklist

  • Cube the dimension that runs across the axis: \(I_y=bh^3/12\) with \(h\) vertical.
  • Circle: \(I=\pi R^4/4=\pi d^4/64\); polar \(J=\pi R^4/2\) (needed again in S6).
  • Built-up section: \(\bar I+Ad^2\) for every piece; the \(Ad^2\) of the flanges is most of an I-beam's \(I\).
  • Product of inertia: \(\sum A\bar x\bar y\) for rectangles; zero if either axis is a symmetry axis; sign from the quadrant.
  • Axes in the figure: \(y\) left, \(z\) down. "About \(z\)" means the vertical axis: read the question literally and say which axis you used.
  • Derivation: \(\mathrm dF=\sigma\,\mathrm dA\), \(\mathrm dM=z\,\mathrm dF\), \(M=cI\), \(\sigma=Mz/I\), and \(N=0\) puts the neutral axis through the centroid.
  • Keep units: \(\mathrm{mm^4}\) to \(\mathrm{m^4}\) is \(\times10^{-12}\).
