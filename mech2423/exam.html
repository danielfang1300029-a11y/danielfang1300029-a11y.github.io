Exam practice: the written test

Practice for the three parts of the test we know about: drawing a missing view (10 marks), finding the mistakes in a drawing (20 marks) and filling in the title block. Every drawing on this page was written for these notes, with full answers.

How to use this page
  1. Projection: copy each question onto squared paper and draw the answer before opening it. Mark yourself line by line: outline, every hidden line, every centre line.
  2. Find the mistakes: print the checklist. Work through each drawing with it, against the clock, then compare with the numbered answer.
  3. Title block: learn the nine items until you can write them from memory in under a minute.
  4. Night before: read the cram sheet only.
Timing

We don't know the length of the paper. Split your time in proportion to the marks: once you see the total marks and the duration on the day, work out minutes per mark. Example (an assumption, not the real paper): 100 marks in 2 hours is 1.2 minutes per mark, so about 12 min for projection and 24 min for find-the-mistakes. Fill in the title block first: it's quick, sure marks. In find-the-mistakes, sweep the drawing by category (title block, then views, then line types, sections, dimensions, threads) rather than staring at it. Each sweep finds errors the others miss.

1Projection: draw the missing view ★ 10 marks

The one-box method · full version in Ch 3 §7
  1. Read the symbol, then name every view from its position. 1st angle: top view below, right view on the left. 3rd angle: top above, right view on the right.
  2. Box the missing view: width from the front view's columns, height from its rows, depth from the other view (count squares, or use the 45° line).
  3. Find the object's front edge in the top and side views: in 3rd angle it faces the front view, in 1st angle it faces away.
  4. Outline (thick), then every edge: visible if nothing is in front of it, hidden (dashed) if material is.
  5. Centre lines for every hole: a cross on each circle, one line along the axis where the hole is seen side-on. Final check: every line lines up with something in the other views.

Eight problems, easiest first: four in third angle, four in first angle. One square is one unit. Each answer was generated from a single 3D model of the part and checked by computer: every line in every view lines up with the other views, and no other simple solid gives the same two given views.

Problem 1Third anglewarm-up
Given the FRONT and TOP views, draw the RIGHT view.
Hint

The top view has three lines inside it. The line at 2 is where the tall part ends. What makes the lines at 3 and 4? The front view shows a notch 1 deep. Does it run right through from front to back, and can you see it from the right?

Answer

From the right you see the end of the base (height 2) and, above it, the right face of the upright, both over the full depth: a rectangle 4 × 5 with a visible line at height 2. The groove (the notch between 3 and 4 in the front view) runs from front to back. It sits behind the right-hand end of the base, so its floor is a hidden line at height 1 across the full depth.

Problem 2First anglewarm-up
Given the FRONT and RIGHT views, draw the TOP view.
Hint

First angle: the top view goes below the front view, and its bottom edge is the object's front. The right view sits on the left, and its left edge is the front, so the step at its top left is a rebate along the front. The hidden lines in the two given views fix the hole in width and depth.

Answer

Outline 6 × 4. A visible line 1 square above the bottom edge: the rebate along the front top. A circle Ø1 with centre lines: 1.5 from the left (the front view's hidden lines at 1 and 2) and 2.5 from the front (the right view's hidden lines at depths 2 and 3), so 2.5 squares up from the bottom edge. Hidden lines at 3 and 5, full depth: the slot under the block, which the front view shows open at the bottom.

Problem 3Third anglestandard
Given the FRONT and TOP views and the pictorial, draw the RIGHT view.

Pictorial

Hint

The pictorial shows a back wall with a hole through it and a notch in the front edge of the base. In third angle the right view's left edge is the object's front. Which features are behind the right-hand end of the part?

Answer

An L: the base over the full depth up to height 2, and the back wall from depth 3 to 4 up to height 5, on the right (the back faces away from the front view). Hidden lines: the wall's hole seen side-on, at heights 3 and 4 between depths 3 and 4, with its axis at 3.5; the base hole at depths 1.5 and 2.5, height 0 to 2, with its axis; the back face of the front notch, at depth 1 from height 0 to 2. All are behind solid material when seen from the right.

Problem 4First anglestandard
Given the FRONT and TOP views and the pictorial, draw the LEFT view.

Pictorial

Hint

First angle: the view from the left goes on the right, and its right edge is the object's front. From the left you look straight at the tall upright. What is on its face, and what lies behind it?

Answer

Outline: depth 4 by height 6, with the 1 × 1 step at the top on the left (the back). The hole goes right through the upright, so it is a visible circle Ø1 at depth 2, height 4, with centre lines. Hidden lines, all behind the upright: the top of the base, at height 2 for depths 1 to 4, dropping to height 1 for depths 0 to 1 where the front rebate is; and the top of the rib at the far end, at height 3 across the full depth.

Problem 5Third anglestandard
Given the FRONT and RIGHT views, draw the TOP view.
Hint

The right view shows a step at the front top of the high block. The front view shows a counterbored hole (hidden, a wide shallow part over a narrow hole) in the low part and a groove under the high block. Which of these can you see from above?

Answer

Outline 6 × 4 with a visible line at 3, where the high block ends. A visible line 1 above the front (bottom) edge, across the high block: its front step. The counterbore: two visible circles, Ø2 and Ø1, centred 4.5 from the left and 2 from the front, with centre lines. From above you look straight into it. Hidden lines at 1 and 2, full depth: the groove under the high block.

Problem 6First angleharder
Given the TOP and RIGHT views, draw the FRONT view.
Hint

First angle: the top view is below the front view and the right view is on its left. The top view shows a slot open at the back and the hidden lines of a hole running front to back. The right view tells you the hole's height and the step on the right.

Answer

Outline 6 wide by 4 high, stepping down to 3 on the right from x = 4. A visible circle Ø1 at x = 1, height 1.5, with centre lines: the hole runs through from the front face to the back. Hidden lines at 2 and 3, full height: the slot at the back, behind the front face.

Problem 7Third angleharder
Given the FRONT and TOP views, draw the RIGHT view.
Hint

A bearing bracket: a base with two holes, a back plate with a large bore, and a groove under the base. The two base holes are the same distance from the front, so in the right view they fall on top of each other.

Answer

An L: the base 4 deep by 2 high, and the back plate from depth 3 to 4 up to height 6, on the right. Hidden lines: the Ø2 bore seen side-on, at heights 3 and 5 between depths 3 and 4, with its axis at height 4; the two base holes, which coincide, at depths 1 and 2 with one axis at 1.5; the groove under the base, a line at height 1 across the full depth.

Problem 8First anglehardest
Given the FRONT and RIGHT views, draw the TOP view.
Hint

First angle: the right view is on the left of the front view, with the object's front on its left edge. It shows a step at the front top, a hole running left to right, and a line at depth 3 from a notch at the back corner. The front view adds a channel underneath.

Answer

Outline 6 × 4 with the back right corner notched 1 × 1. That is the top right corner: in first angle the back is the top edge of the top view. A visible line 1 above the bottom edge: the front step. Hidden lines at depths 1.5 and 2.5 across the full width, with an axis at depth 2: the hole. Hidden lines at 2 and 4, full depth: the channel underneath.

Marking yourself

A typical scheme for a 10-mark view: outline in the right place and orientation (3–4), each internal visible line (about 1 each), each hidden line (about 1 each), centre lines (1), correct line types throughout (1). The commonest ways to lose marks: the view mirrored (front edge on the wrong side), a missing hidden line, a missing axis centre line on a side-on hole.

2Find the mistakes ★ 20 marks

The question gives a part drawing with planted errors. You circle each error and say what's wrong. Errors can come from any chapter: title block (Ch 2), views and line types (Ch 2–3), sections (Ch 6), dimensions (Ch 5), threads and gears (Ch 4). Sweep the drawing with the checklist, one category at a time.

The exam checklist (print this)

☐ 1 · Title block

  • ☐ All nine items: part name, DWG no., qty, material, sheet X of Y, drawn by + date, unit, scale, projection symbol
  • ☐ Symbol matches the layout (top view below = 1st angle)
  • ☐ Scale is a recommended one (1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, 2:1, 5:1 …)

☐ 2 · Views and projection

  • ☐ Each view in the right place for the angle; views line up
  • ☐ Side views not mirrored (front edge on the correct side)
  • ☐ Every hidden feature has hidden lines (except in sections)
  • ☐ Every circle has two centre lines crossing at short dashes, extending about 8 mm past the edge
  • ☐ Every side-on hole, shaft or boss has an axis centre line
  • ☐ Holes on a bolt circle sit on a chain-line PCD circle
  • ☐ No line across a smooth tangent join

☐ 3 · Line types

  • ☐ Visible outlines thick; everything else thin
  • ☐ Hidden = dashed; centre lines and pitch circles = chain
  • ☐ Cutting plane = chain, thick at the ends, with arrows + letters
  • ☐ Thick : thin at least 2 : 1

☐ 4 · Sections and hatching

  • ☐ Thin lines at 45°, evenly spaced, the same angle and spacing over one part
  • ☐ Hatching stops at the outline: no gaps, no overrun, never across a hole
  • ☐ Adjacent parts hatched in opposite directions
  • ☐ Not hatched when cut lengthwise: ribs, webs, spokes, gear teeth, shafts, bolts, nuts, keys, pins, balls
  • ☐ No hidden lines in the section; all visible edges behind the plane drawn
  • ☐ Section labelled (SECTION A–A) to match the cutting plane

☐ 5 · Dimensions (BS)

  • ☐ Each size given once: no repeats, no closed chains
  • ☐ Not to hidden lines; holes in their circular view
  • ☐ Ø for circles and cylinders; R only for arcs under 180°
  • ☐ Holes located from their centres; leaders aim through the centre
  • ☐ Text above the line, read from the bottom or right; no trailing zeros
  • ☐ Smaller dims nearer the view; dimension lines don't cross; small gap at extension lines
  • ☐ True size written, whatever the scale; values agree with the drawing

☐ 6 · Threads and gears

  • ☐ External thread: major Ø thick, minor Ø thin, thick thread-end line
  • ☐ Internal thread end view: minor Ø thick, major Ø thin ¾ circle
  • ☐ Thread designation M + Ø (× pitch)
  • ☐ Gear: tip circle thick, pitch circle thin chain, root circle omitted in end view; teeth unhatched
How these drawings are marked

Each drawing is a 20-mark question. For every error: 1 mark for circling the right place, the rest for saying what is wrong and what it should be (the rule). With 8 errors that's 2.5 marks each (1 + 1.5); with 10 errors, 2 each (1 + 1). Marking a correct feature as an error usually costs nothing but time. Only the planted errors are wrong: everything else on these drawings is drawn to the course's rules, so you can also use them as models.

Drawing 1 · Written for these notes · 20 marks
Support bracket, first angle: front view, top view and section A–A. Find the 8 errors (2.5 marks each: 1 for the place, 1.5 for the rule and correction).
Hint: where the errors are

Check the title block box by box, the section hatching, and every hole in every view. Two errors are in dimensions.

Answer: 8 errors, numbered
  1. Scale missing from the title block. Correct: Fill in SCALE 1 : 1. Rule: Scale is one of the nine required title-block items, and "scale must be used and stated on the drawing" (Ch2 sl. 31–32, 35). Ch 2 note
  2. Wrong projection symbol: it shows third angle, but the layout is first angle (top view below the front view, view from the left on the right). Correct: Use the first-angle symbol: cone on the left, circles on the right. Rule: The symbol must agree with the view layout (Ch3 sl. 17). Ch 3 note
  3. Hatching runs straight across the bore in section A–A. Correct: Leave the bore band (between the two lines 20 apart) unhatched. Rule: Hatching goes only on the material the cutting plane cuts. A hole is empty (OtherViews sl. 40–42). Ch 6 note
  4. The lower part of the section is hatched in the opposite direction to the upper part. Correct: Hatch both regions at the same 45° angle and spacing. Rule: One part: same slope and spacing everywhere in the view. Reversed hatching means a different part (OtherViews sl. 43). Ch 6 note
  5. No centre lines on the right-hand Ø12 hole in the top view. Correct: Add two centre lines crossing at the centre (short dashes), running about 8 mm past the circle. Rule: Every circle needs centre lines (Ch3 sl. 23). Ch 3 note
  6. Hidden lines of the right-hand Ø12 hole are missing in the front view (only its axis is drawn). Correct: Draw two hidden lines 12 apart, from the base bottom to the base top. Rule: Hidden features behind material are shown with thin dashed lines (Ch3 sl. 21). Ch 3 note
  7. The bore Ø20 is dimensioned on its hidden lines in the top view. Correct: Dimension it on the circle in the front view (leader through the centre: Ø20). Rule: Dimension from visible lines, not hidden lines, and dimension holes in their circular view (DrawingStandard sl. 29, 38). Ch 5 note
  8. Overall length 100 is given twice (above the front view and below the top view). Correct: Keep only one of them. Rule: Do not repeat dimensions (DrawingStandard sl. 23, 31). Ch 5 note
Drawing 2 · Written for these notes · 20 marks
Flanged bush, third angle: front view as full section A–A, and a right view. Find the 8 errors (2.5 marks each: 1 for the place, 1.5 for the rule and correction).
Hint: where the errors are

One is in the title block, two in the section, two in the dimensions and three in the right view (look at its circles and at the cutting plane drawn on it).

Answer: 8 errors, numbered
  1. The lower half of the section is hatched at the opposite angle to the upper half. Correct: Same 45° direction and spacing on both halves: it is one part. Rule: One part: same slope and spacing in the whole view (OtherViews sl. 43). Ch 6 note
  2. Hidden lines drawn in the section: the Ø9 hole behind the cutting plane, seen through the bore. Correct: Delete them. Behind the cutting plane, draw visible edges only. Rule: Hidden lines are normally left out of sectional views (OtherViews sl. 41). Ch 6 note
  3. The cutting-plane line has no letters, although the section is titled A–A. Correct: Put "A" beside each arrow. Rule: Letters identify the cutting plane and link it to the section label (OtherViews sl. 41). Ch 6 note
  4. The bore is dimensioned R15. Correct: Ø30 (a full circle takes a diameter). Rule: Use Ø for full circles and holes, R only for arcs of less than 180° (DrawingStandard sl. 38–39). Ch 5 note
  5. No pitch-circle (bolt-circle) chain line: the holes have only short solid crosses. Correct: Draw the Ø64 PCD as a thin chain circle through the hole centres, with radial centre lines. Rule: Holes on a bolt circle sit on a chain-line circle (Ch3 sl. 27). Ch 3 note
  6. Dimension written 60.00. Correct: 60. Rule: BS: least number of significant figures, e.g. 20 not 20.0 (DrawingStandard sl. 119). Ch 5 note
  7. Material missing from the title block. Correct: MATL: BRONZE. Rule: Material is a required title-block item (Ch2 sl. 32). Ch 2 note
  8. The bore in the right view is drawn as a hidden (dashed) circle. Correct: Thick continuous circle: the bore runs right through, so its edge is seen directly. Rule: Visible edges are thick continuous lines; dashed lines only for edges hidden behind material (Ch3 sl. 21). Ch 3 note
Drawing 3 · Written for these notes · 20 marks
Drive shaft, first angle: front view and removed section B–B. Find the 10 errors (2 marks each: 1 for the place, 1 for the rule and correction).
Hint: where the errors are

Look closely at the thread (two errors), the section, the cutting plane, four dimensions, the axis and the title block.

Answer: 10 errors, numbered
  1. Thread lines reversed: the minor diameter is drawn thick and the major diameter thin. Correct: External thread: major diameter (the outline) thick, minor diameter thin. Rule: Ch4 sl. 26: external thread, major Ø thick continuous, minor Ø thin continuous. Ch 4 note
  2. No line for the end of the useful thread. Correct: Draw a thick line across the major diameter where the thread ends (30 from the end). Rule: The limit of useful length of thread is a thick line (Ch4 sl. 27). Ch 4 note
  3. Hatch lines run past the outline of section B–B. Correct: Stop the hatching exactly at the thick outline. Rule: Hatching never crosses the outline; it touches it (OtherViews sl. 42). Ch 6 note
  4. Cutting plane B–B drawn as a thick continuous line. Correct: Thin chain line, thick only at the ends, with arrows and letters. Rule: Line type H: chain thin, thick at ends and changes of direction (Ch2 sl. 43). Ch 2 note
  5. Ø missing: the shaft is dimensioned "30" although it is a diameter. Correct: Ø30. Rule: Use the correct symbols such as Ø, R and □ (DrawingStandard sl. 23). Ch 5 note
  6. Over-dimensioned: the chain 80 + 40 and the overall 120 are all given. Correct: Delete one chain dimension (here the 40), or bracket it as an auxiliary dimension (40). Rule: Give an overall dimension and omit one of the chain dimensions (DrawingStandard sl. 30, 58). Ch 5 note
  7. The keyway width "8" in section B–B reads from the left-hand side. Correct: Rotate it to read from the bottom or the right-hand side. Rule: BS aligned system: dimensions are read from the bottom or the right-hand side of the sheet (DrawingStandard sl. 20, 55). Ch 5 note
  8. Date missing next to "DRAWN BY". Correct: Add the date the drawing was made. Rule: "Drawn by & date" is a required title-block item (Ch2 sl. 32). Ch 2 note
  9. The shaft axis (centre line) is drawn only over the keyway. Correct: Run the centre line along the whole axis, a little past both ends. Rule: Axes of symmetrical parts (shafts, cylinders) get a thin chain centre line (Ch3 sl. 23–24). Ch 3 note
  10. The leader of the thread note ends in a dot on the outline. Correct: End it with an arrowhead touching the outline. Rule: Leaders end in an arrowhead when they touch an outline and in a dot when they end within the outline (DrawingStandard sl. 66). Ch 5 note

A completed part title block, HKU format (written for these notes)
  1. 1Part name, in capitals, the largest text in the block.
  2. 2Drawing number: the company's code. Every drawing in a set has its own.
  3. 3Quantity of this part needed per assembly (2 pulleys here).
  4. 4Material, specific enough to buy: "CAST IRON", "STEEL 080M40", "ALUMINIUM 6061".
  5. 5Sheet X of Y: both numbers. A one-sheet drawing is "1 OF 1".

Assembly drawing: title block with parts list sl. 33

Redrawn from slide 33. The parts list (bill of materials) sits directly on top of the title block. Its header row (ITEM · DESCRIPTION · MATERIAL · QTY · REMARKS) is at the bottom, and items are numbered upwards, so new parts can be added on top. Each item number matches a numbered balloon on the drawing, joined to its part by a leader. Balloons are arranged in vertical or horizontal groups. The material box in the title block stays empty: materials are in the parts list.

Flashcards

Say the answer out loud, then open the card.

1 · Part name

What the part is called, in capitals, e.g. SUPPORT BRACKET. Not the assembly name on a part drawing.

2 · DWG No.

The drawing's unique code (company system), e.g. MB-101 or ASM-2. Different on every drawing.

3 · Qty

How many of this part go into one product, e.g. 4 for four identical legs.

4 · MATL

The material, e.g. MILD STEEL, BRONZE. On an assembly the materials go in the parts list instead.

5 · Sheet X of Y

This sheet's number and the total: SHEET 1 OF 2. Fill in both.

6 · Drawn by & date

Your name and the date you drew it. Two separate boxes: a missing date is a missing item.

7 · Dimension unit

ALL DIMENSIONS IN mm. Without it, "50" could be 50 mm or 50 inches.

8 · Scale

Drawing size : real size, e.g. 1 : 2 (half size). Recommended: 1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 1:10 …, 2:1, 5:1, 10:1 … Never write scaled dimension values.

9 · Projection symbol

Truncated cone, side view and end view. 1st angle: cone left, circles right. 3rd angle: circles left, cone right. Must match the layout.

  1. DWG No. missing.
  2. Date missing beside "drawn by".
  3. Scale 1 : 3 is not a recommended scale. Use 1 : 2 or 1 : 5 (Ch2 sl. 35).
  4. Wrong symbol. Top view below the front view means first angle, but the symbol shown is third angle (circles left). Use cone left, circles right.

4Short answers and calculations unknown marks

Nobody knows what fills the rest of the paper. These are the most likely short questions, one or two per chapter. All numbers were checked by computer. Each links to the chapter note.

1 · Decision matrixCh 1 note
A team picks a material for a bracket. Weights: cost 3, strength 2, ease of machining 1. Scores (1 to 5, 5 = best): aluminium 6061 is 3, 2, 5; mild steel is 4, 4, 3; stainless steel is 2, 5, 2. Which material wins?
Answer

Multiply each score by its weight and add: aluminium \(3\times3+2\times2+1\times5=18\); mild steel \(3\times4+2\times4+1\times3=23\); stainless \(3\times2+2\times5+1\times2=18\). Mild steel ranks first; aluminium and stainless tie for second. Score every criterion so that better = higher (cheap scores 5, not 1), or the totals mean nothing (Ch1 sl. 45–47).

2 · Decision strategiesCh 1 note
Name the strategy: (a) take the first option that meets the requirements, because time is short; (b) find as many options as possible and choose the very best; (c) choose the option whose worst outcome is least bad, because failure would be harmful; (d) choose the option with the largest possible payoff, accepting the risk.
Answer

(a) Satisficing. (b) Optimizing. (c) Maximin (maximise the minimum). (d) Maximax (maximise the maximum) (Ch1 sl. 43–44).

3 · Assembly drawings, BOM and balloonsCh 2 note
What can a general assembly drawing show, what goes in its parts list, and what are balloons for?
Answer

All parts in one drawing, as (a) an assembled view with overall dimensions, (b) a sectioned assembly, or (c) an exploded assembly with lines of explosion. The parts list / bill of materials (BOM) gives each part's ID (item no.), quantity, description, material, catalogue number. Each part is identified by a numbered balloon (leader + circle) that refers to the parts list; balloons are arranged in vertical or horizontal groups. Machining and assembly instructions can be added (Ch2 sl. 18; DrawingStandard sl. 69).

4 · Scale and paperCh 2 note
A drawing is at scale 1 : 2. A line measures 35 mm on the paper. What is the real length, and what number do you write on its dimension? What size is A3?
Answer

Real length = 35 × 2 = 70 mm, and the dimension says 70: you scale the drawing, never the dimension values (Ch2 sl. 36–37). A3 = 297 × 420 mm with a 10 mm border; each size is the next one halved (Ch2 sl. 34).

5 · Thread designationsCh 4 note
Explain "M16 × 2 – 4h – 5H" and "3/4 – 10 – UNC – 2A". What is the pitch of the second in mm, and the lead of a two-start M16 × 2 thread?
Answer

M16 × 2 – 4h – 5H: ISO metric, nominal (major) diameter 16 mm, pitch 2 mm, class of fit of this thread 4h, class of fit of the mating thread 5H (optional). 3/4 – 10 – UNC – 2A: nominal Ø 3/4 inch, 10 threads per inch, Unified Coarse series, class 2 (general purpose), A = external (B = internal). Pitch = 25.4 / 10 = 2.54 mm. Lead = number of starts × pitch = 2 × 2 = 4 mm per turn (Ch4 sl. 8, 13–18).

6 · Bolt gradeCh 4 note
A bolt head is marked 10.9. What are its tensile and yield strengths? And for 8.8?
Answer

10.9: the 10 is 1/100 of the tensile strength, so 1000 MPa; .9 is yield / tensile, so yield = 0.9 × 1000 = 900 MPa. 8.8: 800 MPa and 0.8 × 800 = 640 MPa (Ch4 sl. 22–23; the table lists slightly different approximate values, e.g. 1040 / 940 for 10.9).

7 · Gear pairCh 4 note
A pinion with 20 teeth drives a gear with 50 teeth, module 3 mm. Find both pitch circle diameters, the addendum, dedendum, whole depth, clearance, circular pitch, tooth thickness, centre distance, the pinion's tip and root diameters, the gear ratio and the face-width range.
Answer

\(D_1=mN_1=3\times20=60\) mm, \(D_2=3\times50=150\) mm. Addendum \(=m=3\); dedendum \(=1.25m=3.75\); whole depth \(=2.25m=6.75\); clearance \(=0.25m=0.75\); circular pitch \(=\pi m=9.42\); tooth thickness \(=9.42/2=4.71\); centre distance \(=m(N_1+N_2)/2=105\) mm. Pinion tip Ø \(=60+2\times3=66\), root Ø \(=60-2.5\times3=52.5\). Ratio \(=50/20=2.5\). Face width between \(9m=27\) and \(14m=42\) mm (Ch4 sl. 57–60).

8 · Clearance fitCh 7 note
Hole Ø40 H7 has limits 40.000 / 40.025. Shaft Ø40 g6 has limits 39.975 / 39.991. Find the maximum and minimum clearance. What type of fit is it?
Answer

Max clearance = largest hole − smallest shaft = 40.025 − 39.975 = 0.050 mm. Min clearance = smallest hole − largest shaft = 40.000 − 39.991 = 0.009 mm. Both positive, so a clearance fit (H7/g6 is the "sliding" fit in the hole-basis table, Tolerance sl. 48).

9 · Interference fitCh 7 note
45H7/p6: hole 45.000 / 45.025, shaft 45.026 / 45.042. Find the maximum and minimum interference.
Answer

Max interference = largest shaft − smallest hole = 45.042 − 45.000 = 0.042 mm. Min interference = smallest shaft − largest hole = 45.026 − 45.025 = 0.001 mm. The shaft is always bigger: an interference fit (Tolerance sl. 57).

10 · Worst-case stack-upCh 7 note
Three blocks, 20 ± 0.05, 25 ± 0.05 and 14.8 ± 0.05, sit side by side in a slot 60.00 +0.10 / −0.00 long. Find the nominal, largest and smallest gap left over.
Answer

Nominal gap = 60 − (20 + 25 + 14.8) = 0.20. Largest gap: biggest slot, smallest blocks: 60.10 − (19.95 + 24.95 + 14.75) = 0.45. Smallest gap: smallest slot, biggest blocks: 60.00 − (20.05 + 25.05 + 14.85) = 0.05. Check: 0.45 − 0.05 = 0.40 = the sum of all the tolerance bands (0.10 + 3 × 0.10). The gap is always positive, so the blocks always fit (Tolerance sl. 60–66).

11 · Reading a feature control frameCh 7 note
Read the frame [ ⌖ | Ø0.3 Ⓜ | A | B | C ]. And what is wrong with [ ▱ | 0.05 | A ]?
Answer

Left to right: characteristic symbol (⌖ position); tolerance value, with Ø meaning a cylindrical zone 0.3 wide; modifier Ⓜ = at maximum material condition; then the datums in order, A primary, B secondary, C tertiary. So the hole's axis must lie in a Ø0.3 cylinder at its true position measured from A, B, C (bonus tolerance as the hole departs from MMC). ▱ flatness is a form tolerance: it needs no datum, so the "A" is wrong (Tolerance sl. 84–86).

12 · ISO 2768-mKCh 7 note
A drawing note says "General tolerances ISO 2768-mK". What limits apply to untoleranced dimensions of 45, 8, a radius R2, 150 and an angle whose shorter leg is 20 mm?
Answer

m (medium) class, ISO 2768-1: 45 is in "over 30 up to 120", ±0.3 → 44.7 to 45.3; 8 is in "over 6 up to 30", ±0.2 → 7.8 to 8.2; R2 (radii table, 0.5 to 3) ±0.2 → 1.8 to 2.2; 150 is in "over 120 up to 400", ±0.5 → 149.5 to 150.5; angle with a 20 mm leg ("over 10 up to 50") ±0°30′. K is the ISO 2768-2 class for general geometric tolerances (straightness, flatness, perpendicularity, symmetry, run-out) (Tolerance sl. 33–39).

13 · Choosing a processCh 8 note
Choose a machining process for (a) a stepped round shaft, (b) a flat face with pockets, (c) Ø6 through holes, (d) a bearing seat that must reach Ra 0.4 µm. Name the four families of manufacturing processes.
Answer

(a) Turning (workpiece rotates, single-point tool; Ra 0.8–3.2 µm). (b) Milling (tool rotates, multi-point; Ra 0.8–6.3). (c) Drilling (Ra 3.2–12.5). (d) Grinding: the only one that reaches 0.4 (Ra 0.05–0.8). Families: subtractive (turning, milling, drilling), formative (casting, forging, injection moulding, sheet bending), additive (FDM, SLA, SLM), joining (welding, fastening, bonding) (Manufacturing sl. 4, 21).

20 rules most often broken

  1. Views in the wrong place for the projection symbol.
  2. Side or top view mirrored: 3rd angle front edge faces the front view, 1st angle faces away.
  3. Views not lined up (width, height, depth).
  4. Missing hidden lines for holes, slots, steps behind material.
  5. Circle without two centre lines crossing at the centre, about 8 mm past the edge.
  6. Side-on hole or shaft without an axis centre line.
  7. Visible outline drawn thin (thick : thin ≥ 2 : 1).
  8. Cutting plane not thin chain with thick ends, arrows, letters.
  9. Hatching: not 45°, uneven, or different angle/spacing on one part.
  10. Hatching across holes, past the outline, or short of it.
  11. Ribs, webs, gear teeth, shafts, bolts, keys hatched when cut lengthwise.
  12. Hidden lines in a section, or visible edges behind the plane left out.
  13. Dimension repeated or closed chain (overall + every step).
  14. Dimension to a hidden line; hole sized in its rectangular view.
  15. R on a full circle, Ø on an arc; Ø symbol missing on a diameter.
  16. Holes located from their edge; leader not aimed at the centre.
  17. Text not readable from the bottom/right; trailing zeros (60.00).
  18. Larger dimension nearer the view, so lines cross.
  19. External thread: major thick, minor thin, thick end line. Internal end view: thin ¾ circle.
  20. Gear: pitch circle thin chain, root circle omitted, teeth unhatched.
