Fit & tolerance: how much a size may vary

Limits and general tolerances, hole and shaft fits from the ISO tables, worst-case tolerance stack-up, and how to read a geometric tolerance frame. The chapter where the calculation marks are most likely to come from.

0What the test wants

Medium Not confirmed for the test, but if any calculation appears, it most likely comes from here (fits and stack-up). Tolerance notation can also be a planted error in the find-the-mistakes question. You need to be able to:

1Why tolerance, plus surface finish and welding symbols

Must know
  • Tolerance = the total amount a size is allowed to vary = upper limit − lower limit. It's always a positive number.
  • Why: nothing can be made to an exact size, and exact sizes aren't needed. Accuracy is expensive, so tolerances keep function and cost acceptable and stay within machine capability.
  • Tolerances give interchangeability: parts from different subcontractors (gear, bearing, shaft) fit together without hand fitting. The standard used here for limits and fits is BS 4500:1969.

A product is made from many parts mated by geometrical conditions such as concentric, parallel or touching. Picture a car maker that builds some parts on its own line and buys the gear, the bearing and the shaft from three subcontractors. The parts only assemble if each supplier works to agreed limits. The chain of reasoning: you can't make anything to an exact size, exact sizes aren't needed, accuracy is expensive and often not feasible. So how do you make sure the parts are interchangeable? Use a system of limits and fits (BS 4500:1969) that keeps function and cost acceptable and stays within machine capability.

2Dimensional tolerance and how to write it

Must know
  • Limits and fits: a designed-in clearance or interference between two parts (a hole and the insert), specified by tolerances.
  • Dimensional tolerance controls size and location. Geometric tolerance controls shape (form, orientation…).
  • Every functional dimension needs a tolerance (e.g. X.XX ± 0.02), or the assembly may not work.
  • Three ways to write it: unilateral (deviation in one direction only), bilateral (deviations both ways), limit dimensions (upper limit written above lower limit).
  • Instead of tolerancing every dimension, a general (footnote) tolerance in the title block covers all the untoleranced ones.

General (footnote) tolerance

The title block's "tolerances unless otherwise specified" box. The number of decimal places you write picks the tolerance: 2.50 (two places) means ±0.03, and 2.500 (three places) means ±0.005. This inch-drawing convention is why you must never add or drop decimal places casually. The metric equivalent is ISO 2768 (§4).

3Tolerance zone, MMC and LMC, and what tolerance costs

Must know
  • Tolerance zone: the space the tolerance creates. The feature's edge (size) or centre (location) may lie anywhere inside it.
  • MMC (maximum material condition): the size limit with the most material. Shaft MMC = largest shaft. Hole MMC = smallest hole.
  • LMC (least material condition): the opposite limit. Shaft LMC = smallest shaft, hole LMC = largest hole.
  • Tightest fit: both parts at MMC. Loosest fit: both at LMC.
  • Smaller tolerance: better fit but more expensive. Larger tolerance: more economical. The tolerance must be within what the process can achieve (turning about ±0.05, grinding about ±0.005).

Tolerance zones for size and for location

Size: 1.00 ± .02 means the step's edge can be anywhere in a band from 0.98 to 1.02 from the left end, a zone 0.04 wide.

Location: the hole centre is 1.50 ± .02 from two edges. The centre may lie anywhere in the red square, 0.04 × 0.04.

Why this matters later

The corners of that square are \(0.02\sqrt2 \approx 0.028\) from the true centre, but the edge midpoints are only 0.02 away. So a hole can be further off-centre diagonally than straight across, which makes no functional sense. GD&T's position tolerance (§9) uses a round zone (⌀) instead, the same in every direction.

MMC and LMC

Which limit is MMC

Material condition is about the amount of metal. A big shaft has more metal. A small hole leaves more metal in the part. Orange: the material around the hole.

Tab and slot

Tab (an external feature, like a shaft): MMC = .497 (upper), LMC = .495. Slot (an internal feature, like a hole): MMC = .498 (lower), LMC = .502. A slot measured at .501 is accepted. Tightest assembly (both at MMC): clearance = .498 − .497 = .001. Loosest (both at LMC): .502 − .495 = .007.

Tolerance level, cost and process capability

Choose the tolerance to suit the working precision the factory normally achieves. A smaller tolerance gives better assembly fit. A larger one is more economical. Show any special (tighter) tolerance individually on the dimension. The process limits the choice: machining, pressed metal and casting can each hold only so much.

Typical tolerances. Turning, drilling and planing: ±0.05. Milling: ±0.1. Broaching, grinding, honing and lapping: ±0.005. Finish (µm Ra) improves down the list, to 0.012–0.8 for lapping. So a ±0.01 dimension means a grinding operation, which adds a process step and cost.

5★ Hole and shaft fits

Must know
  • Fit = how tight a shaft sits in a hole, set by where the two tolerance zones lie. Clearance: shaft always smaller than hole. Interference: shaft always bigger (pressed in). Transition: could be either.
  • Basic size: the nominal size the limits are applied to (the "30" in ⌀30 H7/k6).
  • Hole basis: the minimum hole size = basic size (hole letter H, lower deviation 0). Shaft basis: the maximum shaft size = basic size (shaft letter h, upper deviation 0).
  • Symbol 40 H8: 40 = basic size, letter = fundamental deviation (where the zone sits), number = IT grade (how wide it is; bigger number = wider). Capital = hole, lower case = shaft. A fit: 40 H8/f7 (hole first).
  • Max clearance = largest hole − smallest shaft. Min clearance = smallest hole − largest shaft. A negative result is an interference.

Holes and shafts are everywhere in machine design: power transmission and fastening features. Change the shaft size relative to the hole and you change how tight or loose they are. That's the class of fit.

First example

A shaft in a hole with nominal ⌀0.5. One diameter is given as limits .51/.47 and the other as .50/.49. The two bands overlap, so depending on the actual parts there may be up to 0.02 clearance or up to 0.03 interference: a transition fit.

If the hole and shaft bands are the other way round, you get 0.03 clearance / 0.02 interference instead. Either way it's a transition fit.

The three classes, drawn to scale

Tolerance zones of the three ⌀30 examples, 1 µm to scale

Blue: hole zone. Red: shaft zone. Heights are deviations from ⌀30 in µm. Clearance: the shaft zone is entirely below the hole zone, so there's always a gap. Transition: the zones overlap. Interference: the shaft zone is entirely above.

Analogy

Think of clearance as relaxed and interference as forced. A clearance fit is a key in a lock that turns freely. An interference fit is a gear pressed onto a shaft so it can't slip. A transition fit is for locating parts accurately: sometimes it slides in, sometimes it needs a tap.

The vocabulary on one picture

Upper and lower deviation are measured from the basic size. The fundamental deviation (the letter) is the deviation nearest the basic size: it fixes where the zone starts. The IT grade (the number) fixes the zone's width.

Tolerance symbol = position letter + IT grade. Internal part (hole): 35H7. External part (shaft): 35h7.

Hole basis and shaft basis

Hole basis: every hole is H (smallest hole = basic size, .500). You change the fit by choosing the shaft letter. Shaft basis: every shaft is h (largest shaft = basic size), and you change the hole letter. Hole basis is more common: holes are made with fixed-size drills and reamers, while a shaft can be turned to any size.

6★ Reading the ISO 286 tables and the worked fits

Must know: the 4-step method
  1. Find the column: the size range "over … inc. (up to and including) …". ⌀30 is in over 18 inc. 30, not 30–40.
  2. Find the row (H7, k6…) and read two numbers in micrometres (µm, 0.001 mm): top = upper deviation, bottom = lower deviation.
  3. Limits = basic size + deviation/1000. H holes always have lower deviation 0.
  4. Max clearance = Hmax − Smin. Min clearance = Hmin − Smax (negative = interference). Both positive: clearance. Both negative: interference. One of each: transition.

The H rows of the ISO hole-tolerance table. The E, F and G rows are read the same way.

Shafts, k6 to r6

K6 for 18–30: +15/+2. p6 for 18–30: +35/+22, and for 30–50: +42/+26. Every deviation is positive, so these shafts are always bigger than the basic size.

Shafts, h, j, js, k5

Every h shaft has upper deviation 0 (the mirror image of H). The js rows are symmetric (±). Note that H7 and h7 have the same width (21 µm for 18–30): the IT grade fixes the width whatever the letter.

Worked example 1: clearance fit ⌀30 H11/c11

⌀30 H11/c11 · loose running

Hole H11: +0.130/0. Shaft c11: −0.110/−0.240. Max clearance +0.37, min clearance +0.11 → clearance fit.

Show the calculation

Hole: 30.000 to 30.130. Shaft: 30 − 0.240 = 29.760 to 30 − 0.110 = 29.890.

\[\begin{aligned}C_{max} &= 30.130 - 29.760\\ &= +0.370\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}C_{min} &= 30.000 - 29.890\\ &= +0.110\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\]

In words: biggest gap = biggest hole with the smallest shaft. Smallest gap = smallest hole with the biggest shaft. Both positive, so there's always clearance.

Worked example 2: transition fit ⌀30 H7/k6

⌀30 H7/k6 · location, slight interference

H7 (18–30): +21/0 µm. k6 (18–30): +15/+2 µm. Max clearance +0.019, max interference 0.015 → transition fit.

Show the calculation

Hole: 30.000 to 30.021. Shaft: 30.002 to 30.015.

\[\begin{aligned}C_{max} &= 30.021 - 30.002\\ &= +0.019\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}C_{min} &= 30.000 - 30.015\\ &= -0.015\ \text{mm}\\ \Rightarrow I_{max} &= 0.015\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\]

In words: one answer positive (gap possible), one negative (overlap possible), so it's a transition fit. The max interference can also be written as −0.015: the minus sign just means "negative clearance".

Worked example 3: interference fit ⌀30 H7/p6

⌀30 H7/p6 · location / interference

H7: +21/0 µm. p6 (18–30): +35/+22 µm. Min interference 0.001, max interference 0.035 → interference fit.

Show the calculation

Hole: 30.000 to 30.021. Shaft: 30.022 to 30.035.

\[\begin{aligned}I_{min} &= S_{min} - H_{max}\\ &= 30.022 - 30.021\\ &= 0.001\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\] \[\begin{aligned}I_{max} &= S_{max} - H_{min}\\ &= 30.035 - 30.000\\ &= 0.035\ \text{mm}\end{aligned}\]

In words: even the smallest shaft is 1 µm bigger than the biggest hole, so it must always be pressed in.

7★ Worst-case tolerance stack-up

Must know
  • Stack-up: the tolerances of several parts add up to give the variation of a gap or overall length in the assembly.
  • Worst case: assume every part is at whichever limit is worst at the same time.
  • Pick a direction. Each dimension is + if it runs in that direction along the chain, − if it runs back. Gap nominal = Σ(+) − Σ(−).
  • Gap tolerance = sum of all the tolerances, whatever their sign: \(T_{ASM} = \sum |T_i|\). Tolerances never cancel.
  • Min gap = positives at their min, negatives at their max. Max gap = positives at max, negatives at min.

Why care? A gap allows a certain motion (slider, bearing, pin), and the gap's value depends on several part dimensions. Analysis goes from the part tolerances to the assembly tolerance. Synthesis goes the other way, sharing a required assembly tolerance out among the parts. Each dimension has a min and a max. The question is how far the required dimension (e.g. a gap) can deviate.

Sign convention

Walk from the head face to the nut face: V runs in the + direction. Coming back through the washers, W, X, Y and Z are −. So GAP = V − W − X − Y − Z.

Method (use a table every time)
  1. Name the gap or length you want, and sketch the chain of dimensions from one side of it to the other.
  2. Write each dimension in a + or − column, with its tolerance in a third column.
  3. Nominal = Σ(+ column) − Σ(− column). Tolerance = Σ(tolerances).
  4. Answer as nominal ± tolerance, then max and min. Compare the min (or max) with the requirement.
\[\begin{aligned}C_{ASM} &= \textstyle\sum C_i \qquad T_{ASM} = \sum |T_i|\\ \textstyle\sum C_i - \sum |T_i| &\le C \le \textstyle\sum C_i + \sum |T_i|\end{aligned}\]

In words: the assembly's nominal is the signed sum of the nominals. Its worst-case tolerance is the plain sum of all the tolerances (e.g. a long rod made from three segments \(C_1 \pm T_1\), \(C_2 \pm T_2\), \(C_3 \pm T_3\) bonded end to end).

Unequal tolerances (e.g. +0.08/−0.06): work out the two limits separately. Upper deviation of the gap = Σ(upper deviations of +) − Σ(lower deviations of −). Lower deviation = Σ(lower of +) − Σ(upper of −).

Tolerance of XR

XR tolerance = +0.34/−0.36 mm.

Show the calculation
\[X_R = X_1 + X_2 - X_3 - X_4 - X_5\]

Upper deviation = upper of the + dimensions − lower of the − dimensions:

\[\begin{aligned}&2(+0.08) - 3(-0.06)\\ &= 0.16 + 0.18 = +0.34\end{aligned}\]

Lower deviation = lower of the + dimensions − upper of the − dimensions:

\[\begin{aligned}&2(-0.06) - 3(+0.08)\\ &= -0.12 - 0.24 = -0.36\end{aligned}\]

In words: the end play is largest when the housing is as big as possible and everything inside it as small as possible, and smallest the other way round. Total width 0.70 = 5 × 0.14.

8Geometric tolerance (GD&T): why it exists

Must know
  • GD&T (geometric dimensioning and tolerancing) defines a part by how it functions, using standard symbols. It controls form/shape, plus orientation, location and run-out.
  • It's a common language for designers, engineers and manufacturers, and very powerful for assemblies, because toleranced parts are sure to fit.
  • Standards: BS ISO 1101 (geometric tolerancing) and BS ISO 5459 (datums).
  • If no form tolerance is specified for a feature, it's acceptable whatever its form variation, as long as the size is in limits.
  • Direct limit tolerance + form tolerance = overall tolerance. The form zone is smaller and floats inside the size zone.

One example part, built up step by step: a plate with a raised step, a hole on the left and a counterbore on the right, drawn in third angle (top view and section A–A). First it's dimensioned with nominal sizes only (⌀25, ⌀35, 20, 70, 100, 30, 60, 10, 10). Then with the conventional ± method: ⌀25 +0.10/0, ⌀35 0/−0.10, 20.00 ± 0.05, 70.00 ± 0.05, 100.00 ± 0.10, 60.00 ± 0.10, 30.00 ± 0.05, heights 10.00 ± 0.10. Then comes the question: what if the top surface must be flat within 0.02 mm?

The flatness example

Without GD&T. Option 1: tighten the height to 10.00 ± 0.01. The whole surface must lie between 9.99 and 10.01, so it's flat within 0.02, but the size is now very expensive to hold. Option 2: keep 10.00 ± 0.10. The surface may lie anywhere from 9.9 to 10.1, so it could be wavy by up to 0.2. Flatness isn't controlled.

With GD&T. Keep the cheap ±0.10 on the height and add a frame to the top surface. Now the surface must lie between two parallel planes 0.02 apart (green), and those planes may sit anywhere inside the 0.2 size zone (red). Size is loose, flatness is tight: each tolerance controls only what the function needs.

Same idea on a table: height 30 ± 1 (a 2-unit size zone) with flatness .50. The .50 zone can move up and down inside the size zone. Direct limit plus form gives the overall requirement: the height limits and the flatness together define what is acceptable.

9The 14 characteristics and the feature control frame

Must know
  • Five groups: Form (4: flatness, straightness, cylindricity, circularity): no datum. Orientation (3: perpendicularity, parallelism, angularity): datum required. Location (3: position, concentricity, symmetry): required. Run-out (2: circular, total): required. Profile (2: line, surface): optional.
  • Frame, read left to right: symbol | tolerance (⌀ if the zone is cylindrical, value, modifier) | datums in order primary, secondary, tertiary.
  • The tolerance value is the width of the zone (the distance between two lines or planes, or the diameter of a cylinder if ⌀).
  • Ⓜ = at MMC: bonus tolerance as the feature departs from MMC. Ⓛ = at LMC (keeps a minimum wall thickness). Ⓟ = projected tolerance zone (threaded holes for long studs).

How to remember the datum column

Form compares a feature with a perfect version of itself (is it flat? round?), so there's nothing to refer to. Every other group compares the feature with something else: perpendicular to what, positioned from what, running out about which axis. That something is the datum. Profile can be either: a free shape, or a shape located from datums.

Tell the look-alikes apart: parallelogram = flatness, circle with two slanted lines = cylindricity (a circle "moving along"), two concentric circles = concentricity, three lines with a long middle one = symmetry, one arrow = circular run-out, two arrows on a base line = total run-out, open arc = line profile, closed arc = surface profile.

Reading a feature control frame

Anatomy of a feature control frame

Read it aloud: "Position within a cylindrical zone of diameter 0.3, at maximum material condition, relative to datums A (primary), B (secondary), C (tertiary)." In summary: symbol = type of tolerance; tolerance value = zone thickness, with ⌀ or R added for a circular or cylindrical form; letters = datums, used when the feature is toleranced relative to one or more datums.

Frame variations. A datum feature is marked by a capital letter in a box joined to a triangle (filled or open) on the feature or its extension line. A form frame (straightness 0,1) has no datum. Datum compartments: A–B = one common datum made from two features (e.g. two bearing journals). ABC in one box = several datums, order not important. A | B | C in separate boxes = order of priority matters (primary, secondary, tertiary). Note the European comma decimal: 0,5 = 0.5.

Bonus tolerance with Ⓜ

Holes ⌀20 ± 0.2 with position ⌀0.3 Ⓜ. The 0.3 applies when the hole is at MMC = ⌀19.8 (smallest hole). A bigger hole has more room around the pin that goes in it, so its position may wander more. The zone grows by exactly the amount the hole exceeds MMC.

On the drawing, and what it means

A flange with four holes (4X): position ⌀0.1 Ⓜ to A within the pattern, and the pattern located ⌀0.1 Ⓜ to A and B Ⓜ. Meaning: the hole axes are perpendicular to datum plane A (primary). The pattern is centred on datum axis B (the spigot), which supplies the second and third planes of the datum reference frame. Because B is at Ⓜ, the whole pattern may shift or rotate a little when the spigot is smaller than its MMC.

11Reading drills: what does this frame mean?

Say each frame aloud in the form: characteristic, zone shape and size, modifier, relative to which datums. Then say what kind of zone it is.

Drill 1. On the top face of a block whose bottom face is datum A:
Answer

Parallelism, 0.05, to datum A. The whole top face must lie between two parallel planes 0.05 apart that are parallel to datum plane A. It's an orientation tolerance, so a datum is required.

Drill 2. On a drilled hole ⌀10 +0.1/0:
Hint

What does ⌀ in front of the value do to the shape of the zone? What does Ⓜ give you when the hole is ⌀10.06?

Answer

Position, cylindrical zone ⌀0.2, at MMC, relative to A, B, C (in that priority). The hole's axis must lie inside a ⌀0.2 cylinder at its true position, located from datums A, B, C by basic dimensions. At MMC (⌀10.00) the zone is ⌀0.2. A hole made at ⌀10.06 gets 0.06 bonus, so ⌀0.26.

Drill 3. On a turned pin:
Answer

Circularity (roundness), 0.01. At every cross-section, the circle must lie between two concentric circles 0.01 apart (radially). It's a form tolerance: no datum.

Drill 4. On the side face of a bracket whose base is datum A:
Answer

Perpendicularity, 0.1, to datum A. The side face must lie between two parallel planes 0.1 apart that are at exactly 90° to datum A. No ⌀, so the zone is two planes, not a cylinder.

Drill 5. On the middle diameter of a shaft supported on two bearing journals A and B:
Answer

Circular run-out, 0.05, relative to the common axis A–B (one datum made from both journals). Spin the shaft about A–B: at each cross-section the dial indicator's total movement must not exceed 0.05.

Drill 6. Spot the mistake: on a top face
Answer

Flatness is a form tolerance, so it takes no datum. The "A" compartment is an error. If the face must be flat and parallel to A, use parallelism to A (which also limits flatness). A plain flatness frame has just two compartments.

12Common mistakes

✗Putting ⌀30 in the "30–40" column. ✓ Ranges are "over … up to and including", so ⌀30 is in 18–30. ✗Using table numbers as millimetres. ✓ They're µm: +21 means +0.021 mm. ✗Max clearance = largest hole − largest shaft. ✓ Pair opposite extremes: largest hole with smallest shaft. ✗Calling a fit "clearance" because the max clearance is positive. ✓ Check both results: one positive and one negative means transition. ✗Shaft MMC = smallest shaft. ✓ MMC = most material: largest shaft, smallest hole. ✗Subtracting tolerances for the − dimensions in a stack-up. ✓ Signs apply to nominals only. Worst-case tolerances always add. ✗Putting every dimension at its minimum to find the minimum gap. ✓ + dimensions at min, − dimensions at max. ✗Giving the stack-up answer as only "±0.4". ✓ State the nominal and both limits, then compare with the requirement and say yes or no. ✗A datum letter in a flatness, straightness, circularity or cylindricity frame. ✓ Form tolerances take no datum. ✗Writing H for a shaft or h for a hole. ✓ Capital = hole, lower case = shaft. Hole first in a fit: H7/k6. ✗Reading ISO 2768-mK as one class. ✓ Two classes: m for sizes (part 1), K for geometry (part 2).

Use the ISO 286 values in §6. Work in mm to three decimals.

1. ⌀25 H7/h6. Find the limits of the hole and the shaft, the max and min clearance, and the type of fit.
Hint

Column 18–30. H7: +21/0. h6: 0/−13.

Answer

Hole 25.000–25.021. Shaft 24.987–25.000. Cmax = 25.021 − 24.987 = 0.034. Cmin = 25.000 − 25.000 = 0. Clearance fit (locational clearance: it can just slide in when both parts are at MMC).

2. ⌀40 H7/n6. Is it clearance, transition or interference? Give the extreme values.
Hint

Column 30–50. H7: +25/0. n6: +33/+17.

Answer

Hole 40.000–40.025. Shaft 40.017–40.033. Cmax = 40.025 − 40.017 = +0.008. Cmin = 40.000 − 40.033 = −0.033 (max interference 0.033). One of each, so transition.

3. A bush is pressed into a housing: ⌀60 H7/p6. Find the min and max interference.
Hint

Column 50–80. H7: +30/0. p6: +51/+32.

Answer

Hole 60.000–60.030. Shaft 60.032–60.051. Imin = 60.032 − 60.030 = 0.002. Imax = 60.051 − 60.000 = 0.051. Interference fit.

4. ⌀12 H7/k6: max clearance and max interference?
Answer

Column 10–18. H7 +18/0, k6 +12/+1. Hole 12.000–12.018. Shaft 12.001–12.012. Cmax = 12.018 − 12.001 = 0.017. Imax = 12.012 − 12.000 = 0.012. Transition.

5. A housing slot is 60 ± 0.1 long. Three parts sit end to end in it: 20 ± 0.05, 20 ± 0.05 and 19 ± 0.05. Find the end gap (nominal, tolerance, min, max).
Answer

Gap = 60 − 20 − 20 − 19 = 1. Tolerance = 0.1 + 3 × 0.05 = ±0.25. Min = 59.9 − 20.05 − 20.05 − 19.05 = 0.75. Max = 1.25.

6. A slot 40 ± 0.2 holds two blocks, 12 ± 0.1 and 27.5 ± 0.1. (a) Find the gap. (b) The gap must never be less than 0.2. Keeping the blocks' tolerances, what tolerance must the slot have?
Hint

Min gap = nominal − Σ tolerances. Set it equal to 0.2.

Answer

(a) Gap = 40 − 12 − 27.5 = 0.5 ± 0.4, so 0.1 to 0.9. The 0.1 minimum fails a 0.2 requirement. (b) Need Σ tol ≤ 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.3. The blocks use 0.2, so the slot may have at most ±0.1 (synthesis: sharing the assembly tolerance out among the parts).

7. Title block: ISO 2768-mK. What are the limits of an untoleranced 70 mm length, and the allowed flatness of a 150 mm face?
Answer

70 is in 30–120, class m: ±0.3, so 69.7 to 70.3. Flatness, K, 100–300: 0.4.

8. Name the group and say whether a datum is needed: (a) cylindricity, (b) angularity, (c) total run-out, (d) profile of a surface, (e) symmetry.
Answer

(a) Form, no datum. (b) Orientation, datum. (c) Run-out, datum. (d) Profile, optional. (e) Location, datum.
