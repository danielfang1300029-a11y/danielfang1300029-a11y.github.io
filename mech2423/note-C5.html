Drawing standard 1: dimensioning

How sizes are written on a drawing: the parts of a dimension, the BS rules for placing lines, arrows and numbers, the symbols (Ø, R, □, SR, CBORE, CSK), how to dimension holes, arcs, chamfers and repeated features, and how to choose which dimensions to give. This is where most of the planted errors in the find-the-mistakes question come from.

0What the test wants

High Dimensioning errors are the commonest planted mistakes in the 20-mark find-the-mistakes question. For each one you circle, say what is wrong and which rule it breaks. You need to be able to:

  • Name the parts of a dimension and draw one correctly in BS style: small gap, value above the middle of the line, reading from the bottom or the right (§2, §5, §7).
  • Spot the classic rule breaks: repeated or redundant dimensions, dimensions to hidden lines, crossing lines, centre line used as a dimension line, missing Ø / R, dimensions inside the part, holes located by their edges (§3, §6, §13).
  • Dimension circles, arcs, holes, chamfers, counterbores, countersinks and repeated holes (×n, PCD) the standard way (§10–§12).
  • Choose parallel vs chain dimensioning and explain tolerance build-up; pick functional dimensions (§9, §13).
  • Tell BS from ANSI at a glance (§14). Use the checklist (§16) on every drawing in the test.

1Drawing standards sl. 5–7

Quick pass

Drawing standards are the agreed conventions and guidelines for how engineering drawings are created, presented and interpreted, so that a drawing means the same thing to everyone who reads it. Different countries use different standards according to their needs.

CodeFull form
ANSIAmerican National Standards Institute
BISBureau of Indian Standards
ISOInternational Organization for Standardization

Two major standards: BS/ISO (ISO is based on BS; used internationally in about 150 countries, especially Europe) and ANSI (minor differences from ISO; common in the Americas). This course draws to BS; the differences from ANSI are in §14.

2Dimension terminology sl. 8–9

Must know
  • Extension line (also called projection line): thin line carried out from the feature, with a small gap at the outline, running a little (about 2–3 mm) past the dimension line.
  • Dimension line: thin line with an arrowhead at each end, touching the extension lines. It shows the distance being measured.
  • Arrowhead: narrow, filled, length ≈ 3 × its width (sl. 14).
  • Dimension text (value): the number, in BS near the middle of the dimension line and above it.
  • Size dimensions (S) say how big a feature is; position dimensions (P) say where it is (sl. 9).
The parts of a dimension, BS style (redrawn for these notes)
  1. Dimension line: thin, arrowheads at both ends, parallel to the size being measured.
  2. Extension (projection) line: thin, perpendicular to the dimension line.
  3. Arrowhead: filled, length about 3 × width, its tip just touching the extension line.
  4. Small gap between the outline and the start of the extension line.
  5. The extension line runs a short distance past the dimension line.
  6. Value near the middle, just above the line (BS).
  7. Smaller dimension nearer the outline, larger one outside, so no dimension line crosses an extension line.
  8. A centre line may be extended and used as an extension line (here, to locate the hole).
The handout's two terminology figures sl. 8–9

Left (sl. 8): the four components: extension lines, dimension line, arrowhead, dimension text. Right (sl. 9): "S indicates size, P position". The slot width, overall sizes, hole size ("S Drill, 2 holes") and corner radius ("SR") are sizes. The distances that place the slot and the holes (from the right-hand face and the bottom) are positions. The side view repeats one width in brackets, "(S)": a reference dimension (§7).

3Dimension lines vs projection lines: good or not? sl. 10–15

Must know
  • Avoid line crossings. A dimension line must not cross another dimension line or an extension line. Extension lines may cross each other.
  • A centre line (and its extension) may be used as an extension line, but never as a dimension line.
  • Put dimensions outside the view, smaller ones nearest the outline, rows well spaced.
  • A dimension line must not be an outline or lie along one, and dimensions belong on the face where the feature shows (not on its far side).
Good or not? (1) sl. 11

✗ Left: the dimension lines are drawn inside the outline, run from edge to edge across the part, cross each other and cross the hole's centre lines; the slot is dimensioned with lines floating inside it. ✓ Right: every dimension is outside the view. The hole is located from the right-hand face and from the slot level, the slot by its position and width along the bottom, overall sizes outermost. Extension lines cross only extension lines.

Good or not? (2) sl. 12

Both are acceptable (both ticked). Top: the 40 and 50 sit in one aligned row with the 130 outside them (smaller nearer, larger further). Bottom: each step dimensioned right next to the face it measures, 130 outermost. Note the long extension lines in the top version: they are fine, because extension lines may be long; what must not happen is a dimension line crossing one.

Good or not? (3) sl. 13

Two mistakes, both crossed out. (i) The leaders for Ø15 and Ø28 cross each other inside the circles, and the Ø15 text is jammed into the □55 dimension. (ii) In the side view the 15 is placed outside (above) the 55 and its extension line runs through the 55 dimension line: the smaller dimension must sit nearer the outline. The corrected version is sl. 81 (§10).

Conclusion: the correct anatomy sl. 14–15

Sl. 14: extension (projection) lines are perpendicular to the dimension line, start with a small gap, end with a short extension past it; the arrowhead is about 3× as long as it is wide. Sl. 15: the smaller dimension goes nearest the outline, the hole's centre lines are carried out as extension lines, and the rows are well spaced. Slide text: "Avoid line crossing. Centre line & their extension can be used as projection line but not dimension line."

5Systems of dimensioning: aligned vs unidirectional sl. 18–21

Must know
Aligned system (BS)Unidirectional system (ANSI)
Text directionaligned with (parallel to) the dimension linealways horizontal
Read frombottom or right-hand side of the sheetbottom edge only
Text positionmiddle, on top of the dimension linemiddle, in a break in the dimension line

So in BS a vertical dimension's number is turned to read from the right: written upwards, on the left of its line. A vertical number that reads from the left (turned the other way) or sits below a horizontal line is wrong in BS.

Which way to read sl. 19

Aligned: 1st choice is from the bottom, 2nd choice from the right. Unidirectional: from the bottom only.

The two systems compared sl. 20–21

Aligned (sl. 20): "30" above the line ✓, below the line ✗; vertical "30" written upwards on the left of the line (reads from the right) ✓; the other two orientations ✗. Unidirectional (sl. 21): numbers horizontal, set in a gap in the line ✓; a rotated number ✗. The last panel: hole sizes Ø40, Ø20, Ø15 given in the circular view ✓; the Ø40 given in the side view between hidden lines ✗ (rule 14, §6).

7Dimension value: precision, placement, orientation, nature sl. 46–64

Slide 46 splits the rest of the topic into three: dimension value (this section), leader lines (§8) and dimension methods (§9). Slide 48 lists the concerns for a value: precision, location, orientation, style for linear and circular lines, and nature (exact, reference or note).

Must know
  • Precision: calculate to full accuracy, but report only to the accuracy of the input and of the process. d = 0.40 mm → C = πd = 1.26, not 1.25663706. BS: least significant figures (20 not 20.0); below 1 write 0.25 not .25.
  • Location (BS): near the middle of the dimension line and above it. Read from the bottom or right. Inclined dimensions follow the line; avoid the 30° zones either side of vertical.
  • Angles may be written horizontally to make them easier to read.
  • Redundant dimensions are normally omitted; if useful (e.g. overall length) give them in brackets: an auxiliary (reference) dimension, (140).
  • Dimensions aligned in rows; no dimension line crossing an extension line; extension lines crossing each other: no gap.
  • Centre line may be the extension line; never shorten an extension line; repeated features: 4X Ø.375; small spaces: arrows and/or value outside.

Precision sl. 49

Good practice: calculate to the greatest accuracy possible, then report within the accuracy of the given input and what the manufacturing process can achieve. A wire of diameter d = 0.40 mm has circumference C = πd = 1.2566… mm. Writing C = 1.25663706 is bad: the input has two decimal places, so report C = 1.26. On a drawing, a value like 29.87 where the design size is 30 is the same mistake: it was read off (scaled from) the drawing or CAD instead of being the intended size.

Where the number goes (BS) sl. 50

Value near the middle of the line, and above sl. 50

Every value sits near the middle of its dimension line, just above it. The vertical diameters are written upwards, readable from the right. Small spaces (the 12 and 15) still keep the number above the line.

Radius and diameter examples sl. 51–54

Holes and arcs (BS) sl. 51–52

Holes: a diameter line through the centre with arrows touching the circle, or a leader whose line points at the centre. Arcs: R, with a line along the radius (from the centre, or in line with it). Sl. 52: (a) small hole Ø10: "the leader points to centre of circle"; (b) Ø10 with arrows outside the extension lines; (c) Ø26 between extension lines above the circle; (d) Ø32 with one long arrowed line through the centre and the value outside; (e) Ø32 along a diameter inside a large circle.

Notes for repeated holes and a sectioned part sl. 53–54

Sl. 53: 4 × Ø4 means four holes Ø4, through (no depth given); Ø9.5 × 25 means Ø9.5, 25 deep, where the depth is the cylindrical part (the drill point is extra). Sl. 54: on a section, diameters are given as linear dimensions with Ø (Ø100, Ø30, Ø40, Ø50), because the section shows the true widths.

Reading direction, inclined and angular values sl. 55–57

Readable from the bottom or right; inclined dimensions sl. 55–56

Sl. 55: angle values follow their arcs but are always turned so they read from the bottom or the right. Sl. 56: for linear dimensions on inclined edges, the number turns with the line; keep dimensions out of the hatched zones (about 30° either side of the vertical, top-left and bottom-right), where the text would be nearly upside down.

Horizontal angles; auxiliary dimension sl. 57–58

Sl. 57: to make reading easier, angles may all be written horizontally. Sl. 58: redundant dimensions are normally left out, but an overall length is often useful: give it in brackets, (140) = 50 + 90, and call it an auxiliary dimension. Brackets tell the machinist "for information, not to be measured to".

Alignment, crossings, centre lines, shortened lines sl. 59–62

Dimensions should be aligned sl. 59

CORRECT: the step widths share one row. NO!: each step's dimension at a different height, so the reader's eye jumps around.

Dimension line crossing a projection line sl. 60

CORRECT: small inside, large outside; where extension lines cross, they cross with no gap. NO!: big inside and small outside, so an extension line cuts through a dimension line, top and right.

Centre line extension; never shorten extension lines sl. 61–62

Sl. 61: "centreline used as an extension line" is correct. Sl. 62 NO!: the extension lines have been shortened: they start well away from the step and the top face, leaving a big gap, so it is not clear which edge each dimension belongs to. Extension lines start with only the small standard gap.

Repeated features and small spaces sl. 63–64

Repeat features; insufficient space sl. 63–64

Sl. 63: give a repeated feature once, with the count: 4X Ø.375 (four holes). Sl. 64: when a feature is too small for arrows and number between the extension lines, put the arrows outside pointing in, and the number inside, above, or outside on an extended dimension line.

8Leader lines sl. 65–69

Must know
  • A leader is a thin continuous line from a note or value to the feature. It ends in an arrowhead touching an outline, or a dot inside an outline (on a surface).
  • It meets the outline at a large angle (not nearly parallel); never horizontal or vertical into the part; keep it short, don't cross other leaders or dimension lines.
  • For a hole or circle, the leader must point at the centre (if extended it passes through the centre).
  • Note text: BS allows aligned or horizontal, sitting above a short horizontal shoulder; leader from the first or last word (rule 19).
  • Balloons (part numbers in circles) on an assembly use leaders ending in a dot on the part, balloons lined up in vertical or horizontal groups.
Arrow on the outline, dot inside; long leaders sl. 66–67

Sl. 66: the plating note ends with an arrowhead touching the face outline, at a large angle. Sl. 67: ✗ long leaders from distant R5 notes cross the section; ✓ each R5 placed beside its own fillet with a short leader. Same idea in BS and ANSI.

Leader through the centre; balloons sl. 68–69

Sl. 68: for a hole, the leader extended must pass through the centre. Sl. 69: balloons 1–3 point at the parts with dot-ended leaders, the balloons stacked in a neat vertical group.

9Dimension methods sl. 70–80

Must know

Chain vs parallel: tolerances add up along a chain (tolerance build-up). Parallel dimensioning has no build-up: each feature is held to its own tolerance from the datum.

Symmetrical part: full view and half view sl. 71–72

Sl. 71: full view ("All fillet radii 1mm unless specified"). Sl. 72: only the upper half is drawn; the axis of symmetry carries two short thin parallel lines at each end. The Ø65 and Ø100 dimension lines run a short distance past the axis, have an arrowhead at one end only, and still give the full diameter.

Parallel and superimposed running dimensioning sl. 73–74

Sl. 73 parallel: 50, 130, 210 to the hole centres and 260 overall, all from the datum feature (left face). Sl. 74 superimposed running: the same values on one dimension line; the common origin indicator (small circle) marks zero, each value is written at its arrow end.

Chain dimensioning sl. 75

Chain: 50, 80, 80, 50: each dimension starts where the last ended. (Check: 50 + 80 + 80 + 50 = 260; positions 50, 130, 210 as in sl. 73.)

Class discussion: chain vs parallel sl. 77–78

Slide answer: "The advantage of parallel dimensioning is that there is no build-up of tolerances."

Tolerance build-up, worked (redrawn for these notes from sl. 78)

Chain: the third hole is at 25 + 25 + 25 = 75, but each step may be off by ±0.5, so it can land anywhere from 73.5 to 76.5: 75 ± 1.5. Parallel: it is dimensioned directly, 75 ± 0.5.

In words: worst-case tolerance of a position reached through a chain = the sum of the tolerances of every link: \(t_{\text{total}} = t_1 + t_2 + \dots + t_n\). Here \(0.5+0.5+0.5 = 1.5\). (Statistical stack-up is in Chapter 7.)

Combination; coordinates sl. 79–80

Sl. 79 combined: big-hole positions parallel from the datum (50, 130, 210, 260), each small hole chained 26 from its partner because their spacing is what matters. Sl. 80 co-ordinates: each hole has an X and a Y measured from one origin (the bottom-left corner), written as single ordinate numbers along two edges; typical for mould plates and CNC work.

10Circles, diameters and radii sl. 81–88

Must know
  • Full circle → Ø. Arc ≤ 180° → R. Sphere → SØ or SR.
  • A radius dimension line passes through, or in line with, the centre of the arc and carries one arrowhead, touching the arc.
  • Small unimportant fillets and rounds: just R + value, arrow on the arc, centre not located.
  • If the radius is fixed by other dimensions (e.g. slot ends = half the width), write R with no value.
  • Very large diameters: shortened dimension lines (one arrowhead, line passing the centre).

11Chamfers, holes and hole positions sl. 89–97

Must know
  • 45° chamfer: one note, length × 45° (e.g. 2 × 45°), length along the axis. Other angles: give the length and the angle with two separate dimensions (e.g. 6 and 15°, 3.5 and 30°).
  • A hole with no depth given is a through hole. A blind hole needs its depth: Ø9.5 × 25 DEEP (depth = cylindrical part).
  • Repeated holes: count × size: 4 × Ø10, 3X Ø15 × 20 DEEP.
  • Repeated holes on a circle: n × Ø EQUI SP ON Ø.. PCD (pitch circle diameter). Unequally spaced: give the angles.
  • Holes positioned by coordinates: the pitch circle radius/diameter is then only an auxiliary dimension, in brackets: (R90).
Chamfers sl. 89–90

(a) 45° chamfers in one note, 5 × 45°, 2 × 45°; or the angle and the diameter it reaches (45°, Ø36). (b) "Chamfers at angles other than 45°": the angle and a length are dimensioned separately (30° and 3.5; 15° and 6).

Through holes; three blind holes sl. 91–92

Sl. 91: "Where no drill depth is specified, through holes are required": 4 × Ø10 means four Ø10 holes right through. Sl. 92: 3X Ø15 × 20 DEEP (three blind holes), 100 between centres, R15 ends, the 25 thickness on the side view.

Hole size: use a half section sl. 93–94

Sl. 93: the small blind hole's Ø8.50 and depth 16 are on hidden lines: poor. Sl. 94: cut a section G–G through the hole; now the hole is drawn in visible lines and its size and depth are dimensioned on the section. This is the "if essential, draw it in section" rule (§13).

Holes on a pitch circle sl. 95–96

Sl. 95 (equally spaced): one note, 8 × Ø10 EQUI SP ON 70 PCD, plus the angle that fixes the pattern, 22½° = half of 360/8 = 45°. Sl. 96 (unequally spaced): 3 × Ø10, the pitch circle Ø70, and the angles 75° and 135° to place each hole.

Coordinates make the radius auxiliary sl. 97

The holes are placed by X, Y coordinates from the boss centre: (90, 0), (63, 63) and (23, 86). Those coordinates control the positions, so the pitch radius is only for information: (R90) in brackets. Check: √(63² + 63²) ≈ 89.1 and √(23² + 86²) ≈ 89.0, so the holes are on R ≈ 89–90, which is why the radius must not also be a controlling dimension.

13Dimension selection and other concerns sl. 109–118

Must know
  • Not on hidden detail. If a hidden feature must be dimensioned, draw that view in section.
  • Cylinders, tapers and symmetrical features are located by their axes, not their surfaces.
  • Never shorten extension lines.
  • Castings and forgings: location dimensions from machined surfaces (or axes of machined bores), not from rough cast faces.

Functional requirements: (1) the roller must rotate on the pin; (2) clearance under the hexagon head of the pin; (3) the end of the pin must stand through the nut.

Pin: f1 threaded end length, f2 shank length under the head (f2 > f5). Base: f4 wall thickness, f5 outside width over the walls, f6 inside gap. Roller: f3 length (f3 < f6).

Slide 121 gives the same L-shaped plate (5.0 long, 3.0 high on the left, 1.5 high on the right, two Ø.5 holes) dimensioned twice, (a) and (b). Slide 122 is a Mentimeter vote "Which drawing is correct?" with no answer shown, so the answer below is worked out here.

The two drawings sl. 121
1 2 3 3 4 5

Pins mark the problems in (b).

Answer: (a) is correct

(a) gives every size and position exactly once: step width 1.5, heights 3.0 and 1.5, hole height .75, hole positions 1.0 and 2.5 (chained from the first hole), overall 5.0, and one note Ø.5 DRILL 2 PLACES. Nothing can be worked out from anything else. (b) has the same information plus extras that break the rules:

  2. 2 · The inner 1.5 (step depth) is redundant (3.0 − 1.5 = 1.5) and is written inside the part (rule 11).

16Dimensioning checklist for the exam

Run this on every dimensioned drawing in the test
  1. Lines: small gap at every extension line? Extension lines run a little past the dimension line, never shortened? Arrowheads touch the extension lines?
  2. Crossings: any dimension line crossed by another line? (Smaller dimension should be inside.) Leaders crossing each other or dimension lines?
  3. Centre lines: a centre line used as a dimension line (error) or only as an extension line (fine)?
  4. Text (BS): number above the middle of the line? Reads from the bottom or the right? 0.25 not .25, no needless decimals (20 not 20.0), no scaled values like 29.87?
  5. Symbols: Ø on every diameter, R on every arc, □ on squares, SR/SØ on spheres? Full circle never R, arc never Ø?
  6. Count: is any size given twice (in two views, or a chain + overall)? Any value that can be worked out from others (bracket it or delete it)? Any feature with no size or position at all?
  7. Where: dimension on a hidden line? Hole sized on its side view instead of its circular view? Value inside the part, far from its feature, or on the far side of a view?
  8. Holes: located by centres (axes), not edges? Leader pointing through the centre? Count given (4 × Ø10)? Depth for blind holes, none for through holes? PCD and EQUI SP for a hole circle? CBORE / CSK / SFACE used for the right feature?
  9. Radii and chamfers: radius line through or in line with the centre, one arrowhead? 45° chamfer as length × 45°; other angles as length + angle?
  10. Method: chain where the positions are critical (tolerance build-up)? Casting located from a machined face? Functional sizes given directly?

For each error you circle, write the fix and the rule in a few words: "R5 leader not in line with arc centre: radius line must pass through the centre".

17Spot the mistake (written for these notes)

Each drawing has 4–6 planted dimensioning errors, the kind used in the 20-mark question. Everything not listed in the answer is drawn correctly (and the dimensions agree with the drawn shape). Circle each error and name the rule before opening the answer.

Drill 1 · Written for these notes
A stepped block 80 × 50 with a hole, BS. Find 6 errors.
Hint

Add up the top row. Look at which way every number reads, which side of its line it sits, and which lines cross on the left.

Answer
  1. 36 + 44 with 80 overall: over-dimensioned (44 = 80 − 36). Omit 44 or bracket it (44) as auxiliary. Rules 6 and 8.
  2. No gap: the right-hand extension line of the 80 touches the outline. Leave the small standard gap. Rule 2.
  3. 32 outside the 50: the hole's centre-line extension crosses the 50 dimension line. Put the smaller 32 inside, 50 outside. Rule 9 (sl. 60).
  4. "12" with no Ø on a hole. Write Ø12. Symbols (sl. 16, 23).
  5. 30 reads from the left: BS aligned text must read from the bottom or the right. Turn it to read from the right. Sl. 20, 55.
  6. 80 written below its dimension line. BS: near the middle, above the line. Sl. 50.

Correct as drawn: the hole's centre line used as an extension line for the 32 and the 18 (allowed); the long extension line for the 44 (extension lines may be long).

Drill 2 · Written for these notes
A plate with three Ø10 through holes; plan above, front view below. A note says: "hole positions are critical relative to face A (left end)". Find 5 errors.
Hint

How are the holes positioned, given the note? Where is the hole size given? What is the little vertical "20" drawn along?

Answer
  1. Chain 20, 40, 40 where positions from face A are critical: the third hole could be off by three tolerances (build-up). Use parallel (20, 60, 100 from A) or superimposed running dimensions. Sl. 73–78.
  2. Ø10 on the front view between hidden lines: dimension holes in the circular view, never to hidden lines, and give the count: 3 × Ø10 on the plan. Rules 5 and 14.
  3. Centre line used as a dimension line: the 20 (hole row from the front edge) is drawn along the hole's vertical centre line, inside the view. Carry the centre line out as an extension line and dimension outside. Sl. 15.
  4. 15 inside the front view: there is room outside; dimensions go outside the part. Rule 11.
  5. Shortened extension line on the right of the 120: it starts far from the corner, so it's unclear which edge it belongs to. Sl. 62, 114.
Drill 3 · Written for these notes
A turned shaft with three diameters (design sizes Ø30, Ø40, Ø24) and both ends chamfered, BS. Find 5 errors.
Hint

Check every diameter for its symbol and its value. Add up the bottom row. Read the chamfer note's leader.

Answer
  1. "40" with no Ø on a diameter in the rectangular view: write Ø40. (A diameter inside the view of a stepped shaft is acceptable when outside would need long extension lines crossing other steps.)
  2. "29.87": a scaled / over-precise value. The design size is 30: report to the accuracy intended, Ø30, with its Ø. Sl. 49, 119.
  3. Leader from the end of the first line of the two-line note. Take it from the start of the first line or the end of the last line. Rule 19.
  4. 40 + 50 + 30 and 120 overall: closed chain plus overall. Omit one step (e.g. the 50) or bracket the 120 as (120). Rules 6, 8; sl. 58.
  5. Ø24 reads from the left: in BS a vertical value reads from the right. Sl. 20.
Drill 4 · Written for these notes
A plate with a semicircular end (R20), a concentric Ø16 hole, a 60° V-notch 15 deep and a rounded corner R5, BS. Find 5 errors.
Hint

Arcs vs circles. Extend each radius/diameter leader: does it go through a centre? How is the hole located?

Answer
  1. Ø40 on a 180° arc: arcs of 180° or less take a radius, R20. Rule 15.
  2. R5 leader not in line with the arc's centre: the radius line must pass through, or be in line with, the centre, with its single arrowhead on the arc. Sl. 85–86.
  3. Ø16 leader does not point at the centre of the hole. Extended, a hole's leader must pass through the centre. Sl. 52, 68.
  4. 60° written inside the V: place angular dimensions outside, between extension lines from the flanks. Rule 12.
  5. Hole located to its edge (72 = 80 − 8): locate holes and arcs by their centres (axes): give 80 from the left end to the centre. Rule 17; sl. 112–113.

Correct as drawn: notch position 30 to its axis, the 25 from the notch tip (its extension line crosses the part, which is allowed), 25 inside 40.

Drill 5 · Written for these notes
A flange Ø120 with a Ø50 bore and six Ø10 holes equally spaced on a Ø90 pitch circle. Find 5 errors.
Hint

Could a machinist drill the six holes from this note alone? Is anything said twice? Look at what the Ø50 arrows sit on, and at the outside size.

Answer
  1. Hole note incomplete: no count and no pitch circle. Write 6 × Ø10 EQUI SP ON Ø90 PCD. Sl. 95.
  2. 60° between holes repeats what EQUI SP already says (360/6 = 60°): redundant. (An angle is needed only to fix the pattern's start, or for unequal spacing.) Rule 7; sl. 95–96.
  3. Ø50 drawn on the centre line: a centre line must never be used as a dimension line. Use a separate dimension line or a leader to the bore. Sl. 15.
  4. R60 on a full circle: complete circles are sized by diameter, Ø120. Sl. 51; rule 15.
  5. Long leader ("THROUGH") running across the whole part and crossing other lines. Keep leaders short; and "through" is the default when no depth is given, so the note is not needed. Sl. 67, 91.
Drill 6 · Written for these notes
Plan and section of a plate with (left to right) a counterbored hole, a countersunk hole and a blind drilled hole. Find 4 errors.
Hint

Match each note to what the section shows. How does each leader end? Is anything written on the hatching?

Answer
  1. Wrong feature word: the left hole is a counterbore (flat-bottomed step in the section) but the note says CSK. Write Ø8 CBORE Ø14 × 5 DEEP. Sl. 98–101.
  2. Blind hole with no depth: "Ø6" alone means a through hole, but the section shows it stopping 12 deep. Write Ø6 × 12 DEEP (depth of the cylindrical part). Sl. 53, 91.
  3. Dimension written on the hatched section (the 5 counterbore depth), inside the part. Keep it outside the boundary and out of hatched areas; here it is already in the note anyway (repeat). Rules 7, 11; sl. 56.
  4. Leader ends in a dot on the outline of the countersink: a leader ending on an outline takes an arrowhead; a dot is for ending inside an outline. Sl. 66.

Correct as drawn: hole positions 20, 50, 80 parallel from the left face; 40 and 20 on the views that show them; Ø8 CSK Ø16 × 90° for the countersink.

★Cram sheet

Anatomy (BS)

  • Extension line: thin, small gap at the part, ~2–3 mm past the dimension line, never shortened.
  • Dimension line: thin, arrowheads 3 : 1, touching extension lines.
  • Value: middle, above the line; reads from bottom or right.
  • Rows: first ≥ 3 × text height out, then 2 × apart; small inside, large outside.
  • Centre line: OK as extension line, never as dimension line.
  • Extension lines may cross (no gap); dimension lines must not be crossed.

Symbols

Holes & arcs

  • Holes: circular view, by centres, leader through centre, n × Ø; no depth = through.
  • Hole circle: n × Ø EQUI SP ON Ø PCD; coordinates → radius in ( ).
  • Radius: line through/in line with centre, one arrowhead; R with no value if fixed by other sizes.
  • Chamfer 45°: 2 × 45°; other angle: length + angle.
  • CSK: Ø CSK AT 90° TO Ø. CBORE: Ø CBORE Ø × depth. SFACE: no depth.

The 19 rules (short)

  1. 3 × / 2 × text height
  2. gap at outline
  3. between views
  4. most descriptive view
  5. not to hidden lines
  6. overall + omit one of chain
  7. no repeats
  8. no over-constraint
  9. dimension lines not crossed
  10. close to feature
  11. not inside part
  12. angles outside
  13. rounds to theoretical corner
  14. cylinders rectangular view, holes circular view
  15. arcs R not Ø
  16. stagger numbers
  17. holes by centre, circular view
  18. slot: overall or centres + R
  19. leader from first/last word

Methods

  • Parallel (datum) · running (origin ○) · chain · combined · coordinates.
  • Chain: tolerances add (3 × ±0.5 → ±1.5). Parallel: no build-up.
  • Half view: line past the axis, one arrow, full value.
  • Locate by axes; casting from machined faces; functional sizes direct.

18Common mistakes

✗Giving every step and the overall size. One of them must go (or be bracketed). ✗Dimensioning a hole on the view where it is dashed. Size it where it is a circle. ✗Drawing a dimension along a centre line, or along an outline. ✗Putting the bigger dimension inside the smaller one, so lines cross. ✗Writing R on a full circle or Ø on a fillet; forgetting the symbol altogether. ✗Locating a hole or boss from its edge instead of its centre. ✗BS drawing with numbers in a break of the line, all horizontal, or .25: that is ANSI style. ✗A blind hole with no depth (reads as through), or a spotface with a depth. ✗Copying values off the scale or CAD (29.87) instead of the design size. ✓When marking a drawing: for every error, write the fix and the rule. "Wrong" alone earns little.

19Practice (written for these notes)

1. A bar has holes at 30, 70 and 110 from its left end. Each is chained from the last with ±0.2. What is the worst-case position tolerance of the third hole? What would it be with parallel dimensioning?
Hint

How many toleranced links lie between the left end and the third hole?

Answer

Chain 30, 40, 40: three links, \(0.2+0.2+0.2 = 0.6\), so 110 ± 0.6. Parallel (30, 70, 110 from the end): 110 ± 0.2.

2. Write the BS note for: eight Ø12 holes equally spaced on a 100 mm pitch circle; and for a Ø10 hole counterbored Ø18 to a depth of 8.
Hint

Count × size, spacing, pitch circle. Hole, then feature, then size and depth.

Answer

8 × Ø12 EQUI SP ON Ø100 PCD (plus one angle if the pattern's start matters). Ø10 CBORE Ø18 × 8 DEEP (symbols: Ø10 ⌴ Ø18 ↧ 8).

3. A slot is 60 long overall and 16 wide with semicircular ends. Give the two acceptable ways to dimension it, and say which radius note goes with each.
Hint

Rule 18, and sl. 87 on R without a value.

Answer

(i) Overall 60 and width 16; the ends are then fixed, marked R with no value (R8 would be redundant). (ii) Centre distance 44 (= 60 − 2 × 8) and R8; the width follows.

4. True or false, with the rule: (a) an extension line may cross another extension line; (b) a centre line may be used as a dimension line; (c) a value of 0.5 in BS is written .5; (d) a hole note with no depth means drill through.
Answer

(a) True, with no gap at the crossing (rule 9, sl. 60). (b) False: extension line yes, dimension line never (sl. 15). (c) False: BS writes 0.5; .5 is ANSI (sl. 119). (d) True (sl. 91).

5. A cast bracket has one machined face. Where should the location dimensions of its machined holes be measured from, and why?
Answer

From the machined face (or the axis of a machined bore), not from rough cast surfaces: the machinist can set up and measure only from a true surface, and cast faces vary from part to part (sl. 115).
