Ch.5 Elasticity · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.5Previous: guide Ch.4Next: guide Ch.6

Every Ch.5 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (128 unique questions, 133 parts with repeats) sorted into seven strategies. Four are pure calculation (C5.1–C5.4) and three are statement checking (C5.5–C5.7) with a small, fixed stock of facts. Learn the ready answers and the statement tables, then drill.

0How Ch.5 is examined

  • Where: midterms (usually 3–6 parts after the Ch.4 block) and the early part of finals; 26 sittings from 2020 to 2025 have a Ch.5 question.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): online quiz with numeric blanks to 2 d.p. ("[Answer26 = …]"), A/B direction blanks, multiple choice, "how many statements are correct" (answer A = 0 … E = 4), and True/False sets. No marks are shown.
  • Paired questions: two consecutive questions often share one background (oil market, demand relation). The second needs your UNROUNDED result from the first.
  • Answers are exact. Keep 4+ decimals in your working and round only the entered answer.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
  • Three parts that mix Ch.5 with later chapters are solved in later guides: 2022 Spring Final Q12 (C7.1), 2022 Spring Final Q14 and 2025 Spring Supp Q25B (C11.7).
Memorise for Ch.5
  • \(\varepsilon_X = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta X}\cdot\dfrac{X}{Q}\): coefficient × X/Q on a linear demand. With slope = dP/dQ: \(\varepsilon = \dfrac{1}{\text{slope}}\cdot\dfrac{P}{Q}\).
  • Midpoint formula: divide each change by the AVERAGE of its two values.
  • \(Q = a - bP\): revenue max at \(P^* = a/2b\), \(Q^* = a/2\), |ε| = 1.
  • Log-linear coefficients are the elasticities; \(P^kQ = c\) has ε = −k; fixed spending −1, fixed quantity 0, fixed price −∞, ray supply +1.
  • Shift of s%: \(\%\Delta P = s/(E_s + |E_d|)\); %ΔQ uses the elasticity of the curve that did not move.
  • Signs: cross + substitutes, − complements; income + normal, − inferior; price elasticity of demand negative.
  • Linear supply Q = a + bP: a > 0 inelastic, a = 0 unit, a < 0 elastic, all heading to 1.
  • Determinants: substitutes, time, narrow definition, necessity vs luxury, budget share.

C5.1 Computing an elasticity 28 parts · 26 unique

Looks likeA linear demand Q = a − bP + cPB + dI with one point, or two (P, Q) or (I, Q) observations; "income / cross / price elasticity is ___", "c = ___", "PA = ___".

DoElasticity = (coefficient of X) × X / Q. Solve for any missing value first (I, PB, c), then the rest. Two observations → midpoint formula.

WatchUse the coefficient of the variable asked about; keep its sign; never round the intermediate value (c or PB) before the follow-up.

C5.2 Revenue and the revenue-maximising price 12 parts · 12 unique

Looks likeTwo (price, quantity) observations and "linear demand", or Q = A − bP − kI with an income; "to maximise revenue, price at ___ and sell ___".

DoFit Q = a − bP; P* = a/(2b), Q* = a/2. Income version: plug income in first.

WatchP* may lie outside the observed prices; use the fitted intercept; give both blanks.

C5.3 Constant elasticity, log-linear demand, special consumers 28 parts · 27 unique

Looks likeln Q = k + α ln Px + β ln Py + γ·(…); curves like P²Q = 5; "buys $80 worth / 100 grams regardless of price"; MC = c·q.

DoLogged term → coefficient is the elasticity. Non-logged term → only its sign (coefficient × exponent). Fixed spending −1, fixed quantity 0, fixed price −∞, ray supply +1.

WatchExtra numbers (QX = 150 …) are distractors; a negative exponent flips the sign; unit elastic demand is a curve, not a line.

C5.4 Predicting %ΔP and %ΔQ from a shift 23 parts · 22 unique

Looks likeElasticities of demand and supply plus "demand/supply increases by s%" or "by 315 barrels per day at any given price"; direction blank + size blank.

Do%ΔP = s / (Es + |Ed|); %ΔQ = (elasticity of the curve that did NOT shift) × %ΔP.

WatchConvert barrels to a % of the initial quantity; add the sizes in the denominator; the dollar price is a distractor.

C5.5 Reading shifts: elasticity, revenue, surplus 14 parts · 14 unique

Looks like"Only one curve shifted; P up 2.1%, Q up 8.1%"; "increase in supply will ___ revenue"; Q = a + bP and the sign of a or ab; T/F on surplus after a shift.

DoSame-direction moves trace supply, opposite moves trace demand. Revenue: demand shift → with the shift; supply shift → depends on |Ed|.

WatchDemand elasticity is irrelevant for a demand shift; "always raises surplus" claims are "not necessarily" → False.

C5.7 Determinants of elasticity (ranking pairs) 8 parts · 8 unique

C5.1Computing an elasticity

28 parts, 26 unique · newest first within each form · Note §3 Computing elasticity

Do
  1. Write the general rule: \(\varepsilon_X = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta X}\cdot\dfrac{X}{Q}\), and on a linear demand \(\dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta X}\) is just the coefficient of X (with its sign).
  2. Find what is missing at the point:
    • Income not given → solve the demand equation for I.
    • Cross elasticity given, \(P_B\) unknown → \(P_B = \varepsilon \cdot Q_A / c\).
    • Cross elasticity given, c unknown → \(c = \varepsilon \cdot Q_A / P_B\).
    • "At the revenue-maximising point" → first find \(Q^* = a/2\) (C5.2).
    • Only Q given on Q = a − bP → \(P = (a - Q)/b\).
  3. Plug in. Follow-up "\(P_A\) = ___": put the UNROUNDED value back into the demand equation (\(cP_B = \varepsilon Q_A\) exactly).
  4. Two observations, "mid-point formula": \(\%\Delta Q = \dfrac{Q_1 - Q_0}{(Q_0+Q_1)/2}\), same for X, divide.
Ready answer · linear demand \(Q_A = a - bP_A + cP_B + dI\)

\(\varepsilon_{own} = -b\,\dfrac{P_A}{Q_A}\), \(\varepsilon_{cross} = c\,\dfrac{P_B}{Q_A}\), \(\varepsilon_{I} = d\,\dfrac{I}{Q_A}\). In words: coefficient × (that variable ÷ quantity).

Two-part "work backwards" pairs: \(cP_B = \varepsilon_{cross}\,Q_A\), then \(P_A = \dfrac{a + cP_B - Q_A}{b}\).

Income elasticity from a linear demand 6 questions

2024 Fall Midterm Q26marks not shownincome-from-linear

Background (shared with Q25):

A restaurant in Utopia faces the following demand relation for dim sum:
Q = 1950 − 5P − 1.5I
where P is the price of dim sum (in $ per unit), Q is the quantity of dim sum and I is the income in Utopia (in $). Suppose the income in Utopia is $70.

The income elasticity of demand for dim sum at the revenue-maximizing point will be 26.

Hint

Two steps: find the revenue-maximising point (midpoint of the demand line), then income elasticity there.

Solution
  1. Plug in income: \(a = 1950 - 1.5(70)\) \(= 1845\), so \(Q = 1845 - 5P\).
  2. Revenue is maximised at the midpoint: \(P^* = \dfrac{1845}{2 \times 5} = 184.5\), \(Q^* = \dfrac{1845}{2} = 922.5\).
  3. Income elasticity there: \(\varepsilon_I = (−1.5) \times \dfrac{70}{922.5}\) \(= −0.1138\).

Marker note. Use Q at the revenue-maximising point, not at some other point; the coefficient of I is −1.5, so the good is inferior.

Answer: −0.11

(i) Demand becomes more elastic as quantity increases.
(ii) Any point on the demand curve that is above the midpoint is elastic.
(iii) Supply becomes more elastic as quantity increases if it has a positive x-intercept.
(iv) Any point on a supply curve with a positive x-intercept is inelastic.
40

  • A) 0
  • B) 1
  • C) 2
  • D) 3
  • E) 4
Hint

Linear demand: |ε| falls as Q rises. Supply with an x-intercept: inelastic, ε rises toward 1.

Solution
  1. (i) False: Moving down a linear demand (Q up, P down) makes P/Q smaller, so demand becomes LESS elastic.
  2. (ii) True: Above the midpoint |ε| > 1.
  3. (iii) True: Along supply, more Q means higher P, and ε = bP/(a + bP) rises toward 1.
  4. (iv) True: With a > 0, ε = bP/(a + bP) < 1 everywhere.
  5. 3 true → option D.

Answer: D (3)

Check each statement with εs = bP/(a + bP) for Q = a + bP.
