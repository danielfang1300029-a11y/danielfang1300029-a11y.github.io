Ch.4 Comparative statics · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.4Previous: guide Ch.3Next: guide Ch.5

Every Ch.4 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (175 unique questions, 187 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. Ch.4 is predictable: four of the strategies are sign-and-direction logic, and two are the same linear-market calculation asked in a chain of 3–7 parts. Learn the cards and ready answers, then drill.

0How Ch.4 is examined

  • Where: 24 of the sittings 2020–2025 have a Ch.4 question; the biggest block is midterms (typically Q9–Q30), with a few parts in finals.
  • Two kinds of question. (1) Direction logic: MC "how many events…", T/F, increase/decrease/uncertain blanks, 4-step event sequences. (2) A chained calculation on one linear market: equilibrium → new equilibrium after a fixed shift → CS/PS before and after → lobbying → compensation. One early slip costs every later part, so check the first equilibrium twice.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): an online quiz; fill-in numeric blanks to 2 d.p., A/B choice blanks, MC, True/False. No marks are shown.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.4
  • Own price → movement along the curve; every other factor → shift.
  • Demand shifters (income, related prices, expectations, tastes, buyers) vs supply shifters (input prices, technology, sellers, expectations, joint products).
  • Normal vs inferior; substitutes vs complements; only inferior goods can be Giffen.
  • Two-shift table (C4.3) and one-cause rule: same direction → demand, opposite → supply.
  • \(P^* = \dfrac{(a + k_D) - (c + k_S)}{b + d}\) for \(Q = a - bP\), \(Q = c + dP\).
  • CS = ½ × Q × (choke price − P). PS = ½ × Q × (P − supply start), or the trapezoid ½ × P × (c + Q) when supply has a positive Q-intercept.
  • Locals: original demand + domestic supply. Lobby = |change in surplus|; compensation window = [loss, gain].

C4.1 Read a demand relation 7 parts · 7 unique

Looks likeA demand equation with Px, Py, income (sometimes as a power or inside ln) and population; "x is a normal/inferior good; x and y are substitutes/complements".

DoSign of the income term (how it moves as income RISES) → normal/inferior; sign of the Py term → substitutes (+) or complements (−).

Watch"+ income−0.7" is INFERIOR (the term shrinks as income grows); ignore the own-price slope and population.

C4.2 Classify an event: shift or movement 29 parts · 28 unique

Looks likeA list of events and "how many cause an increase/decrease in demand (or supply, or the price paid)"; T/F on definitions; "movement along demand to the right/left".

DoOwn price → movement only. Otherwise name the side (D or S) and direction; then count only the matches.

WatchSupply-side events never move demand; inferior goods reverse income; joint products: by-product price up → main supply up.

C4.3 Two shifts at once 25 parts · 23 unique

Looks likeTwo events in one market and "price ___, quantity ___ (increase / decrease / uncertain)"; or a price–quantity table and "the single cause is ___".

DoTranslate each event to D±/S±; the variable both push the same way is certain, the other is "uncertain". Data: same direction → demand, opposite → supply.

WatchOne blank is always "uncertain"; labour markets: workers = supply, employers = demand.

C4.4 Chain reactions across linked markets 23 parts · 21 unique

Looks likeTwo linked markets (substitutes, complements, shared input, two transport modes, a currency) and a 4-step event sequence, a (PY, QY) pair, or a count of exchange rates.

DoShift in market 1 → price in market 1 → shift in market 2 → price in market 2. Pairs: both P and Q must move strictly the right way.

WatchShared input = a SUPPLY link. Currency rates are quoted per Utopian dollar: invert. An unchanged P or Q is never right.

C4.5 New equilibrium after a fixed shift 45 parts · 43 unique

Looks likeLinear demand and supply and an extra buyer/seller of k units "at any price" (foreign buyer, buyback, imports, labour import, aid), a fixed budget, a population change, or a world price.

DoWrite both as Q = …; add k to the Q side of the curve it joins (M/P for a fixed budget); solve demand = supply; 2 d.p.

WatchConvert P = … forms first; flat supply → price fixed; small open economy → P = world price, read each curve separately.

C4.6 Who gains, who loses 58 parts · 53 unique

Looks likeThe same sets continued: CS, PS, total before and after; "willing to spend up to ___ to lobby for/against"; compensation window; total surplus with a population inflow.

DoLocal buyers on the ORIGINAL demand, local sellers on DOMESTIC supply, at the new price. Lobby = |change|; compensation window = [loser's loss, winner's gain].

WatchPS is a trapezoid when supply has a positive Q-intercept; read the A/B labels each time (for/against swap places between papers).

C4.1Read a demand relation

7 parts, 7 unique · newest first within each form · Note §3 Demand shifters

Do
  1. Find the income term. Ask: as income rises, does this term rise or fall? (A negative power like income−0.7 FALLS as income rises; a coefficient in front flips it again.) Rises → normal; falls → inferior.
  2. Find the other good's price term. Positive → substitutes; negative → complements. (In ln form, the sign of the ln Py coefficient.)
  3. Ignore the own-price term and population. Pick the option with both words.
Ready answer · reading signs
TermAs income risesGood is
\(+c\cdot\text{INC}\), \(c \gt 0\)Q upnormal
\(-c\cdot\text{INC}\)Q downinferior
\(+\text{income}^{-k}\)term shrinks → Q downinferior
\(-c\,(\text{income})^{-k}\) (also inside ln)term grows → Q upnormal

Py coefficient: + substitutes, − complements. Every paper so far has used one of these four rows.

\(Q_x = 162 - 0.9P_x + 0.5P_y + \text{income}^{-0.5}\)

  • B) x is a normal good; x and y are complements
  • C) x is an inferior good; x and y are substitutes
  • D) x is an inferior good; x and y are complements
Hint

Look only at the SIGN of the income term and of the other good's price term. Ignore the own-price slope and population.

Solution

C4.2Classify an event: shift or movement

29 parts, 28 unique · newest first within each form · Note §4 Shift or movement?

Do
  1. Is the event a change in the good's own price? → movement along the curve; it never shifts anything. Score it "no".
  2. Otherwise decide the side. Demand: income, prices of substitutes/complements, expected future price, tastes/information, number of buyers. Supply: input prices, technology, number of sellers, prices of joint products or other uses of the inputs.
  3. Decide the direction (careful with inferior goods and joint products), then count only events that match exactly what is asked.
  4. "Price paid by buyers rises" = demand up OR supply down. "Movement along demand to the right/left" = supply up/down.
Ready answer · recurring statements
StatementVerdict
The demand curve of an inferior good can be upward slopingTrue (Giffen)
The demand curve of a normal good can be upward sloping / is always downward, vertical or horizontalFalse / True
Supply shows the minimum price sellers must receive for one more unitTrue ("maximum" is False)
Producer surplus = extra units beyond demand / = cost of the marginal sellerFalse (PS is an area)
Marginal benefit = willingness to pay minus priceFalse (that is consumer surplus)
Income up → normal-good demand shifts leftFalse (right)
Complement dearer → normal-good demand shifts left; complement cheaper → rightTrue
Supply curve slopes down only if the good is inferiorFalse
Ready answer · joint products

Main product and by-product (wine & grapeseed oil, low-fat milk & cream): by-product price up → supply of the main product up. Anything that raises demand for the by-product (cheaper fried food, cheaper ice-cream making) works through that price.
