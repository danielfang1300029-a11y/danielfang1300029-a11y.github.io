Ch.9 Public goods and the commons · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.9Previous: guide Ch.8Next: guide Ch.10

Every Ch.9 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (84 unique questions, 88 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. This chapter is very predictable: two calculation templates (vertical summation; commons free entry vs optimum, then a fee) cover 76 of the 88 parts.

0How Ch.9 is examined

  • Where: Finals only (and the 2025 Spring Supp); 14 sittings have a Ch.9 question. Questions come in sets of 3–5 parts on one story.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): fill-in numeric blanks (2 d.p.), "greater than ___ and less than ___" windows, the A/B/C table of kink points, and a few MC items. No marks are shown.
  • Answers are exact. Counts of users are whole numbers; quantities of a public good are 2-d.p. decimals. One wrong early part (the optimum n*) ruins the fee window and the policy value after it, so check n* by comparing neighbours.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.9
  • The 2 × 2 box (rival × excludable) and which types markets provide well.
  • Public good: add MBs vertically; kink at the smaller choke point; check the segment before solving MSB = MC.
  • Many identical payers: MSB = head-count × WTP.
  • Commons: free entry = largest n with own payoff ≥ outside option; optimum maximises total income; for \(a - bn\): \(\lfloor (a-o)/b \rfloor\) vs nearest integer to \((a-o)/2b\).
  • MEC of the n-th user = (n − 1) × fall in each user's payoff.
  • Fee window = (gap of user n* + 1, gap of user n*); value of policy = total(n*) − total(free entry).
  • Negotiation cost MC → perfectly competitive market.

1The six strategies at a glance

Which strategy?
  1. Words "rival", "excludable", or "provided efficiently by free markets"? → C9.1.
  2. Two named people with MB formulas and one shared quantity (fireworks, fountain, lights)? → C9.2. Many residents with a fixed WTP each? → C9.3.

C9.1 Classify goods by rivalry and excludability 4 parts · 3 unique

Looks likeA definition ("non-rival and non-excludable is called ___") or a list of goods: "how many may be provided efficiently by free markets?"

DoTwo yes/no tests (rival? excludable?), then read the 2 × 2 box. Only private goods are provided efficiently by markets.

WatchCommon resource = rival; public good = non-rival (both non-excludable). The key treats crude oil as a common pool.

C9.2 Public good: add two MB curves vertically 18 parts · 18 unique

Looks likeTwo people's linear MB for fireworks / a fountain / street lights; asks for points A, B, C of the kinked total MB, the MSB at some Q, or the optimum for a given MC.

DoMSB = MB₁ + MB₂ (each cut off at 0). Kink at the smaller choke point. Check which segment the MC line hits, then solve MSB = MC there.

WatchAdd PRICES (vertically), not quantities. Do not keep a negative MB in the sum. Use the exact fraction for the kink.

C9.3 Public good: many payers with a constant WTP 5 parts · 5 unique

Looks likeA town of N residents, each (or each group) willing to pay a fixed amount per extra unit; MC is a rising line (100y, 10000 + 20700Q).

DoMSB = Σ (head-count × WTP), a constant. Set MSB = MC (+ any external cost) and solve.

WatchMultiply by the number of people. Turn "three-eighths of 2000" into 750 first. Add environmental cost to MC.

C9.4 Commons / congestion: free entry vs social optimum 32 parts · 30 unique

Looks likeN people choose between a shared resource whose payoff falls with users (cattle, steers, trees, pigeons, beach, a congested road, an ER) and a fixed outside option. Asks "individually ___", "society ___", MEC, total income.

DoIndividually: largest n with own payoff ≥ outside option (round DOWN). Socially: maximise total income of everyone; for linear payoffs round (a − o)/2b to the NEAREST integer.

WatchIntegers only; count non-users in total income; convert time to money only if asked for dollars; equal costs (travel time) cancel.

C9.5 Corrective fee / toll / tax / subsidy and value of the policy 21 parts · 20 unique

Looks likeSame commons story, then: "If a $F toll is charged, how many…", "the fee should be greater than ___ and less than ___", "implement only if C is less than ___".

DoCharge → redo the individual rule with the charge. Window → (gap of user n*+1, gap of user n*). Policy value → total at optimum − total at free entry.

WatchConvert a toll into minutes; subsidies to the alternative give the same window; fee revenue is a transfer, not a cost.

C9.6 Externality carry-over: abatement and negotiation costs 8 parts · 8 unique

Looks likeMC "which situation has the smallest negotiation cost?", or a factory with n² treatment cost and a fixed damage per ton.

DoNegotiation: always the perfectly competitive market. Abatement: treat the k-th ton while 2k − 1 ≤ damage; minimum tax = MC of the last ton treated.

WatchTwo-party deals still involve bargaining; use the MARGINAL treatment cost.

C9.1Classify goods by rivalry and excludability

4 parts, 3 unique · newest first within each form · Note: Four types of goods

Do
  1. Rival? Does my use leave less for you? Excludable? Can a non-payer be kept out cheaply?
  2. Read the box: rival + excludable = private; rival + non-excludable = common resource; non-rival + excludable = club; non-rival + non-excludable = public.
  3. "Provided efficiently by free markets" = private goods only. Public goods are under-provided (free riders); common resources over-used.
Ready answer · the 2 × 2 box
ExcludableNon-excludable
RivalPrivate good (jeans, burger)Common resource (ocean fish, free pasture)
Non-rivalClub good (Netflix, cable TV)Public good (defence, lighthouse, fireworks)

C9.2Public good: add two MB curves vertically

18 parts, 18 unique · newest first within each form · Note: Efficient quantity

Do
  1. Write \(MB_1 = a_1 - b_1Q\), \(MB_2 = a_2 - b_2Q\). Choke points \(a_1/b_1\), \(a_2/b_2\).
  2. A = \(a_1 + a_2\). B: x = the smaller choke point; y = the other person's MB there. C = the larger choke point.
  3. "MB to society at Q": plug Q into both and add (drop a negative one).
  4. Optimum: if MC at B ≥ height of B → steep part: \(a_1 + a_2 - (b_1+b_2)Q = MC\). Otherwise → the remaining person's line alone.
Ready answer · kinked total MB

With \(MB_i = a_i - b_iQ\) and person 1 dropping out first (\(a_1/b_1 \lt a_2/b_2\)):

\(A = a_1 + a_2\); \(B = \left(\tfrac{a_1}{b_1},\ a_2 - b_2\tfrac{a_1}{b_1}\right)\); \(C = \tfrac{a_2}{b_2}\).

Optimum with MC = m + dQ: steep part \(Q = \dfrac{a_1+a_2-m}{b_1+b_2+d}\) if MC at B ≥ height of B; else \(Q = \dfrac{a_2-m}{b_2+d}\).
