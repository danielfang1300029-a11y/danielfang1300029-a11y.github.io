← Start HereChapter note · Ch.8Next: guide Ch.9

Every Ch.8 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (166 unique questions, 171 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. Most of it is predictable arithmetic on equations or process tables; C8.3 and C8.6 need a short decision procedure.

0How Ch.8 is examined

  • Where: finals only (plus the 2025 Spring Supp), in long blocks of 5–12 questions sharing one background (a market with an externality, a process table, or two flatmates). 14 sittings since 2020 have Ch.8 questions.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): online quiz. Fill-in numeric blanks (2 d.p.), letter blanks (tax / subsidy / ceiling / floor; process letters), "larger than ___ and smaller than ___" ranges, multiple choice, True/False and "how many are true". No marks shown.
  • Answers are exact. One wrong number early in a block (Q_m, Q*, the ranking) spoils every later blank, so check the first one twice.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.8
  • \(MSB = D + MEB\), \(MSC = S + MEC\); efficient Q where MSB = MSC; market Q where D = S.
  • Welfare = area between MSB and MSC up to the Q traded; welfare loss = triangle between them from \(Q_m\) to \(Q^*\).
  • Pigouvian amount = net marginal external effect at Q*; external cost → tax, benefit → subsidy. Permit price = tax.
  • Coase: rights well defined + negligible negotiation costs → efficient outcome either way; the right only decides who pays.
  • Bargaining ranges: no-deal surplus to no-deal surplus + whole gain.
  • Process tables: value per ton, rank once; tax range between the last ton kept and the first cut; cost to society excludes taxes.
  • Equal marginal costs of reduction under permits; cost \(n^2\) → marginal cost \(2n - 1\).

C8.1 Social optimum and welfare 43 parts · 43 unique

Looks likeDemand, supply and an MEB/MEC (or an "SMB" equation); blanks for market Q and P, CS, PS, external benefit/cost, efficient Q, total welfare, welfare loss. Also one-off "how many incinerators / hectares".

DoPut every curve in "P = …" form; market: D = S; optimum: MSB = MSC; welfare = area between MSB and MSC up to the Q traded.

WatchInvert "Q = a − k·SMB"; welfare uses SOCIAL curves, CS/PS use private ones; only the NET externality moves Q*; welfare can be negative.

C8.2 Pigouvian tax or subsidy 19 parts · 19 unique

Looks likeSame background as C8.1, then "impose a [tax/subsidy/ceiling/floor] of ___", "who bears more", "CS falls by ___", permit/licence price and revenue, subsidy bill.

DoNet MEC − MEB at Q*: positive → tax, negative → subsidy, of that size. Revenue or bill = amount × Q*.

WatchEvaluate at Q*, not Q_m; ceilings and floors are never the answer; "free to buyers" is a different subsidy.

C8.3 Coase with discrete choices 19 parts · 19 unique

Looks likeTwo technologies or two house designs with gains and damages; who holds the right; a lawyer cost; MC options for the side payment. Per-ton versions on a process table.

DoTotal surplus of each option → efficient one. Zero costs: efficient wins either way. With a cost: start from the right-holder's own choice; switch only if gain > cost.

WatchA liable firm already picks the efficient option (no lawyer). The payment must lie between payer's gain and receiver's loss.

C8.4 Continuous bargaining (MB vs MEC) 19 parts · 14 unique

Looks likeTwo flatmates: MB = a − bQ for one, MEC = cQ for the other; no deal / costless deal / a negotiation cost; "larger than ___ and smaller than ___".

DoNo deal: Q₀ = a/b (or 0). Deal: Q* = a/(b + c). Ranges run from the no-deal surplus to no-deal + whole gain.

WatchThe victim's no-deal surplus is negative; total welfare is the same under either right, the split is not.

C8.5 Least-cost pollution control 60 parts · 60 unique

Looks likeA process table (A–G, tons, cost or profit per firm) or MC-of-reduction lines; unregulated choice, tax → process, tax range for a target, efficient level, demand for emissions, permits, uniform command, cost of control.

DoTurn the table into the value of each ton; rank all tons once; read every follow-up off that ranking.

WatchLetters may run in reverse; profit vs cost tables; "by" vs "to" two-thirds; tax payments are not a social cost.

C8.1Social optimum and welfare

43 parts, 43 unique · newest first within each form · Note §7 Doing the numbers

Do
  1. Write each curve as \(P = a - bQ\) (demand) or \(P = c + dQ\) (supply). From \(Q = 955 - 5P\): \(P = 191 - 0.2Q\).
  2. Externality given as a SMB equation \(Q = a - k\,SMB\)? Invert: \(SMB = a/k - Q/k\). Then \(MEB = SMB - D\).
  3. Market: D = S → \(Q_m, P_m\). CS, PS: triangles on the private curves.
  4. Social: \(MSB = D + MEB\), \(MSC = S + MEC\); \(Q^*\) where they meet.
  5. Welfare at any Q: \(W(Q) = AQ - \tfrac12 BQ^2\) with \(MSB - MSC = A - BQ\). Welfare loss \(= W(Q^*) - W(Q_m) = \tfrac12 B (Q^* - Q_m)^2\).
  6. Projects (incinerators, hectares): MB = construction + environmental cost per unit; whole numbers → discrete MB.
Ready answer · linear social curves

With \(MSB - MSC = A - BQ\): \(Q^* = A/B\); \(W(Q) = AQ - \tfrac12BQ^2\); welfare loss \(= \tfrac12B(Q^* - Q_m)^2\).

Constant MEB or MEC = e: external total = \(e \times Q\). Linear \(MEB = e_0 + e_1Q\): total \(= e_0Q + \tfrac12e_1Q^2\).

Units: thousand units × thousand dollars = million dollars.
