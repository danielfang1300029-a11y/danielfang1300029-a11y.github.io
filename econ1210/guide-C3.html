Ch.3 Supply, demand and market equilibrium · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.3Previous: guide Ch.2Next: guide Ch.4

Every Ch.3 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (93 questions, no repeats) sorted into six strategies. This chapter is predictable and mechanical: the same setups (two Utopias, two consumer groups, a table of marginal values) come back every year with new numbers. Learn the cards and ready answers, then drill for speed.

0How Ch.3 is examined

  • Where: mostly midterms (Q11–Q20), sometimes the opening questions of a final; 23 sittings 2020–2025 have a Ch.3 question.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): an online quiz with numeric blanks, a few A/B choice blanks, True/False and "how many statements are correct". No marks are shown.
  • Questions come in chains. One setup feeds two or three blanks (equilibrium → surplus → welfare loss). An early slip costs every later blank, so check the equilibrium before moving on.
  • Answers are exact to 2 d.p. Keep full precision (calculator memory) until the end. Surplus answers can run to millions; enter all digits.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.3
  • Switch forms: \(P = a - bQ \iff Q = (a - P)/b\); choke price = P at Q = 0.
  • Market curve = horizontal sum: add quantities at the same price, only for groups that are active.
  • Kink at the second group's choke (minimum) price; check every answer's segment.
  • CS = area under demand above price; PS = area above supply below price; on a kinked curve, sum each group's triangle.
  • Table curves: pool and sort (WTP high→low, MC low→high).
  • Free-market Q* maximises total surplus; any other quantity loses the area between the curves.
  • Shortage → buyers bid price up; surplus → sellers cut price; in equilibrium not everyone trades.

C3.2 Horizontal summation of continuous curves 33 parts · 33 unique

Looks likeTwo groups (East/West Utopia, children/adults, two firms) with linear curves; total quantity at a price, CS/PS at a price, WTP or MC at a total quantity, or the three kink points.

C3.3 Discrete market curves from tables 11 parts · 11 unique

Looks likeA table of marginal WTP or marginal cost for the Q-th unit, two or three people; "WTP for the 7th unit", "MC of the 6th unit", "price range for 6 units".

DoPool every entry and sort (WTP high→low, MC low→high); the k-th entry is the k-th unit.

WatchNever read across row k; "assuming a normal good" is irrelevant; range = [k-th, (k+1)-th).

C3.4 Equilibrium with summed (kinked) curves 19 parts · 19 unique

Looks likeTwo consumer groups and one supply (or two supplier groups and one demand); "equilibrium price and quantity", then "consumer surplus at the equilibrium".

DoSum the groups, solve on the two-group segment, CHECK the price against the kink; redo with one group if the check fails.

WatchThe two-group answer can look fine but sit above a group's choke price; one-group CS uses only that group.

C3.5 Welfare loss from a forced quantity 11 parts · 11 unique

Looks likeSame setup as C3.4, then "reduce by 50%", "force 325 units", "completely ban", or a licence cap.

DoLoss = area between demand and supply from the forced quantity to Q*; ban = whole CS + PS.

WatchSplit the area at a kink; over-production also loses surplus; licence: wage off the demand curve.

C3.6 Equilibrium and disequilibrium logic 8 parts · 8 unique

Looks likeTrue/False or "how many are correct" about who trades in equilibrium and who moves the price.

DoApply the two rules: at P* only some buyers/sellers trade; under a shortage buyers bid up, under a surplus sellers cut.

WatchThe false statements swap the actor (sellers under excess demand, buyers under excess supply) or say "all".

C3.1One demand, one supply: read, solve, surplus

11 parts, 11 unique · newest first within each form · Note §9 Market equilibrium

Do
  1. Write both curves in the same form. Price form \(P = a - bQ\), \(P = c + dQ\) → \(Q^* = (a-c)/(b+d)\), then \(P^* = a - bQ^*\).
  2. Reading horizontally (quantity at a price): plug P in. Vertically (price at a quantity, or highest WTP = choke price): plug Q in, or set Q = 0.
  3. Excess demand at an imposed price: \(Q_d - Q_s\). Expenditure = \(P \times Q\).
  4. Surplus: CS = \(\tfrac12 Q^*(a - P^*)\), PS = \(\tfrac12 Q^*(P^* - c)\). A price change: CS change = trapezoid between the two prices.
  5. One trader: buyer surplus = WTP − price; seller surplus = price − reservation price.
Ready answer · price-form line pair

\(P = a - bQ\), \(P = c + dQ\): \(Q^* = \dfrac{a-c}{b+d}\), \(P^* = a - bQ^*\), \(\text{CS} = \tfrac12 b (Q^*)^2\), \(\text{PS} = \tfrac12 d (Q^*)^2\).

Quantity form \(Q = A - kP\): choke price \(A/k\).

Equilibrium price and quantity 2 questions

2024 Fall Final Q9marks not shownequilibrium

Setup (shared with Q10):

Let the market demand curve for Coca Cola be \(P = 56 - 5.9Q\) and the supply curve be \(P = 22 + 1.3Q\).

The equilibrium price is $09A. The equilibrium quantity is 09B.

Hint

Single linear demand and supply in price form: set them equal, solve for Q, then plug back for P.

Solution
  1. Both curves are in price form; set them equal: \(56 - 5.9Q = 22 + 1.3Q\).
  2. Q* = (56 − 22)/(5.9 + 1.3) = 34/7.2 = 4.7222.
  3. \(P^* = 56 - 5.9 \times 4.7222 = 28.1389\).

Marker note. Keep Q* unrounded when you compute P*; rounding Q* first moves P* in the second decimal.

Answer: 28.14, 4.72

One trader's surplus 1 question

2024 Fall Final Q11marks not shownone trader

Maggie tries to sell her used Economics textbook online. She asks for $211 as best offer, but is willing to sell for anything over $59. Brian submits an offer of $134, which Maggie accepts. Maggie's 11A (A. consumer; B. producer) surplus is $11B.

Hint

One trader's surplus: seller gets price − lowest acceptable price. Is Maggie buying or selling?

Solution
  1. Maggie is the seller, so her gain is producer surplus.
  2. Producer surplus = price received − reservation price = 134 − 59 = 75.
  3. The $211 asking price is never paid; it plays no part.

Marker note. The asking ("best offer") price is a distractor; only the agreed price and the reservation price matter.

Answer: B (producer), 75.00

C3.2Horizontal summation of continuous curves

33 parts, 33 unique · newest first within each form · Note §4 Market demand · Note §7 Market supply

Do
  1. Rewrite every group as \(Q = \dots\) and note its choke price (demand: P at Q = 0) or minimum price (supply: P at Q = 0).
  2. Quantity at a price: add the quantities of the groups that are active at that price; the rest give 0.
  3. CS / PS at a price: sum each active group's own triangle: \(\tfrac12 q_i (a_i - P)\) or \(\tfrac12 q_i (P - c_i)\).
  4. WTP or MC at a total quantity: kink quantity = first group's Q at the second group's choke (minimum) price. If the total is at most that, invert the first group alone; otherwise invert the summed segment.
  5. Kink points: A = (0, higher choke), B = (first group's Q at the lower choke, lower choke), C = (sum of Q-intercepts, 0).
Ready answer · two linear groups

Price form \(P = a_i - b_iQ\) ↔ quantity form \(Q_i = (a_i - P)/b_i\). Market (both active): \(Q = \sum a_i/b_i - P\sum 1/b_i\).

Kink quantity \(= (a_1 - a_2)/b_1\) (demand, \(a_1 \gt a_2\)); \((c_2 - c_1)/d_1\) (supply, \(c_1 \lt c_2\)).

C3.3Discrete market curves from tables

11 parts, 11 unique · newest first within each form · Note §8 Curves from tables

Do
  1. Pool every number in the table into one list.
  2. Demand table: sort high → low. Supply table: sort low → high. The k-th entry is the marginal WTP / MC of the k-th unit of the market.
  3. At a price: each supplier makes every unit with MC ≤ P (each buyer buys every unit with WTP ≥ P).
  4. Exactly k units: \(\text{MC}_{(k)} \le P \lt \text{MC}_{(k+1)}\). Efficient split of k units = the k cheapest entries.
Ready answer
