Ch.12 Price discrimination · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.12Previous: guide Ch.11

Every Ch.12 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (98 unique questions, 99 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. Five of them are pure calculation with a fixed recipe; the sixth is a True/False set. Learn the cards and ready answers, then drill.

0How Ch.12 is examined

  • Where: finals only (plus the 2025 Spring Supplementary), as the closing block of the paper (about Q54–Q74). 14 sittings have a Ch.12 question. Midterms never include it.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): online quiz with numeric blanks, A/B choice blanks (e.g. "A. as a combo; B. separately"), True/False and "how many are true". Usually one scenario feeds 3–6 blanks in a row, so an early slip costs several marks. No marks are shown on the papers.
  • Answers are exact to 2 d.p. Keep 4 decimals inside a calculation (prices like 5.763…, 8.192…) and round only at the end.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.12
  • Single price from a WTP list: try each WTP, profit = count × (P − MC) − FC.
  • Perfect PD: serve all WTP ≥ MC; profit = Σ(WTP − MC); CS = 0; DWL = 0; MR = demand.
  • Coupon hurdle = two separate single-price markets split at the cut-off.
  • Linear group \(Q = a - bP\): \(P = \tfrac12(a/b + MC)\); profit \(Q^2/b\); CS = DWL = \(Q^2/(2b)\).
  • Same choke price ⇒ same price ⇒ discrimination changes nothing.
  • Arbitrage is blocked when cost per unit ≥ price gap (the gap is the lowest blocking cost).
  • Bundling with two buyers: for each good and for the bundle, take the larger of 2(low WTP − c) and (high WTP − c), with c = 2MC + packaging for a bundle.
  • Coupon value = demand price at the rationed quantity − set price.

C12.2 Third-degree PD with two linear demands 32 parts · 32 unique

Looks likeTwo groups, each \(Q = a - bP\), constant MC; single-price regime vs dual-price regime: prices, quantities, Δprofit, ΔCS, ΔDWL, lobbying, regulation value.

DoGroup price = ½(choke + MC). Single price = same rule on the summed demand. Profit Q²/b, CS = DWL = Q²/(2b) per group.

WatchSame choke price → identical prices, every "difference" is 0; DWL single = ½(P − MC)·B(P − MC); keep 4 d.p. inside.

C12.3 Segmented markets and arbitrage 7 parts · 7 unique

Looks likeTwo separated markets, one with an unknown intercept Y; "same price if Y = __", "Q and P in market B", "lowest transport cost that prevents arbitrage".

DoMonopoly price in each = ½(choke + MC). Equal prices ⇔ equal choke prices. Blocking cost = price gap.

WatchChoke = intercept ÷ slope; the gap is the LOWEST blocking cost ("higher than" the gap).

C12.4 Bundling vs separate sale 13 parts · 13 unique

Looks likeTwo buyers × two goods WTP table (numbers or multiples of x), maybe MC, packaging, fixed cost; "separately or combo, profit __".

DoEach good: max{2(low − MC), high − MC}. Bundle: same on summed values with cost 2MC + packaging. Pick the larger; subtract fixed cost once.

WatchA combo needs two items (2 × MC); pricing at the high WTP can win; read whether a blank wants price, profit or the letter.

Looks likeLinear shirt demand, a low set price P₀ and a fixed quantity ("hunger marketing"); shortage, coupon value, then a CMO selling coupons through a middleman with commission = P₀.

C12.1Reservation-price tables

20 parts, 20 unique · newest sitting first; a scenario's parts stay in paper order · Note §2 Single price · Note §3 First degree · Note §4 Hurdles

Do
  1. Sort the reservation prices from high to low. Cross out anyone below MC for every scheme (they are never served).
  2. Single price: only a WTP can be the best price. For each, profit = (number with WTP ≥ P) × (P − MC) − FC. Pick the largest.
  3. Perfect PD: sell to everyone with WTP ≥ MC; profit = Σ(WTP − MC) − FC; CS = 0; DWL = 0. This is also the efficient quantity.
  4. Coupons (hurdle): split the list at the cut-off. List price = step 2 on the no-coupon group only; discount price = step 2 on the coupon group only. Coupon value = list − discount.
    • No profitable discount price → the key prints 99999 (no discount sale).
    • CS = Σ(WTP − price paid); PS = sum of the two group profits (before FC); DWL = Σ(WTP − MC) over buyers with WTP ≥ MC who don't buy.
  5. Curves (P = A − bQ, MC = c + dQ) with perfect PD: Q where demand = MC; PS = ½·Q·(A − c); CS = DWL = 0.
Ready answer · reservation prices

Single price \(P^*\) = the WTP that maximises (count ≥ P) × (P − MC). Perfect PD profit = Σ(WTP − MC) over WTP ≥ MC. Coupons = two separate single-price searches; the list price never changes because of the coupons.
