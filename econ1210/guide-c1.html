Ch.1 Cost–benefit analysis · Exam guide

← Start HereChapter note · Ch.1Next: guide Ch.2

Every Ch.1 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (104 unique questions, 110 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. This chapter is predictable: learn the six cards below and the ready answers, then drill.

0How Ch.1 is examined

  • Where: the opening questions (usually Q2–Q8) of almost every midterm and final; 26 of the sittings 2020–2025 have a Ch.1 question.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): an online quiz. Fill-in numeric blanks, A/B direction blanks ("larger / smaller than ___"), multiple choice, "how many statements are true", True/False. No marks are shown on the papers.
  • Answers are exact. Enter numbers to 2 decimal places. A near miss scores nothing, so finish the arithmetic rather than estimating.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.1
  • Opportunity cost = money paid for the activity + net value of the single best alternative given up.
  • The four job-wording rules (C1.1 ready answer).
  • Sunk = already paid and not recoverable → ignore. Refundable ticket is worth max(valuation, refund).
  • Marginal rule: do more while \(MB \ge MC\). Discrete \(MB(n) = TB(n) - TB(n-1)\).
  • \(TB = an - bn^2\): \(MB = a - 2bn\); optimum \(n^* = (a-P)/2b\); surplus \(b(n^*)^2\); integer answer = nearest whole number.
  • Split formula \(n = (a_1 - a_2 + b_2T)/(b_1+b_2)\) and the corner test.
  • Revealed bounds \(MB(n+1) \lt P \le MB(n)\).

1The six strategies at a glance

Which strategy?
  1. One activity vs a list of alternatives, asking "should she…" or "opportunity cost is…"? → C1.1. If tickets, rent or refunds decide it → C1.2.
  2. A formula for one activity (TB, MB) and a price? → C1.3. The choice is given and a price/valuation is unknown → C1.4.
  3. A fixed total split between two uses with formulas → C1.5; with a table of totals → C1.6.

C1.1 Opportunity-cost ledger 30 parts · 28 unique

Looks likeA choice between one activity and two or three alternatives, with a fee, wages, and "willing to pay" amounts; "X is at least ___", "opportunity cost is ___".

DoWrite each option's net value; compare the activity with the BEST alternative only; solve the inequality for the unknown.

WatchCosts paid either way cancel; job-wording rules; the OC adds the ticket price to the one best alternative, never all of them.

C1.2 Sunk vs avoidable costs 13 parts · 13 unique

Looks likeTwo tickets for events at the same time (refundable or not) and an offer to swap; or a trip with a ferry ticket, rent, food and lost wages.

DoTicket: worth max(watching, refund) if refundable, watching only if not. Trip: add only costs you avoid by staying home.

WatchNon-refundable = sunk = 0; food counts only as the EXTRA over normal days; ticket prices are not valuations.

C1.3 Marginal rule for one activity 14 parts · 14 unique

Looks likeTB, MB (and maybe AB, TC) formulas for one activity and a price per unit; "consume ___", "economic surplus ___".

DoDivisible: MB = price, then surplus = TB − cost. Whole numbers: discrete MB = TB(n) − TB(n−1), last n with MB ≥ price.

WatchNever use AB; round a whole-number answer to the NEAREST integer of the continuous one; fixed costs never move n.

C1.4 Marginal rule read backwards 6 parts · 6 unique

Looks likeYou are told what someone chose (22 hours, 28 movies, bought at $7 but not at $19) and asked for bounds on a price or valuation.

DoLast unit chosen passed, next failed: MB(n+1) < price ≤ MB(n). For yes/no purchases, bracket the valuation.

WatchFixed subscription fees do not enter; "cost of consuming" is the price, so it changes when the price does.

C1.5 Splitting a fixed total with formulas 16 parts · 15 unique

Looks likeA fixed total (hours, cups, land, budget) split between two uses, each with an MB (or MC) formula; AB given as a distractor.

DoCorner test first, then set MB₁(n) = MB₂(T − n) (MC for cost) and solve for n.

WatchCorner answers are common (8 of 15); equalise MARGINAL values; enter 2 d.p.

C1.6 Splitting from a table 31 parts · 28 unique

Looks likeA table of TOTAL output (or total cost) for 1–8 workers in three places; usually a pair of questions: allocate N, then a follow-up.

DoTurn columns into marginal values; hand units out one at a time to the best next marginal value; reuse that order for follow-ups.

WatchWork with differences, not totals; cost tables go to the LOWEST marginal cost; "hire at a wage" = count marginals ≥ wage.

C1.1Opportunity-cost ledger

30 parts, 28 unique · newest first within each form · Note §8 Opportunity & sunk cost

Do
  1. List every option, including the one being asked about. Next to each write its net value = what it is worth to the person − what it costs them in money.
    • "Willing to pay $V" → worth V. "Earns $w per hour for h hours" → w × h.
    • A cost paid in every option (meals, rent you pay anyway) → leave it out.
    • A cost paid only if you don't choose the activity (rent at home) → it is a saving of the activity.
  2. Take the largest net value among the alternatives (one number).
  3. Threshold questions: write "activity ≥ best alternative" (or ">" as worded) and solve for the unknown. The A/B blank is the direction of that inequality.
  4. "Opportunity cost of going" questions: money paid for the activity + best alternative.
Ready answer · job wording
WordingNet value of the job
"enjoys it just enough that he is willing to do it for free"its pay
"willing to pay the MTR fare of $F to reach the job and do it for free"its pay (enjoyment F and fare F cancel)
"would be unwilling to do it for less than $R" (unpleasant)pay − R
"indifferent between doing one or the other for free"money only

Threshold form: activity worth \(B - \text{fee}\) vs alternatives \(A_1, A_2\): do it iff \(B - \text{fee} \ge \max(A_1, A_2)\).

Ready answer · True/False on college enrolment

Boom (high wages) → OC of college up → enrolment falls. Recession → OC down → enrolment rises. Fewer alternatives → OC of X down → X more likely. Every printed statement so far reverses one of these, so all have been False.

2025 Spring Supp Q5marks not shownthreshold

Calvin is deciding whether to pursue a one-year overseas full-time master programme. The total tuition fee and the total overseas accommodation expenses are respectively $114000 and $28000. If Calvin doesn't pursue the master programme, he needs to pay a yearly rent at his domestic flat for $66000, and he can pursue either one of the following two activities: (1) be a domestic volunteer helper, and he is willing to pay $17000 to be a domestic volunteer helper for a year; or (2) work as a full-time domestic research assistant that would earn $28000 for a year. In addition, Calvin is willing to pay $X to get the master qualification to improve his competitiveness in the job market. Based on the information, Calvin should choose to pursue the master programme if X is 05A (A. larger, B. smaller) than 05B.

Hint

Ledger: two options (master vs stay). The $66000 rent is paid only if he stays at home.

Solution
  1. Master: benefit \(X\), plus the $66000 rent he no longer pays; costs 114000 + 28000 = 142000.
  2. Stay: best alternative = max(volunteer 17000, research assistant 28000) = 28000.
  3. Go if \(X + 66000 - 142000 \gt 28000\), i.e. \(X \gt 104000\).

Marker note. The rent is a cost of staying, so going abroad saves it. Do not add it to the cost of the master.

Answer: A (larger), 104000
