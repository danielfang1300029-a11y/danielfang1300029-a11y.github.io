Every Ch.10 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (108 unique questions, 111 parts with repeats) sorted into six strategies. This chapter is predictable: the same background sets come back with new numbers. Learn the formulas in the ready-answer boxes, then drill.

0How Ch.10 is examined

  • Where: Finals only (and the 2025 Spring Supplementary), usually a long block of 5–8 questions sharing one background (Q41–Q64 range). 14 sittings 2020–2025 have Ch.10 questions.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): online quiz. Mostly numeric fill-in blanks; a few letter blanks (which option, higher / lower / same) and one MC on the type of industry. No marks are shown on the papers.
  • Answers are exact numbers to 2 d.p. A set of eight questions chains its answers, so one slip costs several marks. Carry full precision and round only what you type.
  • Key quirk: the 2025 Spring Supplementary key rounded early (q* = 7.84, slope 1.90), so its printed answers differ from exact values in the second decimal or more. See ★ Answer-key notes.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.10
  • Hire while \(P \times MP \ge w\); profit = PQ − wL − FC; fixed cost never changes the short-run choice.
  • Shut-down price = min AVC; exit price = min AC; supply = rising branch of MC above min AVC.
  • \(AVC = q^2 + bq + c\): minimum at \(q = -b/2\).
  • \(TC = Aq^2 + Bq + F\): shut-down B, exit \(B + 2\sqrt{AF}\), \(q^* = \sqrt{F/A}\), SR industry supply \(P = B + (2A/n)Q\).
  • LR (free entry): P = min AC, profit 0, \(n = Q/q^*\). Constant cost: LR price never changes.
  • MC = q, TVC = 0.5q²: \(q^* = \sqrt{2FC} = P\).
  • SR vs LR comparison table (C10.6 ready answer).

C10.1 Hiring from a production table 13 parts · 13 unique

Looks likeA table of workers → total output, a wage, a fixed cost and a price; "hire ___ workers and produce ___", "profit ___", "MP of the k-th worker".

DoDifferences give MP; hire while P × MP ≥ wage; profit = P × Q − wage × L − FC.

WatchMP is a difference of rows, not output ÷ workers; a new fixed cost never changes hiring; keep the minus sign on a loss.

C10.2 One firm's cost curves 17 parts · 17 unique

Looks likeAVC and MC formulas (quadratic) or a cost-curve figure with letters; "shut down below ___", "AVC minimised at ___", "quantity supplied ___", "fixed cost ___".

DoMin AVC at q = −b/2 gives the shut-down price; supply = larger root of MC = P; FC = (AC − AVC) × q; profit = (P − AVC)q − FC.

WatchTake the rising root of MC = P; shut-down uses min AVC, not min AC; in the figure, AVC's falling branch also crosses the price line.

C10.3 Industry of identical firms, SR & LR 38 parts · 38 unique

Looks likeTC = Aq² + Bq + F for a typical hotel/firm and a linear demand; eight-question sets on shut-down/exit prices, SR supply P = A + BQ, SR and LR equilibrium, a shock.

DoShut-down B; exit B + 2√(AF) at q* = √(F/A); SR supply P = B + (2A/n)Q; LR: P = min AC, Q from demand, n = Q/q*.

WatchThe SR slope is 2A/n, not 2A; profit subtracts F; after a shock, n is fixed in the SR; the 2025 key rounds early.

C10.4 Constant-cost industry after a demand shift 15 parts · 15 unique

Looks likeLinear demand and SR supply (equations or a figure) at a LR equilibrium; "WTP rises by $w"; "k new firms enter"; "AC is minimised at q = ___".

DoInitial P, Q from demand = supply; LR price unchanged; new Q = old Q + (demand slope) × w; q = ΔQ ÷ entrants; AC = TC/q = P.

WatchDo not intersect the new demand with the old SR supply (that is the short run); WTP up by w shifts price, not quantity.

C10.5 Increasing-cost industry 17 parts · 14 unique

Looks likeMC = q, TVC = 0.5q² (or AVC = αq + β) and a LR price; then FC depends on the number of firms, FC = C × n/100.

Doq = P at the LR equilibrium; n = Q/q; FC = q²/2 → C; new n → new FC → q = √(2FC) = P.

WatchFC is per firm and changes with n; the option letter for "increasing cost" moves between sittings.

C10.6 SR vs LR comparison blanks 11 parts · 11 unique

Looks likeBlanks "P1 is ___ P0", "q2 is ___ q1", "π2 is ___ π0" (higher / lower / same) after a demand change in a constant- or decreasing-cost industry.

DoShort run: n fixed, everything moves with demand. Long run: profit back to 0; constant cost → P2 = P0, q2 = q0; decreasing cost → P2 below P0.

Watchπ0 = 0 in any LR equilibrium; compare P2 with P1 and with P0 separately; decreasing cost after an increase: P2 < P0 < P1.

C10.1Hiring from a production table

13 parts, 13 unique · newest first within each form · Note §4 How much to produce · Note §13 Exam toolkit

Do
  1. Write the marginal product of each worker: output with n workers − output with n − 1.
  2. Value each worker's extra output: \(P \times MP\) (the extra revenue that worker brings).
  3. Hire every worker with \(P \times MP \ge w\); stop at the first who fails. Output = the table's total at that headcount.
  4. Profit \(= P \times Q - w \times L - FC\). If revenue would not even cover the wage bill, hire nobody (short-run shut-down).
  5. "The fixed cost becomes …": same headcount and output; only profit changes.
Ready answer · hiring

Hire L = last worker with \(P \times (Q_L - Q_{L-1}) \ge w\). Profit \(= PQ_L - wL - FC\). Fixed cost: affects profit only.

C10.2One firm's cost curves

17 parts, 17 unique · newest first within each form · Note §6 Shut-down condition · Note §13 Exam toolkit

Do
  1. Formulas \(AVC = q^2 + bq + c\), \(MC = 3q^2 + 2bq + c\): AVC is lowest at \(q = -b/2\); the shut-down price is AVC there.
  2. Quantity supplied at price P: solve \(MC = P\), keep the larger root (MC's rising branch). When \(P = c\) this is \(q = -2b/3\).
  3. Fixed cost: \(AC - AVC = FC/q\) (e.g. "+ 28/q" → FC = 28). "Zero profit at P" → \(FC = (P - AVC)q\).
  4. Profit \(= (P - AVC) \times q - FC\).
  5. Figure: FC = (AC − AVC) × q at the quantity where both are labelled (z: a − c). If price c ≥ min AVC e, produce where the price line meets MC (x); profit = (c − d) × x − FC.
Ready answer · \(AVC = q^2 + bq + c\)

\(q_{\min} = -b/2\); shut-down price \(= c - b^2/4\). At \(P = c\): supply \(q = -2b/3\), \(AVC = c - 2b^2/9\), profit \(= \tfrac{4|b|^3}{27} - FC\).

Every formula set 2020–2023 used P = c, so the factorisation \(q(3q + 2b) = 0\) does the work.

Ready answer · cost-curve figure

FC = (a − c) × z. At price c: produce x (option C), profit = (c − d) × x − (a − c) × z.
