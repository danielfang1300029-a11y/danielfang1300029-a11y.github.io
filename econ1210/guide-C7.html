Every Ch.7 question from 2020 Spring to 2025 Spring Supplementary (122 unique questions, 126 parts with repeats) sorted into seven strategies. Most of it is predictable algebra on two straight lines; the statement sets need the checklist in C7.1.

0How Ch.7 is examined

  • Where: finals only (plus the 2025 Spring Supp), usually as a long set of 4–8 linked blanks on one market; 14 sittings have Ch.7 questions. Later parts reuse earlier answers, so one slip costs several blanks.
  • Format (unchanged since 2020): numeric blanks to 2 d.p., A/B choice blanks ("shortage / surplus", "at least / at most", "higher / lower"), MC, "how many are true", True/False. No marks are shown.
  • Exact answers. Keep 4 decimals inside a chain and round only what you enter.
  • No formula sheet. Memorise the box below.
Memorise for Ch.7
  • Binding: ceiling below Pe, floor above Pe. Traded quantity = the short side.
  • Q-form to price form: \(Q = A - BP\) has price intercept \(A/B\); \(Q = mP - k\) starts at \(P = k/m\).
  • Ceiling: PS triangle to \(P_c\); max CS = triangle + rectangle up to \(h = P_d(Q_s)\); DWL triangle between \(Q_s\) and \(Q_e\).
  • Bribe = waiting cost per unit = \(h - P_c\); CS = \(\tfrac12 Q_s(a - h)\); waiting adds \((h - P_c)Q_s\) to the loss.
  • Buyback adds G to quantity; to reach zero: floor ≥ a, tax ≥ a − c, buy \(Q_s(a)\).
  • Subsidy: \(s = P_s - P_b\) at one Q; spending \(sQ\); DWL \(\tfrac12 s(Q - Q_e)\).

C7.1 Is the control binding? Shortage / surplus 47 parts · 43 unique

Looks likeA ceiling or floor (or minimum wage, price guarantee) on a pair of linear curves or a schedule; "shortage of ___", "quantity transacted ___"; T/F and "how many can happen".

DoFind Pe; test binding (ceiling below, floor above); plug the control into both curves; traded = the short side.

WatchNon-binding controls (a $99 floor under Pe = 100); "must/always" statements; price controls never SHIFT a curve.

C7.2 Surplus and DWL under a binding control 24 parts · 24 unique

Looks like"Producer surplus ___ and maximum possible consumer surplus ___", "minimum possible deadweight loss", "limit lost gains from trade to X: minimum C".

DoQ = short side; the traded units go to the best buyers and cheapest sellers; CS (ceiling) or PS (floor) is a trapezoid; DWL is the triangle back to Qe.

WatchUse demand height at Qs, not at Qd; convert Q-form equations to price intercepts; reverse DWL: binding root.

C7.3 Bribery vs waiting in line 21 parts · 21 unique

Looks likeA ceiling with a shortage and "competitive bribery", "waiting in line", or a list of 7 people's WTP.

DoFull price = demand height at Qs. Bribe (or time cost) = full price − ceiling. CS = triangle above the full price. Waiting adds (bribe × Qs) to the loss.

WatchCS + PS is the same under both; with discrete WTP the bribe must beat the FIRST LOSER's net value.

C7.4 Ceiling on an input and policy choice 8 parts · 8 unique

Looks likeA ceiling on crude oil / boba, an input supply curve Q = kP, maybe "10% goes to this market"; then "which policy maximises CS?".

DoInput quantity = k × ceiling (× share) caps output; price from DEMAND at that Q; CS = ½Q × (intercept − P). Compare CS across options.

WatchThe output price RISES; apply the share before demand; waiting-in-line CS < ideal CS.

C7.5 Seller quality response (dilution) 4 parts · 4 unique

Looks likeVodka diluted "100 to x": cost falls by x, WTP falls by 2x, price stays at the ceiling.

DoSolve Qd(Pc + 2x) = Qs(Pc + x) for x; TS = area between the shifted curves.

WatchPrice = ceiling; surplus uses the NEW curves.

C7.6 Driving quantity to zero: buyback vs floor vs tax 12 parts · 12 unique

Looks likeFirearms / cannabis with P = a − bQ, P = c + dQ; "government purchases G units", "reduce to zero", "suppliers lobby".

DoBuyback adds G to quantity. Zero private quantity needs P ≥ a; buyback buys Qs(a) at a; floor ≥ a; tax ≥ a − c; lobbying = buyback PS.

WatchAdd G horizontally; floor direction "at least"; floor and tax leave sellers PS = 0.

C7.7 Subsidy as the alternative 10 parts · 10 unique

Looks like"Instead, a subsidy so that buyers pay ___ / workers get ___ / usage rises by ___": subsidy, spending, DWL, which policy is better.

DoFix the target side, read Q off that curve, read the other price at that Q; s = gap; spending = sQ; DWL = ½ s (Q − Qe).

WatchSpending uses the subsidised Q; subsidy DWL is unchanged by a parallel demand shift; any DWL > 0 is worse than no policy.

C7.1Is the control binding? Shortage / surplus

47 parts, 43 unique · newest first within each form · Note §1 Ceilings: binding?

Do
  1. Find the unregulated equilibrium (\(Q_d = Q_s\)). Keep 4 decimals.
  2. Binding test. Ceiling binds only if \(P_c \lt P_e\); floor / minimum wage / guarantee only if \(P_f \gt P_e\). Not binding → nothing changes (Q = Qe).
  3. Binding: plug the control price into both curves. Shortage = \(Q_d - Q_s\); surplus (or unemployment) = \(Q_s - Q_d\). Traded = the smaller one.
  4. Only demand given plus "Q units are traded"? Excess demand = \(Q_d(P_c) - Q\).
  5. Statements: draw the picture; ask (a) can it be non-binding? (b) which side is short? (c) is it a movement along a curve (price controls) or a shift (only events like income, tastes, costs)?
